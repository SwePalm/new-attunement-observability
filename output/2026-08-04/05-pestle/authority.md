### Political
Core Shift Thesis:
Authority over frontier AI is being acquired by assertion rather than adjudication: the EU AI Office took competence upward before national capacity existed, and the FTC claimed preemptive force from a federal scheme with no binding output standards.
Forces:
1. The Commission's AI Office gained direct enforcement powers over general-purpose AI model providers on 2 Aug 2026 (model access for evaluation, corrective measures, market withdrawal requests, fines up to the higher of EUR 15 million or 3 percent of worldwide annual turnover), with no formal proceeding opened against a named provider as of 3 Aug 2026.
2. The Digital Omnibus on AI (in force late Jul 2026) gave the AI Office exclusive supervisory competence over AI systems built on a provider's own GPAI models and over systems integrated into very large online platforms and search engines, relocating competence away from national authorities that mostly do not exist: only 9 of 27 member states had clearly designated both a market surveillance and a notifying authority as of 17 Jun 2026 (12 partial, 6 unclear).
3. The FTC voted 2-0 on 1 Jul 2026 to propose that state AI laws are impliedly preempted where they conflict with a federal regulatory scheme, naming Colorado's AI Act, published in the Federal Register 7 Jul 2026 with comments closing 31 Jul 2026: preemption pursued through administrative reinterpretation rather than statute or ruling, read by commentators as "signaling and pressure, not legal preemption".
4. The electoral market operates as a substitute venue while adjudication stalls: USD 60 million of Big Tech and AI corporate spending in the 2026 midterm cycle, USD 50.1 million of it to the Leading the Future PAC, Meta separately spending USD 65 million toward non-federal super PACs opposing state lawmakers who support AI regulation, and Anthropic pledging USD 20 million to a group backing lawmakers who favour regulating AI.

### Economic
Core Shift Thesis:
With no adjudicated rule, compliance is priced against posture rather than law: firms either build to the strictest operative statute or hold at the weakest level pending preemption, and the gap between those two budgets becomes a competitive variable.
Forces:
1. Illinois SB 315 (signed 6 Jul 2026) puts dated prices on the strict posture: disclosure statements from 1 Jan 2027, an annually retained independent third-party auditor from 1 Jan 2028, and civil penalties up to USD 1 million initial and USD 3 million subsequent, enforced by the Illinois Attorney General. Inference, not fact: a third-party frontier audit supply market must be constituted to serve this, and none is recorded in the sweep.
2. EU exposure is now indexed to group revenue rather than local activity, since the 3 percent figure is of worldwide annual turnover, which converts the EU from a per-market compliance cost line into a consolidated balance-sheet risk.
3. Political expenditure is competing with compliance investment as a route to a favourable rule. Sweep-confirmed: the USD 50.1 million and USD 65 million figures. Inference, not fact: that firms are treating these as substitutes rather than complements, since no firm's allocation reasoning is in the record.
4. The federal funding lever remains unpriced: NTIA Administrator Arielle Roth said on 30 Jun 2026 that non-deployment guidance was targeted for "this summer", it had not issued, and the only state-law condition attached to BEAD participation described in the reporting concerns California's net neutrality law rather than AI statutes, so USD 42 billion of nominal leverage carries no discount rate for state legislatures.

### Social
Core Shift Thesis:
Protection is allocated by residue rather than design: whether a person sits inside a governed perimeter now depends on whether their legislature held its nerve under preemption pressure, and which grievance channel exists depends on whether their member state ever constituted an authority.
Forces:
1. State participation contracted while stringency rose: 29 states with 109 AI laws as of 1 Jul 2026 against 39 states with 121 laws at the same point in 2025, with part of the drop attributed to federal preemption pressure and no clean partisan or BEAD-driven pattern, in the same quarter Illinois enacted the country's strictest frontier statute.
2. The states still legislating are targeting everyday exposure rather than frontier models: New Jersey enacted the FAIR Act on algorithmic rent pricing on 20 Jul 2026, and roughly 30 California AI bills were live with appropriations hearings set for 3-5 Aug 2026.
3. For residents of the 18 member states without both authorities designated, the practical route for a complaint runs to a supranational office rather than a national one. Inference, not fact: no complaint pathway has been tested under the post-Omnibus allocation, and the structural point rests on the designation data alone.
4. The external pattern reappears inside firms as escalation into offices without leverage: peer-reviewed mapping of 351 organisations found AI oversight roles unevenly distributed, usually placed below executive level, and frequently symbolic in the specific sense of a title without authority or resources (graded this run against authority-2026-02-04).

### Technological
Core Shift Thesis:
While constitutional authority stays unadjudicated, operational authority is being specified, versioned and shipped: authorization protocols are turning "who authorised what" into a machine-checked claim evaluated at the moment of action rather than years later in a courtroom.
Forces:
1. The OpenID Foundation's AuthZEN working group published the Access Request and Approval Profile and a Model Context Protocol tool authorization profile on 15 Jun 2026, making delegated authority, approval, attestation and risk assessment first-class inputs to an authorization decision when an agent requests a high-value action, rather than out-of-band workarounds.
2. IETF Internet-Draft draft-klrc-aiagent-auth-00 (2 Mar 2026, informational) maps OAuth 2.0 delegation patterns onto agents and requires the delegating user or system identifier in the token "sub" claim so authorization decisions and audit trails resolve to a human principal; its 3 Sep 2026 expiry is the near-term observable of whether this line is adopted or lapses.
3. The AI Office's powers include model access for evaluation, which converts a regulatory competence into a technical integration requirement on providers (evaluation access surfaces, reproducible evaluation environments, retained logs). Inference, not fact: no access demand has been made of a named provider, so the interface requirement is implied by the power rather than observed.
4. Illinois routed critical safety incident reports and catastrophic risk assessments to the Illinois Emergency Management Agency and the Office of Homeland Security rather than a consumer or technology regulator, which requires providers to build incident classification and reporting pipelines to a civil-contingency schema rather than a consumer-protection one.

### Legal
Core Shift Thesis:
The question of who may rule AI is on track to be settled by non-decision rather than doctrine: the flagship vehicle is more likely to be mooted than decided, the preemption theory is untested, and ordinary enacted state statute is currently the only unambiguous authority.
Forces:
1. xAI v. Weiser (D. Colo., 1:26-cv-01515) has had no docket entry since 27 Apr 2026, Colorado AI Act enforcement was halted by joint motion of the parties rather than a contested ruling, and Colorado then rewrote the challenged statute through SB 26-189 (signed 14 May 2026), pointing at mootness rather than merits; ledger claim authority-2026-08-04 tests termination without a merits ruling by 2027-06.
2. Whether the FTC's implied-preemption theory survives contact with a final document is ledger claim authority-2026-08-02 (resolve by 2027-03); the underlying contradiction is that preemption is claimed from a federal scheme protecting accurate AI outputs while the same administration declines to enact binding federal output standards.
3. Illinois SB 315 is the most attackable strict statute in the country and therefore the likely next vehicle: Attorney General enforcement, a first-of-kind third-party audit duty, and penalties to USD 3 million; ledger claim authority-2026-08-03 tests a filed DOJ Task Force or industry challenge by 2027-06.
4. The EU deferrals (Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 Dec 2027, Annex I embedded-product obligations to 2 Aug 2028) narrow near-term EU legal exposure to GPAI duties, concentrating enforcement and litigation on model providers rather than deployers; per CAL-002 these replacement dates should be treated as contestable rather than fixed, and the same discipline applies to Illinois's 1 Jan 2027 and 1 Jan 2028 dates.

### Environmental
Core Shift Thesis:
The authority US states are losing over AI models is reappearing in the same months as authority over the electricity and land those models require, so physical-layer regulation becomes the operative substitute for algorithmic-layer regulation.
Forces:
1. FERC instituted Section 206 proceedings against all six RTOs and ISOs on 18 Jun 2026 (PJM EL26-67, SPP EL26-68, NYISO EL26-69, MISO EL26-70, CAISO EL26-71, ISO-NE EL26-72), requiring each to justify or reform large load integration rules with pro forma cost recovery agreements to prevent cost shifting onto other customers, and doing so through six RTO-level dockets rather than one national rulemaking (this run's power sweep).
2. Virginia enacted the first US state tax on data center electricity consumption, USD 0.011 per kWh on all electricity consumed at each data center per month, effective 1 Jul 2026 through 1 Jul 2028, applying to utility-supplied and self-generated power and capped at USD 600 million of general fund revenue per fiscal year (this run's power sweep). A state that may be preempted on AI conduct is not preempted on kilowatt-hours.
3. The same federal executive expanded the Ratepayer Protection Pledge to 187 signatories on 23 Jul 2026 (23 governors, 55 investor-owned utilities, 105 cooperatives and public-power entities, 27 data center developers, voluntary negotiation rather than rule) while intervening against the Colorado AI Act on 24 Apr 2026, so state bargaining power over the physical layer grew in the same quarter state legal authority over the algorithmic layer was contested (this run's power sweep contradiction).
4. Market surveillance is the physically grounded limb of the AI Act (inspection, withdrawal of a product from a market), and it is the limb 18 of 27 member states had not fully constituted as of 17 Jun 2026, while Annex I embedded-product obligations slid to 2 Aug 2028, leaving AI in machinery, vehicles and medical devices under pre-existing sectoral safety regimes for two further years. Inference, not fact: no physical-product withdrawal has been attempted under the new allocation.
