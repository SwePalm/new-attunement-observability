# Evidence sweep, power, 2026-08-04

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The July sweep framed power along two axes, capital (~$700B capex) and jurisdiction (federal preemption of state AI law). A third axis opened in June and July 2026 that the July sweep did not carry: cost allocation. FERC instituted Section 206 proceedings against all six RTOs/ISOs on large load integration (18 Jun 2026), Virginia's per-kWh data center electricity tax took effect (1 Jul 2026), the White House Ratepayer Protection Pledge was expanded (23 Jul 2026), and House Energy and Commerce advanced H.R. 9340 (21 Jul 2026).
- The antitrust vector the July sweep named as "the next competition-law frontier" did not materialize in 1H 2026. The EU acted instead through industrial policy, proposing the Cloud and AI Development Act on 3 Jun 2026 with a four-level sovereignty framework and a public procurement mechanism, not through competition enforcement.
- Two things complicate open claim power-2026-07-02. Capex composition shifted toward memory (roughly 30% of hyperscaler capex in CY2026 versus about 8% in CY2023), and the funding channel shifted from operating cash to debt at record scale (about $225B of hyperscaler and related bond issuance through midyear 2026). Nominal capex can clear the $600B threshold without the physical buildout the claim was written to track.
- Local veto power showed up as a measurable quantity for the first time: about 75 US projects worth roughly $130B blocked or delayed in Q1 2026 alone, with active opposition groups doubling from 396 to 833. Absent from the July sweep.

Confirmed Developments:
- FERC issued Section 206 orders instituting proceedings against all six RTOs/ISOs on 18 Jun 2026 (PJM EL26-67, SPP EL26-68, NYISO EL26-69, MISO EL26-70, CAISO EL26-71, ISO-NE EL26-72, 195 FERC para 61,214), requiring each to show its tariff is just and reasonable without large load provisions or to propose reforms across five categories (study process, cost transparency, co-location, flexible load with behind-the-meter generation, and electrically proximate large load terms), with responses due within 60 days and pro forma cost recovery agreements to prevent cost shifting onto other customers (source: https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2026/6/ferc-issues-section-206-show-cause-orders-directing-all-six-rtos-isos-to-justify-or-reform-large-load-integration-rules/, Jun 2026) (source: https://www.caiso.com/documents/jun-18-2026-order-instituting-proceeding-large-loads-and-co-located-loads-el26-71.pdf, Jun 2026)
- Virginia enacted the first US state tax on data center electricity consumption, $0.011/kWh on all electricity consumed at each data center per month, approved 22 Jun 2026 in the biennial budget (HB 30, Item 3-5.24#1c), effective 1 Jul 2026 through 1 Jul 2028, applying to utility-supplied and self-generated power, capped at $600M of general fund revenue per fiscal year with pro-rata refunds above the cap (source: https://budget.lis.virginia.gov/amendment/2026/2/HB30/Introduced/CR/3-5.24/1c, Jun 2026) (source: https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/6/virginia-legislature-approves-tax-on-data-center-electricity-consumption, Jun 2026)
- The White House expanded the Ratepayer Protection Pledge on 23 Jul 2026 to 187 signatories (23 governors, 55 investor-owned utilities, 105 cooperatives and public-power entities, 27 data center developers), committing large data center operators rather than ratepayers to fund the generation and infrastructure their projects require; the mechanism is voluntary negotiation of separate rate structures, not rule (source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/07/president-trumps-ratepayer-protection-pledge-secures-american-ai-dominance-protects-consumers/, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.powermag.com/white-house-expands-data-center-ratepayer-pledge-as-congress-moves-to-codify-protections/, Jul 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Congress moved to convert the voluntary pledge into statute: the Ratepayer Protection Act (H.R. 9340, Evans and Castor), introduced 18 Jun 2026, cleared House Energy and Commerce 52-0 on 21 Jul 2026, amending PURPA to require large-load customers of 100 MW or more to cover the full cost of grid upgrades serving them (source: https://www.powermag.com/white-house-expands-data-center-ratepayer-pledge-as-congress-moves-to-codify-protections/, Jul 2026)
- The EU proposed the Cloud and AI Development Act on 3 Jun 2026, pairing a four-level cloud sovereignty assurance framework (Level 1 EU infrastructure and data, Level 4 EU control with high-assurance cybersecurity certification and no derogations) with a joint EU-level public procurement framework, an "open source first" principle for public bodies, and data centre acceleration zones with a 12-month maximum permitting timeline, targeting a tripling of EU data centre capacity in 5-7 years against an EU-provider cloud share that fell from about 29% in 2017 to about 15% in 2022 (source: https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2026/06/11/the-eu-cloud-and-ai-development-act-in-depth/, Jun 2026) (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cloud-and-ai-development-act, Jun 2026)
- Off-balance-sheet obligations are becoming the dominant form of AI infrastructure leverage: Nikkei counted $1.65T of hidden debt across five US tech giants against $1.35T of reported debt, and Moody's identified $1.2T of off-balance-sheet obligations with over $820B tied to data centers still under construction (source: https://fortune.com/2026/07/31/ai-debt-hypescalers-capex-capital-spending-hidden-borrowing-bond-issuance/, Jul 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- Community and local-government veto is now a quantified constraint on the buildout, not a rhetorical one: Data Center Watch counted at least 75 US projects worth about $130B blocked or delayed in Jan-Mar 2026, roughly matching the whole of 2025 in one quarter, with active opposition groups doubling from 396 at end-2025 to 833 by Mar 2026 across 49 states (source: https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/data-center-opposition-sharply-rising-2026-study-finds-rcna349728, Jun 2026)
- The predicted convergence of competition authorities on AI infrastructure did not happen. A first-half 2026 review finds no formal US, EU or UK inquiry opened specifically into compute, datacenter tenancy or energy cooperation; the UK CMA closed its cloud market investigation on 31 Mar 2026 by accepting AWS and Microsoft commitments (source: https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/insights-technology-antc-antitrust-competition-technology-1h-2026, Jul 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- Enforcement of the Colorado AI Act is suspended by court order. xAI filed X.AI LLC v. Weiser (D. Colo. 1:26-cv-01515) on 9 Apr 2026; DOJ moved to intervene on 24 Apr 2026, the first federal intervention in a challenge to a state AI law, arguing the Act compels and authorizes discrimination; the court granted a joint xAI and Colorado AG motion on 27 Apr 2026 suspending enforcement until the legislative session and rulemaking conclude and the preliminary injunction motion is decided, and had not ruled on the merits as of mid-2026 (source: https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-us/knowledge/publications/de3ad9de/xai-sues-doj-intervenes-enforcement-of-colorado-ai-act-suspended, 2026) (source: https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/client-alerts/doj-joins-xai-in-lawsuit-challenging-colorado-ai-act, Apr 2026)
- FERC's April commitment to act on the large load interconnection ANOPR (Docket RM26-4-000) by end of June 2026 was met through a different instrument than a rule: six utility-specific Section 206 proceedings rather than a generic rulemaking, moving the venue from one national docket to six RTO-level ones (source: https://www.ferc.gov/news-events/news/ferc-act-large-load-interconnection-docket-june-2026, Apr 2026)

Capital Movements:
- AI infrastructure financing crossed from cash to credit at record scale. S&P Global counted about $225B of bonds from hyperscalers and related entities through midyear 2026, a 973.7% year-over-year jump, with roughly $400B projected for the full year (source: https://fortune.com/2026/07/31/ai-debt-hypescalers-capex-capital-spending-hidden-borrowing-bond-issuance/, Jul 2026)
- Investment-grade bond issuance backing AI investment, spanning hyperscalers, data center developers (Hut 8, Beacon Point, QTS) and adjacent issuers (SpaceX, Nvidia, NTT), reached $218B through 8 Jul 2026 (source: https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/bond-issuance-backing-ai-investment-tops-250b-testing-limits-of-voracious-investor-demand, Jul 2026)

Technical Changes:
- The bill of materials behind the capex number shifted: SemiAnalysis estimates memory will consume roughly 30% of total hyperscaler capex in CY2026 against about 8% in CY2023 and CY2024, with DRAM prices expected to more than double in CY26, meaning a rising share of announced spend buys the same physical capacity (source: https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/memory-will-consume-30-percent-of-hyperscaler-spending-this-year, Apr 2026)

Contradictions:
- The same federal executive is simultaneously expanding state and utility bargaining power over data center energy costs (Ratepayer Protection Pledge, 23 Jul 2026, with 23 governors) and stripping state legal authority over AI deployment (DOJ intervention against the Colorado AI Act, 24 Apr 2026). States gain leverage over the physical layer at the moment they lose it over the algorithmic one.
- The EU's Cloud and AI Development Act sets sovereignty tiers to reduce dependence on non-EU providers while its acceleration zones and 12-month permitting are most usable by the capital-rich hyperscalers the tiers are meant to displace.
- Both the White House pledge and the Virginia tax are justified as protecting ratepayers, but one is voluntary and firm-negotiated while the other is compulsory and capped, and the compulsory instrument sunsets in 2028 while the voluntary one has no expiry.
