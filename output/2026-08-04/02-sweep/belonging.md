# Evidence sweep, belonging, 2026-08-04

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The 2026-07 sweep knew of one companion-chatbot statute (California). The count is now 14 or more enacted state laws, with Hawaii SB 3001 signed in July 2026 and Wyoming HB 102 effective 1 Jul 2026.
- New York S9051B, the instrument on which Phase A confirmed belonging-2026-07-01, is still unsigned: the bill's last recorded action is 5 Jun 2026 and it had not been delivered to the governor as of early August 2026.
- The evidence base moved from correlational surveys to peer-reviewed mechanism claims. De Freitas et al. reached final publication in Journal of Consumer Research (Apr 2026) and Ha et al. named two distinct harm mechanisms in The Lancet Child and Adolescent Health (Jun 2026). Neither was in the 2026-07 sweep.
- Capital Movements was "None" last month. There are now dated market figures: $327.6M in H1 2026 consumer spend across companion apps, split nearly evenly between romantic/NSFW and general-purpose apps.

Confirmed Developments:
- Hawaii enacted SB 3001, the Artificial Intelligence Disclosure and Safety Act, in mid-July 2026, requiring AI disclosure, barring AI from posing as a licensed mental health professional, banning features designed to create emotional dependence in minors (unpredictable rewards, simulated relationships, manipulative engagement), mandating crisis-resource referral, with civil penalties up to $1,000 per violation capped at $1M per operator (source: https://www.westhawaiitoday.com/2026/07/16/hawaii-news/hawaii-enacts-ai-laws-to-protect-children/, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/watershed-year-for-chatbot-safety-measures-14-new-state-laws-enacted-so-far-in-2026, Jul 2026)
- New York S9051B passed the Senate 60-0 on 4 Jun 2026 and the Assembly on 5 Jun 2026 but remains awaiting gubernatorial action, with an effective date of 1 Jan 2027 and NY Attorney General enforcement at up to $25,000 per violation (source: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S9051/amendment/B, Jun 2026) (source: https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/07/trust-issues-july-2026, Jul 2026)
- Ha, Figueroa, McGray and colleagues published a paper in The Lancet Child and Adolescent Health (DOI 10.1016/S2352-4642(26)00166-5) naming two mechanisms, relational displacement and maladaptive relational learning, and reporting that 64% of US adolescents use interactive AI, 42% have used chatbots for friendship advice, and 19% for romantic relationship guidance (source: https://news.asu.edu/20260629-science-and-technology-ai-companionship-poses-risks-teen-development-study-shows, Jun 2026) (source: https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/intersections/202607/are-the-kids-alright-teens-usage-of-ai-companions-is-up, Jul 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Ofcom is running structured persona testing of ChatGPT, Grok, SnapMyAI and Talkie using fictional 13-17 and 18+ accounts on Ofcom-managed devices, examining sign-up, engagement, chatbot responses and safety features, with completion expected by end of 2026 (source: https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/transparency-notice-research-on-ai-chatbot-social-media-and-video-sharing-platforms-to-explore-online-safety, Jun 2026)
- The Ha study is the front end of an NIMH-funded 18-month longitudinal study recruiting 300 adolescents and their romantic partners, using real-time mobile device data rather than self-report (source: https://news.asu.edu/20260629-science-and-technology-ai-companionship-poses-risks-teen-development-study-shows, Jun 2026)
- A second independent longitudinal instrument is standing up: the University of Minnesota Teen and AI Study, co-led by Xiaoran Sun, examining adolescent use of AI companions for emotional support, motivations, harms, and stigma (source: https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/07/07/u-of-m-conducting-research-on-growing-trend-of-teens-turning-to-ai-chatbots, Jul 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- The De Freitas, Oguz-Uguralp, Uguralp and Puntoni paper reached final peer-reviewed publication in Journal of Consumer Research 52(6), April 2026, reporting across five studies that AI companions alleviate loneliness comparably to human interaction and better than watching videos, that users underestimate the effect, and that feeling heard rather than distraction or self-disclosure drives the reduction (source: https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/52/6/1126/8173802, Apr 2026)
- The researchers naming the displacement risk explicitly argue against blanket bans and for redesign with developmental safeguards, which cuts against the access-restriction direction of the 2026 statutory wave (source: https://neurosciencenews.com/ai-chatbots-teen-relational-displacement-30971/, Jul 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- Ofcom opened its first Online Safety Act investigation into an AI companion service on 15 Jan 2026, into Novi Ltd (Joi.com, roughly 6.5M monthly global users and 100,000-300,000 UK monthly users) over highly effective age assurance duties; the case was still open with no provisional decision as of August 2026 (source: https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/investigation-into-novi-ltds-compliance-with-the-duty-to-prevent-children-from-encountering-pornographic-content-through-the-use-of-age-assurance, Jan 2026)
- Australia's eSafety Commissioner reported findings from transparency notices issued to Character.AI, Nomi, Chai and Chub AI, finding reliance on self-declared age, inadequate content monitoring and no routing to mental health resources on self-harm signals; since the notices some providers improved age assurance and one withdrew from Australia (source: https://www.biometricupdate.com/202603/companion-chatbots-not-doing-enough-to-protect-kids-esafety-report, Mar 2026)
- The 2026 state statutes cluster their effective dates ahead: Colorado HB 1263 on 12 Aug 2026, Connecticut SB 5 on 1 Oct 2026, Georgia SB 540, Oregon SB 1546, Rhode Island S 2195 and Washington HB 2225 on 1 Jan 2027, and Idaho SB 1297, Iowa SF 2417 and Nebraska LB 525 on 1 Jul 2027, so most of the wave is not yet operative (source: https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/watershed-year-for-chatbot-safety-measures-14-new-state-laws-enacted-so-far-in-2026, Jul 2026)
- The EU Digital Fairness Act, aimed at dark patterns, addictive design and unfair personalisation, has still not been proposed; the Commission consultation closed 24 Oct 2025 and the proposal is expected Q3-Q4 2026, with no published text addressing AI companions (source: https://techbriefly.com/2026/07/13/eu-big-tech-digital-fairness-act-consumer-harms/, Jul 2026)

Capital Movements:
- Appfigures Intelligence data shows AI companion apps took $427.3M in cumulative consumer spend across 165.3M downloads since late 2022, of which H1 2026 alone accounted for $162.8M across 214 romantic/NSFW apps plus $164.8M across general companion apps; top grossing were Zeta ($33M), Tipsy Chat ($15.2M) and ChatBox ($13M), all pre-platform-fee and excluding web-only services (source: https://decrypt.co/373395/how-much-your-boyfriend-spending-ai-girlfriends, Jul 2026)

Technical Changes:
- OpenAI deployed an age prediction model on ChatGPT consumer plans that infers likely minors from account age, activity timing, usage patterns and stated age, auto-applying content restrictions, with Persona identity verification as the appeal path and EU rollout following (source: https://openai.com/index/our-approach-to-age-prediction/, Jan 2026)

Contradictions:
- The same product class registers as loneliness relief and as isolation driver depending on measurement window: five JCR studies show momentary and week-long relief, while the Folk and Dunn four-wave panel shows increased emotional isolation at four months.
- The regulatory wave gates access by age, but the strongest longitudinal harm evidence (Folk and Dunn, n=2,149) and the strongest benefit evidence (De Freitas et al.) are both drawn from adults, who are covered by none of the enacted statutes.
- Almost every enacted 2026 statute restricts features rather than access, yet the researchers who named the developmental risk mechanism recommend redesign over restriction, meaning the statutory and scientific consensus align on method while the advocacy push for bans does not.
