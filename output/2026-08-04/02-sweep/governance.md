# Evidence sweep, governance, 2026-08-04

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The 2026-07-03 sweep framed 2 August 2026 as a transparency date only. The event that actually landed is broader: from 2 August 2026 the AI Office gained enforcement powers over general-purpose AI models and the penalty framework became operative alongside Article 50 disclosure (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august, Jul 2026).
- The previous sweep missed the US litigation channel entirely. DOJ intervened in xAI v. Weiser on 24 April 2026, a federal court then suspended enforcement of the Colorado AI Act, and Colorado repealed and replaced SB 24-205 with SB 26-189 on 14 May 2026 (source: https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/client-alerts/doj-joins-xai-in-lawsuit-challenging-colorado-ai-act, Apr 2026) (source: https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189, May 2026).
- Genuinely new since 3 July 2026: Illinois SB 315 signed 6 July, FTC proposed policy statement published 7 July, China's agent Implementation Opinions became enforceable 15 July, and the Commission published final Article 50 guidelines on 20 July.
- The Phase A gap ("compliance attention decouples from actual obligations") now has measurable proxies: national authority designation counts and the absence of any announced Article 50 proceeding. Two of this run's ledger candidates are built on them.

Confirmed Developments:
- The bulk of the EU AI Act became applicable on 2 August 2026: Article 50 transparency duties (AI disclosure by chatbots, deepfake labelling, machine-readable marking), AI Office enforcement powers over GPAI models, and the penalty framework, with a grace period to 2 December 2026 for Article 50(2) marking on systems already on the market and fines up to EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations, Jul 2026)
- Illinois Governor Pritzker signed SB 315, the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act, on 6 July 2026, making Illinois the third state with a frontier-developer regime; from 1 January 2027 a large frontier developer may not develop, deploy or operate a frontier model in Illinois without a current disclosure statement on file, plus a published frontier AI framework, incident reporting, and annual third-party audit (source: https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/illinois-imposes-transparency-and-safety-obligations-on-frontier-ai-systems, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/68f0462c/illinois-enacts-artificial-intelligence-safety-measures-act, Jul 2026)
- The US Department of Justice moved to intervene on 24 April 2026 in xAI v. Weiser (D. Colo., No. 1:26-cv-01515), the first federal intervention against a state AI statute, arguing the Colorado AI Act's disparate-impact duty and diversity exemption violate equal protection; days later the court suspended enforcement of the Act pending a preliminary-injunction ruling (source: https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-intervenes-xai-lawsuit-challenging-colorados-algorithmic-discrimination, Apr 2026) (source: https://www.reuters.com/world/us-justice-department-intervenes-xai-challenge-colorado-tech-law-2026-04-24/, Apr 2026)
- Colorado repealed and replaced its landmark AI Act: SB 26-189 (Automated Decision-Making Technology) was signed 14 May 2026 and takes effect 1 January 2027, dropping the algorithmic-discrimination duty in favour of consumer notice, a 30-day post-adverse-outcome explanation, human review on request, developer documentation, and three-year record retention, with AG rulemaking due by the same date (source: https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189, May 2026) (source: https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/colorado-replaces-landmark-ai-act-an-overview-of-the-new-sb-26-189-framework.html, May 2026)
- China's CAC, NDRC and MIIT jointly issued Implementation Opinions on the Standardized Application and Innovative Development of Intelligent Agents on 8 May 2026, enforceable from 15 July 2026, the first national framework treating AI agents as a distinct regulated category, with a three-tier decision-authorization model and mandatory filing, testing and recall for agents in healthcare, transportation, media and public safety (source: https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/china-issues-first-national-policy-framework-dedicated-to-ai-agents/, May 2026) (source: https://www.rimonlaw.com/china-ai-law-brief/, Jul 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Co-regulatory instruments are absorbing compliance ahead of enforcement: roughly 190 organisations, about half of them small and recent companies, signed the Commission's Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content by the end of July 2026, before the marking obligations applied (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/strong-backing-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content, Jul 2026)
- States are now drafting preemption defences into the statute itself: SB 315 lets the Illinois Emergency Management Agency designate federal requirements as "substantially equivalent", deeming compliant developers compliant with Illinois law, a design intended to blunt Commerce Clause challenges and the patchwork argument (source: https://www.akerman.com/en/perspectives/illinois-sb-315-a-state-strategy-for-enduring-national-ai-safety-standards.html, Jun 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- EU enforcement capacity is thin at the moment obligations bite: as of 17 June 2026 only 9 of 27 Member States had clearly designated both a market surveillance authority and a notifying authority, 12 were partial, and 6 had neither, against a legal deadline of 2 August 2025 (source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/national-implementation-plans/, Jun 2026)
- The most-cited US state AI regime is inoperative: the federal court stay in xAI v. Weiser covers both SB 24-205 and its replacement SB 26-189 and runs until 14 days after a ruling on a preliminary-injunction motion that will only be filed within 28 days of the state finalising rulemaking, so a state law enacted in 2024 is still unenforced in August 2026 (source: https://www.lawfuel.com/colorado-ai-act-2026-sb-26-189/, Jul 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- The FTC published a proposed policy statement on 7 July 2026 (docket FTC-2026-0859, File No. P264200, comments closed 31 July 2026) treating undisclosed steering of AI outputs away from user-expected objectives as deceptive under Section 5, expressly including steering undertaken to comply with state laws, and naming Colorado SB 26-189; it responds to Executive Order 14365 of 11 December 2025 (source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/07/2026-13628/policy-statement-concerning-the-suppression-of-accuracy-in-artificial-intelligence-systems, Jul 2026)
- The AI omnibus postponed the Member State obligation to establish at least one national AI regulatory sandbox from 2 August 2026 to 2 August 2027, deferring supervisory infrastructure while advancing disclosure duties (source: https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/05/07/artificial-intelligence-council-and-parliament-agree-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/, May 2026)
- No comprehensive federal AI statute exists and no federal preemption of state AI law has been enacted as of 31 July 2026; the federal layer remains executive orders and procurement memoranda (source: https://vorplabs.com/ai-regulatory-updates/united-states, Jul 2026)

Capital Movements:
- Norm Ai raised a USD 120 million Series C led by Khosla Ventures on 7 July 2026 at a USD 1.2 billion valuation, pricing regulatory-compliance agents as infrastructure rather than tooling (source: https://www.reuters.com/technology/legal-ai-startup-norm-ai-hits-12-billion-valuation-after-120-million-funding-2026-07-07/, Jul 2026)

Technical Changes:
- The Commission's final Article 50 guidelines (20 July 2026) specify a layered machine-readable marking approach combining digitally signed metadata with imperceptible watermarking, generally free detection tools from providers, and watermark-detection interoperability by 2 February 2027 (source: https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations, Jul 2026)
- China's agent framework converts governance into a runtime architecture requirement: every agent action must be pre-sorted into user-only, authorization-required, or autonomous tiers, with the user retaining a right to know and final decision power over autonomous actions (source: https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/china-issues-first-national-policy-framework-dedicated-to-ai-agents/, May 2026)

Contradictions:
- The same EU package that held the 2 August 2026 transparency date postponed the national regulatory sandbox obligation by a year and left CEN-CENELEC standards unfinished, so disclosure duties were accelerated relative to the state capacity meant to supervise them.
- The federal executive argues that state AI laws force undisclosed output steering, but the one state regime it has litigated against has already been repealed and replaced with a disclosure-only framework, so the asserted harm and the live statute no longer match.
- Illinois enacted a frontier-safety law with an explicit federal off-ramp, meaning a state is legislating in order to be preempted, which inverts the fragmentation narrative used to justify preemption.
