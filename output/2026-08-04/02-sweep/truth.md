# Evidence sweep, truth, 2026-08-04

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The 2026-07 sweep recorded EU marking duties as deferred and Article 50 as pending. Since then the Commission closed the implementation loop: adequacy decision on the Code of Practice (8-9 Jul 2026), final Article 50 Guidelines (20 Jul 2026), about 190 signatories by end July, and Article 50 legally applicable from 2 Aug 2026.
- Assistant-answer liability moved from one German trial ruling (Munich, May 2026, which retroactively satisfied truth-2026-07-03) to a US trial ruling: Delaware Superior Court denied Google's motion to dismiss in Starbuck v. Google on 24 Jul 2026.
- Absent from the July sweep entirely: California's AI Transparency Act became operative on the same calendar day as EU Article 50 (2 Aug 2026), so provenance marking is now a two-jurisdiction legal duty rather than an EU-only one.
- No movement on truth-2026-07-02: no banking association or prudential regulator has mandated out-of-band verification. Consistent with CAL-003, the observable hardening remains in payments-network rules and insurance underwriting.

Confirmed Developments:
- The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations became legally applicable on 2 Aug 2026, and on 20 Jul 2026 the Commission published final Guidelines defining directly interactive AI systems, synthetic content, deepfakes and AI-generated text on matters of public interest, plus exemptions such as standard editing (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-transparency-ai-generated-content, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/, Jul 2026)
- The Commission and the AI Board issued an adequacy decision on the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content (8-9 Jul 2026), and by end July 2026 about 190 organisations had signed, 83 to Section 1 for providers and 152 to Section 2 for deployers (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/strong-backing-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/, Jul 2026)
- Google announced its signature of the transparency Code on 24 Jul 2026 and Meta published its own signing statement in the same week, putting both platform incumbents inside the EU marking framework (source: https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/public-policy/eu-ai-act-transparency-code-of-practice/, Jul 2026) (source: https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/meta-is-signing-the-eu-ai-act-code-of-practice-on-transparency-of-ai-generated-content/, Jul 2026)
- The Superior Court of the State of Delaware denied Google's motion to dismiss in Starbuck v. Google LLC, C.A. No. N25C-10-211 MAA, on 24 Jul 2026, holding that Starbuck adequately pleaded defamation per se including publication and actual malice for statements generated by Google's Bard AI (source: https://storage.courtlistener.com/pdf/2026/07/24/starbuck_v._google_llc.pdf, Jul 2026) (source: https://reason.com/volokh/2026/07/24/conservative-commentator-robby-starbucks-lawsuit-alleging-google-ai-had-defamed-him-can-go-forward/, Jul 2026)
- The California AI Transparency Act (SB 942, as amended by AB 853, which moved the operative date from 1 Jan 2026 to 2 Aug 2026 to align with the EU AI Act) became operative on 2 Aug 2026, requiring covered providers to offer a free public detection tool, embed latent machine-readable provenance disclosures, and offer manifest disclosures (source: https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2025/10/california-ai-transparency-act-amendments-signed-into-law/, Oct 2025) (source: https://www.techtimes.com/articles/322713/20260802/california-ai-transparency-act-operative-midjourney-has-no-watermark-fines-start-today.htm, Aug 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Provenance obligations are becoming date-synchronised across jurisdictions by design rather than coincidence: AB 853 explicitly moved California's operative date to 2 Aug 2026 to align with the EU AI Act (source: https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2025/10/california-ai-transparency-act-amendments-signed-into-law/, Oct 2025)
- Public service broadcasters are bidding to be designated trust anchors inside national AI governance frameworks, with the EBU asking the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance on 7 Jul 2026 for open, interoperable information-integrity standards so that technology companies cannot unilaterally control information infrastructure (source: https://www.ebu.ch/news/2026/07/ai-governance-must-put-trust-information-integrity-and-child-safety-front-and-centre-backed-by-real-accountability, Jul 2026)
- Named non-compliance is now publicly enumerable on day one of enforcement: Midjourney was reported as shipping neither C2PA Content Credentials nor a known pixel watermark on 2 Aug 2026, while OpenAI, Google, Adobe, Meta, ElevenLabs and Stability AI were reported as having deployed watermarking or credentials (source: https://www.techtimes.com/articles/322713/20260802/california-ai-transparency-act-operative-midjourney-has-no-watermark-fines-start-today.htm, Aug 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- Enforcement capacity lags the statute badly: as of 17 Jun 2026 only 9 of 27 member states had designated both a market surveillance authority and a notifying authority, and the Commission's single-point-of-contact list held 8 of 27 as of Mar 2026, against a legal deadline of 2 Aug 2025 (source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/national-implementation-plans/, Jun 2026) (source: https://epthinktank.eu/2026/03/18/enforcement-of-the-ai-act/, Mar 2026)
- The instrument that produced near-universal frontier-model buy-in is voluntary: the Commission states adherence to the Code is optional, signature remains open indefinitely, and compliance may equally be demonstrated by alternative equivalently adequate means (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content, Jul 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- The Code of Practice sets a technical runway past the statute: 2 Dec 2026 for machine-readable marking under Article 50(2) for generative systems already on the market before 2 Aug 2026, and 2 Feb 2027 for signatories to implement a watermark-detection interoperability solution (source: https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/, Jul 2026)
- California layers a second and third wave onto the same regime: large online platform provenance-detection and user-facing disclosure duties from 1 Jan 2027, capture-device latent disclosure from 1 Jan 2028, with civil penalties of 5,000 dollars per violation per day enforceable by the Attorney General, city attorneys and county counsel (source: https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2025/10/california-ai-transparency-act-amendments-signed-into-law/, Oct 2025) (source: https://www.techtimes.com/articles/322713/20260802/california-ai-transparency-act-operative-midjourney-has-no-watermark-fines-start-today.htm, Aug 2026)

Capital Movements:
- Sensity AI (Amsterdam, deepfake forensics) was awarded a 4.9 million euro European Innovation Council Accelerator package including a 2.5 million euro equity-free grant, selected among 38 companies from 1,760 proposals and the only cybersecurity company in the cohort (source: https://www.biometricupdate.com/202607/sensity-ai-gets-eic-funding-for-forensics-focused-deepfake-detection-models, Jul 2026)

Technical Changes:
- Watermark removal is measurably harder than the 2026-07 sweep assumed. Across six state-of-the-art removal attacks in four families, independent detectors identified processed outputs at over 98 percent true-positive rate under a 1 percent false-positive budget, so removal substitutes one detection signal for another rather than restoring deniability; the authors argue current benchmarks omit forensic stealth as a criterion (source: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09203, May 2026)

Contradictions:
- The marking duty is live in the EU from 2 Aug 2026 and the detection-interoperability deadline lands 2 Feb 2027, while fewer than half of member states have stood up the authorities that would enforce either.
- Both new statutory regimes (Article 50, SB 942) regulate whether content is marked as synthetic, not whether an answer is true, yet the two rulings that actually moved liability this year (Munich I on AI Overviews, Delaware on Bard) both turned on a false statement about a person, which no marking requirement would have prevented.
- The Code of Practice earned industry signature because it is voluntary, and the same voluntariness is what the enforcement-capacity gap makes load-bearing.
