# Evidence sweep, perception, 2026-08-04

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The 2026-07 sweep recorded only the deferral narrative around EU provenance rules. Since then the Article 50 transparency obligations became applicable on 2 Aug 2026 and the accompanying Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content (published 10 Jun 2026) closed its initial signature window on 31 Jul 2026 with about 190 signatories, including Anthropic, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Mistral and OpenAI as Section 1 providers.
- Watermarking consolidated around one cross-vendor layer. Last sweep treated SynthID as a Google-only deployment; Google has now named Apple, ElevenLabs, Kakao, NVIDIA and OpenAI as SynthID interoperability partners, and Apple confirmed SynthID marking inside Apple Intelligence at OS level.
- Provenance governance moved from capture devices to the distribution layer. TikTok upgraded to the C2PA Steering Committee in Jul 2026 reporting more than 3 billion videos labeled. Last sweep contained no platform-side provenance evidence.
- US state synthetic media law moved from statute to enforcement: 31 states now have election deepfake laws and the first live investigations (Oregon, Michigan, Arizona) are running, while California and Hawaii provisions were struck down on First Amendment grounds. None of this was in the 2026-07 sweep.

Confirmed Developments:
- The European Commission published the final Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content on 10 Jun 2026 and reported approximately 190 signatories by end of Jul 2026 (83 organisations for Section 1 provider marking obligations, 152 for Section 2 deployer labelling obligations), one day before the AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations became applicable on 2 Aug 2026, with penalties up to EUR 15 million or 3% of global annual turnover (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/strong-backing-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/24/the-eus-new-ai-labelling-rules-what-every-organisation-needs-to-know-102ne35, Jul 2026)
- Google signed the Code on 24 Jul 2026 and named Apple, ElevenLabs, Kakao, NVIDIA and OpenAI as partners for interoperable SynthID watermarking; Apple's own 8 Jun 2026 announcement states that Image Playground generated images and photos edited with Apple Intelligence automatically carry a hidden SynthID watermark (source: https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/public-policy/eu-ai-act-transparency-code-of-practice/, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/apple-intelligence-brings-powerful-ai-capabilities-into-everyday-experiences/, Jun 2026)
- TikTok upgraded from C2PA General Member to Steering Committee Member, reporting more than 3 billion videos labeled as AI-generated using Content Credentials, creator labeling tools and invisible watermarking, plus 86 million fake accounts removed in Q1 2026 and new tests of detection systems aimed at AI spam accounts in politics, financial advice and medical content (source: https://channelx.world/2026/07/tiktok-expands-ai-literacy-initiatives-and-joins-c2pa-steering-committee/, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/c2pa-welcomes-tiktok-to-steering-committee-advancing-the-adoption-of-content-credentials-at-a-global-scale-302836730.html, Jul 2026)
- US state election synthetic media statutes entered their first enforcement test: 31 states have such laws as of Jul 2026, and the Oregon Secretary of State's office is investigating Jonathan Lockwood under SB 1571 (2024) over unlabeled AI videos of Rep. Janelle Bynum, with exposure up to a USD 10,000 fine, alongside a Michigan damages suit and an Arizona suit over unlabeled AI billboards (source: https://azcapitoltimes.com/news/2026/07/21/state-ai-deepfake-laws-face-first-big-test-in-2026-midterm-elections/, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.ijpr.org/politics-government/2026-07-09/over-the-top-ai-campaign-ads-will-test-oregon-disclosure-law, Jul 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Detection is being rebuilt as a continuously retrained adversarial service rather than a static model: a 14 Jul 2026 preprint reports that static detectors lose 45-50% of performance on real-world content and presents a continuously competed system reaching 0.915 AUC on images and 0.822 on video on Deepfake-Eval-2024 (source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13234, Jul 2026)
- The Code of Practice sets two follow-on compliance dates beyond 2 Aug 2026: 2 Dec 2026 for AI systems already on the market before that date, and 2 Feb 2027 for providers to implement interoperability solutions (source: https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/24/the-eus-new-ai-labelling-rules-what-every-organisation-needs-to-know-102ne35, Jul 2026)
- The European Commission states that two task forces will launch in Sep 2026 to work on best practices and implementation feedback for the Code, and that the signatory list will be continuously updated (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/strong-backing-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content, Jul 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- Peer-reviewed evidence that labelling produces a spillover in the wrong direction: an ICWSM 2026 study of 877 German participants rating Instagram-style posts found "AI-generated" and "Misleading" labels reduced perceived authenticity by about 0.27 standard deviations, but exposure to labeled content slightly increased perceived authenticity of unlabeled images, about one fifth the size of the direct effect (source: https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/42721, 2026)
- The provenance layer is broken at distribution: a World Privacy Forum technical review of C2PA reports that multiple implementers identify metadata stripping on upload and sharing as the primary obstacle to C2PA interoperability, and that C2PA can authenticate forged deepfakes (source: https://worldprivacyforum.org/posts/privacy-identity-and-trust-in-c2pa/, Jun 2025)
- TikTok's 3 billion labeled videos were reported alongside research cited as finding that small overlay labels of the kind major platforms deploy produced no statistically significant improvement in deepfake identification, belief, or sharing rates, with only a full-screen blocking interstitial showing effect (source: https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320282/20260713/tiktok-has-labeled-3-billion-ai-videos-here-what-research-says-they-miss.htm, Jul 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations and the associated enforcement powers became applicable on 2 Aug 2026, requiring providers to mark synthetic outputs with digitally signed metadata and imperceptible watermarking and to supply detection solutions, and deployers to label deepfakes and certain AI-generated text with visible markers, preferably the standardised EU "AI" icon (source: https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/24/the-eus-new-ai-labelling-rules-what-every-organisation-needs-to-know-102ne35, Jul 2026)
- US state deepfake law is being narrowed by courts rather than expanded: provisions were struck down on First Amendment grounds in California and Hawaii, California's labeling law survives, and Montana's statute is under challenge, while the Federal Election Commission has not set a federal rule (source: https://azcapitoltimes.com/news/2026/07/21/state-ai-deepfake-laws-face-first-big-test-in-2026-midterm-elections/, Jul 2026)

Capital Movements:
- Deel acquired Israeli deepfake and identity verification startup Clarity for USD 40-50 million in cash and shares on 3 Aug 2026, folding continuous identity verification into a workforce platform serving over 40,000 organisations; Clarity had raised USD 16 million since 2022 (source: https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/bkeyex0sgl, Aug 2026)
- Sensity AI, an Amsterdam deepfake detection firm, was selected by the European Innovation Council for EUR 4.9 million in total funding including a EUR 2.5 million equity-free grant, to develop foundational deepfake detection models and real-time analysis in resource-constrained environments (source: https://www.biometricupdate.com/202607/sensity-ai-gets-eic-funding-for-forensics-focused-deepfake-detection-models, Jul 2026)

Technical Changes:
- SynthID is being positioned as a cross-vendor invisible watermark layer paired with C2PA manifests rather than a single-vendor feature, with Google naming five external partners and framing interoperable watermarking as the Article 50 compliance route (source: https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/public-policy/eu-ai-act-transparency-code-of-practice/, Jul 2026)
- Provenance marking moved into the operating system: Apple states that Apple Intelligence photo edits (Clean Up, Extend, Spatial Reframing) and Image Playground generations automatically embed a hidden SynthID watermark (source: https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/apple-intelligence-brings-powerful-ai-capabilities-into-everyday-experiences/, Jun 2026)
- C2PA published Content Credentials 2.3 and operates a conformance program with a public conforming-products list, with the older Interim Trust List frozen to new entries from 1 Jan 2026 (source: https://c2pa.org/conformance/, 2026)

Contradictions:
- The EU marking regime rests on digitally signed metadata and machine-readable marks, while the distribution layer that carries most content routinely strips exactly that metadata at upload, so the legally mandated signal is destroyed by the systems where perception actually happens.
- TikTok scaled labeling to 3 billion videos and took a seat on the standard's steering committee in the same month that research reported its label format changes neither belief nor sharing rates.
- Google signed the transparency Code and simultaneously warned that platforms flooded with overlapping AI labels and legal disclosures will confuse consumers and harm European competitiveness, so the leading provenance vendor is also the leading voice against label proliferation.
- Labelling reduces perceived authenticity of labeled content while raising perceived authenticity of unlabeled content, meaning a partially labeled ecosystem may increase total credulity rather than reduce it.
