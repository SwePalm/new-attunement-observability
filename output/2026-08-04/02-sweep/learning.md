# Evidence sweep, learning, 2026-08-04

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The 2026-07-03 sweep recorded "None binding" under Regulatory Shifts and "None verified" under Capital Movements. Both are now wrong: Ohio's statutory district AI-policy deadline fell on 1 July 2026, Maryland's Artificial Intelligence Ready Schools Act took effect 1 June 2026 with a costed MSDE budget line, and FutureEd counts 10 enacted state AI-education bills as of 13 July 2026.
- The last sweep's lead Confirmed Development (a systematic review reporting significant positive AI tutoring effects) is complicated by a March 2026 Stanford SCALE review the last sweep missed: 20 high-quality causal studies out of more than 800 papers, and none of student AI use conducted in US K-12 classrooms.
- Ledger claim learning-2026-07-01's funding conjunct is being met sub-nationally rather than nationally (Maryland general funds of $610,100 in FY2027), a channel the claim did not anticipate and cannot grade.
- Frontier-lab distribution shifted from student-facing tutors to teacher-facing free tiers (Anthropic, 14 July 2026), moving the unit of adoption from the learner to the verified educator.

Confirmed Developments:
- Ohio Revised Code Section 3301.24 required every school district, community school and STEM school in Ohio to adopt a policy on the use of artificial intelligence not later than 1 July 2026, and districts adopted under the mandate through July 2026 (source: https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3301.24, Sep 2025) (source: https://www.wnewsj.com/2026/07/16/blanchester-school-board-adopts-new-ai-policy-under-state-mandate/, Jul 2026)
- Maryland's Artificial Intelligence Ready Schools Act (SB 720) took effect 1 June 2026 and gives all 24 local school systems 120 days from the release of MSDE guidance to adopt an aligned AI policy and designate an AI coordinator (source: https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/fnotes/bil_0000/sb0720.pdf, May 2026) (source: https://marylandmatters.org/2026/07/06/maryland-school-districts-face-fall-deadline-to-set-ai-policies/, Jul 2026)
- Stanford's SCALE Initiative published "The Evidence Base on AI in K-12: A 2026 Review", identifying only 20 high-quality causal studies among more than 800 papers and stating there are no high-quality causal studies of student AI use conducted in US K-12 classrooms (source: https://scale.stanford.edu/research-in-action/understanding-evidence-base-ai-k12-education, Mar 2026) (source: https://beibindex.wordpress.com/2026/05/01/paucity-of-high-quality-causal-studies-supporting-ai-use-in-k-12/, May 2026)
- The House Education and Workforce Committee marked up and advanced H.R. 8747, the K-12 AI Literacy and Readiness Act of 2026, at its 21 July 2026 markup; the bill would amend the Elementary and Secondary Education Act of 1965 to make AI curriculum and educator training permissible uses of existing federal funds (source: https://www.congress.gov/committee-schedule/weekly/2026/07/20, Jul 2026) (source: https://legis1.com/news/house-education-bills-title-ix-ai-panel-advances, Jul 2026)
- Anthropic launched Claude for Teachers on 14 July 2026, giving verified US K-12 educators free premium access with sign-up open through 30 June 2027, curricula mapped to academic standards in all 50 states, and a pilot in Detroit Public Schools Community District (source: https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-teachers, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.chalkbeat.org/2026/07/14/anthropic-launches-claude-for-teachers-as-ai-companies-battle-for-classrooms/, Jul 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- The Digital Education Council's AI in Higher Education Global Survey 2026 drew 45,398 responses (27,284 students, 18,114 faculty) across 35 countries, finding 88% of students and 77% of faculty use AI, while 64% of faculty report having had AI literacy training and fewer than three in ten students report seeing the results (source: https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/q6zw9wco2mttajdbd1jyeonjnpiovu, Jul 2026)
- The University of Chicago Law School published an "AI-Resilient" strategy on 9 July 2026 banning electronic devices from core first-year classes, requiring oral discussion or defence of all upper-level substantial research papers, and stating the rationale in learning terms rather than integrity terms ("the whole point is to do things the hard way, because that's how you learn") (source: https://www.law.uchicago.edu/news/uchicago-law-unveils-new-ai-strategy-statement, Jul 2026)
- The Illinois State Board of Education released 409 pages of non-mandatory K-12 AI guidance on 9 July 2026, developed with a blue-ribbon panel and disclosing that initial drafts were produced with help from ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini (source: https://news.wttw.com/2026/07/16/illinois-board-education-issues-ai-guidance-written-help-ai, Jul 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- The largest current AI tutoring trial remains unreported: Eedi's second RCT with Google DeepMind, a four-arm design with 1,525 students across 10 UK secondary schools that began in April 2026 and ran 12 weeks with results expected in summer 2026, has published no results as of 4 August 2026, so the n>1,000 evidence base the field is waiting on does not yet exist (source: https://www.eedi.com/news/just-launched---our-second-ai-tutor-rct, Apr 2026)
- State legislative motion is thinner than the headline suggests: FutureEd's 13 July 2026 tracker counts 77 AI-in-education bills across 27 states but only 10 enacted, in Alabama, Idaho, Maryland, Oklahoma, Utah and Virginia, and most enacted measures compel policy-writing rather than curriculum or funding (source: https://www.future-ed.org/legislative-tracker-2026-state-ai-in-education-bills/, Jul 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- Ohio's statutory AI-policy duty for all districts, community schools and STEM schools came due on 1 July 2026 under Ohio Revised Code Section 3301.24 (source: https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3301.24, Sep 2025)
- The Florida State Board of Education scheduled a 5 August 2026 workshop on a proposed rule requiring public and charter schools to include artificial intelligence in their internet safety guidelines (source: https://www.wokv.com/news/local/florida-could-require-schools-set-rules-ai-use/RL6PJROT3JHHJIFYCBHGVE4KII/, Jul 2026)
- The European Commission and the OECD jointly published the AILit AI literacy framework for primary and secondary education on 18 June 2026 as a voluntary common reference, not binding law (source: https://education.ec.europa.eu/node/3537, Jun 2026)

Capital Movements:
- Maryland's SB 720 fiscal note projects general fund expenditure increases of $610,100 in FY2027 (3.0 positions at $430,618, $150,000 contractual for professional development module creation, $29,505 operating), then $418,600 in FY2028 rising to $477,300 in FY2031, with $500,000 already appropriated contingent on enactment (source: https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/fnotes/bil_0000/sb0720.pdf, May 2026)
- Digital Promise's Gates Foundation-backed K-12 AI Infrastructure Program, a $26m multi-year initiative, ran an Open Source AI Model for Tutoring (EDU AI) RFP of up to $8,000,000 with one award anticipated, submissions closing 31 July 2026 and estimated grant start November 2026; the program named its first four smaller grantees on 29 June 2026 (source: https://k12-ai-infrastructure.org/edu-ai-rfp/, Jun 2026) (source: https://digitalpromise.org/2026/06/29/digital-promise-announces-first-grantees-of-the-k-12-ai-infrastructure-program/, Jun 2026)

Technical Changes:
- Claude for Teachers ships as a teacher-scoped product rather than a tutor: nine integrated edtech platforms (ASSISTments, Brisk Teaching, Canva Education, Coteach, Diffit, Eedi, MagicSchool, Snorkl, TeachFX), K-12 terms that block model training on teacher inputs, a FERPA-aligned data processing agreement, and access restricted to verified educators aged 18 and over (source: https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-teachers, Jul 2026)

Contradictions:
- Mandate volume rose in the same year the causal evidence base was shown to be near-empty: Ohio, Maryland and Oklahoma made district AI policy a legal duty while SCALE found no high-quality causal study of student AI use in a US K-12 classroom.
- The money that exists funds policy-writing and teacher access, not outcome measurement: Maryland's costed line pays for three positions and professional development modules, while the single largest learning-outcomes grant in the field is a philanthropic RFP with one anticipated award.
- Institutions are simultaneously buying AI for teachers (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google free tiers) and removing devices from classrooms (UChicago Law), which means the same institution can expand and restrict AI in the same term.
