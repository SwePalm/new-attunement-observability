# Evidence sweep, creativity, 2026-08-04

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The 2026-07-03 sweep's frame ("training is largely fair use") is now jurisdiction-specific. Munich Regional Court I held on 31 Jul 2026 in GEMA v Suno that training a generative music model on protected works without a licence infringes even where the training took place in the US, and that the trained model is itself an infringing reproduction.
- A private-ordering layer for AI creative work appeared in three weeks and did not exist at the last sweep: a voluntary two-tier track labelling program (10 Jul 2026), label-proposed chart eligibility principles (29 Jul 2026), and IFPI's rollout of those principles across its own official charts (30 Jul 2026). This matches CAL-003 (non-state hardening leads statute).
- Bartz v. Anthropic moved from announced settlement to final approval on 20 Jul 2026, closing the payout question the July sweep left open at "$1.5B settled".
- No movement on two open ledger claims: creativity-2026-07-01 (no new $100M+ franchise-scale licensing deal since logging) and creativity-2026-07-03 (no collective-licensing body for individual creators with a named major AI lab as customer; Created by Humans remains the reference point with no new announcement in Jul 2026).

Confirmed Developments:
- Munich Regional Court I ruled on 31 Jul 2026 that Suno infringed GEMA-represented works, granting injunctive relief, disclosure and a declaration of damages over six recordings including "Rasputin", "Daddy Cool" and "Mambo No. 5"; the court asserted jurisdiction over US-located training under section 131 of the German Collecting Societies Act, held the model itself an infringing reproduction, placed liability on the provider rather than users, and held that compliance with the EU AI Act is not a defence to copyright claims (source: https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nfis/gema-notches-a-second-transatlantic-ai-copyright-win-in-germany/, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.juve-patent.com/cases/munich-regional-court-stops-suno-using-gema-protected-music/, Jul 2026)
- Eleven record companies (Believe, BMG, Concord, Dirty Hit, Glassnote Records, HYBE, Mom+Pop Music, Partisan Records, Sony Music, Universal Music Group, Warner Music Group) proposed global principles on 29 Jul 2026 restricting chart eligibility for GenAI recordings to tracks that are properly authorised and lawful, substantially human made, and free of stream or chart manipulation concerns; IFPI announced on 30 Jul 2026 that it is rolling the principles out across 11 official chart programmes it directly manages in over 30 countries in Latin America, MENA, Africa and Southeast Asia, and working with its national group network on 20+ further official charts (source: https://www.sonymusic.com/sony-music-corporate-non-home/believe-bmg-concord-dirty-hit-glassnote-records-hybe-mompop-music-partisan-records-sony-music-universal-music-group-and-warner-music-group-set-out-proposal-for-global-principles-for-eligibil/, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.ifpi.org/ifpi-rolls-out-global-principles-for-the-eligibility-of-recordings-developed-using-ai-in-official-music-charts-worldwide/, Jul 2026)
- Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin granted final approval on 20 Jul 2026 to the $1.5B Bartz v. Anthropic class settlement, with authors and publishers entitled to roughly $3,000 per infringed work and only about 350 authors opting out (source: https://www.reuters.com/world/us-judge-approves-anthropics-15-billion-settlement-copyright-lawsuit-2026-07-20/, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/digital/copyright/article/100888-judge-gives-final-approval-in-1-5-billion-settlement-in-anthropic-copyright-case.html, Jul 2026)
- Eight music-industry bodies (IFPI, RIAA, A2IM, WIN, IMPALA, The Grammys, SAG-AFTRA and the Human Artistry Campaign) announced a unified voluntary track labelling program on 10 Jul 2026 distinguishing "AI-Generated" recordings (GenAI produced the entirety or primary portion of the creative elements, including an AI lead vocal or key instrumental) from "AI-Assisted" recordings (created substantially by humans with GenAI used for some expressive elements) (source: https://www.ifpi.org/music-community-introduces-new-labelling-program-to-distinguish-generative-ai-in-sound-recordings/, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.riaa.com/music-community-introduces-new-labeling-programto-distinguish-generative-ai-in-sound-recordings/, Jul 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- About 190 organisations had signed the EU Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content by end Jul 2026 (83 signatories to Section 1 for providers, 152 to Section 2 for deployers), including Aleph Alpha, Anthropic, Black Forest Labs, Cohere, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Mistral, OpenAI and Synthesia as providers and Bulgari, Fastweb, Getty Images, Iberdrola, Lenovo and Lufthansa as deployers, with about half being small or recently founded companies (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/strong-backing-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content, Jul 2026)
- A German court applied US fair use doctrine itself rather than deferring to US courts, finding it inapplicable because "simple, open-ended prompts" produced outputs substantially similar to the originals, which creates a route for EU collecting societies to reach training conducted entirely offshore (source: https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nfis/gema-notches-a-second-transatlantic-ai-copyright-win-in-germany/, Jul 2026)
- Suno, a defendant in major-label litigation, publicly endorsed the industry labelling proposal on the day it launched, positioning generative platforms as participants in disclosure regimes rather than opponents of them (source: https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/suno-sued-by-major-labels-says-transparency-is-important-as-record-industry-proposes-ai-labeling-system/, Jul 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- Fully AI-generated tracks exceeded 50% of daily uploads to Deezer at peak in Jun 2026 (about 90,000 tracks per day), yet AI music remains only 1-3% of total streams and up to 85% of those streams are detected as fraudulent, so the phenomenon measured at scale is royalty fraud on the supply side rather than listener substitution on the demand side (source: https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/, Jul 2026) (source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/music-streamer-deezer-says-more-than-50-of-daily-uploads-are-ai-generated/, Jul 2026)
- No chart compiler had formally adopted the labels' eligibility framework as of 30 Jul 2026: Billboard, the Official Charts Company in the UK and chart bodies in Australia, Germany, France and Japan had issued no policy, leaving IFPI's own charts as the sole implementation (source: https://www.techtimes.com/articles/322175/20260730/labels-united-chart-eligibility-ai-music-divided-which-ai-qualifies.htm, Jul 2026)
- The approved Anthropic settlement pays authors roughly $3,100 per book, split with publishers, after more than $100M in plaintiffs' legal fees, and leaves intact the underlying holding that training on lawfully acquired books is fair use, so the largest payout on record does not establish a recurring training-licence entitlement (source: https://www.npr.org/2026/07/27/nx-s1-5904606/anthropic-vs-bartz-ai-copyright-lawsuit-pros-cons, Jul 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- The European Commission published its final Article 50 Guidelines on 20 Jul 2026 and confirmed the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content as adequate; the final text holds that references buried in terms of service, generic labels such as "assistant", or metadata alone will not suffice, extends deepfake labelling to content posted on globally accessible platforms reaching EU audiences, and adds a carve-out for closed industrial or B2B environments (source: https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations, Jul 2026)
- Article 50 transparency rules apply from 2 Aug 2026, but generative AI systems placed on the market before that date have a grace period for the marking obligation until Dec 2026, and content generated before 2 Aug 2026 needs no retroactive labelling (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/quick-facts-transparency-rules-ai-systems, Aug 2026)

Capital Movements:
- Suno raised a $400M Series D at a $5.4B valuation led by Bond Capital on 3 Jun 2026, with IVP, Forerunner, Union Square Ventures, Alkeon and Quiet participating, eight weeks before the Munich judgment ordered it to stop training on GEMA repertoire (source: https://siliconangle.com/2026/06/03/generative-ai-music-startup-suno-ai-raises-400m-5-4b-valuation/, Jun 2026)

Technical Changes:
- The industry labelling program uses visual icons paired with metadata and delivery systems, and is scoped to sound recordings only, excluding lyrics, composition, music videos and cover art (source: https://www.ifpi.org/music-community-introduces-new-labelling-program-to-distinguish-generative-ai-in-sound-recordings/, Jul 2026)
- Signatories to the EU Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content must have an interoperability solution for watermark detection in place by 2 Feb 2027, moving provenance from per-vendor marking toward cross-vendor detection (source: https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations, Jul 2026)

Contradictions:
- From 2 Aug 2026 EU law requires AI-generated output to be marked as such, while the Munich court held on 31 Jul 2026 that AI Act compliance is no defence to copyright infringement, so the same track can be lawfully labelled and unlawfully produced.
- The music industry built chart gatekeeping and consumer labelling for a category that accounts for 1-3% of listening, while the harm actually measured at scale is bot-driven royalty fraud, which chart eligibility rules do not address.
- Suno raised at a $5.4B valuation on 3 Jun 2026 and was ordered on 31 Jul 2026 to stop training on the repertoire of Europe's largest music collecting society; capital pricing and legal exposure moved in opposite directions within eight weeks.
- Authors won the largest copyright settlement on record while the legal rule underneath it (fair use for lawfully acquired training material) forecloses the recurring licence those same authors sought.
