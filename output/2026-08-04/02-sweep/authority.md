# Evidence sweep, authority, 2026-08-04

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- EU authority moved from timeline politics to live enforcement. The 2026-07 sweep treated the EU only as "consolidated authority through simplification"; on 2 Aug 2026 the AI Office's direct enforcement powers over general-purpose AI providers switched on, and the Digital Omnibus on AI (in force late Jul 2026) gave that office exclusive supervisory competence over systems built on a provider's own GPAI models.
- The US preemption fight shifted from courts to administrative reinterpretation. The FTC issued the preemption-adjacent guidance the July sweep listed only as "tasked", while the flagship litigation vehicle (xAI v. Weiser) has had no docket entry since 27 Apr 2026.
- The BEAD lever behind ledger claim authority-2026-07-03 remains unpulled: NTIA had still not issued non-deployment guidance as of 30 Jun 2026 and did not tie it to state AI laws.
- New dimension not in the ledger: states expanded authority in the audit direction (Illinois SB 315, signed 6 Jul 2026, first US third-party frontier model audit mandate) even as the number of states enacting any AI law fell year on year.

Confirmed Developments:
- On 2 Aug 2026 the European Commission's AI Office gained direct enforcement powers over general-purpose AI model providers, including model access for evaluation, corrective measures, market withdrawal requests and fines up to the higher of EUR 15 million or 3 percent of worldwide annual turnover; neither source reports any formal proceeding opened against a named provider as of 3 Aug 2026 (source: https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/eu-ai-act-enforcement-phase-begins.html, Aug 2026) (source: https://qz.com/eu-ai-act-enforcement-fines-openai-anthropic-google-080326, Aug 2026)
- The Digital Omnibus on AI entered into force in late July 2026 and reallocated supervisory authority upward: the AI Office now holds exclusive supervisory competence over AI systems based on GPAI models from the same provider or group and over AI systems integrated into very large online platforms and search engines, while Annex III high-risk obligations moved to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I embedded-product obligations to 2 Aug 2028 (source: https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/eu-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes, Jul 2026)
- The FTC voted 2-0 on 1 Jul 2026 to seek comment on a proposed policy statement asserting that state AI laws may be impliedly preempted where they conflict with a federal regulatory scheme, naming Colorado's AI Act as an example; it was published in the Federal Register on 7 Jul 2026 as a proposed statement with comments closing 31 Jul 2026 (source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-seeks-public-comment-policy-statement-addressing-ai-accuracy, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/07/2026-13628/policy-statement-concerning-the-suppression-of-accuracy-in-artificial-intelligence-systems, Jul 2026)
- Illinois enacted SB 315, the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act, on 6 Jul 2026, becoming the first US state to require large frontier developers to retain an independent third-party auditor annually (from 1 Jan 2028), with disclosure statements from 1 Jan 2027, Illinois Attorney General enforcement, and civil penalties up to USD 1 million for initial and USD 3 million for subsequent violations (source: https://gov-pritzker-newsroom.prezly.com/gov-pritzker-signs-nation-leading-artificial-intelligence-safety-law, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260715-illinois-raises-the-bar-on-frontier-ai-what-developers-need-to-know, Jul 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- EU enforcement authority is centralizing partly by default rather than by design: as of 17 Jun 2026 only 9 of 27 member states had clearly designated both market surveillance and notifying authorities under the AI Act, against a 2 Aug 2025 deadline, with 12 partial and 6 unclear (source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/national-implementation-plans/, Jun 2026)
- States outside the litigation spotlight kept legislating through late July 2026: New Jersey enacted the FAIR Act on algorithmic rent pricing on 20 Jul 2026, and roughly 30 California AI bills were still live with appropriations hearings set for 3-5 Aug 2026 (source: https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/ai-legislative-update-july31-2026, Jul 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- Fewer states are legislating even though the total law count held up: 29 states had enacted AI legislation as of 1 Jul 2026 (109 AI laws) against 39 states (121 AI laws) at the same point in 2025, with the author attributing part of the drop to federal preemption pressure while finding no clean partisan or BEAD-driven pattern (source: https://www.techpolicy.press/where-state-ai-legislation-stands-half-way-into-2026/, Jul 2026)
- The BEAD funding lever remains rhetorical: NTIA Administrator Arielle Roth said on 30 Jun 2026 that non-deployment guidance was now targeted for "this summer", and the only state-law condition attached to BEAD participation described in the reporting concerns California's net neutrality law, not AI statutes (source: https://broadbandbreakfast.com/roth-ntia-now-targeting-non-deployment-guidance-this-summer/, Jun 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- Illinois routed frontier-model oversight through non-technology institutions: the Attorney General enforces, while the Illinois Emergency Management Agency and Office of Homeland Security receive critical safety incident reports and catastrophic risk assessments, placing frontier AI authority inside emergency management rather than a consumer or technology regulator (source: https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260715-illinois-raises-the-bar-on-frontier-ai-what-developers-need-to-know, Jul 2026)
- Commentators reading the FTC statement characterise it as "signaling and pressure, not legal preemption", an administrative claim of authority that no court has yet tested (source: https://stateline.org/2026/07/10/trump-administration-targets-state-ai-laws-over-ideology/, Jul 2026)

Capital Movements:
- Big Tech and AI corporations spent USD 60 million in the 2026 midterm cycle, USD 50.1 million of it to the Leading the Future PAC, with Meta separately spending USD 65 million toward non-federal super PACs opposing state lawmakers who support AI regulation and Anthropic pledging USD 20 million to a group backing lawmakers who favour regulating AI (source: https://www.citizen.org/article/corporate-supremacist-super-pacs-500-million-midterm-crypto-ai-sports-betting/, Jun 2026)

Technical Changes:
- The OpenID Foundation's AuthZEN working group published two drafts on 15 Jun 2026, the Access Request and Approval Profile and a profile for Model Context Protocol tool authorization, making delegated authority, approval, attestation and risk assessment first-class inputs to an authorization decision rather than out-of-band workarounds when an agent requests a high-value action (source: https://openid.net/openid-foundation-advances-authorization-for-the-agent-era-with-new-authzen-working-group-drafts/, Jun 2026)
- IETF Internet-Draft draft-klrc-aiagent-auth-00 (2 Mar 2026, informational, expires 3 Sep 2026) maps OAuth 2.0 delegation patterns onto agents and requires the delegating user or system identifier to be carried in the token "sub" claim so authorization decisions and audit trails resolve to a human principal (source: https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-klrc-aiagent-auth-00, Mar 2026)

Contradictions:
- The FTC asserts implied preemption of state AI laws on the theory that a federal scheme protects accurate AI outputs, while the same administration declines to enact binding federal AI output standards, so preemption is claimed from a regulatory scheme that has not been built.
- The EU's simplification measure produced concentration: the Digital Omnibus was framed as burden reduction, yet its practical effect is to move supervisory competence from unbuilt national authorities to the AI Office.
- The venue supposed to settle US federal-state AI authority may never rule. Colorado rewrote the challenged statute while xAI v. Weiser was stayed by consent, and the docket has been silent since 27 Apr 2026.
- Federal pressure reduced the number of states legislating without reducing the volume or stringency of law: Illinois passed the most demanding frontier-model statute in the country in the same quarter that state participation fell.
