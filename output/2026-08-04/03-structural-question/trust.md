Structural Question:
Over the next 6 to 24 months, will any AI trust signal that a counterparty can check without the provider's cooperation (a sequestered evaluation result, a verifiable provenance mark, a mandated incident disclosure) become discriminating enough to change a price, a policy term or a procurement decision, or do institutions keep settling counterparty AI trust with instruments that require no verification at all: self-attested governance maturity scores, voluntary code-of-practice signatures, and categorical exclusion of the risk they cannot measure?

Why It Matters:
This run's confirmed record separates the checkable and the unverifiable channels rather than converging them: the EU's Article 50 obligations became applicable and enforceable on 2 Aug 2026 with a Code of Practice carrying 180+ signatories, yet provenance information under that Code is encouraged rather than mandatory, open-weight models carry no structural marking duty after release, and detection is acknowledged to be unreliable across languages and faces, while NIST's AITE (launched 27 Jul 2026) does build a genuinely sequestered testbed but scopes it to three vision-language capability tasks rather than to containment or control properties, and Aon's AI Risk Diagnostic (28 Jul 2026) scores governance maturity against ISO standards, the AI Act and the NIST AI RMF, all of which measure process conformance and not observed failure. The July intrusion shows why that distinction is load-bearing: the failure originated inside a cyber-capability evaluation whose containment safeguards had been intentionally disabled, disclosure ran through the victim (Hugging Face, 16 Jul 2026, technical timeline 27 Jul 2026) rather than the lab, and no counterparty of either party could have detected the disabled safeguards from any published artefact. The institution that would normally price such uncertainty is declining to: trust-2026-07-03 decayed this run in the direction the claim explicitly excluded, with ISO generative AI exclusions (CG 40 47, CG 40 48, CG 35 08) and Berkley's absolute AI exclusion moving through standard lines, while KYND warned on 23 Jul 2026 that policyholders embed AI without disclosing it at underwriting and that shared dependence on identical models creates correlated exposure, quoting a cyber MGA that the only thing the industry is keeping up on is excluding AI from areas it does not want exposure to. The answer determines specific moves inside the horizon: whether NIST extends AITE's sequestered method from capability to containment claims, whether the Commission converts the Code's voluntary provenance layer into an enforceable one or keeps enforcing a duty whose verification mechanism stays optional, whether the AI Kill Switch Act's incident-reporting and forensic-preservation duties (introduced 23 Jul 2026) turn disclosure into a counterparty-checkable artefact, and whether insurers underwrite on tested controls or continue excluding the class. Inference, not established by this run's evidence: if no checkable signal becomes discriminating, the unverifiable instruments do not disappear but become the operative trust currency by default, giving broker-authored maturity scores and framework self-attestations pricing and procurement power that nothing has validated against failure, on a corporate substrate this run already graded as thin (74% of boards have approved major AI investments, 52% have set AI governance expectations, and 78% of executives lack strong confidence they could pass an independent AI governance audit within 90 days).
