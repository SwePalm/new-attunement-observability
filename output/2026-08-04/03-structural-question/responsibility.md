Structural Question:
Does the mechanism Washington used in HB 2225, declaring an AI violation a per se unfair or deceptive act under a pre-existing general consumer protection statute rather than writing a bespoke AI cause of action, get attached by any state before mid-2028 to AI systems that make consequential economic decisions (credit, hiring, housing, care triage), or does the child-safety framing that shields that mechanism from federal preemption also fix it permanently inside the one product category where the harm is emotional?

Why It Matters:
This run's corrected record shows individual remedy already exists, but built twice by different means: Oregon SB 1546 (signed 31 Mar 2026, effective 1 Jan 2027) and California SB 243 (in force 1 Jan 2026, the only one of the three currently operative) each write a bespoke measure of the greater of actual damages or $1,000 per violation, while Washington HB 2225 (signed 24 Mar 2026, effective 1 Jan 2027) supplies no damages measure of its own and instead makes a violation a per se unfair or deceptive act under the state Consumer Protection Act, borrowing that older statute's private cause of action. Only the borrowed hook travels cheaply, because it asks no legislature to define an AI system and no court to settle the product-versus-service question that In re: ChatGPT Product Liability Cases (JCCP 5431) has left with no dispositive ruling and no trial date, and the judicial route to settling it is thinning: responsibility-2026-07-01 decayed this run because the Jan 2026 Garcia settlement removed the appellate vehicle before the Eleventh Circuit could rule. What protects the hook is also what pins it down, since the 11 Dec 2025 executive order names transparency, reporting, documentation and safety-testing regimes as preemption targets while expressly sparing otherwise lawful state child-safety AI laws, so the companion lane is federally sheltered by exactly the framing that will not stretch to a loan denial, and the one state that did reach consequential decisions moved the opposite way when Colorado deleted its duty of reasonable care on 14 May 2026 under SB 26-189 after xAI sued and DOJ intervened. Inference, not established by this run's evidence: if the hook stays sealed inside relationship simulation, the three statutes stop functioning as a beachhead and start functioning as an alibi, evidence that individuals can already sue, offered in place of a remedy for the harms that carry money; two narrow observables separate those futures inside the horizon, whether any 2027 state session attaches a per se unfair-practice designation to a non-companion AI use, and whether the undefined unit of "violation" is fixed at per message, per session or per user, since that alone decides whether the remedy already on the books is nominal or class-scale.
