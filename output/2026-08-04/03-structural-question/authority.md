Structural Question:
When no venue rules, whose claim of authority do regulated firms actually build to: over the next 6 to 24 months, is authority over frontier AI allocated by adjudicated law (a court merits ruling on preemption or an enacted federal statute) or by unadjudicated assertion from bodies that have either not yet built the capacity to enforce (the EU AI Office, holding exclusive competence across 27 member states) or not yet tested the legal theory (the FTC's implied preemption of state AI laws)?

Why It Matters:
On 2 Aug 2026 the AI Office gained direct enforcement powers over general-purpose AI model providers, including model access for evaluation, corrective measures and fines up to the higher of EUR 15 million or 3 percent of worldwide annual turnover, and the Digital Omnibus in force in late Jul 2026 gave it exclusive supervisory competence over AI systems built on a provider's own GPAI models, yet as of 17 Jun 2026 only 9 of 27 member states had clearly designated both a market surveillance and a notifying authority. On the US side the FTC voted 2-0 on 1 Jul 2026 to propose a policy statement asserting implied preemption of state AI laws from a federal scheme that imposes no binding output standards, commentators read it as "signaling and pressure, not legal preemption", and the case that would test the question (xAI v. Weiser) has had no docket entry since 27 Apr 2026, which this run's grading recorded as a surprise: the flagship vehicle may be mooted rather than decided. Firms therefore face two simultaneous, unadjudicated claims of authority in the same quarter that Illinois SB 315 (signed 6 Jul 2026) imposed the strictest US frontier duties, annual third-party audits from 1 Jan 2028 and penalties to USD 3 million, and routed them through the Attorney General and emergency management rather than a technology or consumer regulator. The answer sets whether compliance is built to the strictest operative statute or held at the weakest pending preemption, and whether providers meet AI Office demands substantively or contest competence (inference, not fact: neither posture is yet observable, because no formal proceeding against a named provider had opened as of 3 Aug 2026, and a further inference is that capacity gaps in 18 member states make the AI Office's practical reach narrower than its formal competence).
