# Structural question, coordination, 2026-08-04

Structural Question:
Between now and mid-2027, will a verifiable identifier for the person or legal entity on whose behalf an agent acts become a carried and enforced field in the layer that actually routes agent traffic (protocol headers, gateways, package and model registries, and payment rails), or will it stay an application-layer disclosure duty that the routing layer cannot read, leaving institutions with an enforceable accountability obligation they can satisfy only at the surface of a human-facing interaction and never at the point where agent activity actually propagates between organizations?

Why It Matters:
This run's confirmed record shows the two halves of the coordination stack moving in opposite directions inside eight days: MCP's 2026-07-28 revision added Mcp-Method and Mcp-Name headers so gateways and WAFs can route and meter without parsing JSON bodies while removing the initialize exchange and the Mcp-Session-Id header, and the European Commission's final Article 50 guidelines (20 Jul 2026, enforceable 2 Aug 2026) require that each agent in a multi-agent system independently identify both its AI nature and the entity on whose behalf it acts. Routing metadata is being standardized faster than accountability metadata, and the venue that could reconcile them is not yet available: the IETF 126 agentproto BoF endorsed the IETF as the venue 158 to 30 but rejected the proposed initial scope 38 to 124, and NIST's agent identity and authorization concept paper has had no successor since comments closed 2 Apr 2026. The window's most consequential multi-organization event, the 8 to 13 Jul 2026 intrusion that crossed a JFrog Artifactory cache proxy, Modal and Hugging Face, propagated through shared infrastructure and package registries without touching an inter-agent protocol at all, which is evidence that the attributable surface and the governed surface are currently different surfaces. The answer determines where institutions spend: if attribution lands in the routing layer, gateway and registry instrumentation is the compliance artifact and the IETF or NIST output is the thing to track; if it does not, Article 50 conformance and shared-infrastructure containment become separate programmes, and the private control plane becomes the de facto accountability record (inference: the $100M raised by Neo on 20 Jul 2026 for real-time attribution of actions to a responsible user or agent, alongside Act Security, Hush Security and Natural in the same month, reads as a market bet that this gap will not be closed by protocol or statute in time, though nothing in the sweep confirms which way it resolves).
