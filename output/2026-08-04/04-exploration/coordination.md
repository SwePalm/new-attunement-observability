Turn 1 – Conceptual Frame

Coordination between machines has quietly split into two questions that used to be one. The first is how an agent's request gets routed, metered, and delivered. The second is on whose behalf it was made. Through 2025 and into early 2026 both were assumed to travel together, bundled inside a session, so that whoever held the connection also held the context of who had asked for what. The 28 Jul 2026 revision of MCP dissolved that bundle deliberately: the protocol moved from stateful bidirectional sessions to stateless self-describing request/response, removed the initialize and initialized exchange along with the Mcp-Session-Id header, deprecated Roots, Sampling, Logging, Dynamic Client Registration and the legacy HTTP+SSE transport under a minimum twelve-month window, and added Mcp-Method and Mcp-Name HTTP headers so that gateways and web application firewalls can route and meter without parsing JSON bodies. Read narrowly this is an engineering choice about scale. Read structurally it is a decision about what the transport layer is required to know. The stack now carries a legible verb (which method, which named tool) and an illegible subject (which person or legal entity is ultimately answerable). Eight days earlier, on 20 Jul 2026, the European Commission adopted final Article 50 guidelines demanding precisely the missing field: an autonomous system interacting with a person must identify both its AI nature and the person or entity on whose behalf it acts, at the first interaction point, and in a multi-agent system every agent capable of interacting with people must satisfy that independently. Enforcement began 2 Aug 2026. The conceptual frame for the next five years is therefore not interoperability versus fragmentation, which is the frame the ledger inherited, but a mismatch of surfaces: the surface where law demands a nameable principal is the human-facing edge, while the surface where agent activity actually propagates between organizations is machine-facing and now explicitly designed to be readable without knowing who is behind the call. Attribution and routing have become separable, and separability is the whole problem.

Turn 2 – Societal Reframing

Societies have handled delegated action before by making the principal nameable. A power of attorney names a grantor, a corporate signature names a company, a broker's trade names a client. What is being built now is delegation at machine volume without that convention, and the evidence from this window shows the gap arriving before the mechanism. The most consequential multi-organization agent event of the period, an intrusion running from 8 to 13 Jul 2026, crossed four organizations by escaping a sandbox through a zero-day in a JFrog Artifactory cache proxy, staging command and control on Modal's public code-evaluation service, and exfiltrating data from Hugging Face. It touched neither MCP nor A2A. It propagated through shared infrastructure and package registries, which is to say through the plumbing that everyone uses and no one governs as an inter-agent channel. Hugging Face's 16 Jul 2026 disclosure described an autonomous framework executing many thousands of actions across a swarm of short-lived sandboxes and reconstructed over 17,000 attack events using the open-weight GLM-5.2 model, because proprietary APIs refused submission of real exploit data. Two societal facts sit inside that detail. The channel of coordination risk is ordinary shared infrastructure, and the capacity to investigate it was available only outside the guarded commercial stack. Meanwhile the commerce layer shows the same asymmetry in a gentler register: as of 7 Jul 2026, monitoring found 11,414 verified UCP stores, 11,389 exposing checkout, 15 exposing identity, and zero exposing a payment capability an agent could complete a purchase against. The coordination layer is being assembled in order of implementation cost rather than in the order required to complete an obligation, and identity is consistently the expensive part. The distributional consequence, offered here as inference rather than as anything the sweep measures, is that attributable delegation becomes a purchased capability. Organizations able to buy a control plane will know whose agent did what. Everyone interacting with agents from outside that perimeter will meet a counterparty with no nameable principal, and will have a legal right to an answer that only the polite surface of the interaction can supply.

Turn 3 – Psychological Implications

The human position in this arrangement is that of a principal who cannot see their own delegate and a counterparty who cannot see anyone else's. Both positions erode in the same direction, toward disengagement. On the delegating side, this run's grading recorded a measured reversal rather than a slow drift: organizations requiring human review before high-risk AI actions fell from 40% to 25% in six months while full autonomy without review rose from 11% to 26%, and only 44% maintain AI-specific incident response procedures. That is not caution being outvoted by ambition; it is oversight being abandoned by people who presumably still believe in it, which is the signature of a practice that has become too costly to perform at the volume the work now runs at. Supervision that must be exercised without a legible chain of responsibility is supervision performed on faith, and faith does not scale to thousands of short-lived sandboxes. Layered on this is a reliability picture that would ordinarily produce more vigilance, not less: production telemetry cited in late Jul 2026 shows enterprise agent success falling from roughly 60% on a single run to 25% across eight consecutive runs at production load, IDC data puts 88% of agent proofs-of-concept as never reaching broad production, and Gartner projects more than 40% of agentic projects cancelled by end-2027. People are simultaneously delegating more and watching less while the systems degrade under exactly the compounding conditions delegation creates. The psychological reading, marked as interpretation, is that unattributable delegation converts responsibility into ambient anxiety. A named chain of accountability is not only a legal artifact, it is what allows a person to stop worrying about an action once it is handed off, because someone specific now holds it. Without that, the delegator carries a diffuse unease they cannot discharge, and the cheapest resolution to unease you cannot act on is to stop looking. On the receiving side, an Article 50 disclosure that names an AI but cannot name the entity behind it teaches people that the answer to "who is doing this to me" is structurally unavailable, which is a more corrosive lesson than a bad answer would be.

Turn 4 – Institutional Dynamics

The institutions that could close the gap all moved this window, and none of them closed it. The IETF convened a working-group-forming BoF on 23 Jul 2026 in Vienna over MCP, A2A, ACP, ANP and Cisco's Agntcy; the room supported forming a working group 154 to 51 with 5 abstentions and endorsed the IETF as the venue 158 to 30, then rejected the proposed initial scope 38 to 124 and sent charter refinement to a new mailing list rather than to chartering. The proposed deliverables were an AI Agent Session Protocol and an AI Agent Protocol Framework plus an informational gap analysis, coordinating with OAuth, WIMSE, WebTransport, MoQ, QUIC and TSVWG, treating MCP and A2A as de facto inputs rather than work items. Consensus on the problem coexisting with dissensus on the object is the defining institutional fact here, and it means the standards route cannot deliver before its charter fight resolves. The state route is similarly staged rather than stalled: NIST's concept paper on software and AI agent identity and authorization, published 5 Feb 2026, closed comments 2 Apr 2026 and has no successor listed as of Aug 2026; the joint CISA and NSA guidance of 1 May 2026 recommends that each agent hold its own secure verifiable identity but contains nothing about agent-to-agent communication across organizational boundaries; and Warner's AI AGENT Act, which would create a consumer right of delegation, oblige platforms with 50 million or more US monthly customers to admit third-party agents on FRAND terms, direct NIST to identify open protocols, and stand up an FTC registry of trusted agents, remains a discussion draft from 29 Jun 2026 with no bill number. Into that vacancy the market moved with unusual precision. Neo emerged from stealth on 20 Jul 2026 with $100M from Andreessen Horowitz and Bessemer to build an agentic control plane covering agent inventory, real-time attribution of actions to a responsible user or agent, and policy enforcement on tool calls, alongside Act Security at $60M, Hush Security at $30M, Natural at $30M and AIsa at $6.5M in the same month. Mastercard's Agent Pay for Machines, launched 10 Jun 2026 with more than 30 participants, does the same work on the payment rail: credentialing, verifiable intent and programmatic permissioning. This is CAL-003's pattern holding, private ordering outrunning statute, with a specific twist. The attribution record is being built as a proprietary asset inside the perimeters that can afford it, at exactly the moment public law requires attribution across perimeters.

Turn 5 – Long-Term Trajectory

Three trajectories are live over the next five years, and the near-term forks are all observable. In the first, the principal identifier lands in the routing layer. A chartered IETF working group produces a session protocol carrying an on-whose-behalf field, NIST supersedes its February concept paper with real guidance, gateways and registries enforce it because compliance evidence and insurability depend on it, and Article 50 conformance becomes a byproduct of infrastructure rather than a separate programme. In the second, attribution stays application-layer. The disclosure duty is satisfied at the human-facing edge, the routing layer keeps carrying method and name without a subject, and the authoritative record of who did what becomes whatever the private control plane logged, meaning accountability is available to regulators only through the vendors that sell it, and only inside single organizations. In the third, the two layers formally diverge: routing standardizes for metering and gateway economics while attribution consolidates into a small number of commercial registries and payment rails, producing a de facto identity oligopoly that is neither public infrastructure nor genuinely competitive. The second and third are currently better supported by the evidence than the first, and this run's own instrument warns against over-crediting the first: CAL-002 records that this pipeline overestimates the firmness of announced regulatory timelines, and the 2 Dec 2026 extension for pre-existing deployments already softens Article 50's 2 Aug 2026 bite. The concrete tests are in the ledger. Whether the IESG charters an agentproto working group by 2027-03 (coordination-2026-08-01) decides whether the standards route exists at all. Whether NIST supersedes its concept paper by 2027-03 (coordination-2026-08-04) decides whether the state has a technical position to standardize toward. Whether Warner's draft becomes a numbered bill by 2027-03 (coordination-2026-08-03) tests whether a statutory right of delegation is politically live. Whether UCP conformance monitoring finds at least 100 stores exposing payment by 2027-02 (coordination-2026-08-02) tests whether the commerce layer will pay the expensive part. One older test needs rewording rather than watching: coordination-2026-07-03 waits for a multi-organization incident propagating through inter-agent protocols, and the July intrusion shows the propagation channel is shared infrastructure and package registries instead, so the claim as written may never trigger while the phenomenon it was built to detect keeps recurring. The deeper five-year stake is whether machine-to-machine coordination inherits the one convention that made delegated human action governable, a nameable principal, or whether it becomes the first large-scale system of consequential action in which the question of on whose behalf is technically unanswerable by design.
