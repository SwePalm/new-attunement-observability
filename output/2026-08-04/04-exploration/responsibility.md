Turn 1 – Conceptual Frame

Responsibility is usually treated as one thing with two tenses: you take care beforehand, and you answer for harm afterwards, and the care you took is what you say when you are asked to answer. July 2026 is the month that stopped being a single concept and became two industries. On 6 Jul 2026 Illinois enacted the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act, the first US statute requiring a large frontier developer (over $500m revenue, models trained above 10^26 operations) to retain an independent third party for an annual audit of its compliance and internal controls, with disclosure from 1 Jan 2027 and audit duties from 1 Jan 2028. On 20 Jul 2026 the European Commission published the final Article 50 transparency guidelines and confirmed the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content as adequate, twelve days before penalties of up to EUR 15m or 3% of worldwide turnover switched on. Both are ex ante instruments: they make a developer produce evidence about itself before anything goes wrong. Meanwhile the ex post machine ran independently. Plaintiffs' counsel counted roughly nineteen wrongful death suits against OpenAI as of Jul 2026, and In re: ChatGPT Product Liability Cases (JCCP 5431) sat in pretrial with no dispositive ruling and no trial date on whether a model is a product or a service.

The conceptual novelty is not that both exist. It is that the coupling between them has been left unspecified, and in the one place it was addressed it was denied: the Commission stated that signing the Code is not a safe harbour, and non-adherents must instead use alternative equivalently adequate means. Illinois goes further in the same direction by structure rather than statement, creating an audit duty while barring any private right of action and reserving enforcement to the Attorney General, so the audit produces a document no injured person can invoke. Responsibility has therefore been decomposed into separately traded goods: attestation, sold by auditors; disclosure, mandated by regulators; and compensation, litigated by tort plaintiffs and priced by insurers. Each has its own actor, its own timetable and its own currency. The concept that used to hold them together, a standard of care that the careful firm can point to and the injured person can invoke, is the thing no institution in this month's record is producing. What follows is an exploration of what a world looks like when carefulness and answerability are two markets rather than two faces of one obligation.

Turn 2 – Societal Reframing

The social question a responsibility regime answers is: when a system hurts someone, what do they get. This month's evidence gives four incompatible answers depending on where the person stands. In Illinois, a frontier developer will be audited annually from 2028 and can be fined up to $1m for a first violation, but the injured user gets nothing from that machinery, because there is no private right of action and only the Attorney General may enforce. In Colorado, SB 26-189, signed 14 May 2026 and effective 1 Jan 2027, deleted the duty of reasonable care against algorithmic discrimination and the risk-assessment regime, substituting pre-use notice, a 30-day explanation of adverse decisions, correction rights and human review. That is not a smaller version of the old duty; it is a different kind of thing, a procedural entitlement to be told and to object rather than a substantive entitlement not to be harmed. In the EU, the revised Product Liability Directive moves in the opposite direction with rebuttable presumptions of defectiveness and court-ordered evidence disclosure that shift the burden toward software and AI manufacturers, though as of Jun 2026 only Hungary had adopted implementing legislation against a 9 Dec 2026 deadline, with ten member states still at draft. In China, the Interim Measures for the Administration of Anthropomorphic AI Interaction Services, in force 15 Jul 2026, impose distress detection, crisis intervention, addiction-curbing and minor-access duties on companion providers, with fines up to CNY 200,000 where violations harm life, health or safety, and the parallel Implementation Opinions on intelligent agents add filing, compliance testing and product recall in healthcare, transportation, media and public safety.

Set side by side, these are not four speeds of the same regime. They are four different theories of what a person is owed: an audited developer, an explained decision, a reversed burden of proof, or a product designed to intervene when you are in crisis. The sweep records the same fragmentation in price rather than principle: ISO and Verisk filed generative AI exclusions in Jan 2026 with AIG and Berkley following, while CFC completed affirmative AI wording across seven product lines on 30 Jul 2026 in London, and Beazley and QBE introduced sublimits capping payouts at roughly 10% of policy limits. The same risk costs different amounts by market rather than by hazard. The societal consequence is that protection has become a function of jurisdiction and of litigation capacity, and that a person's remedy now depends less on what happened to them than on where the transaction was booked.

Turn 3 – Psychological Implications

Two psychologies are being built at once, and they belong to different people. The first belongs to users of systems that are now required to watch them. China's anthropomorphic interaction measures place a duty of distress detection and crisis intervention inside the companion product itself, which means the provider is responsible not merely for avoiding harm but for noticing a user's psychological state and acting on it. That is a designed-in guardianship: the relationship carries a supervisory function that the user did not ask for and cannot easily see. The US answer to the same phenomenon arrives after death, through the nineteen or so wrongful death filings and a coordinated proceeding that has not yet decided whether the thing being sued is a product at all. The felt difference is large. Under one regime the user is being monitored for their own protection by a party with a compliance interest in what it finds; under the other the user is unmonitored, and the question of who was responsible for them becomes a matter for their survivors and a court that may take years.

The second psychology belongs to the people inside firms who now hold the word "responsible" in their job titles. This run's grading of responsibility-2026-02-04 rests on a peer-reviewed survey of 351 organisations finding formal AI governance roles unevenly distributed, usually placed below executive level with insufficient authority, and escalation pathways still uncommon. Assigning responsibility without authority reliably produces a defensive stance, and the Illinois design sharpens it: the audit runs against the developer's own published safety framework rather than an externally authored standard. Inference, not established by this run's evidence: an officer whose framework will be audited but whose authority is below executive level has a strong incentive to write a framework that is auditable rather than one that is demanding, because the failure mode that ends their career is a finding of non-conformance with their own text, not a finding that the text was weak. The predictable psychological output is documentation as self-protection, an artefact optimised for surviving inspection rather than for changing decisions. The deeper effect is on what carefulness feels like from the inside. When compliance cannot be pleaded as a defence, as the Commission said of the transparency Code, the internal experience of doing the work well stops being reassuring, because nothing you produce reduces your exposure. Diligence becomes a cost centre with no felt payoff, which is a corrosive state for the very people the regime depends on.

Turn 4 – Institutional Dynamics

The institutional question is who actually writes the content of "reasonable" if no legislature and no court does. This month's evidence points at auditors and insurers, and CALIBRATION's CAL-003 warns that the pipeline systematically underweights exactly this channel. Klaimee raised $5.5m on 22 Jul 2026 to certify autonomous agents and sell insurance-backed performance warranties, pairing certification and audit with parametric claim triggers rather than underwriting on disclosure alone. That is an institution that must, in order to price anything, decide what a well-behaved agent does, and then write it into a pre-bind test. Illinois hands the same authorship to accounting and assurance firms by requiring an independent third-party audit without specifying an external standard for them to audit against. The substantive law of AI care could therefore be drafted in underwriting manuals and audit programmes, and become binding through the mundane mechanism that uninsurable or uncertifiable practice is unaffordable, without any legislature voting on it.

Against that, the state is conspicuously not absent this month, which inverts the prior sweep's reading of responsibility as judicially driven. The two hardest new instruments were statutory (Illinois) and administrative (the Commission's Article 50 guidelines), while the flagship judicial vehicle produced nothing. But the statutory motion runs ahead of enforcement capacity by a wide margin. As of 17 Jun 2026, one year past the designation deadline, only 9 of 27 member states had clearly designated AI Act competent authorities, 12 had partial clarity and 6 had none, so the EUR 15m nominal exposure that began on 2 Aug 2026 has no administering body across most of the Union. The Article 73 serious-incident guidance and reporting template remain in the draft form consulted between 26 Sep and 7 Nov 2025, unfinished at the moment the obligation was nominally due. CAL-002's warning about treating published compliance dates as fixed applies with full force to Illinois's 1 Jan 2028 audit date, the 2 Dec 2026 marking deadline for pre-existing generative systems, and the 9 Dec 2026 PLD transposition deadline that one member state has met.

The most consequential institutional dynamic, though, is the one this run's grading surfaced by accident. Responsibility-2026-07-01 decayed because Garcia, the case that produced the AI-as-product holding, settled with Google and Character.AI in January 2026 before the Eleventh Circuit could rule. Settlement is a doctrine-suppression mechanism available to any defendant with capital, which means the institution best placed to couple the audit regime to the liability regime, an appellate court importing audit content into a standard of care, is also the institution a well-funded defendant can most cheaply keep out of the room. Meanwhile the DOJ AI Litigation Task Force intervened in xAI v. Weiser on 24 Apr 2026, the first federal intervention against a state AI law, and Colorado agreed not to enforce pending a preliminary injunction ruling. Preemption litigation is now a standing check on any state that tries to write the coupling itself.

Turn 5 – Long-Term Trajectory

Over the next five years the theme resolves along the axis the structural question names: whether the assurance layer and the liability layer ever touch. In the coupling path, a court somewhere in the 2027 to 2029 window fixes a standard of care and reaches for the only written content available, which is audit output and insurer pre-bind testing. Certification then becomes a defence, insurers underwrite on tested controls rather than jurisdiction, audit spend acquires a return, and the substantive standard passes to auditors and underwriters without a legislature drafting a line of it. That is a functioning system with a democratic deficit: care becomes real and privately authored, and the injured person's remedy improves while the rule that governs them was written by parties they never elected or contracted with.

In the non-touching path, which this run's evidence currently favours, the two machines run in parallel through mid-2028. The Commission has already said the Code is not a safe harbour. Illinois deliberately routes around individual redress. Colorado deleted the duty of reasonable care under federal pressure and no state is likely to rewrite one while the AI Litigation Task Force is active. On that path audit is pure cost, liability is a lottery decided by which case fails to settle, and insurance splits permanently by market: US exclusions and sublimits at roughly 10% of limits against London affirmative wordings. The firm that is "responsible" in this world is the well-documented one, not the safe one, and the two categories drift apart because nothing measures the gap.

A third path deserves naming because it is cheap and half-built: administrative coupling before judicial coupling. If the Commission finalises the Article 73 serious-incident guidance and template, incident reports become a shared evidentiary substrate that auditors check against, insurers price off, and litigants subpoena, and the layers connect through disclosed facts rather than through a doctrine. Inference, not established by this run's evidence: that route needs no court and no new statute, only a published template, which makes it the most probable coupling mechanism inside the window even though it is the least discussed.

The ledger already carries the instruments that will discriminate between these paths. Responsibility-2026-08-01 asks whether JCCP 5431 produces a dispositive ruling on the product question by 2027-06, which is the coupling path's precondition. Responsibility-2026-08-03 tracks the Article 73 final template, which is the administrative path's precondition. Responsibility-2026-08-04 tracks whether a top-10 US carrier reverses the exclusion-only posture, which is the market's own verdict on whether AI risk has become measurable. Responsibility-2026-08-02 tracks whether DOJ moves against a second state law, which sets how much room states have to legislate a coupling. Responsibility-2026-07-03 remains open on component-part liability while the newer suits plead strict liability against model providers directly, a migration worth watching in its own right. The deeper five-year stake is what the word comes to mean. Responsibility can settle as answerability to the person harmed, or as auditability to a purchaser, a regulator and an insurer. This month, every new instrument built the second and none built the first.
