## 3. Institutional Realism Audit

Assessment: **moderate realism**

Notes:

- Friction is present: the undefined "primarily relies" term, guidance that arrives later than the operative date, notices that record employers' euphemisms, slow and rare challenges. These match the sweep's Counter-Signals and Emerging Signals.
- Transitions are implied, not completed: guidance is awaited and agreements spread unevenly. The text does not present an enforcement regime as stable.
- Power asymmetries are visible in who writes the form, who is excluded from the notice, and who bears the unpaid year.
- The world is somewhat too coherent. One logic, that the law watches exits while the adjustment happens at entry, explains the notice form, the staffing agency, the rejection template and the bargaining lag. The sweep supports this reading only as one interpretation: it reports weak payrolls beside low announced cuts and says the pattern "points to" a freeze, while also recording energy costs, an Iran war and rate risk as cited causes. A single reading is made to carry most of the world.
- No private right of action is modelled for SB 947 even though AB 1883 is reported to carry one; the enforcement texture is thinner than the statute landscape implies.

## 8. Overall Evaluation

Score (1–10): **7.8**

Forecast Integrity Level: **moderate**

Stability Risk: **medium**

Summary:
The scenario is structurally clean, honours the vantage and form rules, passes the citation gate on every Confirmed Development, and anchors all six emerging signals in dated sweep items. Its horizon is disciplined, its timelines for guidance and enforcement run slower than the statute, which is the correction the pipeline's regulatory-speed bias calls for, and the bargaining channel is given the lead over statute where the evidence supports it. Its weakness is epistemic concentration. The whole world rests on one interpretation of two noisy monthly series, that employers are freezing entry rather than cutting staff, and on a job class (the throughput-measured corroborator) that appears only as an inference from the statute's text. The sweep itself records that weak payrolls were not concentrated in AI-exposed sectors and that energy costs, war and rate risk were the stated reasons for restraint. The scenario compresses these into an AI-at-the-entrance story and presents the 2028 world as more settled than a first year of operation under undefined statutory terms would allow. Institutional realism is moderate: friction is visible, but enforcement is thin and the system explains itself too neatly. Its Irreplaceable Thing is genuinely distinct in kind from recent runs, though the closing logic and documentary form resemble the agency scenario. Overall the artifact is sound on sourcing and horizon and fragile on its central causal reading.
