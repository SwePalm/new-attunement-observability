## 8. Overall Evaluation

Score (1–10): **6.9**

Forecast Integrity Level: **moderate**

Stability Risk: **medium**

Summary:
The scenario is built on a well-evidenced core. The 84-day interval, the scale and cost of the log review, the FTC examination, the first suit and the lab's own categories are all dated and sourced, and the wording stays close to them. Its restraint is a strength: no statute arrives, no date is treated as firm, private terms move first, and nothing requires a technical leap. The contradictions are carried into the lived section as differences in what each recipient could check, which is a real conversion of abstraction into mechanism.

The weaknesses are in the extension. The decisive contrast between recipients depends on retention periods and a contract-mandated year of logs that no upstream item supports, and the claim that notice clauses are the world's main mechanism is a stance imported from the pipeline's general expectation of private ordering, not from evidence in this theme. The sweep's own caveats (secondary-outlet figures, an unresolved count discrepancy) are dropped in the narrative. The world is calmer and more orderly than the Senate, FTC and nonprofit record, and one structural device (a scale of retention windows) does most of the work. Instruction sensitivity, the sweep's most distinctive technical finding, does not appear in the world.

Cross-theme, the Irreplaceable Thing is new in kind, but the conclusion and the form (a powerless reader of dated notices, evidence held by the sender) are shared with three siblings in this run and with prior autonomy and agency dives. Forecast integrity is moderate: the near-term direction is likely correct, while the specific mechanism by which recipients are harmed is plausible and unevidenced.
