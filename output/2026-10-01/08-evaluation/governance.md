## 3. Institutional Realism Audit

Assessment: **moderate realism**

Notes:

- Friction is located sensibly. The platform forwards a notice that cites a different edition from its own footer, the state line declines to advise, the county clerk ticks a box with no definition, and no one is malicious. The outcome is a paid opinion that goes unread. Nothing resolves.
- Power asymmetries are visible: the platform decides how to read coverage and the maker bears the cost of being wrong; the library volunteer has no standing; the large developers hold the auditor relationship.
- The world is somewhat too tidy in its theme. Each vignette demonstrates one property of definitional churn, which approaches the list-like shape recorded against earlier scenarios. Three definition editions in circulation within a short window is a strong extension of the observed single-week change at SB 1000. Whether an app built on a third-party model would count as a "covered provider" at all is a question the scenario does not test and the evaluator has not verified.
- Transitions are implied, not stabilised: the federal term is proposed, not enacted, state dates are the only fixed points, and enforcement is absent. The scenario does not present a settled order.
- The frontier developers, the actual object of the structural question, appear only as a footnote to a webpage; the institutional realism of that relationship is not tested.

## 8. Overall Evaluation

Score (1–10): **6.7**

Forecast Integrity Level: moderate

Stability Risk: medium

Summary:
The scenario is structurally compliant, restrained in tone and well disciplined on horizon, and it applies the calibration memo as restraint rather than repeating its flagged biases: federal clocks are slowed, no statute is assumed, and private and state channels lead. Its six Signals Emerging items are accurately drawn from dated, sourced sweep entries, and no upstream citation gate fails.

Its weaknesses are epistemic rather than literary. The structural question is about who may examine a frontier developer; the scenario is about a small maker's coverage question, and the bridge between the two is thematic. The pre-release access, capability monitoring, test capacity and cross-border incident layers that the PESTLE built are barely used, so the world is narrower than the evidence. Two inventions carry load in Section 3: public auditor grades, which the sweep's "no disclosure requirement" does not support, and a numbered series of definition editions, which extends a single observed change. One forecast (a proposed federal term) and two small details (an overseer's office, a committee chair) are stated as fact without upstream grounding.

Institutional friction is credible and power asymmetries are visible, but the vignettes each demonstrate a single property of one logic, and the sweep's own contradictions are unused. Convergence with the siblings and the preceding runs is moderate: the Irreplaceable Thing is distinct, while the dated-paper gesture and the kind person at a desk repeat established patterns. Forecast integrity is moderate: the probable direction is defensible, the lived world is partly constructed.
