# Forces and feelings, governance, 2026-10-01

## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)

Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
The Political forces make the federal answer to frontier risk a signed, voluntary commitment and a decree about vocabulary: the 29 Sep 2026 Joint Commitment has no penalty, regulator or deadline, and the executive order tells agencies to say "super intelligence" while a definition is still 60 days from being proposed. The Social forces show what that does to the people who must act on the words. Public trust pools at the visibly adversarial local actors (state attorneys general, consumer groups), and small developers and volunteers are asked to apply terms that no enforcer has applied. The more the centre produces official language, the less the language is relied on by anyone below it. Directly grounded in the sweep: critics call the accord an industry marking its own homework, and it sits beside California duties for the same developers.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
The Economic forces create a paid market for assurance whose buyer is the audited party: each signatory chooses and contracts its own auditor, and a board committee reviews the report. The Legal forces are drifting the other way, towards definitions that are widened by state law (SB 1000 removing the user threshold on 30 Sep 2026) and that no court or attorney general has yet applied. The firms purchase examination that no law requires, while the laws that require examination are being drafted by bodies that have not yet examined anyone. Whichever definition of "independent" is first enforced will reprice every audit already sold. The seller of an audit therefore has an interest in the definition staying unsettled.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
Philosophically, governance is meant to be the stable background against which actors plan: a rule means what it says long enough to be relied on. In lived reality, the vocabulary of the rule is the thing that moves. "AI detection tool" became "disclosure verification tool" on enactment, a small provider became covered overnight, and a federal term for the most capable systems is being coined by decree before any text defines it. A person can obey the letter of every instrument and still not know which one applies to the thing in front of them. The ideal is certainty about meaning; the practice is a continuing negotiation about it, conducted mostly by people with counsel.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)

Normal Absurdity 1:
Every form that touches a model carries a small footer naming the edition of the definitions it was printed under, and a tick-box for "SI-assisted" that clerks tick for anything with a model in it, from a spell-checker to a triage system, because the box has no definition to refuse it. Nobody finds the box odd. (A lived expression of the executive order directing agencies to say "super intelligence" and the State Department's "SI" instruction, Sep 2026.)

Normal Absurdity 2:
Companies publish the name of their independent auditor in the way restaurants post an inspection grade, and people learn which auditors are considered thorough by word of mouth, as one learns which inspector is strict. Nobody asks who appointed the auditor, because the answer is on the same page as the grade. (From the Joint Commitment's design of partnerships with external auditors chosen by each company.)

Normal Absurdity 3:
Meetings of the signatory companies are announced afterwards with a one-line notice that "a regular meeting was held", and the notice is filed by librarians, law firms and journalists as a dated event in its own right, with a reference number, though it has no minutes. (From the pledge to "meet regularly" on standards.)

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing

The Irreplaceable Thing:
A settled definition: a term in a rule whose meaning is the same on the day you read it as on the day it is applied to you.

Explanation:
Scarce by a mechanism the sweep and PESTLE name. California's SB 1000 changed the covered-provider definition and renamed the verification tool on the day it was signed, and the 29 Sep 2026 order gave the science adviser 60 days to propose a legal meaning for "super intelligence" and to say whether it overrides existing definitions (Political 2, Legal 1 and 3). With every instrument on its own clock, the word is the only thing a small provider, a volunteer or a clerk can hold on to, and it is the first thing to move. It is institutional and material: printed, stamped with an edition, found or lost. Not a person, a witness or a promise; a protagonist can pursue it by trying to find out which edition governs. It differs in kind from the recent Irreplaceable Things, each a person, a presence or a record of an event.
