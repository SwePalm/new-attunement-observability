# Evidence sweep, desire, 2026-10-01

Window note: about six days since the 2026-09-25 sweep. Several items below are dated slightly before 25 Sep but were not in the previous sweep; their dates are stated.

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- California's SB 903, the bill that would have banned advertising chatbots as "therapy", was vetoed by Newsom on 30 Sep 2026 as "overly broad". The 2026-09-25 sweep listed it as awaiting signature. The same governor signed SB 1119 and SB 867 on the child-safety side three weeks earlier (source: https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/newsom-vetoes-overly-broad-california-000920849.html, Sep 2026).
- OpenAI added to its ads product on 30 Sep 2026: bulk product-feed campaign creation, product review tracking, wider conversion reporting, and a launch in Southeast Asia and Taiwan, taking the stated footprint to 70+ countries. No change to Sponsored Agents (still limited alpha) was reported (source: https://www.seroundtable.com/openai-chatgpt-ads-updates-42194.html, Sep 2026).
- The Commission's EU KIDS Act proposal (17 Sep 2026) sets AI companions as disabled by default for minors and bans design features that simulate relationships likely to create emotional dependency. It was not in the previous sweep (source: https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/eu-commission-proposes-a-new-kids-act-8313562/, Sep 2026).
- No lawsuit between Amazon and Meta over Muse, no suit against SB 1119, no FTC 6(b) output and no Dutch leidraad draft were found. NBC News reported on 29 Sep 2026 that in its tests some retailers, including Amazon and Walmart, blocked Muse despite Meta naming Walmart as a partner (source: https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/ai-agent-muse-shop-e-commerce-amazon-wal-mart-wayfair-buy-rcna599069, Sep 2026).

Confirmed Developments:
- The European Commission formally proposed the EU KIDS Act on 17 Sep 2026. For AI companions it requires that they be disabled by default for minors and prohibits design features that simulate interpersonal relations likely to create emotional dependency, manipulate behaviour or exploit children's vulnerabilities. Press coverage on 16 Sep 2026 described a plan to restrict under-15s from AI chatbots, social media and games, with age verification and parental controls. Adoption is described as unlikely to be immediate, and most obligations would apply six months after entry into force (source: https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/eu-commission-proposes-a-new-kids-act-8313562/, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/eu-plans-ban-ai-chatbots-134346913.html, Sep 2026)
- OpenAI's 30 Sep 2026 ChatGPT Ads update added bulk campaign and ad-group creation from spreadsheets, a Products tab showing review status and rejections, conversion reporting through the Insights API beyond a campaign's primary goal, and separate internal, public and legal account names. Ads reached Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam and Taiwan from 24 Sep 2026, and one outlet's summary says ChatGPT Ads is now in 70+ countries (source: https://www.seroundtable.com/openai-chatgpt-ads-updates-42194.html, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.adgully.com/post/21151/openai-expands-chatgpt-ads-into-seven-asian-markets-as-ad-platform-hits-1-billion-arr-pace, Sep 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Ad performance outside the Anglophone markets is stronger. A Graphite report on Similarweb panel data, published 29 Sep 2026, found ChatGPT ad click-through above 1% in Brazil (1.91%), Mexico (1.55%), Japan (1.53%) and South Korea (1.21%), against 0.6% to 0.75% in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand. The 31 European markets were excluded (source: https://www.searchenginejournal.com/chatgpt-ad-ctr-markets-report/591384/, Sep 2026)
- Retailers are not uniformly honouring Muse partnerships. NBC News tests on 29 Sep 2026 found some retailers blocking the agent, headlined as Muse being unable to shop at Amazon or Walmart (source: https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/ai-agent-muse-shop-e-commerce-amazon-wal-mart-wayfair-buy-rcna599069, Sep 2026)
- Pennsylvania's House passed HB 2006 on 29 to 30 Sep 2026 by 133 to 70, a chatbot safeguards bill with age verification for all users, parental consent for minors, bans on assisting suicide, violence or isolation of minors, and periodic AI-status notices. It now goes to the Senate. It is a second state-level vehicle after California (source: https://www.mychesco.com/a/news/government/pennsylvania-house-passes-ai-chatbot-safeguards-sending-bill-to-senate/, Sep 2026)
- German consumer-advice coverage on 26 Sep 2026 says ChatGPT ads in the EEA launched without personalised advertising but can be targeted to the content of the current conversation. It cites Verbraucherzentrale NRW that commercial communication must be recognisable as such under German law (source: https://www.it-daily.net/it-sicherheit/datenschutz-grc/werbung-bei-chatgpt-zu-beachten, Sep 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- Meta's stated monetisation for Muse is a small fee on completed transactions with free usage at first, and the 24 Sep 2026 coverage reports no ads inside Muse. This keeps the personal agent's revenue unit the purchase rather than the impression, unlike ChatGPT (source: https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/meta-maps-muse-monetization-strategy-110000882.html, Sep 2026)
- Google has not put ads in the standalone Gemini app. On 30 Apr 2026 its chief business officer said Google is open to it if AI Mode formats work, but no launch was found in this sweep, so the assistant-advertising shift is so far an OpenAI story, not an industry-wide one (source: https://www.androidauthority.com/google-gemini-could-soon-get-ads-3662450/, Apr 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- Newsom vetoed SB 903 on 30 Sep 2026, calling it "overly broad" because it would limit clinicians' use of care tools. The vetoed bill included a ban on advertising therapy delivered by chatbots. Author Sen. Padilla called the decision disappointing (source: https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/newsom-vetoes-overly-broad-california-000920849.html, Sep 2026)
- Pennsylvania HB 2006 passed the House 133 to 70 and moved to the Senate, with Attorney General enforcement and civil penalties up to USD 100,000 per violation per day (source: https://www.mychesco.com/a/news/government/pennsylvania-house-passes-ai-chatbot-safeguards-sending-bill-to-senate/, Sep 2026)
- The EU KIDS Act proposal of 17 Sep 2026 is the first EU legislative text to name AI companions for minors. It runs in parallel to the DSA designation of ChatGPT (source: https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/eu-commission-proposes-a-new-kids-act-8313562/, Sep 2026)

Capital Movements:
- Amazon announced on 10 Sep 2026 that select US brands using Amazon Ads and its demand-side platform can run campaigns inside ChatGPT, with ads as text or image units below responses. This routes a retail-media seller into OpenAI's inventory (source: https://cryptobriefing.com/amazon-openai-chatgpt-advertising-partnership/, Sep 2026)
- Meta's expansion of Muse to small businesses on 29 Sep 2026 adds a free tier with usage limits and paid plans for higher usage, tied to Meta ad accounts and business tools (source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/meta-is-expanding-its-ai-agent-muse-to-small-businesses/, Sep 2026)

Technical Changes:
- Muse for Small Business connects to Shopify, Slack, Dropbox, Asana, Stripe, QuickBooks and Instagram and Facebook analytics, using a business's product information, brand voice and common customer questions (source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/meta-is-expanding-its-ai-agent-muse-to-small-businesses/, Sep 2026)

Contradictions:
- The EU KIDS Act text bans companion design that fosters emotional dependency while the SB 903 veto shows California declining to restrict chatbot "therapy" advertising. The two jurisdictions treat reliance-fostering design differently by user group (minors versus adults).
- Meta named Walmart as a Muse partner while NBC tests found some retailers, reportedly including Walmart, blocking it. Only the headline and a summary excerpt of the NBC piece could be read, so the retailer-by-retailer detail is unverified.
- The Padilla reaction to the veto was found only in a Spanish-language IV Press page, which returned HTTP 429, and a Sacramento Bee page, which could not be fetched. The veto itself rests on one opened article plus search listings.
