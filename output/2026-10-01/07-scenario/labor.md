# Scenario, labor, 2026-10-01

## 1. Title & Core Question
- Title: Planned Positions
- Core Question: When the law watches who is removed from work and nothing watches who is never admitted to it, who pays for the year in which a person learns to do the job, and who will be left to check the machine's decisions once nobody has had that year?

## 2. Context Summary (Translation Layer) – Why This Future Exists
In September 2026 California made two choices about AI at work: employers may not discipline or fire people on an automated system's say-so alone, and layoff notices must name any work that automation has taken over. Both govern people who are already on a payroll. The same month, American companies announced their lowest September hiring plans since 2011 and their lowest September layoffs since 2022, and payrolls barely grew, a pattern that looks like a freeze rather than a purge. Employers pointed to energy costs, a war and interest rates, with AI as one more reason not to replace a role. New York had already shown that a disclosure box for AI-linked layoffs can sit empty for a year. By 2028 the new duties operate, notices are filed, reviewers countersign, and the entry-level job has become something companies announce more often than they fill.

## 3. Future World Snapshot (The Lived Experience) – A Day in This Future
Vantage: person subject to the system / entry-level hiring pipeline. Form: a sequence of notices received over eleven months.

**14 March, Halloran Benefits, application portal.** Thank you, Renee. Your application for Claims Associate (Entry) was reviewed by an automated system and corroborated by a human reviewer. We will not be proceeding. Planned positions this year: 300. Open positions: 0.

**2 April, Halloran careers page.** Our hiring plan stands. We remain committed to growing our team. Status of this role: future opening.

**30 May, Brightline Staffing.** Contract: Corroboration Analyst, eleven weeks, $19.50 an hour. You will confirm that automated recommendations for adverse action are supported by the record. Target: forty confirmations an hour. Renee took it. Nobody there had worked a claim.

**19 June, dashboard.** Your rate is forty-four an hour. Reminder: corroboration confirms support, it does not re-decide. Flags overturned this week: 0.

**8 August, Halloran to 212 employees, statutory notice.** Functions affected: claims intake. AI-automated functions: none applicable. Reason: workflow optimisation. Renee's contract ended three days later. Contractors received no notice, and the form had no box for them.

**3 September, Dolores Vance, retired claims lead, by message.** I got a year to be wrong on small claims. Marked up in red, every Friday. Nobody budgets the year now. They budget the answer.

**17 October, Foothill Credit Union, careers page.** Associate, twelve-month rotation. Your first quarter will not be measured. Applications received: 1,912. Positions: 2.

Renee read the third line aloud in the kitchen, as if checking it was meant for her. Her brother asked what a credit union paid for a quarter of not being measured. She said: salary, and he asked what the catch was. She said there was no catch; that was the odd part. She spent the evening on the cover letter, wrote "I would like to be taught" in the first line, deleted it, wrote it again. At eleven the portal said her application had been corroborated by a human reviewer. She could not tell whether that was good news.

## 4. Behavioral Shifts (Human Lens) – How People Adapt
Applicants learn to write for two readers: the screening system and the reviewer who countersigns it. They load applications with exact phrases from the posting and keep a spreadsheet of "planned positions" they are waiting on, checking weekly. Graduates take contract corroboration work because it is the only paid job with a start date, and then find that it cannot be listed as experience in the field they want, since it contains no decisions.

Inside firms, managers learn that the safest way to satisfy the human-review rule is speed and consistency. A reviewer who overturns flags is asked to explain; one who confirms is not. Staffing agencies sell corroboration by the hour. Experienced staff who once trained juniors spend their time on exceptions and are told there is no budget to coach.

Unions and works councils adapt in the other direction. Bargaining teams add hiring plans and apprenticeship numbers to notice-and-bargain language, because they find that protection against being replaced means little when the replacement is never advertised.

## 5. Structural Forces (System Lens) – What Holds This World Together
Three forces hold the world in place. First, an adjustment margin the law does not reach. Employers kept announced layoffs low and hiring plans lower through 2026, and the pattern of weak payrolls beside low cuts was read as a freeze. The California duties on termination and layoff notice are triggered by an exit, so a role that is never filled generates no paper.

Second, a disclosure format that records what employers are willing to write. New York's AI checkbox drew no AI attributions in its first year, and the California notices that name automated functions mostly carry "none applicable" or a neutral label. The key phrase in the termination statute, whether a decision "primarily relies" on a system, was left undefined, so firms adopted the cheapest countersignature process and wait for the first guidance, which arrives later than the operative date. Challenges are slow and rare.

Third, bargaining as the place where the introduction of AI is settled. Agreements such as the Blizzard and SUNY contracts spread unevenly: a German metal round bargained job security and profit-sharing, and AI arrived there as a side declaration, not a demand. Where no union exists, the rule is whatever the employer's own plan says.

## 6. Reflection & Implications – Questions This World Asks Us
If a human must confirm a machine's decision, who is responsible for making sure that human has ever been taught to judge?

A law counts the people who are let go. Who counts the people who were never let in?

Is a year of paid inexperience something an employer owes the future, or a cost it may decline?

## 7. Pullback Layer: From Possibility to Probability
### 7.1 Signals Emerging (Plausible Zone) – Early Signals We Already See
- California's governor signed the No Robo Bosses Act and an AI-specific layoff-notice law on 30 Sep 2026, a year after vetoing a broader predecessor.
- American employers announced the fewest September job cuts since 2022 and the lowest September hiring plans since 2011, with AI cited in about 9% of September cuts, behind economic conditions and closings.
- Realized payroll growth fell to 29,000 in September 2026, with earlier months revised down, while cuts stayed low, which points to a hiring freeze rather than layoffs.
- New York's AI box on layoff notices drew no AI attributions from more than 160 filers in its first year.
- Business groups are already questioning the undefined "primarily relies" standard in the termination law, a likely basis for guidance requests.
- Germany's largest bargaining round of 2026 framed the future of work as job security and profit-sharing, with talks beginning 7 Oct 2026 and no mention of AI in the demand.

### 7.2 Probable Direction (Near-Term Future) – Where We're Likely Headed
The likeliest path is gradual rather than sudden. The California notice duty starts in 2027 and the termination duty later that year, and the first guidance on what counts as reliance on an automated system will probably come later than the statute implies, from an agency or a court rather than the legislature. Employers will build corroboration into HR software and staff it cheaply. Layoff notices will exist and will rarely name AI. Aggregate trackers will keep recording AI as one cited reason among several, rising and falling with the month, so that no single release shows a collapse. The adjustment will occur at the entrance, in postings that are announced and not opened, and will be visible only in series that pair plans, postings and realized hires. Some bargaining agreements will carry AI language by 2028, mostly in sectors with existing unions. A narrow first lawsuit is plausible; a national rule is not.

### 7.3 Preferred Path (Intentional Future) – The Path We Could Choose Instead
- Employers fund junior roles as training lines in their budgets and report them separately from output targets.
- Regulators define "primarily relies" early, and require a corroborating reviewer to have documented authority and time to overturn a decision.
- Statistics agencies and private trackers publish occupation-level series pairing hiring plans, postings and realized hires, so non-admission can be counted.
- Unions and works councils extend notice-and-bargain language to the hiring plan for any job family where AI is introduced.
- Notice formats ask employers to describe, in text, how automation changed staffing, and make the answers public.
- Individuals document the decisions they were allowed to make, and ask schools and agencies to credit them as experience.

## 8. Connect to Today
### Skills We May Need
- Reading a notice for what it leaves out.
- Asking for the record behind a decision, and knowing which law lets you.
- Bargaining about the cohort that is not yet in the room.
- Counting what is not hired, not only who is let go.
- Teaching a junior the long way when the quick way is cheaper.
- Telling a countersignature from a judgement.

### Signals & Refractions
- A layoff notice with a field for automated functions that reads "none applicable" shows how a disclosure format records what employers choose to write, as New York's checkbox did.
- A hiring-plan announcement of hundreds of positions beside a careers page with none open shows the gap between planned and posted work that the Challenger figures hint at.
- A contract clause requiring bargaining before AI is introduced, as in the Blizzard agreement, shows where this question is actually being answered.

## 9. Final Insight
The statutes of this period were written for the afternoon someone is let go, and in that afternoon they work: it is documented, countersigned and noticed. The morning nobody is hired produces no paper at all, and a society can fill its files for years without recording it. Renee turned down the contract renewal in November, and with it the one line on her record that anyone could verify. She does not regret it, though she can no longer say what, exactly, she has instead.
