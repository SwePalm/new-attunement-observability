## 1. Title & Core Question
- Title: Edition Four
- Core Question: When the federal answer to frontier AI is a signed promise and the binding rules are written one state at a time, who can tell a small maker which words apply to them, and what does a person do while the meaning of the words is still being settled?

## 2. Context Summary (Translation Layer) – Why This Future Exists
In the autumn of 2026 the White House had six leading developers sign a commitment to check themselves, with auditors each company chooses, no penalty and no deadline. Congress kept drafting and did not pass a general AI law. California widened its transparency rules to any generative system open to the public, whatever its size, and renamed the tool that verifies disclosure. A presidential order told agencies to call the most capable systems "super intelligence" and asked for a legal definition of the term within sixty days. Nothing came to a stop: the rules multiplied faster than the means of applying them. Two years on, the large firms follow all of it through counsel and standing meetings. Everyone else learns what applies to them from notices, footers and forms, and from whoever will read them aloud.

## 3. Future World Snapshot (The Lived Experience) – A Day in This Future
Vantage: person subject to the system / a kitchen table and a public library (form: a day)

The notice from the app store arrives at breakfast, and Iqbal Sheikh reads it twice. The photo-description app he built for his blind brother is, it says, a publicly accessible generative system in California, and so needs a disclosure verification tool unless it counts as assistive technology. He does not know which it is. The footer of the notice is stamped Edition 5. The body cites Edition 3.

At the library the definitions binder is open at Edition 4. The volunteer at the help desk is kind and says at once that she has no standing to tell him anything; she can read the pages aloud. He rings the number on the notice and gets a recording: coverage questions are "pending the federal definition". The state attorney general's line does not advise on coverage and has published no case under the Act.

In the corridor, the woman ahead of him is arguing with a county clerk about a tick-box beside her benefits claim: "SI-assisted". The clerk has ticked it. The only model involved was a spell-checker. The box has no definition to refuse it.

Back at the desk he finds, on the big assistants' sites, a grade from an independent auditor. At the bottom of the page: auditor appointed by the company. He asks the volunteer whether a kitchen counts as having an auditor. She does not laugh. The library's bulletin, which she files as a dated item, reports only that a regular meeting of the signatories was held.

By afternoon he has three answers on a card in her handwriting. The store: covered. A law firm's blog: assistive, exempt. The county's cover sheet: his kind of app is "SI". Each is dated and none agrees. He asks which to rely on. She says she cannot say. He asks who can. She says it would be whoever writes the next edition, and that nobody has told her who that is.

Before closing he asks her to put the library's date stamp on the notice, "so there is one thing in this with today's date that nobody can change". She stamps it.

## 4. Behavioral Shifts (Human Lens) – How People Adapt
People stop asking what a rule says and start asking what edition it is. Small makers keep a dated folder of every notice, term and cover sheet they have received, because the only defence they have is showing that they acted on the words as they stood on a given day. Libraries, tenants' groups and community colleges become informal reading rooms for official language, staffed by people with no authority who are asked for judgments anyway.

Larger firms adapt differently. Their staff read every instrument and attend the meetings where the norm is shaped, and they treat the voluntary commitment as a floor they will be asked about in procurement. Insurers and buyers begin to ask for the auditor's name, which makes the name matter more than the findings. Among ordinary users, the "SI" label is learned as a nuisance: people tick it, expect nothing, and notice only when a form is refused for lacking it.

## 5. Structural Forces (System Lens) – What Holds This World Together
Three forces hold the world in place. The first is voluntary attestation at the federal level. The six signatories choose their own auditors, a board committee reviews the reports, and a promised overseer in the White House has an office but no power to compel. A Commerce committee that would test models before release exists only as a bill from the minority party, which the Commerce committee chair objected to when its sponsor tried to pass it.

The second is state law carrying the binding duties. California's widened transparency act covers systems of any size, and the other state dates, such as the Illinois obligations for the largest developers, are the only fixed points any firm can plan against. Enforcement is the missing piece: attorneys general have announced no case, so coverage is decided by each platform's reading of its own risk.

The third is definitional churn. The executive order's clock produced a proposed federal term, but a proposal is not a statute, and each version that appears sits beside the state and EU versions without replacing them. The US-China incident channel stays a dialogue between leaders with no named agency, so it supplies no shared terms to anyone below.

## 6. Reflection & Implications – Questions This World Asks Us
If the meaning of a rule changes faster than a small maker can read it, is the rule still addressed to them?
When the examiner is chosen by the examined, what should the public be told about the choice, and by whom?
Who is responsible for explaining a word to a person who has no lawyer, when the person nearest to them has no standing to do so?

## 7. Pullback Layer: From Possibility to Probability
### 7.1 Signals Emerging (Plausible Zone) – Early Signals We Already See
- On 29 Sep 2026 six developers signed a White House "Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities" with company-chosen auditors, a board review of audit reports, no penalty, no regulator and no deadline, and a pledge to meet regularly on standards.
- Governor Newsom signed SB 1000 on 30 Sep 2026 as an urgency statute: it removed the one-million-user threshold from California's AI Transparency Act, renamed the verification tool and added an assistive-technology exemption.
- Democratic senators introduced a bill on 24 Sep 2026 requiring frontier models to be given to a Commerce Department committee 45 days before release; reports say Cruz objected when Warner tried to pass it.
- A 29 Sep 2026 executive order directs agencies to use the term "super intelligence" and gives the science adviser 60 days to propose legislative language defining it.
- Trump said he would appoint someone to oversee compliance with the commitment, following a 19 Sep announcement of an "AI Force"; no name or instrument has been published.
- The US and China agreed during Xi's 23 to 25 Sep 2026 visit to a "Dialogue on Superintelligence" and to explore an incident communication channel, with no named agency or date.

### 7.2 Probable Direction (Near-Term Future) – Where We're Likely Headed
The probable path is layered and slow. Congress does not pass a comprehensive AI statute within the window, which is the realistic middle case rather than a pessimistic one, and governance stays split between states, executive action and courts. The voluntary commitment gains a few more signatories and a named overseer, but announced dates keep sliding, so an oversight office and a standing audit format appear later than their sponsors say. The proposed federal definition of "super intelligence" arrives as a proposal and is argued over rather than enacted. California and Illinois carry the binding duties, and the first enforcement actions, when they come, set the working meaning of "covered" and "independent" more than any text does. Insurers and large buyers, moving faster than regulators, turn the commitment's four layers into contract terms. The US-China channel remains a dialogue. The swing variable is the first attorney general or court to apply a state definition to a small provider: that one decision could settle more than the next three federal documents. Watch whether any instrument defines how an auditor is appointed, not only whether one exists.

### 7.3 Preferred Path (Intentional Future) – The Path We Could Choose Instead
- Individuals and small makers: keep a dated record of every notice and term received, with the edition cited, and file it before it is needed.
- Companies: publish who appointed each auditor, what the auditor was allowed to test, and whether adverse findings could be released without the company's consent.
- Libraries and community groups: keep a shared, dated archive of the definitions in force, and say plainly that reading them aloud is not advice.
- State attorneys general: publish early guidance on how coverage and the assistive exemption apply to very small providers.
- Congress and the White House: attach a stable definition to each term when it is introduced, with a date before which it will not be altered.
- Regulators and courts: state which edition of a definition governs a dispute, so that a person can know it on the day they act.

## 8. Connect to Today
### Skills We May Need
- Reading a rule by its date as well as its text.
- Telling an auditor from a witness, when both carry a signature.
- Keeping a dated file of everything official that you are sent.
- Saying "I have no standing to tell you that" and then helping anyway.
- Asking who appointed the person who is checking.

### Signals & Refractions
- The edition problem is already visible in miniature: California's transparency act changed its covered-provider definition and the name of its verification tool on a single day in September 2026.
- The "SI" label is a present fact, not an invention: agencies were directed to adopt the term in the same week that its legal meaning was left to be proposed.
- The self-chosen auditor is already the design of the commitment that six developers signed.

## 9. Final Insight
Iqbal did not get his answer. By the following week he had paid forty dollars for a lawyer's one-page opinion on Edition 4, which the store did not read, and he had refused the store's suggestion to withdraw the app until matters were settled. The binder at the library was reordered, with Edition 5 filed in front and a note that earlier notices stayed under the old text. His brother opens the app each morning, on the version Iqbal would not take down.
