# Forces and feelings, dependency, 2026-10-02

## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)

Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
Supervisors and standard-setters have named single-provider dependence as a risk and asked for "active management" of it (APRA, Apr 2026; the FSB consultation of 10 Jun 2026), yet no model provider sits inside a UK or DORA designation list, so the duty to manage the risk lands on the practitioner who reads the notices. The political layer announces that someone must be in charge of continuity; the social layer, in which 91% of executives do not fully understand their dependencies, supplies a person with a calendar and no mandate. The conflict is between a public expectation and a private job description.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
Cheap, fast removal is rational for the provider: a 61-day floor, a notice of 20 days at the short end, a named customer cut off after a change of owner (sweep, Sep and Oct 2026). Continuity is binding for the customer, who owes it to its own clients and, if regulated, to a supervisor that expects exit strategies. The contract that governs the relationship grants no exit assistance and cites terms of use as the ground for ending access. One side prices flexibility, the other is held to permanence, and both sit on the same paper.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
By definition a dependence is harmless only if the party can leave, so everyone agrees an exit must exist. But an exit is only real once exercised, and exercising it means interrupting the live system the exit exists to protect, with a real customer-visible cost on a day when nothing is wrong. The people who most believe in the principle are the ones who cannot afford the test, and the checklist (inventory, monitoring, gateway) lets them stop one step short without ever feeling that they have.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)

Normal Absurdity 1:
Asking "which console are you on?" before giving anyone a deadline. Two browser tabs show the same model with two different dates, and colleagues on different cloud channels give each other different answers without surprise, in the way two neighbours give different bin days. (A lived expression of the Google Developer API and Vertex AI pages disagreeing on Gemini 2.5.)

Normal Absurdity 2:
A wall calendar of model expiry dates kept beside the fire-extinguisher inspection card, with identifiers written in marker like use-by dates on milk. Each autumn the date of the oldest one is crossed out and a replacement, "better on paper", is pencilled in for the next slot.

Normal Absurdity 3:
Reading the status page aloud at the morning meeting the way others read the weather: "Major, resolved, no cause." Nobody asks for a cause any more, and a team that keeps its own spreadsheet of past outages, sorted by month, is thought thorough rather than odd.

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing

The Irreplaceable Thing:
A rehearsed exit: a switch to a second provider that your own people have thrown once, end to end, on a quiet day, and watched hold.

Explanation:
Scarce by a mechanism the sweep and PESTLE name. Notice is short and set by the provider (61 days at Anthropic, a 20-day low at OpenAI), Google's two channels give two dates, and the CSA checklist recommends inventories, monitoring and gateways but not a live rehearsal, so organisations complete the document and skip the practice (Technological 1, Economic 1, Social 2). APRA's finding of untested exit strategies names the gap. The thing is a practised capability, held in muscle and in a runbook that has been corrected by use, and it cannot be bought or inherited. A protagonist can pursue it by getting leave to cause a small, planned failure, and by paying the cost of the day.
