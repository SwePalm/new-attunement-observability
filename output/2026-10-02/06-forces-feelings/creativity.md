## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)

Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
The states and platforms that act on AI-made music mark the synthetic end (Spotify's AI Persona badge keyed to artist identity, EU transparency duties on generators, a UK labelling taskforce), while the public demand voiced by Kelly Clarkson and others is for a way to tell what people did, not what machines are. The political layer labels the machine, the social layer wants the person, and no instrument serves the second wish. A band that made a record whole in one room holds a fact the labels cannot represent.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
The Ross affirmance leans on a "rapidly developing" market for licensing training data, so every bilateral deal (Universal and ElevenLabs, Suno and BMG, Warner and Believe) makes the next court less friendly to unlicensed training. Yet the economic value of those deals, and the share that reaches artists, is undisclosed, and Suno answers revenue questions with "far beyond" rather than a number. The deals strengthen the legal case in public while their terms stay confidential, so the people whose work is licensed cannot see what the evidence is worth.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
The philosophical premise of human creative work is that a performance is valuable because it happened once, with consequences, and could have gone otherwise. The lived reality is that revision is the ordinary condition of recorded work and that regeneration is now one click, so the honest answer to "did you make all of it" is a spectrum nobody itemises (the Affleck admission, "lots of things", without saying what). Prizing the unrevised and being unable to afford or prove it are the same person's problem.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)

Normal Absurdity 1:
Introducing a record by what it is not flagged as. A musician says "I'm unmarked" or "I'm just a regular profile", because the platform has a badge for synthetic personas and no word for anyone else. Inference: this is a plausible lived expression of the Spotify badge keyed to identity, whose track rows are still described as "coming soon".

Normal Absurdity 2:
Opening every recording session by saying out loud, for the room and a phone propped on a speaker, that the next take is the take, and then arguing about whether the thing the bass amp did at 2:51 counts as a patch. A listener at the other end hears the result and has no way to know any of this happened.

Normal Absurdity 3:
Everyone in a band knowing which of their publishers or labels has signed an AI deal and nobody knowing what share, if any, reaches them (no label has disclosed an allocation mechanism). The band checks their chances with a shrug, the way one checks weather in another city.

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing

The Irreplaceable Thing:
The unrevised take: one performance by people in a room, made once and whole, with nothing patched, fixed or regenerated afterwards, that can still be told apart from the rest.

Explanation:
The scarcity has named mechanisms. Generation is now one pass: Suno's Speech beta (1 Oct 2026) produces narration and a soundtrack together, so redoing is the default and finishing is optional. The labels that exist mark the synthetic, as Spotify's AI Persona badge does by artist identity and as AI Act Article 50 does by output, and the 2 Oct 2026 sweep and grading files show no instrument anywhere that marks a human recording as made whole. Platforms police volume as fraud (Deezer, Jul 2026), not process. So the object is not forbidden, only unrepresentable: it can be made, and nobody can be told. A protagonist can pursue it concretely, by booking the room, holding the line against the patch, and deciding what to do with a take that has a flaw in it.
