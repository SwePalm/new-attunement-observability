# Structural question, creativity, 2026-10-02

Structural Question:
Over the next 6 to 24 months, as music and film are sorted into human and synthetic, will the sorting line be keyed to the maker's identity (as in Spotify's AI Persona badge), to the licence status of the training (as in the bilateral Suno, ElevenLabs and label deals and the reasoning of the Ross appeal), or to the process by which a given recording was made, and will any institution mark the unrevised human recording, or will only the synthetic ever receive a label?

Why It Matters:
The theme's centre moved in this run from who gets paid to what counts as what. The Third Circuit's affirmance in Thomson Reuters v. Ross on 29 or 30 Sep 2026, the first US appellate ruling that AI training on copyrighted material was not fair use, rests on a licensing market the court described as "rapidly developing", while its reach to generative music is disputed and Judge Mehta dismissed the Penske Media and Chegg suits the same week (source: https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/us-appeals-court-rejects-fair-use-defense-over-ai-training-as-thomson-reuters-wins-copyright-case-backed-by-riaa-and-nmpa/, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.androidauthority.com/google-ai-overviews-publisher-lawsuit-win-3718333/, Oct 2026). Meanwhile the only live labelling mechanism from the four named streaming services, Spotify's AI Persona badge, is keyed to synthetic artist identity rather than to how audio was made, and neither Billboard nor the Official Charts Company has issued eligibility rules (creativity-2026-08-03 and creativity-2026-08-05 both graded open on 2 Oct 2026; source: https://support.spotify.com/us/article/ai-persona-badge/, Sep 2026). Whichever key wins fixes who must prove what, and a recording made by people in one room with nothing regenerated currently has no category in any of them (inference from the sweep and grading files, not a sourced finding).
