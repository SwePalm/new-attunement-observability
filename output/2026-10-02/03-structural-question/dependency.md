# Structural question, dependency, 2026-10-02

Structural Question:
Over the next 6 to 24 months, will the interval between a provider's notice and a model's removal, and the channel on which a customer happens to read that notice, be fixed by provider deprecation pages and bilateral commercial terms (a 60-day floor at one lab, six-month notices at another, an ownership-based cutoff for a named customer, two Google channels with different dates), before any supervisor (HM Treasury under the UK Critical Third Parties regime, the European Supervisory Authorities under DORA, the FSB through its final sound practices) obliges a regulated customer to hold an exit that has actually been exercised, so that the only continuity plan most dependent organisations have is the one they have never tested?

Why It Matters:
The theme's centre moved this run from outages to the calendar of removals. Anthropic notified developers on 30 Sep 2026 that claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929 retires on 30 Nov 2026, 61 days of notice and about twelve months after release, which ended the earlier observation that every model released since mid-2025 was still Active (source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations, Sep 2026). On the same week Google dropped the shutdown date for Gemini 2.5 on its Developer API while its Vertex AI page still lists 20 Oct 2026 for the same models, so the date a customer faces depends on the channel they bought through (source: https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deprecations, Oct 2026) (source: https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/learn/model-versions, Oct 2026). OpenAI told Cursor on 28 Aug 2026 that its model access ends on 12 Nov 2026, the first recorded case in this ledger of a provider cutting a named downstream customer for ownership reasons (source: https://bdtechtalks.com/2026/09/04/openai-cursor-ban/, Sep 2026). Against this, the supervisory instruments are slow: the UK designation list still holds only four cloud providers (dependency-2026-08-01, open), the DORA list of 19 providers contains no foundation-model API provider (dependency-2026-09-03, open), and the FSB final report is due in October 2026 but may not address substitutability beyond generic third-party risk (dependency-2026-08-02, open). The one confirmed regulator signal, APRA's April 2026 finding of single-provider dependence with untested exit strategies (dependency-2026-07-01, confirmed), names the gap this question is about (source: https://www.apra.gov.au/apra-letter-to-industry-on-artificial-intelligence-ai, Apr 2026). On the record of CAL-003, private ordering is likely to lead and supervision to follow.
