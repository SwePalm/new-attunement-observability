# Theme exploration, dependency, 2026-10-02

Turn 1 – Conceptual Frame

Dependence is usually discussed as a quantity of use: how much of a business, a habit or a life runs through one provider. That frame makes the remedy sound like a ratio, use less of it, spread the load. The evidence of this run points to a different definition. What makes a dependence dangerous is not how much is leaned on but whether the leaning party can still leave. Reliance with a working exit is a choice renewed daily. Reliance without one is a condition. The two look identical from outside until the day someone has to move.

Models make this distinction unusually sharp because a model identifier is a promise of sameness that the provider alone controls. A customer who writes "claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929" into a configuration is renting a particular behaviour, and the rental has a term. Anthropic notified developers on 30 Sep 2026 that this model retires on 30 Nov 2026, 61 days later and about twelve months after release (source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations, Sep 2026). OpenAI's own page shows the same structure at different lengths, with notices dated 1 Oct 2026 that remove three models on 1 Apr 2027 and four speech models on 6 Jan 2027 (source: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations, Oct 2026). A year, in this market, is a long life for a model, and a tenancy is the more accurate image than a purchase.

If dependence is the loss of the capacity to leave, then exit is not an event but a capacity, and capacities decay without use. A switch that has never been thrown is a hypothesis. The Australian prudential regulator said as much in April 2026, finding regulated entities heavily dependent on a single AI provider across several use cases with exit strategies that had not been tested (dependency-2026-07-01, confirmed; source: https://www.apra.gov.au/apra-letter-to-industry-on-artificial-intelligence-ai, Apr 2026). The word "untested" carries the philosophical weight: an exit that exists on paper has the same status as a fire door painted on a wall.

The frame also changes what counts as the provider's power. The provider does not need to be hostile or even careless to hold it. It needs only to own the clock. When the interval between notice and removal is set by one party and absorbed by the other, dependence becomes a relation of schedule before it is a relation of price. The structural question this run asks is who will write the rules of that schedule, and the working answer, so far, is: the party whose models are being retired.

Turn 2 – Societal Reframing

Seen at the scale of a society rather than a firm, dependence on model providers resembles a dependence that most people do not experience as one. A person using a bank, a clinic or a council service does not choose a model, read a deprecation page or hold an API key. The dependence is inherited through institutions, and it is invisible at exactly the point where it would be most useful to see. The survey figure that circulates in practitioner commentary is a measure of that invisibility: 91% of executives, in the IBM Institute for Business Value study, do not fully understand their AI dependencies (source: https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-triple-ai-outage-concentration-risk-202609/, Sep 2026). A German-language account of the same study adds that 81% of firms expect severe disruption from a one-week provider outage and 71% find switching difficult (source: https://www.business-punk.com/tech/grosser-ki-blackout-warum-81-prozent-der-firmen-zittern/, Jun 2026). Taken together the figures describe a society that knows it is exposed and does not know to what.

The Cursor case shows a second societal shift: dependence is no longer only on a provider's reliability but on a provider's view of who the customer's owner is. OpenAI told Cursor on 28 Aug 2026 that its model access ends on 12 Nov 2026 after SpaceX's acquisition of Cursor's parent, citing doubt about use within OpenAI's terms (source: https://bdtechtalks.com/2026/09/04/openai-cursor-ban/, Sep 2026). Cursor's chief executive put OpenAI models at about 5% of traffic, which reads as reassurance and also as evidence of how much hedging the most sophisticated customers have already done. Most downstream users are not Cursor. For a small organisation, a provider's decision about a corporate parent can function as a change in the weather.

The societal reframing, then, is that dependence has acquired a political dimension without a political process. Decisions about who may keep using a capability, and until when, are made in terms-of-service reviews and deprecation notices, not in forums anyone votes in. Inference, labelled as such: the more of ordinary institutional life runs on such terms, the more continuity becomes a matter of private contract, and the public learns of its exposure only when something stops.

Turn 3 – Psychological Implications

The people closest to this dependence are not executives but the engineers, product owners and operations staff who read the notices. Their psychological position is peculiar. They are responsible for continuity but do not control the clock, they are told by every checklist to prepare for a failure that has not happened, and they work under a manager who has been told, truthfully, that the current model works. The emotional register is closer to living under a lease than to ownership: the home is real, the tenant does the repairs, and a letter can arrive.

Notices create a specific cognitive load. Google's two channels for the Gemini 2.5 family now disagree: the Developer API page, last updated 1 Oct 2026, says no shutdown date has been announced and the models will keep being served until further notice, while the Vertex AI page lists 20 Oct 2026 for the same models (source: https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deprecations, Oct 2026) (source: https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/learn/model-versions, Oct 2026). A reader holding two dates for one model must decide which to believe, and the cost of choosing wrongly falls on them. The reasonable response is vigilance, and vigilance is expensive to sustain across a dozen providers and a model lifespan of about a year.

A quieter effect is the substitution of a document for a practice. The Cloud Security Alliance note recommends dependency inventories, synthetic monitoring, multi-provider gateways and incident-notification clauses in vendor contracts (source: https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-triple-ai-outage-concentration-risk-202609/, Sep 2026). Each is sensible. Inference: an inventory is easy to complete and produces the feeling of control, whereas a rehearsal of the switch interrupts real work and risks real customers, so organisations that tick the first often postpone the second. The checklist then becomes a way of feeling ready, which is different from being so.

There is also a loss in the pattern that rarely gets named. Teams tune prompts, evaluation sets and habits to a specific model's behaviour, and when it is retired the thing lost is not a service but a known character. The replacement named in the notice is better on paper and unknown in practice. Inference: some of what looks like resistance to migration is the loss of the only version of the system a team had learned to read.

Turn 4 – Institutional Dynamics

Institutions respond to dependence at three speeds. Providers move fastest, and they move through documentation: a deprecation page, a changelog, a status feed. The status feed is revealing. Anthropic's incident feed shows a Major incident on 29 Sep 2026 across claude.ai, Claude Code and the API, and another on 22 Sep, each closed with impact-and-resolution text and no root-cause account (source: https://status.claude.com/api/v2/incidents.json, Oct 2026). The ledger holds two open claims waiting for a post-mortem, one on the June suspension of the Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models and OpenAI's July degradation (dependency-2026-07-03) and one on a named 2026 availability incident (dependency-2026-08-06), and neither has resolved. The provider's institutional habit is to restore, not to explain.

Customers move at the speed of contracts and procurement, which is slower but binding. This is where incident-notification clauses, audit rights and exit assistance would live, and the ledger's earlier calibration holds that private ordering has tended to lead regulation in 2026. Supervisors are slowest. The UK Critical Third Parties list of 10 Jul 2026 names AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft and Oracle and no model provider (dependency-2026-08-01, open). The first DORA list of 19 designated providers, published 19 Nov 2025, contained no foundation-model API provider and no second list has appeared (dependency-2026-09-03, open; source: https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/european-supervisory-authorities-designate-critical-ict-third-party-providers, Nov 2025). The FSB's final sound practices are expected in October 2026, and its June consultation already refers to supply chain concentration (dependency-2026-08-02, open).

The institutional dynamic that follows is a mismatch of clocks. A model's lifetime is roughly a year; a notice is two to six months; a supervisory designation cycle is closer to a year or two. A rule that arrives after a model family has been retired three times will be written about a generation of technology that no longer exists. The institutions with the most direct power over continuity, the model providers and the customers' own procurement teams, are not the ones with a public duty to maintain it.

Turn 5 – Long-Term Trajectory

Over the next five years the most probable development, stated as inference, is not a crisis but a normalisation: forced migration becomes an annual operating event, like a certificate renewal, scheduled and resented. Organisations that treat it as routine will build the machinery for it, including evaluation sets that follow the work rather than the model, gateways that make a second provider reachable, and contracts that give notice periods a floor. Anthropic's 60-day minimum floor, kept this month at 61 days, and its longer floors for the newest models, show that the highest-capability providers are already publishing commitments of this shape (source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations, Sep 2026). A floor is not a guarantee, and a customer still has to be able to move inside it.

The scarce thing in that world is not a second provider, which will be available, but a practised ability to use one. Organisations differ less in what they have bought than in what they have rehearsed. Supervisors will probably catch up first in finance, where APRA has already spoken and where the FSB and the Treasury Committee's recommendation for designation by the end of 2026 point the same way, though the history of announced regulatory dates in this corpus counsels slowness. The partial reversal on the Gemini Developer API is a reminder that deadlines can also be withdrawn, and that a customer who migrated early has lost something a customer who waited did not.

Two uncertainties remain. First, whether ownership-based cutoffs of the Cursor kind spread, which would make dependency a function of the customer's capital structure as well as its architecture. Second, whether the market will price exit readiness, through insurance questions, procurement scorecards or audit findings, before any statute requires it. If it does, the cost of a rehearsal will become visible as a line in a budget, and the people who ran one will have an advantage that does not appear in any model benchmark. If it does not, the default plan will remain what it is today: read the notice, hope the replacement behaves, and discover the exit when needed.
