The previous sweep recorded only district-level litigation (source: https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/us-appeals-court-rejects-fair-use-defense-over-ai-training-as-thomson-reuters-wins-copyright-case-backed-by-riaa-and-nmpa/, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/court-deals-blow-use-copyrighted-works-train-ai/, Oct 2026)
- Suno opened its Speech beta to all users on 1 Oct 2026, a model that generates narration and a matching soundtrack in one pass, and its CEO said on 2 Oct 2026 that subscribers and annual revenue are "far beyond" the previously disclosed 2 million and $300 million, without giving new figures. Suno's v6 blog of 9 Sep 2026 still describes artist opt-in products as under development with no date (source: https://www.unite.ai/suno-launches-speech-beta-pairing-voice-and-music-in-one-model/, Oct 2026) (source: https://musically.com/2026/10/02/suno-is-far-beyond-2m-subscribers-and-300m-of-annual-revenue/, Oct 2026)

Confirmed Developments:
- The US Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit affirmed in Thomson Reuters v. Ross Intelligence that copying Westlaw headnotes to train a competing AI legal search tool was not fair use. The ruling is dated 29 Sep 2026 by one outlet and 30 Sep 2026 by others, and was reported as a 32-page opinion. The RIAA and NMPA had filed an amicus brief, and the MBW report notes the district judge limited his reasoning to non-generative AI, so direct application to Suno and Udio is limited (source: https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/us-appeals-court-rejects-fair-use-defense-over-ai-training-as-thomson-reuters-wins-copyright-case-backed-by-riaa-and-nmpa/, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/court-deals-blow-use-copyrighted-works-train-ai/, Oct 2026)
- Suno launched a Speech beta on 1 Oct 2026, open to all users on mobile and web after a month of limited testing, generating spoken audio with original background music in one track. Suno says it screens uploaded audio and lyrics for unauthorized use and that artist opt-in programs are still being developed (source: https://www.unite.ai/suno-launches-speech-beta-pairing-voice-and-music-in-one-model/, Oct 2026) (source: https://musically.com/2026/10/02/suno-is-far-beyond-2m-subscribers-and-300m-of-annual-revenue/, Oct 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- The Authors Guild and the AAP welcomed the Ross ruling on 1 Oct 2026, and the opinion was reported to acknowledge a "rapidly developing" market for licensing AI training data, a point the music industry's licensing deals can cite against fair use defences in generative cases (source: https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/digital/copyright/article/101414-aap-authors-guild-applaud-decision-in-ross-copyright-infringement-case.html, Oct 2026)
- Anthropic told Australian lawmakers it accepts rejection of a broad text and data mining exception but proposed a conditional opt-out through robots.txt, and Australia's public broadcasters ABC and SBS countered that robots.txt is routinely ignored and asked that AI firms pay for news content. Anthropic and OpenAI were due to testify in Sydney the following week (source: https://thenextweb.com/news/abc-sbs-ai-news-anthropic-opt-out, Oct 2026)
- Studios and talent are disclosing AI use openly. Ben Affleck said on 1 Oct 2026 that he used AI "for lots of things" on his Netflix film Animals (release 9 Oct 2026) without saying what, and described using InterPositive, his AI startup that Netflix acquired (source: https://theplaylist.net/animals-ben-affleck-says-he-used-ai-for-lots-of-things-on-his-new-movie-but-wont-say-what-20261001/, Oct 2026)
- Kelly Clarkson said on a podcast released 30 Sep 2026 that she supports AI in music but that AI artists should not compete on the same charts as humans, repeating the separate-chart framing without any chart body acting on it (source: https://stereogum.com/2513179/kelly-clarkson-loves-ai-for-music-but-thinks-ai-artists-should-compete-on-separate-charts/news, Oct 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- Courts are not uniformly siding with content owners against AI. On 30 Sep 2026 Judge Amit Mehta dismissed the Penske Media and Chegg antitrust suits over Google's AI Overviews, writing that publishers' expectation of traffic "is not an agreement"; Penske Media is the parent of Billboard (source: https://www.androidauthority.com/google-ai-overviews-publisher-lawsuit-win-3718333/, Oct 2026)
- The Ross holding has limited reach for generative music. Coverage states the district judge confined his ruling to non-generative AI, so Suno and Udio can still argue that generative training differs, and both continue to assert fair use (source: https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/us-appeals-court-rejects-fair-use-defense-over-ai-training-as-thomson-reuters-wins-copyright-case-backed-by-riaa-and-nmpa/, Sep 2026)
