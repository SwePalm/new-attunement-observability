Confirmed Developments:
- Newsom signed SB 1000 (Becker) on 30 Sep 2026 as Chapter 861, Statutes of 2026, amending the California AI Transparency Act. It removes the 1,000,000 monthly-user threshold, so any person who produces a publicly accessible generative AI system in California is a covered provider. It eliminates the duty to offer a manifest (visible) disclosure and keeps the latent (machine-readable) disclosure duty, with civil penalties of 5,000 dollars per violation per day. It took effect immediately as an urgency statute (source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billHistoryClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB1000, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/261001-california-revamps-ai-transparency-requirements, Oct 2026)
- Newsom signed AB 2713 (Fong) on 30 Sep 2026 as Chapter 856, Statutes of 2026. Large online platforms must show whether content was generated or substantially altered by generative AI, or captured by a recording device, and let users inspect provenance data. Platforms may not knowingly strip provenance data. Only data that complies with widely adopted specifications is covered. Platform duties start 1 Jan 2027 and capture-device manufacturer duties 1 Jan 2028 (source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billHistoryClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB2713, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/261001-california-revamps-ai-transparency-requirements, Oct 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Detector-based verdicts are now being debated as policy design, not only applied. A 26 Sep 2026 analysis of the Goncourt decision notes that the Académie announced no systematic testing of other selected books, no publication of verification results and no standard procedure, so doubt could extend to the whole selection (source: https://rezonodwes.com/2026/09/le-goncourt-face-a-lia-une-decision-forte-des-questions-en-suspens/, Sep 2026)
- Contested detector evidence in the Orélien case is being read through a political frame. The Globe and Mail (1 Oct 2026) reports that some minority writers suggest the accusation was politically motivated, and that publishers continued to back the author after the Goncourt decision (source: https://www.theglobeandmail.com/opinion/article-the-ai-scandal-rocking-frances-literary-elite-has-deep-political/, Oct 2026)
- SynthID Bio ties provenance marking to biosecurity: DNA synthesis companies could use the mark to identify trusted AI-designed orders, and public databases (Protein Data Bank, UniProt, GenBank) could label synthetic submissions (source: https://www.unite.ai/deepmind-embeds-verifiable-watermarks-in-ai-designed-proteins/, Sep 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- Detector reliability remains contested outside the novel case. A 29 Sep 2026 student-newspaper report at Florida Atlantic University describes false accusations from Turnitin and cites a figure of 61.3 percent false positives for non-native English speakers (the same 2023 Stanford figure the 25 Sep sweep recorded, so not new evidence) (source: https://www.upressonline.com/2026/09/flawed-ai-detection-is-breaking-trust-within-particular-fau-classrooms/, Sep 2026)
- The Conversation (27 Sep 2026) reports that French AI expert Benoît Raphaël found Haitian novels and Orélien's pre-2023 writings scored as human on the same tool, which supports the detector, while the author and his publisher say the novel was drafted in 2019; the two readings are not reconciled (source: https://theconversation.com/a-bestselling-novel-was-pulled-from-a-french-literary-prize-after-ai-accusations-what-next-292963, Sep 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- SB 1000 also replaces the AI detection tool duty with a "disclosure verification tool" that allows third-party alternatives, restricts personal data collection by providers, and requires providers to notify licensees and report unresolved licensee noncompliance to the Attorney General after a 72 to 96 hour remediation window. The media exemption narrows to non-user-generated video games, and an assistive-technology exemption runs to 1 Jan 2029 (source: https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/261001-california-revamps-ai-transparency-requirements, Oct 2026) (source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billHistoryClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB1000, Sep 2026)

Technical Changes:
- On 30 Sep 2026 Google DeepMind announced SynthID Bio, which embeds a signature into AI-designed protein sequences and predicted 3D structures. In lab tests on three targets (VEGF-A, SARS-CoV-2 spike RBD, PD-L1) watermarked designs matched unwatermarked ones on hit rate, binding affinity and sequence diversity. For AlphaFold 3 the mark is built into the model weights. Code, lab data and weights were released to researchers (source: https://thenextweb.com/news/google-deepmind-synthid-bio-watermark-ai-designed-proteins, Oct 2026) (source: https://www.unite.ai/deepmind-embeds-verifiable-watermarks-in-ai-designed-proteins/, Sep 2026)
- FIU's analysis (1 Oct 2026) says the Claude text mark works by biasing word choice, appears only in text of roughly five sentences or more that Claude wrote or heavily edited, and survives light editing but likely not substantial rewording (source: https://news.fiu.edu/2026/analysis-why-removing-claudes-watermark-is-harder-than-you-think, Oct 2026)
