Emerging Signals:
- The Center for Digital Thriving at Harvard GSE reported (30 Sep 2026) that 74 percent of teachers and 69 percent of principals described their AI dilemma as cheating-related, and that interviewed teens (31, ages 15-19, Apr-Jun 2026) described deliberately underperforming to avoid false AI accusations, which the authors call cognitive withholding; EdWeek and its GovTech syndication carry the same text, so this is one source (source: https://www.edweek.org/technology/how-ai-suspicions-are-undermining-student-teacher-relationships/2026/09, Sep 2026)
- A University of Wollongong study (published 30 Sep 2026 via The Conversation) re-ran a 2023 law-exam experiment with nine models: AI answers averaged 76.3 percent in criminal law and outperformed 82.5 percent of students (22nd percentile in 2023), with 7 of 18 AI papers at or above the 90th percentile; the authors conclude unsupervised assessment integrity is in question, but it is two subjects at one university (source: https://phys.org/news/2026-09-ai-exams-universities.html, Sep 2026)
- NPR (28 Sep 2026) reports districts experimenting with student-facing AI with little evidence or policy, citing a Stanford review of more than 800 papers and a Dayton, Ohio district that renewed its SchoolAI contract at 78,650 dollars after a 72,600 dollar first year, while its own evaluator says success can only be measured as perception (source: https://www.npr.org/2026/09/28/nx-s1-5759718/ai-schools-experiment-research, Sep 2026)
