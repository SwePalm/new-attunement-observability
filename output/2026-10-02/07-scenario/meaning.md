# Scenario, meaning, 2026-10-02

## 1. Title & Core Question
- Title: Patient Preference
- Core Question: When the law keeps a human in the chair but leaves their disagreement with the system unprotected, who decides whether the human's judgment is allowed to cost nothing?

## 2. Context Summary (Translation Layer) – Why This Future Exists
On 30 September 2026 California's Governor signed a set of laws that fix the place of the human in AI-assisted work: AI stays advisory in clinical care and cannot replace a licensed professional's judgment, automated discipline needs notice that a person reviewed it, and large businesses must make a good faith effort to connect a customer to a person within fifteen minutes. On the same day he vetoed the bill that would have protected a care worker from retaliation for overriding a clinical recommendation. Layoffs were cooling and employers pointed to energy costs and rates rather than AI. By the late 2020s hospitals and other large employers have built the compliance machinery the new laws invite: every recommendation is accepted, modified or overridden, and every override is recorded. Whether that record can be used against the person is left to each employer and, here and there, to a union contract.

## 3. Future World Snapshot (The Lived Experience) – A Day in This Future
Vantage: mid-senior professional inside the institution / a hospital system's clinical decision support committee. Form: one committee sitting, told in speaking turns. The mid-senior vantage is required because the free no is granted or withheld by whoever decides whether the override report gets opened, a desk no unit nurse can reach.

Item six is the override report. The speakerphone sits among a plate of clementines nobody touches.

**Compliance.** For the attestation we show that a licensed nurse exercised judgment on every recommendation. The system records accepted, modified or overridden. Overridden asks for a reason from the list.

**Pharmacy.** The list has eleven reasons. "Other" stops at forty characters. The night shift has worked out that "Clinical judgment" gets a call from the manager and "Patient preference" does not. Patient preference is the fastest-growing reason on the unit.

**Vendor, by phone.** The renewal adds override analytics by nurse and by shift, no extra charge this year.

**Counsel.** The surveillance statute reaches faces and brain signals. It does not reach override rates.

**Human resources.** Discipline letters already say "reviewed by a human" at the foot. The box ticks when the manager opens the file.

**Compliance.** The vendor's own support line promised a person in fifteen minutes, in good faith. I kept the ticket number.

**Ilse, in the chair.** Last quarter a night nurse overrode the dosing suggestion three times in a week and was right each time. I know because the pharmacist told me at the coffee machine. I have not opened that column in two years, and nobody has asked me why.

**Counsel.** Then write it down. A policy that overrides are not used in evaluation.

**Ilse.** And the exception?

**Counsel.** Patterns, for safety. You would need it. Once it is written, a pattern is a reason to look.

**Ilse.** So the unit is told that overrides are safe, except when they are a pattern, and the first nurse to believe it finds out where the exception starts.

**Compliance.** The attestation is due next month.

**Ilse.** Item seven.

## 4. Behavioral Shifts (Human Lens) – How People Adapt
Nurses adapt first, and quietly. They learn which override reasons draw a call and which do not, and they choose the ones that do not, so the record fills with "patient preference" while the actual reason sits in a handover or at the coffee machine. Experienced staff teach newcomers which box to tick the way they teach where the good supply closet is. Managers split into those who open the analytics and those who decide not to, and the unit knows which kind it has.

Elsewhere people go after the missing protection by other routes. Small groups bargain for a clause (a coffee company's baristas won a line that AI cannot be used to make decisions about them), and professional unions bring grievances framed as harm to identity, not only to jobs. Customers learn to keep ticket numbers and to phrase requests for a person as a legal event, since the law gives them fifteen minutes and a standard of good faith. Many give up before the fifteen minutes are up, and the support staff who answer can read the same screen the customer can.

## 5. Structural Forces (System Lens) – What Holds This World Together
Three things hold it in place. First, the statutes fix the seat and not the voice. AI is advisory in clinical care, a human review is noticed on discipline, and a person is promised on the phone, while the veto of the retaliation bill left an override's consequences to each employer's policy. Compliance with those statutes generates the logs, and the same logs make dissent legible.

Second, the pressure that usually carries labour bills has eased. Announced layoffs were at a September low in 2026, AI's share of cuts had fallen to about a ninth, and employers cited energy costs and rates for their caution. Without a displacement wave, a second attempt at the vetoed protection competes for attention, and the new notice duties start slowly: the human-review notice on discipline takes effect in mid-2027 and no regulator has yet tested it.

Third, protection arrives by contract, unevenly. Union-sponsored bills and first contracts carry AI language where a union has leverage, and Kaiser's own Los Angeles bargaining summary listed no AI item at all. Where no union exists, the employer's choice stands.

## 6. Reflection & Implications – Questions This World Asks Us
If a law requires a person to be in the loop but not to be free in it, what exactly has been protected?
A protection that works only because nobody writes it down is fragile, and a protection that is written down may be conditional. Which is the better risk?
When customers are guaranteed fifteen minutes to a person, what should that person be allowed to decide?

## 7. Pullback Layer: From Possibility to Probability
### 7.1 Signals Emerging (Plausible Zone) – Early Signals We Already See
- California signed AB 1979 on 30 Sep 2026, keeping AI advisory in clinical care and barring it from replacing the judgment of licensed professionals, and extending medical confidentiality to health information entered into consumer AI health chatbots.
- The Governor vetoed AB 2575 (retaliation protection for care workers who override or rely on clinical AI) and AB 2656 (public employee notice when AI performs a service) on 30 Sep 2026, the same day he signed the other bills; the reasons were not read in this run.
- SB 947 (human-review notice on automated discipline, effective 1 Jul 2027), SB 951 (advance notice of AI-driven mass layoffs) and AB 1883 (limits on AI workplace surveillance, including emotion recognition and neural data) were signed on 30 Sep 2026; the notice period for SB 951 is unverified.
- AB 1609, chaptered on 28 Sep 2026, requires large businesses to make a good faith effort to connect a customer to a person within 15 minutes or give an appointment within one business day.
- Challenger's September 2026 report showed AI at about 9% of cuts, down from a May peak near 40%, while AI still leads the year to date at 120,136.
- A Verve Coffee Roasters tentative first contract includes a clause that AI cannot be used to make decisions about employees (ratification vote scheduled for 9 Oct 2026), and a Radio-Canada report of 1 Oct 2026 described a user paying C$21 for a support tier that still reached only chatbots.

### 7.2 Probable Direction (Near-Term Future) – Where We're Likely Headed
The probable path is statutes that bind slowly and logs that arrive quickly. The seat clauses will be implemented first, because they are cheap to attest: hospitals and large employers will adopt accepted, modified and overridden fields and a human-review stamp well before any regulator or court tests what those mean. Given how often published dates slip, assume the first enforcement actions under the July 2027 notice duty come late and from grievances more than agencies. A successor to the vetoed retaliation bill is likely to be tried in the 2027-28 session, but the veto lowers its odds, and the middle case is that it narrows or stalls. The faster mover is private: small first contracts, and the nurses' and other unions' bargaining, will carry the first explicit limits on how AI-related records may be used, as they already have for AI decisions about employees. Expect uneven practice by employer and by shift, not a settled rule. The aggregate displacement story may keep cooling, which shifts attention from jobs lost to how work is done.

### 7.3 Preferred Path (Intentional Future) – The Path We Could Choose Instead
- Individuals: write down your own overrides and reasons in a place the system does not control, and ask in writing how override records are used before you rely on any reassurance.
- Employers: decide, before the first audit, whether override rates enter evaluation, and say so in a document that a worker can hold.
- Unions and professional bodies: bargain for the act-level protection that statute did not give, and publish clause language so other tables can copy it.
- Legislators: when the retaliation bill returns, draft it around the act of overriding, not around a job title.
- Regulators and standard setters: when attesting to human judgment, require evidence that the human could depart without penalty, not only that a human was present.

- Compare which AI clauses a union wins at the table with which it loses in the legislature.

## 9. Final Insight
The committee adjourned without a motion, and the minutes say the override report was received. Ilse did not write the policy. The following week she asked the pharmacist to stop telling her at the coffee machine which overrides had turned out right, because what she is told she is expected to act on, and what she has not been told she can truthfully say she has not seen. The pharmacist agreed. A nurse hired in the spring will choose "patient preference" for a year, as her colleagues did, and the older nurses will explain why at the same coffee machine, where Ilse now takes her coffee at a different hour.
