# Scenario, authority, 2026-11-01

## 1. Title & Core Question
- Title: Ten Cells
- Core Question: When every authority asks for an independent examiner and none accepts another's, who is the examiner really working for?

## 2. Context Summary (Translation Layer) – Why This Future Exists
By late 2026 the question of who may demand proof of safety from a frontier AI developer had many answers and no referee. Eight state attorneys general sued OpenAI on 14 October, each in its own state court, and Texas followed on 23 October with a petition that would bar new model releases in the state until a court-approved independent evaluator signs off. The FTC sent investigative demands to two developers and then asked for executive testimony. The White House task force said the labs are ultimately responsible and set no deadline or penalty. New York introduced a bill for state-licensed examiners, and California asked an expert group for onsite verification organisations. Each design names an outside examiner. None says another venue must accept the examiner's finding. Courts and legislatures also move slowly, so this world is the one where the demand for outside proof has arrived years ahead of the people, rules and recognition that would make it portable.

## 3. Future World Snapshot (The Lived Experience) – A Day in This Future
Vantage: frontline operator / a small independent test lab's secure room. Form: a case file, kept as numbered entries with the technician's notes.

Entry 1. The court's order is taped inside the door, beside the badge reader. It lets the lab open one model, in this room, for forty hours. The technician logs in with a card that was issued for this order and no other.
Note: Same model, same checks, as the job before. That job was for a different state.

Entry 2. The release-gate sheet on the wall has ten cells in a row. Nine say "petition pending". One, at the end, says "demand answered". Nobody here has ever seen it cleared, and a visitor asking why is told it is how the sheet comes.
Note: The sheet is updated by hand each morning, in a different pen colour for each cell.

Entry 3. The model's owner has sent two engineers. They sit behind glass and unlock each tool as the technician asks. The technician never sees a tool before it is unlocked.
Note: Access is the thing the owner gives. Is a finding independent if it is given?

Entry 4. A test fails. The technician writes it up in the court's template: pass, fail, method, date. The template has a line, "accepted by", with nothing printed after it.
Note: Left blank. Last time a lawyer wrote in a name, and the name was not recognised by the next court.

Entry 5. A phone call from the owner's counsel, polite. "Would you mind sending the same findings in the other format?" The other format is another state's. Another fortnight of the technician's life goes on a table of fields.
Note: Same facts, retyped. The signature line is where they differ.

Entry 6. At the end, the technician signs. A senior colleague counter-signs. Both signatures go on the page that will be filed, and the document that matters will be the one a second court reads and declines to take.
Note: Nothing here is wrong. It is only worth one court's worth.

Entry 7. The owner's annual report arrives in the post room. A sentence on page nineteen says an investigative demand arrived in October. It is accurate, dated, and sits between a lease and a copyright claim. The technician reads it standing up.
Note: Everyone reads that line like weather.

## 4. Behavioral Shifts (Human Lens) – How People Adapt
Examiners learn to write findings in the vocabulary of whoever will read them, keeping a drawer of templates that differ in headings and not in facts. Lawyers for developers treat each new order as a variant of the last, and some build a standing translation desk. Executives rehearse sentences as if they will be exhibits. Frontline technicians learn that independence is a matter of what they are allowed to see, not of what they think, and some quietly keep their own notes of which tools were locked. Colleagues in other states compare the signature lines of their orders as people once compared forms of identification. Public attention follows the petition with the named defendant and loses interest in the examiner who actually opens the model. Students ask whether the career is law, security or testing and are told it is a patient mix of all three. In small ways, people begin to treat a finding as accurate within its venue and provisional beyond it.

## 5. Structural Forces (System Lens) – What Holds This World Together
State attorneys general are the main enforcement channel, acting through ordinary consumer-protection law in separate courts. Because no notice of removal has consolidated the cases, each court can decide for itself who counts as independent and what conditions it sets. The FTC is the only federal body applying compulsory process to developers, and developers disclose its demands in public filings, so exposure is learned through annual reports. The federal executive track remains voluntary: the task force charter has no stated deadline, and the preemption bill is still a discussion draft, so no federal rule tells states to accept one another's examiners. State legislatures are slower than their introductions suggest. New York's licensing of examiners is a bill, and California's recommendations arrive later than the date first given. The faster channel is private: insurers, large buyers and contract clauses begin to ask which examiner signed, and a few accept one name (inference, as the world's habit and not a present-day fact). The EU's information requests add a further, separate list.

## 6. Reflection & Implications – Questions This World Asks Us
- If an examiner's finding is worth only what one venue gives it, is the examiner an authority or a service?
- Who should pay the examiner, given that the party paying decides what can be seen?
- Is a signature that travels a gain for safety, or a gain for whoever controls the examiner?

### 7.2 Probable Direction (Near-Term Future) – Where We're Likely Headed
The likeliest course is slow fragmentation. The state suits will move at court speed, and the first practical orders on independent testing are more likely to come as case-management or settlement terms than as merits rulings. Legislative proposals such as New York's will probably be amended and delayed, and California's recommendations may arrive late or in softened form, in keeping with how announced regulatory dates have slipped. Federal preemption is unlikely to settle the question inside the horizon. Developers will answer each venue separately and disclose the demands in filings. The faster pressure will be contractual: insurers, procurement buyers and large customers may start to name an acceptable examiner before any statute does (inference). A single, widely accepted signature is possible within five years but would more likely come from market practice than from law.

### 7.3 Preferred Path (Intentional Future) – The Path We Could Choose Instead
- Courts and attorneys general in parallel cases agree, by stipulation, to accept one jointly approved evaluator.
- States that license examiners write mutual recognition into their statutes.
- Developers give examiners access terms in writing, and publish them.
- Examiners are paid through a pooled fund rather than by the party examined.
- Insurers and buyers name the examiner credentials they accept, so a market standard forms in the open.
- The federal task force publishes an incident definition and reporting deadline, so states are not left to guess.

## 9. Final Insight
The technician was offered, once, a standing contract with the owner that would have made every finding faster and every access easier, and declined it. The glass is still there, and so is the blank line. The sheet on the wall has ten cells, and she has stopped asking which one will close first.
