# Evidence sweep, governance, 2026-11-01

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The US executive moved from the accord signature to institution-building. The Super Intelligence Task Force chaired by DNI Jay Clayton held its first meeting and adopted a charter on 15 Oct 2026, opening a 120-day window (to about 12 Feb 2027), with a committee under Emil Michael to draft lab breach and incident notification protocols and a PCAST risk study; no incident definition, deadline or sanction has been set (source: https://thenextweb.com/news/super-intelligence-force-charter-first-meeting-anthropic, Oct 2026) (source: https://www.cdomagazine.tech/us-federal-news-bureau/white-house-ai-task-force-plans-breach-reporting-for-ai-labs, Oct 2026). The previous sweep only had Trump's statement that he would name an overseer.
- The 29 Sep 2026 executive order on "Super Intelligence" terminology reached agencies: a State Department cable reported on 20 Oct 2026 orders the wording across official communications, and the order's 60-day clock for proposed statutory definition language runs to 28 Nov 2026 (source: https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/state-department-orders-super-intelligence-wording-across-all-official-communications/416703/, Oct 2026) (source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence/, Sep 2026).

Confirmed Developments:
- On 15 October 2026 the White House Super Intelligence Task Force, chaired by DNI Jay Clayton with National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross, held its first meeting and adopted a charter with a 120-day work window. Three committees were described: reporting and response (Emil Michael), critical infrastructure (OPM director Scott Kupor), and law enforcement and consumer protection (FTC chair Andrew Ferguson). The President's Council of Advisers on Science and Technology was commissioned to assess risk in cybersecurity and critical infrastructure, biosecurity, and model control. Michael's committee is to develop protocols for labs to give the government early warnings of breaches, hacks and jailbreaks; no incident definition, reporting deadline or consequence for non-reporting has been set (source: https://thenextweb.com/news/super-intelligence-force-charter-first-meeting-anthropic, Oct 2026) (source: https://www.cdomagazine.tech/us-federal-news-bureau/white-house-ai-task-force-plans-breach-reporting-for-ai-labs, Oct 2026)
- On 21 September 2026 New York Governor Hochul and the Department of Financial Services announced RAISE Act implementation: the Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity and Trust (DIGIT) sits within DFS, Marc Gilman is its first full-time hire (Deputy Director for the RAISE Act), large frontier developers will be directed to register starting in November, and compliance and regular reporting begin January 2027, including 72-hour critical safety incident reports and quarterly catastrophic-risk assessments (source: https://governor.ny.gov/news/ai-safety-governor-hochul-announces-next-steps-regulate-major-ai-developers-and-protect-new, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.dfs.ny.gov/reports_and_publications/press_releases/pr20260921, Sep 2026)
- A State Department cable reported on 20 October 2026 instructs staff to use "Super Intelligence" or "SI" in place of "artificial intelligence" in emails, speeches, websites, reports and program names (the American AI Exports Program becomes the American SI Exports Program), implementing Executive Order 14434 of 29 September 2026; legally required language, contracts and grants are excluded for now. The order defines the term as the technologies in the existing statutory AI definition (15 U.S.C. 9401(3)) (source: https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/state-department-orders-super-intelligence-wording-across-all-official-communications/416703/, Oct 2026) (source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence/, Sep 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Twenty House Democrats led by Rep. Greg Casar wrote to OpenAI CEO Sam Altman on 29 Oct 2026, asking for a no-retaliation commitment for safety whistleblowers, continuation of OpenAI's pause on its most capable models until independent oversight exists, and a list of security incidents under investigation; a response is requested by 13 Nov 2026 (source: https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/democrats-press-openai-over-safety-researcher-firings-security-incidents/416954/, Oct 2026)
- Reps. Raskin and Garcia wrote to President Trump on 28 Oct 2026 invoking the Presidential Records Act to demand preservation of chatbot prompts and outputs and a closed briefing by 11 Nov 2026; a White House official said it preserves all required records (source: https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/house-democrats-call-trump-preserve-ai-chatbot-records/416913/, Oct 2026)
- Americans for Responsible Innovation proposed on 26 Oct 2026 that major AI developers file quarterly reports with a new Commerce Department office on how they use AI to build new models, with disclosure of significant research changes within four business days and a whistleblower program; it is a think-tank framework, not a bill (source: https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/policy-proposal-would-require-firms-disclose-how-they-use-ai-develop-new-models/416812/, Oct 2026)
- California Executive Order N-9-26 (18 Sep 2026) sets 16 Nov 2026 for the Government Operations Agency to report on options including shutdown capability, onsite auditors and independent verification; coverage stresses it is a feasibility study and imposes no requirement (source: https://ppc.land/newsom-sets-november-16-deadline-to-study-frontier-ai-kill-switch/, Sep 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- The task force's first meeting produced a process, not rules: the charter assigns primary responsibility for AI risk to the companies, the meeting did not discuss new laws, and the enforcement route is unclear (a White House official floated export controls). This sits beside a voluntary accord with no penalties (source: https://thenextweb.com/news/super-intelligence-force-charter-first-meeting-anthropic, Oct 2026)
- The most visible federal governance output of October 2026 is terminology: the "Super Intelligence" order keeps the existing statutory definition of AI and, per the cable, preserves legally required language, so renaming does not itself add any duty on developers (source: https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/state-department-orders-super-intelligence-wording-across-all-official-communications/416703/, Oct 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- Germany's KI-MIG (AI Act implementation law) has been in force since 29 July 2026 and makes the Bundesnetzagentur the central market surveillance authority, contact point and complaints office for the AI Act, with BaFin and the state media authorities keeping their sectors; a regulatory sandbox must be set up. Not in the previous sweep (German-language primary source) (source: https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/1112336, Jul 2026)
- The Commission accepted Temu's Digital Services Act action plan on 26 Oct 2026 after a May 2026 non-compliance decision and EUR 200 million fine; Temu's interim risk assessment is due 30 Nov 2026. This is DSA enforcement on a marketplace, not AI-specific (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-accepts-temus-action-plan-remedy-breach-digital-services-act, Oct 2026)
- New York's RAISE Act, as amended on 27 Mar 2026, takes effect 1 January 2027, with a DFS office, civil penalties up to USD 1 million for a first violation and USD 3 million after, and USD 1,000 per day for failure to file a disclosure statement (source: https://www.wiley.law/alert-New-York-Finalizes-RAISE-Act-for-Frontier-AI-Models-Law-Takes-Effect-January-1-2027, Apr 2026)
