# Theme exploration, coordination, 2026-11-01

Turn 1 – Conceptual Frame

Coordination is usually pictured as an agreement: two parties settle on a shared way of doing something and then do it. The evidence of this run suggests a more demanding picture. An agreement that exists on paper but cannot be relied on at the moment of use is not yet coordination. It is an intention to coordinate. What makes two organizations able to act together is not the existence of a standard but the existence of a particular, dated, unchanging version of it that both have actually built against. Call that a frozen edition. Without one, each side coordinates with the other's current guess about what the standard says.

Seen this way, the month's confirmed events are about the supply of agreements, not the supply of coordination. The IESG approved the charter of the agentproto working group on 8 Oct 2026, with milestones for a use-cases draft in Dec 2026, an architecture draft in Feb 2027 and dialog-management protocol drafts in Mar 2027 (source: https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/charter-ietf-agentproto/history/, Oct 2026). On 22 Oct 2026 the Agentic AI Foundation announced four more hosted projects, taking its roster to ten (source: https://aaif.io/news/agentic-ai-foundation-announces-four-new-projects, Oct 2026). Each event adds a place where agreement can be made. Neither adds an edition that anyone has yet built against.

The frame also separates two meanings of the word interoperability. One is technical: messages parse. The other is temporal: messages parse today and will still parse when the second party next updates. A protocol that deprecates large parts of itself under a twelve-month window, as the Model Context Protocol did in its 2026-07-28 specification when it removed sessions and the initialize handshake and deprecated Roots, Sampling, Logging, HTTP+SSE and Dynamic Client Registration, is technically coherent and temporally demanding (source: https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog.md, Jul 2026). The party with the engineering staff to track the change coordinates well. The party without it coordinates with whatever version it last had time to read.

A philosophical consequence follows. If coordination depends on a shared edition, then the scarce thing is not cleverness in agents but patience in specifications: the willingness of a standard's authors to stop changing it long enough for others to catch up. Standards bodies reward the opposite behaviour, since a new project, a new working group or a new draft is a visible output, while a quiet period is not.

Finally, the frame asks who bears the cost of a moving target. In a shared edition the cost of keeping current is spread across the community that wrote it. Without one, it falls on each pair of organizations separately, and pairs differ in how much they can pay. The structural question of this run asks whether the venues will converge on a few frozen editions or keep multiplying, and the answer is a question about who pays for coordination, which is a question about power more than about protocols.

Turn 2 – Societal Reframing

Societies have long coordinated across organizational lines through public infrastructure that nobody owns in particular: weights and measures, time zones, railway gauges, postal codes. Their value comes from being boring. A railway gauge that changed every year would still be a standard, in the sense that each change was documented, and yet it would not coordinate anything. The agent-protocol landscape of late 2026 is being built by institutions that speak the language of public infrastructure while operating on the schedule of product releases.

The roster shows this blend. The Agentic AI Foundation, hosted by the Linux Foundation, now lists Agent Router, Agent Skills, A2A, AGENTS.md, agentgateway, goose, MCP, Open Responses, OpenEnv and OpenSandbox, and the newest entries add a model-interface specification and a sandbox layer beside MCP and A2A (source: https://aaif.io/projects/, Nov 2026). Each project was contributed by a company: Anthropic, OpenAI, Hugging Face and Alibaba gave the four newest. A neutral foundation holds assets that private firms built, which is a common route to public infrastructure, but it is also a route by which firms keep the options that matter, since a donor chooses what to donate and when.

The societal question is therefore about legitimacy. A standard becomes public when people with no seat at the table can still rely on it and, if necessary, contest it. The sweep records one sign of this tension. The MCP Agents group was converted from a Working Group to an Interest Group on 16 Oct 2026, with its Tasks scope moved to a separate Tasks and Events group (source: https://modelcontextprotocol.io/community/interest-groups/agents.md, Oct 2026). An interest group discusses; a working group produces. The change may be sensible housekeeping, but it shows that the places where a participant can speak and the places where a specification is decided are not the same places.

There is also a gap between governance and use that societies tolerate in other domains and may not tolerate here. On the Universal Commerce Protocol monitor, 17,056 stores are bound to Checkout while AP2 Mandate is bound on 37 and other payment capabilities on three or four (source: https://ucpchecker.com/capabilities, Nov 2026). The protocol has governance, councils and a draft lodging capability, and the money-moving parts are barely used. Citizens meeting agent commerce will meet the Checkout layer, where the protections live in the less-used layers.

A final societal point concerns who is affected when coordination fails. A failed handoff between two companies' agents lands on a customer, a patient or a supplier who never chose the protocol. Public infrastructure usually answers to such people through regulation. Here, a bipartisan US proposal announced on 1 Oct 2026 would make operators of agents liable when their agents hack other systems, though introduction is unverified (source: https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/ai-firms-should-be-held-liable-their-models-actions-lawmakers-say/416374/, Oct 2026). Liability would reach the operator, not the standard, leaving the standard's authors untouched.

Turn 3 – Psychological Implications

The people who live closest to these standards are not executives but maintainers, integrators and small-company engineers, and their psychology is the most revealing. A person asked to make a system talk to a counterpart's system must decide, on incomplete information, which version of the shared standard to target. This is a decision about trust in the future. Choose the newest edition and risk building on something that changes. Choose the stable one and risk waking up on the wrong side of a deprecation date.

The sweep supplies the raw material for that anxiety. The MCP roadmap, last updated 22 Aug 2026, names five priority areas for the next release, including agent identity and agentic messaging, sets no date, and calls itself current thinking rather than firm commitments (source: https://modelcontextprotocol.io/development/roadmap, Aug 2026). To a maintainer, honesty about uncertainty is welcome and also unsettling: it confirms that the thing being built on will move and gives no calendar for when. The same maintainer reads that Agent Skills, now hosted by AAIF, has no tagged release and that its last commit predates the donation (source: https://github.com/agentskills/agentskills, Nov 2026). A governance announcement arrives without the artefact that would let anyone depend on it.

The psychological result is a particular fatigue: not the fatigue of hard work but the fatigue of work that may be invalidated. People adapt in recognisable ways. Some defer, waiting for a standard to settle before investing, and so fall behind those who bet early. Some over-invest in abstraction layers that insulate them from change, paying a cost in complexity that nobody sees. Some stop reading changelogs, treating each as noise. Each response is rational for the individual and corrosive for the shared system.

There is also a quieter feeling, the sense of being a guest in a house whose owners keep rearranging the furniture. Certification offers a little reassurance. AAIF launched the Model Context Protocol Associate certification on 14 Sep 2026, aligned to the 2026-07-28 specification, and the official Ruby SDK reached the top conformance tier on 29 Sep 2026 (source: https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/agentic-ai-foundation-launches-mcpa-certification-to-validate-mcp-expertise, Sep 2026) (source: https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/, Sep 2026). Credentials and conformance tiers give a person something to hold. They also tie that person's standing to one edition, which will itself age.

For those on the receiving end, such as a customer whose order fails between two agents, the experience is of a world that promises seamlessness and delivers a shrug. Learned resignation is the likely outcome: people stop expecting systems to meet each other and begin routing around them by hand.

Turn 4 – Institutional Dynamics

Institutions respond to the supply of venues in predictable ways. When several bodies can claim a topic, each has an incentive to claim it early, and the incentive to finish is weaker than the incentive to start. The record of the month fits this pattern. The IETF has chartered a working group whose first deliverable is a use-cases draft due in Dec 2026, which is a document about what to build and not a protocol (source: https://datatracker.ietf.org/group/agentproto/about/, Nov 2026). The grading pass found only individual-submission drafts so far and no working-group drafts yet (coordination-2026-09-01, open). Meanwhile AAIF has taken in specifications whose own repositories show slow versioning: Open Responses adopted a final Linux Foundation technical charter on 22 Oct 2026 and moved its schema generation to TypeSpec on 9 Oct 2026, yet its last dated snapshot is 2026-04-24 (source: https://github.com/openresponses/openresponses, Oct 2026).

Standards bodies, foundations and vendors also relate to one another through overlap. MCP's roadmap lists agent identity as a priority and describes an Agent Identity working group as forming, while the same period saw NIST's agent identity and authorization concept paper stay unchanged since 5 Feb 2026 (coordination-2026-08-04, open; source: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/other/2026/02/05/accelerating-the-adoption-of-software-and-ai-agent/ipd, Nov 2026). Three venues now circle the same question, identity, at three speeds. Institutions in this position tend either to merge, which is slow and political, or to coexist through liaison, which is cheap and leaves the user to reconcile.

A second dynamic is the quiet shift of power from the body that writes the specification to the body that hosts the conformance test. A certification such as MCPA, or a public monitor such as the UCP capabilities table, defines in practice what counts as working. Whoever runs such a measure gains influence over adoption without needing to win any argument about the text.

A third dynamic is how institutions under legal pressure behave. The sweep records a proposed US bill on operator liability for agents that attack other systems; if enacted, it would push companies to document which counterpart they were coordinating with and on what terms (inference). Past calibration warns against assuming such bills move quickly: announced dates and first publications slip, while single-actor procedural steps hold. The institutional forecast is therefore for continued venue growth, slow convergence, and private arrangements doing the work in the gap, in keeping with the observation that private ordering often leads regulation.

Turn 5 – Long-Term Trajectory

Within five years the most probable shape of agent coordination is layered and uneven. A few interfaces will become dull in the useful sense: tagged, dated, implemented by thousands of parties, and rarely changed. The Checkout capability, bound on 17,056 stores, shows what that looks like in one domain (source: https://ucpchecker.com/capabilities, Nov 2026). Around that core, a wide band of newer specifications will exist in a state of active governance and thin adoption, each with its own roadmap, its own foundation seat and its own migration windows. The long-term question is how large that band stays and who works inside it.

Several paths are visible from the evidence. In the first, convergence: the IETF working group and the foundations divide the field, drafts reach adoption milestones roughly on time, and by 2028 a handful of editions are widely implemented. This is the path the charters assume, and the calibration record argues against assuming it: first drafts and first publications have tended to slip. In the second, fragmentation: venues keep multiplying, each project keeps its own cadence, and integrators maintain pinned versions of several interfaces. Coordination between two organizations then depends on a written bilateral arrangement naming the versions both will honour, and small organizations that cannot staff that work are priced out of cross-organization agent work. In the third, consolidation by a dominant platform: one vendor's gateway or marketplace becomes the practical meeting place, and its terms substitute for a public standard.

The second path seems the most likely over the next two years, because the sweep shows venue growth without matching tags. Four near-term observations will show which path is forming: whether AAIF lists an eleventh project, whether Agent Skills publishes its first tag, whether Open Responses issues a new dated snapshot, and whether MCP charters an agent identity group, all by Jan 2027 (ledger coordination-2026-11-01 to coordination-2026-11-04, open). None needs an unusual event, and each is evidence about whether governance is producing artefacts.

The philosophical residue is about time. Coordination across organizations needs a shared present: a moment in which both parties agree what the rules are. Standards bodies manufacture that shared present by freezing an edition. If they keep issuing new editions faster than adopters can absorb them, the shared present shrinks, and what remains is a shared intention. Over the next five years the contest is less between open and closed protocols than between those who can afford to keep current and those who cannot, and the winners will include whoever offers to hold the shared present on everyone else's behalf.
