# Forces and feelings, coordination, 2026-11-01

## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)

Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
Standards venues multiply and are praised as openness: a chartered IETF group, ten AAIF projects, fourteen MCP working groups and eight interest groups (Political 1 and 4; sweep, Oct and Nov 2026). Yet the people who must live with the interfaces are volunteers and small-shop engineers who cannot attend that many rooms (Social 4, inference), and the one room that mattered to agent work was converted on 16 Oct 2026 from a Working Group to an Interest Group, where people may speak and not decide. The political layer says anyone may take part. The social layer has a calendar that only paid staff of large vendors can follow.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
Governance is financed generously: four vendors placed four projects in a neutral foundation on 22 Oct 2026, and a certification market grew around one edition (Economic 1 and 4). Contracts, though, need something to name. Agent Skills has no tagged release, Open Responses has no dated snapshot since 2026-04-24, and MCP deprecates parts of itself on a twelve-month clock (Legal 4; Economic 3). Money is spent on the venue, and the legal paper that sets out who owes what cannot cite an edition. The buyer signs for an interface that the seller's own governance body has not frozen.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
Coordination needs a shared present, a moment in which both parties agree what the rules are. Everyone in the field agrees on this and says so in roadmaps. Lived reality is that the roadmap describes itself as "current thinking rather than firm commitments", so the person who tries to build to the shared present is building to a draft. The principle says wait for the edition; the market rewards whoever integrates first. Those who wait fall behind, and those who do not wait carry the migration bill.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)

Normal Absurdity 1:
A line in every integration ticket that reads "targets spec as of" followed by a date and nothing else, filled in the way one writes the weather on a postcard. Nobody asks which edition, because there is no edition to ask about. (A lived expression of Agent Skills and Open Responses having governance but no tag or new snapshot.)

Normal Absurdity 2:
A thin shelf of conference lanyards in the hallway of a small firm, one from each of the foundations, kept because someone may need to prove they were once in a room. A new hire is told which lanyard opens which mailing list.

Normal Absurdity 3:
Checking the public adoption table before a sales call, the way one checks a bus timetable: 17,056 on Checkout, 37 on the mandate, and everyone nods and sells the second as if it were the first.

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing

The Irreplaceable Thing:
A held seat: a standing place in the one room where the interface you depend on is decided, kept long enough to be heard before the change ships.

Explanation:
Scarce by a mechanism the sweep and PESTLE name. The rooms multiply (Political 1: a new IETF group; Economic 1: ten AAIF projects) while the rooms that decide get narrower: the MCP Agents group became an Interest Group on 16 Oct 2026 and Tasks scope moved elsewhere (Political 4; Social 3). Attendance costs paid hours that only large vendors have (Social 4). The seat is institutional standing, not a person's company: it is a chair, a vote or a drafted paragraph that survives the next reorganisation. It differs in kind from a settled term or a rehearsed capability, because it is access to the making of the rule, not to the rule or to a skill. A protagonist can pursue it by turning up, volunteering for the draft nobody wants, and holding the seat through a quiet year.
