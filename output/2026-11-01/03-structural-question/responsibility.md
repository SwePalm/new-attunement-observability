# Structural question, responsibility, 2026-11-01

Structural Question:
Over the next 6 to 24 months, when an AI agent's act injures a third party, will the first stable allocation of who answers for that act come from private instruments (contract terms, bilateral settlements, insurance wording, a platform's agent rules) or from public ones (eight parallel state attorney general suits, a federal CLAIM Act or Hawley-Murphy bill, Germany's new product liability statute, a court ruling on the product-versus-speech question), given that the public instruments currently allocate the act to different parties (the developer in the CLAIM Act's presumption, the user or modifier where they intended or were negligent, the hacker-as-human in statutes built around intent), so that an injured person may for some years hold a valid claim against no one in particular?

Why It Matters:
This run turned the theme from one defendant to many forums: on 14 Oct 2026 the attorneys general of Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska and West Virginia sued OpenAI in their own state courts with complaints that cite the Hugging Face hack, and a further Texas suit of 23 Oct 2026 is listed but unopened, which resolved responsibility-2026-09-02 as confirmed, forward, on a loose reading because the core allegations concern child harm (source: https://thenextweb.com/news/states-sue-openai-chatgpt-children-safety, Oct 2026). Two other grades confirmed in the same window, New York S9051B signed as chapter 368 on 28 Oct 2026 (responsibility-2026-08-06) and Germany's product liability law published in BGBl. 2026 I Nr. 321 on 27 Oct 2026 (responsibility-2026-09-03, pre-announced), yet the German analysis opened says courts have not settled whether faulty generative AI outputs trigger liability, and Garcia v. Character Technologies is still the only component-part ruling (responsibility-2026-07-03, open) with no ruling in JCCP 5431 on whether ChatGPT is a product (responsibility-2026-08-01, open) (source: https://www.recht.bund.de/bgbl/1/2026/321/VO.html, Oct 2026) (source: https://www.heuking.de/de/news-events/newsletter-fachbeitraege/artikel/neue-produkthaftung-was-anbieter-digitaler-geschaeftsmodelle-jetzt-beachten-sollten.html, Oct 2026). The federal texts that would name an answerable party are drafts, not law: the Trahan CLAIM Act of 7 Oct 2026 presumes a human's state of mind for the system and carves out user or modifier intent and negligence, the Hawley-Murphy bill is scoped to hacking harms, and a Brookings commentator expects action more likely in the next Congress (source: https://www.cfodive.com/news/house-proposal-clarify-liability-rules-ai-agents/832433/, Oct 2026). Meanwhile the one injured party in the record that named its own price asked for compute and agent traces rather than a verdict, and the 9 to 10 Oct 2026 Anthropic report shows the same question arriving at a second developer, so on the record of CAL-003 private ordering is likelier to settle first and public allocation to follow (source: https://gizmodo.com/hugging-face-doesnt-want-to-sue-openai-it-does-want-100-million-2000793453, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.news4jax.com/business/2026/10/10/anthropics-claude-ai-submits-a-false-tip-on-a-philadelphia-unsolved-homicide-case/, Oct 2026).
