- The creative-economy legal landscape crystallized in H1 2026: training is largely fair use, pirated acquisition is not, human authorship is constitutionally settled, and the licensing market arrived at billion-dollar scale.

Confirmed Developments:
- Bartz v. Anthropic settled for $1.5B (~$3,000 per work) after the court held AI training on copyrighted books is fair use but storing pirated copies is not (source: https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ce8eaa5f/ai-in-litigation-series-an-update-on-ai-copyright-cases-in-2026, 2026) (source: https://sustainabletechpartner.com/topics/ai/generative-ai-lawsuit-timeline/, 2026)
- The US Supreme Court denied certiorari in Thaler v. Perlmutter (2 Mar 2026), leaving intact the rule that copyright requires human authorship (source: https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/us-supreme-court-declines-to-consider-whether-ai-alone-can-create-copyrighted-works, Mar 2026) (source: https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ce8eaa5f/ai-in-litigation-series-an-update-on-ai-copyright-cases-in-2026, 2026)
- Disney and OpenAI struck a $1B three-year deal licensing 200+ characters for Sora video generation from early 2026, the first franchise-scale licensing of creative IP into a generative platform (source: https://sustainabletechpartner.com/topics/ai/generative-ai-lawsuit-timeline/, 2026) (source: https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ce8eaa5f/ai-in-litigation-series-an-update-on-ai-copyright-cases-in-2026, 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Divergent fair-use outcomes are creating a de facto rulebook: transformative training passes (Bartz, Kadrey partial), competitive-substitution training fails (Thomson Reuters v. Ross), the line is market substitution, not copying per se (source: https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ce8eaa5f/ai-in-litigation-series-an-update-on-ai-copyright-cases-in-2026, 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- The Bartz court explicitly stated authors are NOT entitled to licensing revenue for fair-use training, the $1.5B flowed from piracy, not from training itself, undercutting hopes of a general training-license economy for individual creators (source: https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ce8eaa5f/ai-in-litigation-series-an-update-on-ai-copyright-cases-in-2026, 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- EU watermarking obligations for AI-generated content were delayed to 2 Dec 2026 under the Digital Omnibus, provenance labeling of synthetic creative work slips (source: https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/eu-overhauls-ai-act-just-before-key-deadline, Jun 2026)

Capital Movements:
- IP-holder strategy is bifurcating: litigate acquisition (settlements) while licensing distribution (Disney–OpenAI), creative capital is choosing platform participation over blockade (source: https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/us-supreme-court-declines-to-consider-whether-ai-alone-can-create-copyrighted-works, Mar 2026)

Technical Changes:
- None beyond provenance tooling (tracked under perception/truth).

Contradictions:
- Individual creators got per-work settlement checks while the legal principle established (training is fair use) forecloses their recurring licensing revenue; franchise owners with negotiating power got billion-dollar deals.
- Human authorship is required for copyright at exactly the moment most commercial creative output becomes human-AI blended, making the authorship boundary the new contested zone.
