Turn 1 – Conceptual Frame

Agency in 2026 crossed the line from acting to transacting, and that line matters more than it first appears. Through 2025, delegated AI acted within systems, drafting, scheduling, retrieving, but the consequential act of committing resources remained human. In the first half of 2026 that boundary moved: the Machine Payments Protocol launched in March with Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, Anthropic, OpenAI, and Shopify; American Express debuted agent purchase protection in April; all three major US card networks now support agentic commerce; and agents are estimated to mediate 10–20% of US e-commerce, some $190–385 billion. The conceptual shift is that delegation acquired the power to bind, an agent can now spend, commit, and transact on a person's behalf within a mandate. This reframes the agency question. It is no longer "will AI act autonomously?", it demonstrably does, within bounds, but "where does the human decision actually live once the agent both shapes the options and executes the choice?" The evidence shows delegation expanding through a disciplined, tiered pattern (routine tasks widely delegated, mid-risk bounded, high-impact human-gated), which is genuinely agency-preserving in design. But two developments complicate the tidy picture: persistent memory now lets systems model longitudinal preferences, so the agent increasingly curates the choice set it "merely executes," and containment controls lag adoption badly (only 37–40% report purpose binding or kill switches against ~81% deploying agents). The conceptual frame for the next five years is therefore not delegation-versus-autonomy but the subtler question of whether tiered delegation keeps the human as the author of consequential choices or gradually relocates authorship into the delegate.

Turn 2 – Societal Reframing

Societally, transacting agents restructure the relationship between people and markets in a way that cuts against decades of consumer-protection intuition. The whole apparatus of retail persuasion, urgency banners, end-cap placement, dark patterns, dynamic pricing keyed to human psychology, targets a human decision-maker with human weaknesses. An agent executing a mandate is immune to most of it, which is genuinely emancipatory: for the first time, a layer of delegation could shield ordinary people from manipulation designed to exploit attention and impulse. This is the agency-enhancing face of the shift, and it is real. But the same intermediation creates a new and less-examined vulnerability: whoever influences the agent influences the outcome, and the agent's choices are shaped by preference models, default settings, and platform incentives the person never sees. The persuasion battle does not end; it moves from the shopper's psychology to the agent's ranking logic, from a contest the person can at least perceive to one conducted entirely upstream of awareness. Society thus trades a visible manipulation it had learned to resist for an invisible one it has no folk defenses against. The distributional dimension matters too: sophisticated users will configure, audit, and constrain their agents, exercising enhanced agency; the majority will accept defaults, and defaults are where power concentrates. The agency dividend of delegation, freedom from manipulation, from tedium, from decision fatigue, is real but unequally claimed, accruing most to those with the literacy to command their delegates rather than be quietly steered by them.

Turn 3 – Psychological Implications

Psychologically, delegating the act of choosing changes the person doing the delegating. Preference is not a fixed thing an agent merely retrieves; it is formed and revised in the act of choosing, the small friction of comparing, hesitating, and committing is where taste, judgment, and self-knowledge develop. When an agent absorbs that friction, executing purchases and decisions against an inferred preference model, it does not just save time; it removes the occasions on which preferences are examined and updated. The risk is a quiet ossification of the self: the agent optimizes against who the person was, and the person, no longer practicing the act of choosing, drifts toward being the profile their delegate maintains. This connects to the cognitive-offloading evidence in the autonomy thread, the same mechanism by which unassisted judgment atrophies applies to preference and decision. There is a countervailing psychology too: relief. Decision fatigue is real, and offloading low-stakes choices frees attention for what matters; the emancipation from trivial choosing is a genuine good. The psychological question is whether people retain the capacity and the occasions to reclaim consequential choices from their agents, or whether the convenience gradient, each delegation making the next feel natural, steadily expands the domain the agent owns. The presence of a working "undo," the ability to inspect and override the agent's reasoning, and the deliberate preservation of some unassisted choosing become psychological hygiene, not just security features.

Turn 4 – Institutional Dynamics

Institutionally, the transacting-agent economy is being built infrastructure-first and governance-second, in the now-familiar pattern of the corpus. The rails came fast: payment protocols, card-network support, purchase protection, and a four-layer stack (MCP for tools, A2A for agent coordination, ACP/UCP/MPP for commerce) that is becoming the architectural default. Liability formalized ahead of schedule, the agency ledger's own February claim about insurance and contracts allocating agent-transaction liability graded confirmed early, with ISO default AI exclusions and affirmative products (Armilla, Testudo) pricing the risk. What lags is everything about the human's position in the transaction: containment controls sit at 37–40%, only 24.4% of organizations have full visibility into agent-to-agent communication, and there is no framework governing "agent optimization", the emerging practice of structuring data or paying to influence agents' choices, which the ledger flags as a claim to watch (agency-2026-07-01 tracks agent-mediated commerce share; a future claim will track whether agent optimization becomes a named market). The institutional actors setting the rules are, once again, private: card networks writing agent-transaction terms, insurers pricing agent liability, platforms setting the defaults that most users will accept. Regulation is absent by deferral (the EU omnibus pushed high-risk obligations to 2027–2028). The institutional race specific to agency is whether consumer-protection concepts, disclosure of who influenced an agent's choice, a right to inspect the agent's reasoning, portability of the preference model, get built into the rails before the defaults harden, because once a few platforms own the default agent behavior for hundreds of millions of transactions, the locus of everyday economic agency is effectively set.

Turn 5 – Long-Term Trajectory

Projected over five years, agency divides on whether delegation stays authored or becomes ambient. In the authored path, tiered delegation matures with the human genuinely in command: containment controls become standard (agency-2026-07-03 tests whether they cross 60%), agents are inspectable and overridable, preference models are portable and user-editable, and "agent optimization" is disclosed and regulated as advertising was, people gain a powerful delegate that shields them from manipulation and tedium while retaining authorship of consequential choices. Delegation becomes genuine agency amplification. In the ambient path, delegation deepens faster than governance: defaults harden around a few platforms, preference inference quietly curates choice sets, agent-optimization becomes an unregulated influence market, and the convenience gradient expands the agent's domain until the human's role in everyday economic decisions is nominal, present in principle, absent in practice. The likely path runs between them and splits by literacy: configured, audited agency for the sophisticated minority; accepted-default, quietly-steered delegation for the majority, the agency dividend claimed unequally, mapping onto the same protection-inequality the corpus sees elsewhere, but here the inequality is of authorship over one's own choices. The decisive near-term variables are concrete and partly in the ledger: whether agent-mediated commerce share keeps climbing (agency-2026-07-01, resolve 2027-09), whether a fraud/dispute wave forces the transaction terms open (agency-2026-07-02), whether containment adoption rises (agency-2026-07-03), and whether any consumer-protection concept, inspection, disclosure, portability, gets built into the rails before defaults set. The deeper five-year stake is what kind of freedom delegation delivers: freedom to command one's delegates, or freedom from the labor of choosing that quietly becomes freedom from choosing at all.
