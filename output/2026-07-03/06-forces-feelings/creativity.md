## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)

Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
The law (Bartz) declares training on creative work fair use and therefore free, while the social reality is that individual creators experience their lawfully-used work as taken without consent or payment. The state has resolved the legality and left the grievance intact and unaddressable, a permission that feels like a loss.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
Franchise-scale owners sign billion-dollar licensing deals (Disney–OpenAI) for the exact activity, training/generation on their IP, that the law says requires no license when the IP belongs to an individual. The same underlying use is a $1B transaction or a free input depending solely on the leverage of who owns it.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
Copyright requires human authorship (Thaler), affirming that human creativity is legally special, at the very moment most commercial creative output becomes human-AI blended and the market stops paying a premium for the human part. The law honors human authorship in principle while the economy discounts it in practice.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)

Normal Absurdity 1:
Creators recognizing their own unmistakable style in a platform's outputs and having no one to bill and nothing to claim, filing the recognition away as a fact of professional life, like weather.

Normal Absurdity 2:
The same illustrator's work being worth $1 billion when Disney owns the character it resembles and $0 when the illustrator owns the style it was built from, and everyone in the industry understanding exactly why, and no one finding it strange.

Normal Absurdity 3:
Artists deliberately degrading or poisoning their own public work to make it useless for training, sabotaging their own visibility as the only available form of ownership.

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing

The Irreplaceable Thing:
A stake, a genuine share, however small, in the value one's creative work generates when a machine learns from it.

Explanation:
The 2026 settlement gave franchises leverage, platforms infrastructure, and pirated-book authors a one-time check, but it gave the working individual creator no stake at all in the training economy their work feeds. That stake, not a lawsuit, not a paywall, but a real participatory share, is the scarce thing this world cannot currently produce, because the law removed the royalty hook and no collective institution has replaced it. A protagonist can pursue it concretely: building or joining the cooperative, the training-rights society, the guild extension that aggregates unleveraged creators into a counterparty a platform will actually pay. Its scarcity is precise, leverage exists only at the poles, and the middle has none, and its pursuit is the difference between a creative class that owns a share of its own influence and one that watches its influence monetized by everyone but itself.
