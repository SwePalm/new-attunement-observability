## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)

Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
Governments in both blocs publicly insist AI is too consequential to leave ungoverned while actively dismantling or deferring the only governance that exists, the EU postponing its enacted obligations (Jun 2026 omnibus), the US suing its own states. The public hears urgency and watches retreat; the gap between announced stakes and enacted protection is the story's engine.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
The market prices AI risk faster than courts can allocate it: insurers exclude AI by default and sell coverage back as specialty product, while product-liability doctrine (Character.AI, component-part reach) expands what firms can be sued for. Firms are simultaneously told AI risk is uninsurable-by-default and infinitely litigable, pushed to deploy fast by competition and to slow down by liability, with no statute to arbitrate.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
The rule-of-law ideal says protection should not depend on private power; the lived 2026 reality is that a procurement officer's contract or a union clause protects you and a statute does not. People raised to believe rights come from law discover their effective rights come from bargaining leverage they mostly don't have.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)

Normal Absurdity 1:
Compliance teams maintain elaborate readiness programs for regulations everyone privately assumes will be deferred again, deadline-tracking dashboards where the dates are understood as fiction, updated quarterly with professional seriousness.

Normal Absurdity 2:
Companies buy insurance against laws that do not yet bind and may never: the premium for "AI Act readiness risk" is a line item while the Act itself sleeps, protection against a hypothetical is a real cost, and nobody finds this strange.

Normal Absurdity 3:
When something goes wrong with an AI system, the first institutional question is no longer "was this legal?" but "whose contract covers this?", adjusters and procurement leads arrive where inspectors were expected, and harmed individuals wait to learn whether anyone's paperwork mentions them.

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing

The Irreplaceable Thing:
A promise that holds, the experience of an institutional commitment (a deadline, a rule, a protection) that means what it says without requiring leverage to enforce.

Explanation:
In a world where statutory dates slip, federal power targets state rules, and protection flows through contracts most people never see, the scarcest good is credible commitment itself. Firms crave it for planning; workers and citizens crave it as the difference between rights and favors. Everyone in this world is hedging everything, and is quietly exhausted by it. A protagonist can pursue this thing concretely: fighting to make one deadline, one clause, one appeal process actually mean what it says. Whoever can offer a promise that holds, an insurer whose terms don't shift, a regulator who enforces on schedule, an employer whose notice provisions are honored, commands loyalty out of proportion to the promise's size, because it is the only thing not currently manufactured at scale.
