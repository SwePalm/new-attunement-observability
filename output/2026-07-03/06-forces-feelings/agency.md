## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)

Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
Card networks and platforms build elaborate protection for agent transactions (purchase guarantees, liability terms) while building no protection for the human's authorship of the choice, the transaction is insured, the decision is not. Political/commercial governance secures the money and leaves the agency ungoverned.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
Agents shield people from manipulation aimed at human psychology (an agency gain) while creating an unregulated upstream influence market, "agent optimization", aimed at the agent's ranking logic (an agency loss). The law prices agent-transaction liability ahead of schedule but has no concept for who influenced the agent's choice.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
The promise is that delegation frees people to focus on what matters; the lived risk is that the act of choosing, where preference and self-knowledge are actually formed, is the very thing delegated away, so the person optimizes toward the profile their agent maintains rather than the self they might have become.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)

Normal Absurdity 1:
People proudly "in control" of agents they have never inspected, accepting the default behavior of a system that curates their choices, commanding a delegate whose reasoning they could not describe if asked.

Normal Absurdity 2:
Merchants abandoning decades of shopper-psychology craft to instead court software, SEO for agents, "agent optimization" consultancies, competing to influence a buyer that has no impulses, only a mandate and a ranking.

Normal Absurdity 3:
A person discovering their own preferences by reading what their agent bought for them, outsourcing not just the purchase but the self-knowledge the purchase used to provide.

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing

The Irreplaceable Thing:
Authorship of one's own consequential choices, the retained capacity, and the actual occasions, to be the author rather than the profile of the decisions that shape a life.

Explanation:
Delegation can amplify agency or hollow it, and the difference is authorship: whether the human remains the source of consequential choices or drifts into ratifying what an inferred profile selects. In a world where agents transact, curate, and optimize against who you were, the scarce thing is the preserved act of choosing where it matters, inspectable, overridable, genuinely yours. A protagonist can pursue it concretely: auditing the agent, reclaiming a decision the agent would have made, insisting on the friction that forms preference, building or demanding the inspection and portability rights that keep authorship with the person. Its scarcity is structural, defaults concentrate power, containment lags at 37–40%, and the convenience gradient makes each surrender feel natural, and its loss is the quietest in the corpus, because it feels like freedom right up until the choices stop being yours.
