## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)

Contradiction 1 (Economic vs. Legal):
Markets reward the AI-layoff announcement immediately while the only accountability mechanisms, bargaining obligations, NLRA theories, eventual ROI evidence, operate on multi-year delays. Firms are paid now for claims that can only be audited later, and the auditors (unions, courts, analysts) reach a minority of cases. The engine: decisions that outrun their own verification.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Social):
Employers eliminate the junior roles where judgment is learned while raising judgment requirements for the junior roles that remain (7x senior-skill demands). The economy is consuming the seed corn of the very senior talent it claims to need, and booking the harvest as efficiency.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
The official story says AI frees humans for "higher-value work"; the lived reality of the retained is supervisory solitude (one-person teams spanning former departments) and of the young, anticipatory obsolescence (training for rungs that may not exist). The freedom on offer is the freedom to be the last accountable human in the loop.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)

Normal Absurdity 1:
Earnings calls where executives announce record AI investment, thousands of "AI-related" role eliminations, and an urgent senior-talent shortage in consecutive sentences, and no analyst asks how the three fit together.

Normal Absurdity 2:
New graduates listing "manages AI agents" on CVs for jobs they have never held, because the entry-level job that would have taught them management no longer exists, credentialing for supervision without ever having been supervised.

Normal Absurdity 3:
The workplace ritual of the "AI-attribution guess": colleagues privately handicapping whether this quarter's restructuring is really about the technology, the balance sheet, or the story, while HR communications treat the question as unaskable.

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing

The Irreplaceable Thing:
The first rung, a real role where a beginner is paid to be supervised, allowed to err, and formed into someone worth trusting with judgment.

Explanation:
Everything else in this world is substitutable in principle: tasks route to agents, seniors span wider, credentials multiply. What cannot be synthesized is the apprenticeship experience, the years where competence is built inside tolerated imperfection, under someone obliged to watch. The 2026 economy is liquidating exactly this, at 35–67% posting declines, while demanding its output (judgment, leadership) at entry. A protagonist can pursue the first rung concretely: fighting to keep one apprenticeship program alive, to win one notice-and-bargain clause that slows one restructuring, to be, or to secure, the one supervisor who still forms people. Its scarcity is measurable, its loss compounds silently for a decade, and every institution in the story wants its fruit while defunding its root.
