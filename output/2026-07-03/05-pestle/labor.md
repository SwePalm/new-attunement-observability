### Political
Core Shift Thesis:
Labor displacement is politically unclaimed territory: no major government owns the adjustment, leaving unions and courts as the only institutions contesting AI-attributed restructuring.
Forces:
1. EU omnibus deferral moved recruitment/employment AI obligations (Annex III) to Dec 2027, delaying the strongest statutory constraint on workplace AI precisely during the restructuring wave.
2. US executive suppression of state AI law (Dec 2025 EO, DOJ task force) blocks the jurisdictions most likely to legislate worker protections.
3. California's executive-order approach to AI worker disruption signals states will act through administrative channels where statutes are besieged.
4. Displacement concentrated on young workers is building a generational political constituency with no current partisan home, a latent force both parties will court by 2028.

### Economic
Core Shift Thesis:
The restructuring wave runs ahead of its own evidence: capital reallocates from headcount to compute on a substitution thesis that measured returns do not yet support.
Forces:
1. 123,000+ H1-2026 US tech cuts with AI most-cited; 56% of layoff events name AI/automation, the announcement itself moves markets, decoupling the decision from verification.
2. Gartner-reported absence of reduction-ROI correlation ("budget room, not return") creates an accountability vacuum: no institution audits whether claimed substitution materialized.
3. Explicit headcount-for-infrastructure trades (GitLab cutting 14% to fund AI capacity) formalize labor as the funding source for the capex race.
4. Entry-level contraction (postings -35% since 2023; junior software/data -67%) removes the cheapest skill-formation mechanism firms had, creating a deferred senior-skill cost that current accounting does not book.

### Social
Core Shift Thesis:
The adjustment cost is generationally concentrated: career formation itself is being redistributed away from the young, while aggregate employment statistics conceal the rupture.
Forces:
1. ~14% lower job-finding rate for young workers in AI-exposed occupations while older cohorts hold, the adjustment is intra-generational transfer, not mass unemployment.
2. Entry roles demanding senior skills (7x more likely to require judgment/leadership) relocate skill formation into education systems funded for neither the task nor the scale (23% of grads AI-trained vs 47% reporting AI-changed hiring).
3. Employment growth concentrates in embodied sectors (healthcare, government, hospitality), work is being sorted by delegation-resistance, reshaping status hierarchies around physical presence.
4. Job satisfaction stratifies (record 69% overall; gains among AI-confident, higher-income, male workers), the meaning of work is being redistributed along the same lines as its security.

### Technological
Core Shift Thesis:
Agent platforms crossed from assistive to role-replacing in production, making "team" an optional organizational form for routinizable cognitive work.
Forces:
1. Named support-role replacement at scale (Salesforce 9,000→5,000; Klarna; Paycom) demonstrates production-grade substitution in bounded domains.
2. "One-person team" experiments (Coinbase) test supervisory concentration: one accountable human spanning work previously distributed across teams.
3. Agent benchmark gains (MLE-bench 17%→65%; WebArena to 74.3%) expand the routinizable frontier faster than role definitions adapt.
4. Production unreliability (frontier models failing ~1 in 3 production attempts) caps the substitution frontier and quietly re-creates human exception-handling work inside "automated" processes.

### Legal
Core Shift Thesis:
Collective bargaining is writing the de facto labor law of AI deployment, enforceable, fast, and coverage-limited, while statutes defer and litigation incubates.
Forces:
1. 58 NewsGuild-CWA contracts with AI clauses, Microsoft union notice provisions, Culinary Union bargain-before-deployment and tech-layoff severance: contract as statute-substitute.
2. 38% of union members report automated-management/surveillance provisions, algorithmic management is becoming a mandatory bargaining subject in practice.
3. NLRA theories for AI-driven layoffs are being tested, potentially converting restructuring decisions into unfair-labor-practice exposure.
4. Product-liability doctrine reaching AI supply chains (Character.AI precedent) previews employer exposure when workplace AI harms employees or the public.

### Environmental
Core Shift Thesis:
The same capital flow that cuts headcount builds datacenters, labor and energy are competing claims on the AI investment thesis, and communities host the tradeoff.
Forces:
1. ~$700B hyperscaler capex (2026) is partially funded by workforce reductions, making local datacenter booms and distributed layoffs two faces of one allocation.
2. Datacenter siting concentrates construction and operations employment in energy-rich regions, decoupling AI's job geography from its layoff geography.
3. Grid constraints that delay projects (interconnection queues) also delay the promised local employment, exposing communities to announcement-reality gaps analogous to the layoff-ROI gap.
