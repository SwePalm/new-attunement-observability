### Political
Core Shift Thesis:
Transacting agents arrived with no political framework governing the human's position in the transaction, leaving consumer-protection concepts unbuilt as the rails set.
Forces:
1. EU omnibus deferral (Jun 2026) postponed high-risk obligations to 2027–2028, leaving agent-commerce governance to card networks and contracts.
2. No regulatory concept yet exists for "agent optimization" (paying/structuring to influence agent choices), an emerging influence market forming without disclosure rules.
3. US executive suppression of state AI law removes the venues most likely to legislate agent-transaction consumer protections.
4. Card-network self-governance (agent purchase protection, registered-agent terms) becomes de facto political rule-making for a fifth of some commerce flows.

### Economic
Core Shift Thesis:
Agents crossed from acting to transacting, relocating the persuasion economy from the shopper's psychology to the agent's ranking logic.
Forces:
1. Machine Payments Protocol (Mar 2026, with Visa/Mastercard/Anthropic/OpenAI/Shopify) and Amex agent purchase protection (Apr 2026) give delegated systems binding spend authority.
2. Agents mediate an estimated 10–20% of US e-commerce ($190–385B); x402 processed ~165M agent transactions early, the volume that makes agent-influence commercially decisive.
3. Traditional retail persuasion (dark patterns, urgency, dynamic pricing) loses its human target, forcing merchants toward influencing agents instead.
4. Preference inference from persistent memory lets the agent curate the choice set it executes, the decisive economic act moves upstream of the human.

### Social
Core Shift Thesis:
The agency dividend of delegation, freedom from manipulation and tedium, is real but claimed unequally, accruing to those with the literacy to command their delegates.
Forces:
1. Agents immune to impulse-manipulation genuinely shield users from exploitative persuasion, an emancipatory effect for ordinary consumers.
2. Whoever influences the agent influences the outcome, shifting manipulation from perceptible to invisible (upstream of awareness).
3. Sophisticated users configure and audit agents (enhanced agency); the majority accept defaults (quiet steering), the dividend splits by literacy.
4. Defaults become the locus of power: platform default agent behavior governs hundreds of millions of transactions the users never inspect.

### Technological
Core Shift Thesis:
A four-layer agent stack became the default architecture for delegated action, but the human-facing controls (inspection, override, containment) lag badly.
Forces:
1. MCP (tools) + A2A (coordination) + ACP/UCP/MPP (commerce) is the emerging default stack for transacting agents.
2. Containment controls (purpose binding, kill switches) sit at 37–40% against ~81% deploying agents, the human's stop-button is missing at scale.
3. Persistent memory enables longitudinal preference modeling, making the agent a curator of choice rather than a neutral executor.
4. Registered-agent identity frameworks bind delegated agents to accountable principals, the technical precondition for both protection and inspection rights.

### Legal
Core Shift Thesis:
Agent-transaction liability formalized ahead of schedule via insurance and card-network terms, the agency ledger's own confirmed-early claim, while consumer rights lag.
Forces:
1. agency-2026-02-04 graded confirmed ahead of horizon: ISO default AI exclusions (CG 40 47/48) plus affirmative products (Armilla, Testudo) price agent-transaction liability now.
2. Amex agent purchase protection creates the first commercial liability binding between a delegated agent and its principal.
3. No legal concept of disclosure (who influenced the agent), inspection (the agent's reasoning), or portability (the preference model) yet exists.
4. Product-liability doctrine (Character.AI, component-part reach) previews provider exposure when an agent's transaction causes harm.

### Environmental
Core Shift Thesis:
Continuously-running transacting agents add persistent load to the concentrated compute substrate, coupling everyday economic agency to infrastructure concentration.
Forces:
1. Always-on agents transacting at scale consume persistent compute, unlike episodic human shopping sessions.
2. Agent-commerce concentration on a few payment and platform rails couples consumer agency to a handful of infrastructure providers.
3. The ~$700B compute buildout that powers agent capability ties the expansion of delegated agency to energy and datacenter constraints.
