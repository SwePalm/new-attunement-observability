Structural Question:
Now that US courts have largely settled that AI training is fair use while explicitly denying individual creators a licensing-revenue entitlement, even as franchise-scale owners sign billion-dollar licensing deals, will the creative economy of the next five years bifurcate into IP-holders who monetize AI participation and individual creators who receive neither payment nor protection, and does any mechanism emerge to give unleveraged creators a stake?

Why It Matters:
H1 2026 wrote the rulebook fast: Bartz v. Anthropic settled for $1.5B but on a holding that training is fair use (the money flowed from piracy, not training), Thaler's cert denial fixed the human-authorship requirement, Thomson Reuters v. Ross drew the substitution line, and Disney–OpenAI licensed 200+ characters for $1B. The pattern rewards those with either litigable piracy claims or franchise-scale negotiating power, and structurally excludes the individual creator whose work trains models under fair use with no royalty. If no collective mechanism fills the post-Bartz vacuum, the 0–5 year creative economy consolidates cultural production around a few rights-rich platforms and franchises while hollowing the middle, with consequences for who can afford to make culture at all.
