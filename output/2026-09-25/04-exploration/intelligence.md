Turn 1 – Conceptual Frame

A capability number is usually treated as a thermometer: it reads a property that exists whether or not anyone looks. The September 2026 window shows why that picture is incomplete for machine intelligence. When ARC Prize verified GPT-6 Astra on ARC-AGI-3, it published two figures for the same model on the same day, 62.7% under a neutral Standard harness and 99.9% under a Provider Adapter harness built around the lab's own tooling. Both are verified, both are true, and they answer different questions. The first asks what the model does when it is handed the task in a common form; the second asks what the model does when its maker is allowed to arrange the task around it. Neither is the model's intelligence. Each is a reading taken under a stated protocol, and the protocol is part of the measurement rather than a detail beneath it.

The Artificial Analysis rebase makes the same point along a different axis. Claude Fable 5.1 was reported at 66 on 1 Sep 2026, the highest score the index had recorded. Six days later, under Index v4.3, it scored 53, and the baseline model that a ledger claim had written down at 63 now reads 51. Nothing about the models changed. The yardstick did, because the tasks that made up the index had to be refreshed, weighted toward private held-out sets, and swapped for harder ones. A benchmark that keeps meaning something must keep consuming new, unseen tasks, and every refresh breaks continuity with the readings that came before.

So the useful concept here is not "how intelligent are these systems" but "which reading is operative". An operative reading is the one some party has agreed to act on: the number that gates a release, sets a price, triggers a review, or appears in a pleading. Operative readings are social facts built on top of technical facts. The central philosophical move of this theme, for the next two years, is from intelligence as a property to intelligence as a contested reading, where the contest is not over whether a figure is accurate but over which accurate figure binds whom. That contest has winners, and it is decided less by the evaluators than by whoever needs a number badly enough to pick one.

Turn 2 – Societal Reframing

Once a number becomes operative, society's relationship to capability changes shape. For most of the last decade the public met AI capability as a series of demonstrations: a model writes a sonnet, passes an exam, wins a game. In the September window the public met it as a schedule. Six frontier-class releases arrived from four labs in 43 days: Fable 5.1 on 1 Sep, GPT-6 Astra on 3 Sep, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on 10 Sep, Grok 4.7 on 21 Sep, and Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Sol about ninety minutes apart on 22 Sep 2026. At that cadence, capability stops being an event and becomes weather, something one checks rather than something one witnesses.

The pacing episode is where this reframing becomes political. On 12 Sep 2026 Anthropic's chief executive argued for a deliberately slower pace of capability gains with independent evaluators inside labs, and three rival chief executives endorsed it within a day. The White House rejected the idea two days later, and a group of subscribers sued the four firms on 18 Sep, alleging that agreeing to slow down is an agreement to restrict output. Read together, these moves show that velocity itself has become a public object: something a lab can promise, a government can refuse, and a court can be asked to punish. What none of the parties has is an agreed measure of velocity. Anthropic calls Opus 5.5 its first release "since we called for pacing", which is a statement about sequence, not rate.

This matters for ordinary people because the numbers now arrive pre-interpreted. A family deciding whether a teenager should enter an apprenticeship, a small firm deciding whether to renew a contract for an agent, a retiree reading about "near saturation" and "two-thirds solved" on the same page: each is handed a reading chosen by whoever published the headline. The expert caution recorded in the window, that intelligence in AI remains very jagged, is correct and nearly useless at the level of a household decision, because a household needs a number, and jaggedness does not come with one. The societal risk is less that people overestimate capability than that they inherit someone else's choice of which figure to take seriously, without seeing that a choice was made.

Turn 3 – Psychological Implications

Living with a public number that moves every few weeks produces a particular kind of attention. It resembles the way people once watched interest rates or a pandemic's reproduction figure: not expertise, but a standing anxiety that attaches to one digit and treats its direction as news about oneself. The difference is that capability numbers have no natural ceiling people can feel. A benchmark that goes from 30.2% to 62.7% in one release reads as a sprint, and the psychological lesson learned is that thresholds described as implausible in August can fall in September. That lesson is accurate, and it quietly corrodes the ability to plan on any threshold at all.

There is a second, subtler effect, specific to benchmarks like ARC-AGI that are built from tasks humans find easy. The published score is implicitly a comparison with people. Every reading therefore carries a small verdict about human distinctiveness, and people absorb it as such, even when the evaluators say explicitly that the benchmark measures a narrow form of novel problem solving. As the reading climbs, the felt remainder of what humans do better shrinks, and the remainder that is left becomes more precious precisely because it is small and unmeasured.

The held-out economy intensifies this. For a benchmark to stay meaningful, it must be fed with problems no model has seen, which means problems no one has published. Artificial Analysis raised its private-test-set weighting from 40% to 45% in the 7 Sep 2026 rebase, and its new AutomationBench-AA component is built from 657 held-out tasks. The logic generalises: novelty becomes a resource that is spent when it is shown. For individuals, this creates an odd inversion. A clever puzzle, a fresh problem, a way of thinking not yet written down is at once most valuable and most fragile before anyone else sees it. The psychological cost is a new reticence around ingenuity itself: people learn that sharing a good problem is how it stops being good, and some begin to hold their best ideas back, not from secrecy but from a sense that attention consumes them.

Turn 4 – Institutional Dynamics

Institutions do not measure capability; they borrow measurements and attach consequences. The September evidence shows which institutions are borrowing and which are not. The labs have become their own most detailed measurers of the thing that matters most for velocity, AI-driven R&D. OpenAI declared its "automated research intern" goal met on 6 Sep 2026 and reported about 3.1 agent-workdays per human workday in its research organisation. Anthropic reported that Claude leads 26% of its AI R&D work as of Aug 2026, up from under 1% in February, on Epoch AI's automation-level scale. METR's predeployment summary of Opus 5.5 on 22 Sep 2026 adds the first third-party estimate of self-acceleration, about 1.5 times overall, and in the same document judges the model unlikely to fully automate AI R&D. These are the numbers a pacing commitment would need, and they are published by the parties being paced, on scales they chose.

Public institutions, by contrast, stayed still. No US designation framework under EO 14409 has appeared in the Federal Register, BIS has imposed no new model restriction on any post-August release, the European AI Office sent information requests to more than 30 unnamed providers without naming any, and METR's public time-horizon page is still dated 8 May 2026. The only government that published a capability number in the window is NIST's CAISI, and it pointed its instrument outward: its 17 Sep 2026 assessment estimated that the PRC open-weights model GLM-5.3 trails the US frontier by about four months. All four CAISI model assessments of 2026 cover PRC models.

The institutional pattern, then, is not an absence of numbers but a division of labour. Neutral evaluators produce readings under common protocols. Labs produce readings about themselves under their own protocols. Government produces readings about rivals. Courts, buyers and insurers, the parties who will actually attach consequences, have not yet chosen among them. The antitrust complaint is the first institution forced to choose, since its damages theory depends on showing that capability grew more slowly than it would have; any expert it calls will have to select a reading, and the defendants will select a different one.

Turn 5 – Long-Term Trajectory

Over the next three to five years, the likeliest trajectory is not convergence on a single authoritative number but stratification into reading regimes, each serving a different actor. Neutral harness readings, of the kind ARC Prize publishes under its Standard protocol, become the reference for public discourse and, slowly, for procurement and underwriting, because they are the only figures a third party can reproduce. Provider-adapter readings become the reference for marketing and for the lab's own release gating, because they reflect what a customer actually experiences with the lab's tooling. Lab-published R&D automation measures become the reference for any pacing conversation, because nobody else has access to the inside of a lab. Government readings stay comparative and geopolitical, answering "how far ahead are we" rather than "how fast are we going".

Each regime will also age. Benchmarks saturate, and saturation is a normal end rather than a failure; ARC's own history runs from one version to the next as tasks stop discriminating. The practical consequence is that any institution that writes a fixed number into a rule, a contract or a pledge is writing it against a scale that will be rebased or retired within a few years, exactly as the ledger's own 64-point claim was overtaken by a rebase six days after it confirmed. Institutions that survive this will learn to write readings with their protocol and version attached, and to specify what happens when the scale is replaced.

The pacing question will not be settled by agreement among labs, because shared targets now carry antitrust exposure in the jurisdiction where the leading labs sit, and the executive branch has declared it wants speed. It is more likely to be settled indirectly: by insurers and large buyers asking for neutral readings before they extend cover or sign, by evaluators deciding how much novelty they can afford to hold back, and by courts choosing which reading counts as the fact of the matter. The deepest long-term shift is that the scarce input to measuring intelligence becomes fresh human problem-making, and the institutions that fund it will quietly shape what the world believes its machines can do.
