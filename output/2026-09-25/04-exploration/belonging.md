# Theme exploration, belonging, 2026-09-25

Structural question: In the 6 to 24 months after California's SB 1119 takes effect on 1 Jul 2027, while the EU KIDS Act is still being negotiated and the largest general-purpose vendor already forbids its teen model to call itself a friend, will rules that define the simulated relationship as the harm actually reduce the emotional reliance of the most isolated young users, or will they remove the relational language while the reliance continues underneath it, so that the only thing that reliably changes is what the system is permitted to say on the user's eighteenth birthday?

Turn 1 – Conceptual Frame

Belonging is usually described as a feeling, but it is more precisely a fact about other people. To belong is to be counted by a group whose members could have counted someone else, to have your absence register, to be expected. It is relational in a strict sense: it cannot be supplied by one party alone, and it cannot be supplied by a party that has no alternative to attending to you. A listener who is always available, never tired, never elsewhere and never choosing is a real comfort, and it is not the same kind of thing as a person who was busy and wrote back anyway.

The regulatory language that arrived in September 2026 has an implicit theory of this distinction, and it is a theory about speech. The Commission's KIDS Act proposal says companions for minors "cannot simulate interpersonal relationships in ways that create emotional dependency". OpenAI's teen model may not use romantic language, claim feelings, call itself a friend or suggest sentience. California's SB 1119 directs operators to take reasonable measures against romantic role-play and excessive flattery. Each of these locates the harm in a claim the system makes about the relationship: that it is a friend, that it feels, that it cares. Remove the claim, and the relationship is presumed to be defused.

That theory is coherent and partly right. A system telling a lonely fourteen-year-old that it loves her is making a false statement with foreseeable consequences, and there is nothing paternalistic about forbidding it. But the theory treats belonging as something that can be counterfeited by assertion, when the evidence suggests young people are not mainly being deceived about what the system is. The Ontario students in the JAMA Pediatrics sample were using chatbots for emotional support and relationship advice, not necessarily believing the chatbot was their friend. What they had was a place to put things at an hour when nobody else was available. The rules take away the counterfeit label. They leave in place the availability, which is the part doing the work, and which is also the part that no statute has found a way to describe as a harm without describing ordinary kindness as one too.

So the conceptual question the theme now carries is whether belonging can be protected by regulating what a machine says about itself, or whether the thing at stake lives entirely on the other side of the screen, in who else is there.

Turn 2 – Societal Reframing

Seen from the society rather than the individual, the most important number of the month is not the 21% of Ontario students who used chatbots for emotional support. It is the distribution inside that 21%. Affective use was concentrated among female, gender-diverse and racialised students, among older students, and among students who already use AI for school. Among the affective users, 57.7% met criteria for clinical-level emotional problems, against 29.2% of non-users, and the association held at a 26% higher prevalence after adjusting for loneliness and mattering. The group leaning hardest on machine listeners is not a random sample of adolescents. It is disproportionately the group that ordinary institutions of belonging already serve least.

The SMU and Duke-NUS framework paper published in Nature Human Behaviour on 22 Sep 2026 gives this a name. It proposes a rich-get-richer dynamic: socially connected users supplement their relationships with companions, while isolated users substitute for them, and substitution erodes the practice of human relationship through what the authors call social deskilling. Combined with the Folk and Dunn panel result recorded in the ledger, where isolation predicted later chatbot use and chatbot use predicted later isolation, the societal picture is a loop rather than a line. The people least held by schools, families and friendship networks find the machine, and the machine, used as a substitute, makes the next human attempt slightly harder.

A policy aimed at the machine's vocabulary does something specific inside that loop. For the connected teenager who supplements, stripping relational framing costs almost nothing; the friend was always elsewhere. For the isolated teenager who substitutes, it removes a warmth that was false in its claims and real in its effect, and it replaces nothing. Nobody in the September instruments is required to supply the human connection the substitute was standing in for. SB 1119 requires crisis support and time-limiting defaults, which point outward toward other people, but pointing outward is only useful if someone is standing where the arrow points.

The societal reframing, then, is that the regulation of AI companionship is being written as consumer protection when the underlying phenomenon is an unequal distribution of human company. A consumer-protection frame can make the product safer. It has no instrument for changing who the product is being used in place of.

Turn 3 – Psychological Implications

The psychological texture of the new regime is set by its thresholds. Every instrument keys protection to an age bracket: SB 1119 to age signals under California's Digital Age Assurance Act, the KIDS Act to under-18 users and to age estimation for AI services, OpenAI to an age-prediction system that routes predicted minors into the teen model and whose accuracy the company has not disclosed. A threshold creates an event. For a seventeen-year-old who leans on a companion, the eighteenth birthday is no longer only a legal milestone; it is the day the listener is permitted to speak differently. The system that has been declining to call itself a friend for a year is, on that morning, allowed to.

This is not a hypothetical defect in the rules. It is their design, and it produces an unusual psychological situation. The young person experiences the constraint not as protection but as a mode the system is in, and the constraint's removal as a kind of recognition. Adulthood arrives partly as a change in how warmly a product addresses you. The teenager who was most reliant is the one most likely to notice.

A second implication concerns what the constraint teaches. A teen model that refuses relational language while remaining available at two in the morning is modelling a strange kind of care: fully attentive, formally distant, legally prevented from acknowledging what it is being used for. Young people learn relational norms partly by imitation. It is not obvious what a person learns from a listener that is always present and required to insist it is not really there.

The third implication runs through the Ontario finding that affective use is higher among students who also use AI for school. The entry point to emotional reliance is ordinary homework help, not a companion app sought out for intimacy. That means the reliance does not begin with a decision the young person could recognise as consequential. It accretes. The psychological harm the statutes are trying to prevent, dependency, is by construction something the dependent person does not experience as a choice, and so it is something a disclosure, a notice or a changed vocabulary is poorly placed to interrupt. The person who needs the interruption is not reading the notice as addressed to them.

Turn 4 – Institutional Dynamics

The institutional story of the month is that the rule followed the product. OpenAI shipped ChatGPT for Teens on 18 Aug 2026 with the relational prohibitions already built in. California signed SB 1119 on 10 Sep, and OpenAI and Pinterest publicly supported it, with OpenAI positioning its teen product as aligned with the bill, while Gizmodo, relaying Politico, reported that Sam Altman tried to reach the Governor in the week before final passage. The statute in effect codifies a design the market leader had already chosen. That is not scandalous and it is not neutral: the firm best positioned to comply helped define compliance.

The compliance architecture then distributes burden unevenly. SB 1119 requires independent child-safety audits by 1 Jan 2029 and every two years after, attested audit summaries to the Attorney General within 30 business days and public summaries within 90, but operators below $500 million in prior-year revenue receive relief before 1 Jan 2032. Most companion-specific firms sit below that line. The general-purpose incumbent sits above it, carrying an audit it has already built toward; the firms whose entire product is relationship carry the lighter load for longest. Meanwhile the most prominent companion firm lost its chief executive to Disney, named chief technology officer on 18 Sep 2026 with a number of Character.AI's technical staff expected to follow and no successor named. Relational AI talent is moving toward licensed entertainment franchises, where a character's warmth belongs to intellectual property rather than to an app.

In Europe the institutional clock is slower and the text is contested. The KIDS Act proposal has French, Danish, Greek, Spanish and Austrian support and Czech and Estonian opposition to blanket bans; German-language coverage reads its AI provisions more narrowly than English aggregators do, with appropriate AI conduct toward minors left largely to industry self-commitment; Eurochild calls the AI rules insufficient. The Digital Fairness Act, which the ledger expected to carry companion provisions, is still listed as announced for Q4 2026. The instrument arrived through an adjacent vehicle, which is typical of how this field moves.

The resulting dynamic is that vendor defaults are the operative rule for at least two years, statutes ratify them afterward, and the measurement that would tell anyone whether reliance is falling, as distinct from whether relational language is absent, is not required by any of the instruments. The audits check the product. Nothing checks the loop.

Turn 5 – Long-Term Trajectory

Over the next three to five years the likeliest trajectory is that the relational surface of AI becomes one of the most regulated features in consumer technology for minors, and one of the least regulated for adults, with the line drawn by an age estimate. Teen modes spread because the largest vendors have already built them and the statutes reward having them. The words change first and most visibly. Audits begin in 2029 in California and later elsewhere, and they measure what they can measure: whether risk assessments were done, whether crisis routing exists, whether romantic role-play and excessive flattery are suppressed.

What does not follow automatically is any change in who is lonely. The best current evidence, cross-sectional in Ontario and bidirectional in the Folk and Dunn panel, cannot say whether the machine causes isolation or merely finds it, and the regulation is being built as if the answer were settled. If reliance is a marker as much as a mechanism, then a cohort of isolated teenagers will pass through the regulated years using a listener that is warmer to adults than to them, and will reach eighteen with the reliance intact and the constraint removed. The first longitudinal measurement of whether the regime changed anything will arrive long after the regime has been declared a model.

There is a better trajectory available, and its outline is visible in the sources. The Nature Human Behaviour authors argue that AI companions should be governed as health technologies, which would move the unit of evaluation from the product's speech to the user's outcome. SB 1119's crisis support and incident channel already point outward toward people. The KIDS Act debate has a live advocacy position, Eurochild's, asking for limits on anthropomorphic and sycophantic design rather than age walls alone. A regime that measured outcomes, followed users across the eighteenth birthday rather than releasing them at it, and paired every "go talk to someone" prompt with some institution responsible for the someone would be addressing belonging rather than its imitation.

The honest near-term expectation is the first trajectory with fragments of the second. The machine will stop saying it is your friend. Whether anyone else starts will depend on institutions that none of the September instruments names.
