## 3. Institutional Realism Audit

Assessment: moderate realism

Notes:
- Friction is present and specific: the badge arrives unevenly by app, the Korean regime is shown with its grace period and creator exemption, the EU's date holds while enforcement trails, and removal is cheap. Power asymmetry is visible in the one direction that matters to the theme: the platform decides who is shown what, and the viewer cannot see the decision.
- Clocks are slowed rather than accelerated. The scenario states as a condition, not a finding, that Meta's reach rule has not reached Facebook by late 2027 (perception-2026-09-02 is open, resolve by 2027-03). That is a reasonable restraint but a real bet: Meta extending a rule to a sibling app within fourteen months is at least as plausible as not, and the Tuesday vignette depends on it.
- The world is somewhat too tidy on the viewer side. Every family member has a clear role (the betrayed follower, the sceptical teenager, the unexposed daughter), and no one simply does not care, which is probably the majority posture. Section 4's closing inference partly concedes this.
- Nothing in the scenario credits a platform or regulator with a corrective move between Sep 2026 and Oct 2027 beyond what is already announced. For a fourteen-month window in a theme where the platform layer moved twice in a month, a year of standstill on detection is a strong assumption, and it is the same assumption as Defect 1.

## 7. Cross-Theme Convergence Risk

Assessment: moderate

Notes:
- Comparison data available: the five 2026-08-13 scenarios (autonomy, desire, memory, power, truth) and the five 2026-08-04 scenarios (alignment, authority, coordination, responsibility, trust). No other 2026-09-25 deep dive was available to this evaluation.
- Form is distinct. No prior scenario uses a week of one person's feeds; none opens on a retired baker, none is set in Porto, none uses day-by-day entries.
- Subject overlap with truth (2026-08-13) is the main risk and should not be scored away. Both scenarios rest on watermarking, both use the provider's gated or absent reader, and both treat removal or laundering as ordinary hygiene. truth's Normal Absurdity of self-laundering through translation and this scenario's Saturday remover are the same behaviour in two themes. The two differ in object (truth: whether a writer is believed; perception: whether two viewers see the same thing) and in mechanism (truth: detector scores; perception: distribution withholding), but a reader of both would recognise the same furniture.
- The attractor flagged in the last two runs, that the record of an AI incident is controlled by the party least interested in publishing it, is not the scenario's conclusion, and there is no incident in it. It is adjacent, and this must be said explicitly: Section 6's second question ("who should be able to ask what they were not shown") and the second 7.3 bullet (platforms disclosing whether a restriction is in effect) both describe an information asymmetry in which the party that made a decision is the only one that can see it. In this theme the evidence does lead partway there, because a reach limit is by construction invisible to the person it affects; that is a documented property of Instagram's rule, not a narrative choice. The scenario's emphasis is on divergence between viewers rather than on concealment by the platform, and it does not claim the platform is withholding the record for its own benefit. Still, this is a third run in which a scenario's central unmet need is information held by one party about another, and it should be counted toward that pattern rather than excluded from it.
- Emotional scarcity differs in kind from prior runs (a shared view between two people, rather than a record, a first reading or a disinterested recommendation), though it is again a scarcity of epistemic common ground.

## 8. Overall Evaluation

Score (1–10): 7.5

Forecast Integrity Level: moderate

Stability Risk: medium

Summary:
The scenario's epistemic strength is that it is built on the mechanism that actually moved this month rather than the one the pipeline had predicted. The theme's own claim anticipated friction on labeled content; the world produced a reach penalty on undisclosed content, and the scenario follows the evidence to its less obvious consequence: disclosure that partitions audiences by follow history. Every signal it lists is dated and sourced, the horizon is chosen from the resolve-by dates of live claims, and the harm is kept small and ordinary.

Four weaknesses hold the score down. First, the claim that detection of unlabeled profiles was promised rather than shipped enters the chain as an unverified negative and then supports the central Sunday scene. Second, the most novel technical item in the sweep, that watermarking may alter the outputs it marks, never becomes a lived mechanism. Third, the world assumes a platform layer that stands still for thirteen months, which cuts against the same evidence showing that layer moving twice in a month, and it states that assumption as a condition for only one of the two places it operates. Fourth, the scenario shares most of its furniture with the previous run's truth scenario, and its central unmet need, knowledge of what one was not shown, sits adjacent to the recurring information-asymmetry conclusion even though it does not reproduce it.

Stability risk is medium and has identifiable triggers: Meta extending its rule to Facebook or labeling detected profiles itself would dissolve the Tuesday and Sunday vignettes, and a viewer-facing detection tool from any major vendor would dissolve Friday. All three are live, checkable claims with 2027 resolve-by dates, which is the right kind of fragility: the world can be graded.
