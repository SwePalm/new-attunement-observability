## 3. Institutional Realism Audit

Assessment: moderate realism

Notes:
- Friction is specific and costed in the scenes: two failed face captures, an
  eleven-minute bank verification, a PIN the mother does not have, and a
  session the portal cannot tell from its owner. Transitions are implied, not
  finished. The wallet is fourteen months old and has reached only the
  counters that joined early, and 7.2 keeps the EU age-verification rules in
  negotiation or phase-in through 2028.
- Power asymmetries are visible and are the scenario's main structural claim:
  whoever pays for the check decides who bears it. Fraud-averse institutions
  buy checks on people, and agent buyers buy credentials for agents. This is
  argued in Section 5 and the Final Insight, not just asserted.
- The day is arranged for effect. Every check in it lands on one person, in a
  fixed order that builds toward the contrast, and the agent's one scene is
  frictionless. Nothing in the world has an agent refused, an agent credential
  rejected by a relying party outside its scheme, or a vendor scheme that
  fails. Given that Okta's Agent Gateway was still pre-GA on 22 Sep 2026 and
  the OIDF CAEP interoperability vote slipped, a world where a German bank
  accepts a vendor agent credential in under a second by March 2028 is on the
  optimistic side of the vendor timeline. The pipeline's calibration record
  says vendor dates slip too, not only statutory ones.
- Counter-currents exist but are thin. Section 4's recognition countercurrent
  and the absence of dissent from banks, retailers or privacy advocates make
  the world somewhat too coherent. The privacy case for minimal disclosure is
  conceded (Section 5 and Section 6), which offsets part of this.

## 6. Narrative Inflation Risk

Assessment: low to moderate

Notes:
- The stakes stay small and non-catastrophic: a bottle left on a scale, an
  eleven-minute call, a woken teenager, a portal log that says the wrong
  person did something harmless. There is no breach, no victim and no court.
  The sweep offered a dramatic incident (a frontier agent inside a government
  portal), and the scenario keeps it offstage. That restraint is real.

  present human doubted, the absent agent trusted), and the world has no
  scene that complicates the thesis, such as an agent refused or a check that
  plainly helped the protagonist. The privacy concession in Section 5 and the
  Final Insight's per-checkpoint "modest" framing partly offset this.

  Brandt coda (the pram, the football, "a minute longer than she needs to") is
  the most literary moment in the file. It is kept to one scene and is not
  repeated in the Final Insight, which closes on the direction of rule-writing,

  route. The Medicare incident was found by OpenAI in Aug 2026 and reported by

  the point of access. The one brush with the attractor is the 15:00 line
  that the portal believes Emre did what his agent did. That is a
  misattribution of identity, not control of an incident record, and it is not
  developed.
- Convergence is high on the shape of the Irreplaceable Thing across this run.
  Six of six scenarios now centre a relational, in-person human presence:
  - belonging: being missed
  - intelligence: being stumped together
  - learning: company in the stuck part
  - perception: the same screen as the person beside you
  - agency: having one's own words read back and taken as the measure
  - identity: being recognised without being checked
  These are different objects with the same grammar ("being X-ed by a person
  who is there"). The 2026-08 runs converged on evidentiary scarcity (an
  unobtainable record or witness). This run appears to have swapped that for a
  relational-presence attractor. Recorded as a pipeline-level pattern, not an
  identity finding.
- Mechanism overlap with agency this run is material. Agency's scenario turns
  on a spending mandate an agent executes within limits, and identity's 15:00
  scene uses an agent credential "up to nine hundred euros". Both draw on the
  same Know Your Agent evidence. The two scenarios ask different questions
  (agency asks what the person meant, identity asks who was asked to prove
  anything), but a reader of both will see the same artifact twice.
- Partial overlap with the 2026-08-04 coordination scenario ("on whose
  behalf"). This scenario inverts coordination's finding: the agent's
  principal is named on its credential, while the human proxy has no
  category. That is a different claim in the same territory.
- Tonal homogeneity in closings: the Final Insight ends on who the rules are
  written for, and belonging's and learning's closings this run make a similar
  move (rules govern the measurable thing, not the lived one). The opener tic
  of the 2026-08 corpus ("nothing here requires bad faith") is avoided.

## 8. Overall Evaluation

Score (1–10): 7.5

Forecast Integrity Level: moderate

Stability Risk: medium

Summary:
The scenario's main epistemic asset is that its central claim, an asymmetry in
who bears the burden of proof at the point of access, is assembled from
evidence dated inside a two-week window of September 2026. That evidence points
the same way from several independent directions: inward-only agent identity
mandates (ISM-2133 to ISM-2135), vendor and bank-facing agent credentials
(Blueprint Alliance, Baselayer), statutory human checkpoints (SI 2026/1022, the
EU KIDS Act proposal), and stacked bank verification graded three times in a
row. The citation gate passes cleanly on its literal terms and on the stricter
reading the previous run proposed. The Signals Emerging are all dated and
sourced. The horizon is argued, and statutory and wallet clocks are slowed in
line with calibration.

Three weaknesses hold the score down. First, the world is a composite. Evidence
from the US, Australia, England and Wales and Germany is set in one German city
as though it had all arrived there, and two load-bearing extrapolations (a
German bank accepting a vendor agent credential, wallet age checks at German
self-checkouts) have no European evidence. Second, the scenario slows statutory
clocks but not vendor clocks. Its one frictionless scene depends on a vendor
channel whose own milestones slipped in this window. Third, the day is arranged
so that every check confirms the thesis, and no scene complicates it.

Stability risk is medium. The fastest invalidating event is a public rule, most
plausibly from the Australian taskforce (identity-2026-09-03), requiring
visiting agents to identify themselves at public services. That would narrow
the asymmetry at the portal. A slower one is a wallet programme adding bounded
delegation. Both are open on the ledger and neither is scheduled.
