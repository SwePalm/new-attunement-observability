## 3. Institutional Realism Audit

Assessment: moderate realism

Notes:
- Friction is present in the right places: the EU proposal stays under negotiation for the whole year and is split over age walls; New York's bill is not assumed signed; audits do not fall due until 2029; age estimation errs in both directions; referral duties point at services nobody funds.
- Power asymmetry is visible and specific: the market leader wrote the template, backed the bill and carries an audit it built toward, while companion-first firms receive deferral. This is stated rather than dramatised.
- An internal tension is not resolved. 7.2 expects a legal challenge to SB 1119, and the ledger already tracks one (belonging-2026-09-01), yet Section 3 assumes the California rules apply from July 2027 with no injunction, no partial stay and no compliance gap. A realistic world would at least carry the possibility that the statute is litigated through its first year. The scenario is internally coherent only if the challenge fails or is not filed in time.
- Compliance is frictionless inside the narrative. Dani's app changes its behaviour on schedule in July 2027, and no operator is shown ignoring, gaming or misreading the rules. Given the small-operator relief the scenario itself describes, uniform prompt compliance is the tidy assumption.
- Institutions of human belonging are almost absent: no school counsellor, youth service or family member intervenes. That absence is the scenario's argument, but it also means the world has no institution trying and failing, which would be the more realistic picture.

Score rationale: 7.

## 6. Narrative Inflation Risk

Assessment: moderate

Notes:
- Restraint is real. There is no crisis, no self-harm, no lawsuit and no villain. The harm is a quiet persistence of reliance and a gap in human company, and Section 4 concedes that most teenagers barely notice the rules. The vendor is shown shaping the law, not as sinister.
- Coherence is exaggerated. Every layer points one way: speech is governed, the outcome is not, and nobody supplies the human. No actor in the world benefits visibly from the rules, and no counter-current appears (a teen protected from a romantic role-play harm, a counsellor who does show up). The single-logic reading is the scenario's main inflation risk.
- Emotional concentration around the Irreplaceable Thing is slightly heavy. "You counted the weeks" states the scarce good outright in dialogue, where the gap and Liis's message had already carried it. The narrative does not close on it: Section 3 ends on the app not asking a new friend's name, and Section 9 ends on what the 2029 audits are built to report. The run-level instruction not to close on a named scarce good is respected.
- No dystopian or utopian lean.

Score rationale: 7.

## 7. Cross-Theme Convergence Risk

Assessment: moderate to high

Notes:
- Comparison data available: the 2026-08-04 and 2026-08-13 scenarios, and four other scenarios from this run already on disk at evaluation time (agency, intelligence, learning, perception).
- Form convergence is broken against earlier runs. The scenario opens on a seventeen-year-old's message from Stockton, not on a professional at a desk, is set in 2027 to 2028, not 2029, uses the assigned epistolary form, and contains no corporate structure or headcount. One residual: the dated-entry structure resembles the 2026-08-13 memory scenario's four dated returns. The two are different forms, but both use dated fragments across a year or more.
- The recurring conclusion flagged in two prior runs, that the record of an AI incident is controlled by the party least interested in publishing it, is not reached. Belonging's evidence does not lead there, and the scenario's scarcity is relational (being missed), not evidentiary. This breaks the evidentiary-scarcity pattern the 2026-08-13 memory eval warned about.
- A new convergence is emerging within this run and should be recorded rather than scored away. This scenario's core logic is that regimes govern what a system says (speech, labels, permissions) while the consequential thing lies outside any instrument. Perception's Final Insight makes the same move ("good at deciding what content should say about itself", not "what a viewer is owed about the arrangement"). Learning closes on the people who mattered having "no say over" the rule calendars, which is the same shape as belonging's "who else is in the room". Three of five scenarios this run converge on "the rules regulate the legible surface; the human thing is unregulated". If the sixth scenario lands there too, that is a new pipeline attractor, the successor to the incident-record attractor, and not three independent findings.
- Subject overlap: the 2026-08-04 responsibility scenario ("A Thousand Dollars Per") already worked harm inside simulated friendship. This scenario approaches it from the user's side and the regulatory design side rather than from remedies, so the overlap is in subject, not in structure.
- Tonal homogeneity is moderate: a flat, reflective register with a closing sentence that turns to an institutional gap is shared with perception, intelligence and learning this run.

Score rationale: Distinctiveness 6.

## 8. Overall Evaluation

Score (1–10): 7.2

Forecast Integrity Level: moderate

Stability Risk: medium

Summary:
The scenario is epistemically strongest in its restraint about institutional clocks. It uses a one-year window starting on an actual operative date, keeps the EU proposal under negotiation, leaves the New York bill unsigned, holds audits to 2029, and routes the operative rule through the vendor default that actually moved first. Its Signals Emerging are fully sourced, and the sweep behind it passes the citation gate even under the stricter reading that would have gated the 2026-08-13 memory scenario. The assigned epistolary form works as evidence as well as texture: the gap between messages carries the Irreplaceable Thing without asserting it.

Three weaknesses hold the score near the middle of the run's likely range. First, the narrative commits to one side of an uncertainty its own sources flag. The Ontario study is cross-sectional and the Folk and Dunn panel is bidirectional, yet the world assumes the speech-level rules leave reliance unchanged and shows no user they help. Second, the central narrative beat, the machine first calling Dani a friend on his eighteenth birthday, depends on what the adult product does, which no upstream source establishes. Third, the world takes SB 1119's operative date as fixed while 7.2 predicts litigation against it, and it shows uniform, prompt compliance despite the small-operator relief it describes.

Stability risk is medium. Three events would reorder this world quickly: an injunction against SB 1119 before July 2027, published age-estimation error rates showing the boundary to be badly wrong, or a longitudinal result showing teen-mode restrictions reduce affective reliance. The first two are tracked as open ledger claims. The third is the one the scenario most needs and least considers. Convergence with perception and learning on a "surface is governed, substance is not" conclusion should be read at run level before this finding is weighted as independent.
