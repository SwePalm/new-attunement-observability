## 3. Institutional Realism Audit

Assessment: **moderate realism**

Notes:

- Friction is placed where the evidence puts it. The district misses the July policy date and adopts in October, which matches the grading record (Maryland's 120-day window had two sources disagreeing on the deadline, and Florida's own rule moved on its own clock). The instructor's rule changes after an appeal in another section, which is the kind of adjacent-channel move the grading file observed when Florida's college rule came from a state board rather than a university system. The committee in May defers rather than settles.
- Power asymmetry is visible and specific: the vendor's default persists without anyone deciding; the parent carries a decision without information; the student carries the gaps. The teacher is sympathetic and also not in control of her own page two.
- The world is too tidy in a recognisable way. Each term delivers one clean illustration of one layer (August the consent form and the device split, January the syllabus rewrite and precedence ambiguity, May the committee), and no layer is shown doing anything unexpected. Real school years are noisier: no sick days, no substitute teacher with different rules, no grade actually disputed on AI grounds for the protagonist.
- There is no counter-instance in which the tool helps a learner or in which the opted-in student is better off. The evidence base (0.06 to 0.08 SD, similar to non-AI practice) supports "no clear benefit" rather than "no benefit", and the scenario leans slightly toward the second by showing only the costs of permission instability and none of the gains of access. The Sydney and UNSW all-student access half of the evidence appears only as an older-sibling aside in Section 4.
- The dual-enrollment ambiguity may be less real than depicted. If Florida's articulation framework already settles precedence, the counselor-versus-district exchange is an artifact of this run's retrieval rather than of the world.

## 6. Narrative Inflation Risk

Assessment: **low to moderate**

Notes:

- The tone is neither dystopian nor utopian. No one is harmed, the essay gets a B-plus, and the institutional failures are small and procedural.
- Complexity is collapsed into one dominant logic, layering without precedence, and every beat serves it. That is a legitimate thesis for this evidence, but it leaves no room for a second mechanism (for example, that some students do learn more with access, or that parental opt-out works well in some rooms).
- Emotional concentration around the scarce good is moderate. The library scene is quiet and unforced, but it carries a small heroic charge (the father finishing his route early, the two opted-out students finding each other) that the evidence does not require, and the final paragraph restates the thesis in abstract terms ("four calendars", "a fifth calendar") after the concrete scene has already made it.
- Section 5 opens with a sentence of the form "the world holds because each layer is individually coherent", and Section 9 says the rules "were written to protect that calendar and mostly interrupted it". Both are variants of the reasonable-actors-produce-a-structural-outcome move that earlier evaluations recorded as a closing tic. It is softer here than in the 2026-08 corpus, and it is still present.

The previous runs' attractor was an unobtainable record; this run's appears to be an unmediated human, and if the remaining 2026-09-25 scenarios land there too, it is a pipeline-level attractor rather than independent findings.

## 8. Overall Evaluation

Score (1–10): 7.5

Forecast Integrity Level: moderate

Stability Risk: medium

Summary:
The scenario's epistemic strength is that its world requires almost nothing new. Every instrument in it was adopted, announced or shipped between August and September 2026, and the narrative only asks that they take effect roughly as written and collide in one household in the first school year to which all of them apply. The date is chosen for that reason, the calibration restraints are visibly applied (a late district policy, an adjacent actor setting the operative rule, configuration leading statute), and the signals are stated at the confidence the evidence supports, including the Khanmigo trial's working-paper status.

Three weaknesses hold the score below 8. First, the load-bearing ambiguity, which of two Florida rules governs a dual-enrollment course, is not in this run's evidence and was not checked; the scenario states the absence of a precedence rule as fact. If an articulation agreement already settles it, the January movement's institutional premise weakens. Second, signal selection is one-sided: the restriction half of the sweep is lived, the expansion half (all-student university access) and the strongest reversal evidence (Korea) are not, so the world shows the costs of permission instability without testing whether access helps anyone. Third, distinctiveness is compromised at the level that matters most: the emotional scarcity converges with this run's intelligence, belonging and perception chains on unmediated human co-presence, and the pre-revision draft duplicated intelligence's central scene outright.

Stability risk is medium. Three scheduled or near-scheduled events could reshape the premise quickly: NYC's and LAUSD's 2027-28 decisions, a Google change to Gemini's default for minors (ledger claim learning-2026-09-02), and publication of the Eedi and DeepMind trial results, which could give rules an outcome basis the scenario assumes they lack.
