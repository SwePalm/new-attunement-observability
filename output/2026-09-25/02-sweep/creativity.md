# Evidence sweep, creativity, 2026-09-25

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The 43-day window (2026-08-13 to 2026-09-25) was dominated by music-AI litigation escalating in parallel with licensing. Four new US suits were filed: Round Hill Music against Suno and Anthropic (17 Aug 2026), a right-of-publicity class action led by Jason Isbell against Suno (31 Aug 2026), UMG against DistroKid (15 Sep 2026) and a second UMG and Sony Music suit against Suno aimed at its v6 models (18 Sep 2026). The previous sweep recorded no new suits (source: https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/round-hill-sues-suno-and-anthropic-for-up-to-1bn-apiece-it-isnt-looking-to-settle/, Aug 2026) (source: https://thenextweb.com/news/universal-sony-second-lawsuit-suno-60202-recordings, Sep 2026)
- Suno executed the transition logged as creativity-2026-08-10. It signed Believe and TuneCore on 8 Sep 2026 and launched v6 on 9 Sep 2026 with Warner, BMG and Believe as partners, and UMG and Sony sued over v6 nine days later. Licensing a subset of rights holders did not end litigation from the others (source: https://suno.com/blog/introducing-v6, Sep 2026) (source: https://musically.com/2026/09/21/umg-and-sony-music-file-new-suno-complaint-over-v6-models/, Sep 2026)
- The licensing channel widened beyond Suno. UMG signed ElevenLabs (10 Sep 2026), and all three majors took equity in Stability AI (25 Aug 2026). None of these deals disclosed its consideration, so creativity-2026-07-01 and creativity-2026-08-11 remain unscorable on their value and allocation observables (source: https://slator.com/elevenlabs-umg-licensed-ai-music-deal/, Sep 2026) (source: https://thenextweb.com/news/stability-ai-76m-funding-universal-warner-sony-music-ea, Aug 2026)
- Film and TV re-entered the corpus. The Disney and OpenAI deal fell apart after Sora was shut down. Google has approached Disney, Universal and Warner Bros. Discovery about licensing IP for AI, but no agreement has been reached (source: https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/hollywood-studios-sued-over-ai-100000457.html, Aug 2026)

Confirmed Developments:
- Round Hill Music filed separate complaints against Suno (with Bright Data) and against Anthropic in the Northern District of California on 17 Aug 2026. Each complaint pleads direct infringement, DMCA circumvention of access controls and removal of copyright management information over 500 bellwether works, with damages said potentially to approach or exceed $1B per defendant, and the CEO stated no intent to settle (source: https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/round-hill-sues-suno-and-anthropic-for-up-to-1bn-apiece-it-isnt-looking-to-settle/, Aug 2026) (source: https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/08/17/round-hill-suno-lawsuit-anthropic/, Aug 2026)
- ARIA updated its Charts Code of Practice on 25 Aug 2026, effective from the chart dated 31 Aug 2026. Wholly AI-generated recordings are ineligible, and an AI-developed recording qualifies only if it is substantially human made (humans wrote the song and performed the lead vocal and primary instruments). The change applies the IFPI principles after an AI-assisted Madonna cover by Josh Fawaz reached number 2 on the overall chart (source: https://www.aria.com.au/charts/news/aria-charts-set-eligibility-rules-for-recordings-made-with-ai, Aug 2026) (source: https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c20vl4vm2pno, Aug 2026)
- Jason Isbell, David Lowery, Guy Forsyth and Eduardo Calle filed a proposed class action against Suno in the US District Court for the District of Massachusetts on 31 Aug 2026. It alleges that Suno lets users invoke musicians by name and profits from their identities without consent or compensation. Suno called the claims without merit and said it blocks prompts for specific artists' names (source: https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/music/2026/09/02/jason-isbell-suno-lawsuit/91576102007/, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.pastemagazine.com/music/jason-isbell/jason-isbell-files-class-action-lawsuit-against-ai-company-for-using-musicians-likeness, Sep 2026)
- Suno and Believe announced an opt-in, global partnership on 8 Sep 2026. Music from participating Believe and TuneCore artists and labels will be included in Suno's licensed models, a reversal by a distributor that had been blocking Suno-made tracks about four months earlier (source: https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/music/music-industry-news/suno-announces-partnership-with-tunecore-believe-1236694338/, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/from-elevenlabs-11b-umg-deal-to-hybe-weverses-data-breach-its-mbws-weekly-round-up/, Sep 2026)
- Suno launched the v6 model family (v6, v6-wild, v6-mini) on 9 Sep 2026. It described the models as developed with Warner Music Group, BMG and Believe and stated that it will retire its previous models and move entirely onto v6 (source: https://suno.com/blog/introducing-v6, Sep 2026) (source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/suno-replaces-its-ai-models-with-a-new-one-trained-on-licensed-music-as-copyright-suits-pile-up/, Sep 2026)
- Universal Music Group and ElevenLabs announced a multi-year licensing agreement on 10 Sep 2026, ElevenLabs' first with a major label. The first product is a fan platform, still in development, for remixes, mashups and personalised vocals using participating artists' music. No launch date, commercial terms, participating artists or royalty structure were disclosed (source: https://elevenlabs.io/blog/umg, Sep 2026) (source: https://slator.com/elevenlabs-umg-licensed-ai-music-deal/, Sep 2026)
- UMG sued DistroKid in the US District Court for the District of Delaware on 15 Sep 2026, alleging deceptive trade practices and copyright infringement. The complaint names 1,000 recordings, alleges DistroKid is flooding streaming services with AI-generated "slop" (including accounts releasing hundreds of Suno-made tracks a month) and says DistroKid delivered over half of all tracks to one major service in six months (source: https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/universal-music-group-sues-distrokid-ai-generated-slop/, Sep 2026) (source: https://musically.com/2026/09/16/umg-sues-distrokid-alleging-deceptive-practices-and-copyright-infringement/, Sep 2026)
- UMG and Sony Music filed a second suit against Suno in the District of Massachusetts on 18 Sep 2026 (1:26-cv-14275), asserting 60,202 recordings not in the 2024 case after the court denied leave to amend on 18 Aug 2026. The complaint pleads infringement and section 1201(a) circumvention (YouTube stream ripping) and alleges that v6, trained partly on user interactions with prior models, is "the fruit of the same poisoned tree"; Warner is not a plaintiff (source: https://thenextweb.com/news/universal-sony-second-lawsuit-suno-60202-recordings, Sep 2026) (source: https://musically.com/2026/09/21/umg-and-sony-music-file-new-suno-complaint-over-v6-models/, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.lefigaro.fr/medias/un-vol-massif-accusee-d-utiliser-leurs-titres-pour-creer-des-chansons-par-ia-la-plateforme-suno-attaquee-par-universal-et-sony-20260919, Sep 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Google executives have approached Disney, Universal, Warner Bros. Discovery and other studios about licensing IP for AI models and character outputs, with a reported average of about $40M per character and a possible YouTube advertising revenue share. No agreement has been reached, and NBCUniversal said it is not in active conversations (source: https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/hollywood-studios-sued-over-ai-100000457.html, Aug 2026) (source: https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/417634/googles-ai-needs-throw-billions-to-hollywood-for.html, Sep 2026)
- Model lineage is becoming a liability theory. The UMG and Sony complaint argues that a model trained on licensed data plus preference signals and synthetic outputs from earlier unlicensed models still infringes, which would stop licensing from resetting liability for any lab that retrains on its own prior outputs (source: https://musically.com/2026/09/21/umg-and-sony-music-file-new-suno-complaint-over-v6-models/, Sep 2026)
- Music-AI litigation is spreading from copyright to identity. The Isbell class action rests on artists' names and identities rather than on training copies, which puts it outside any label or publisher licence (source: https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/music/2026/09/02/jason-isbell-suno-lawsuit/91576102007/, Sep 2026)
- Distributors are becoming the next enforcement choke point after generators and streaming services. UMG's DistroKid complaint argues that Content ID conflicts give the distributor recording-level knowledge it fails to act on across other services (source: https://musically.com/2026/09/16/umg-sues-distrokid-alleging-deceptive-practices-and-copyright-infringement/, Sep 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- Licensing is not buying peace. Suno shipped v6 on licensed catalogues from Warner, BMG and Believe, and within nine days the two largest majors sued again, citing those same three deals as proof that a functioning training-licence market exists that Suno bypassed (source: https://thenextweb.com/news/universal-sony-second-lawsuit-suno-60202-recordings, Sep 2026)
- The large film-IP licensing template has not held. The Disney and OpenAI character deal fell apart once OpenAI shut down Sora, and Google has no signed studio agreement, so the $100M+ franchise-deal baseline behind creativity-2026-07-01 currently rests on talks, not transactions (source: https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/hollywood-studios-sued-over-ai-100000457.html, Aug 2026)

Technical Changes:
- Suno's v6 family (v6, v6-wild, v6-mini) replaces all prior models. Suno says v6 was trained on partner-licensed content plus "interactions including creations and preference signals from our community", and that artist-name requests are now translated into musical qualities rather than blocked (source: https://suno.com/blog/introducing-v6, Sep 2026) (source: https://musically.com/2026/09/22/suno-hits-back-at-fundamentally-flawed-umg-and-sony-music-claims/, Sep 2026)

Contradictions:
- Suno told The Tennessean in early Sep 2026 that it blocks prompts for specific artists' names. The UMG and Sony complaint of 18 Sep 2026 says the block was switched off, and Suno's own 22 Sep 2026 statement says artist requests are "translated" into musical qualities. The company's public descriptions of the same control are inconsistent.
- UMG is licensing ElevenLabs, holding equity in Stability AI and suing Suno and DistroKid in the same five weeks. Sony is an investor in Stability while co-suing Suno. The majors are pricing AI access bilaterally and litigating against the actors that give them no stake, not taking a uniform position on generative music.
- The Next Web dates Believe's signing to 8 Sep 2026 and says v6 launched "nine days later", while Suno's blog dates v6 to 9 Sep 2026. The nine-day interval matches the gap to the 18 Sep complaint, so the secondary account appears to be in error. The dates here follow the primary source.
- Source contamination caught: Music Business Worldwide's round-up headline "ElevenLabs' $11B UMG deal" refers to ElevenLabs' valuation, not the deal's value. Read from the title alone, it would wrongly satisfy the $100M+ threshold in creativity-2026-07-01. Also not used, as unverified claims circulating on low-quality aggregator posts (note.com): "Suno hits $470 million annual revenue" and a "$1.4 trillion lawsuit".
- DistroKid's scale is reported two ways: roughly 40% of all new music globally (company figure, per MBW and RAIN) versus over half of all tracks delivered to one unnamed major service over six months (UMG complaint, per Music Ally). These are different measures, not a conflict of fact.
