# Evidence sweep, intelligence, 2026-09-25

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The 2026-08-13 sweep recorded one frontier release in nine days; this 43-day window carried six frontier-class releases from four labs (Claude Fable 5.1 on 1 Sep, GPT-6 Astra on 3 Sep, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on 10 Sep, Grok 4.7 on 21 Sep, Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Sol/Luna on 22 Sep 2026) (source: https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/01/anthropic-claude-fable-5-1/, Sep 2026) (source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/openai-launches-gpt-6-sol-and-luna/, Sep 2026)
- The capability measure the last sweep called static moved furthest: ARC-AGI-3's verified top score went from Claude Opus 5 at 30.2% to GPT-6 Astra at 62.7% (Standard harness) and 99.9% (Provider Adapter harness) on 3 Sep 2026, which confirmed intelligence-2026-08-01 in Phase A (source: https://arcprize.org/blog/astra, Sep 2026)
- New since the ledger's last claims: the frontier labs themselves became the main source of AI-R&D-automation measurement. OpenAI declared its "automated research intern" goal met on 6 Sep 2026, and Anthropic published a first R&D automation measure (Claude "leads" 26% of its AI R&D, as of Aug 2026) about 17 Sep 2026. Public time-horizon measurement did not keep up: METR's page is still dated 8 May 2026 (source: https://www.engadget.com/2251859/openai-says-it-reached-its-goal-of-creating-an-automated-research-intern/, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.unite.ai/anthropic-says-claude-leads-26-of-its-ai-research-and-development/, Sep 2026)
- New policy axis: the 12 Sep 2026 "We Must Pace the Frontier" essay and rival CEOs' endorsements of it turned capability velocity into an explicit policy and litigation object. The President rejected it on 14 Sep, and a Sherman Act class action followed on 18 Sep 2026. None of the four institutional ledger observables (intelligence-2026-08-02, -08-03, -08-04, -08-07) moved (source: https://www.aa.com.tr/en/science-technology/musk-altman-hassabis-back-amodei-s-call-to-slow-pace-of-ai-development/4055591, Sep 2026) (source: https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/openai-anthropic-google-spacexai-hit-with-antitrust-lawsuit, Sep 2026)

Confirmed Developments:
- Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1 (generally available) and Mythos 5.1 (restricted to U.S. entities in trusted access programs) on 1 Sep 2026. Artificial Analysis measured Fable 5.1 (max) at 66 on its Intelligence Index, "the highest score we have measured", with HLE at 59.1% against a previous best of 55.5% (source: https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/claude-fable-5-1, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/01/anthropic-claude-fable-5-1/, Sep 2026)
- OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra on 3 Sep 2026, first to members of its cybersecurity-focused Daybreak program because the model "meets the Critical threshold in cybersecurity under our Preparedness Framework", then to paid ChatGPT tiers. ARC Prize verified it at 62.7% on ARC-AGI-3 Semi-Private under the Standard harness ($26K) and 99.9% under the Provider Adapter harness ($19K) (source: https://arcprize.org/blog/astra, Sep 2026) (source: https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/04/openai-releasing-major-upgrade-to-chatgpt-and-codex-with-gpt-6-astra-details-here/, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/4/openai-unveils-gpt-6-astra-amid-rising-scrutiny-and-safety, Sep 2026)
- On 22 Sep 2026 Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5, which it says performs at Fable 5.1 level on most work at 40% lower cost than Opus 5 ($4/$20 per million tokens). About 90 minutes later OpenAI released GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, which it says reach Astra-level reliability at half the 5.6-series price (source: https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/22/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-opus-5-5-model-details-here/, Sep 2026) (source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/openai-launches-gpt-6-sol-and-luna/, Sep 2026)
- DeepSeek released the open-weights DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on 10 Sep 2026 under an MIT licence: a 552B mixture-of-experts model with 8B active parameters for input and 16B for output, native vision, and 74.2 on DeepSWE v1.1 (Opus 5: 74.0, GPT-5.6 Sol: 73.0) but 30.0 on Terminal-Bench (Opus 5: 43.3). V4 Pro was shut down on 14 Sep 2026 and its traffic rerouted to V4.1-Flash. Chinese-language coverage: Tencent News, 10 Sep 2026 (source: https://www.deepseek.com/en/news/deepseek-v4-1-flash/, Sep 2026) (source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/deepseek-v4-1-flash-debuts-with-0-003-1m-off-peak-cached-input-rate-and-benchmarks-eclipsing-gpt-5-6-sol-claude-opus-5, Sep 2026) (source: https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260910A04UP100, Sep 2026)
- OpenAI announced on 6 Sep 2026 that it had met its goal of an "automated research intern", defined as a system that carries out well-defined research tasks under human direction, including tasks that would take a skilled researcher a few days. It reported 3.1 agent-workdays of effort per human workday in its research organization and median researcher inference use above $600 per day, and it set "automated AI researcher" as the next target, for March 2028 (source: https://www.engadget.com/2251859/openai-says-it-reached-its-goal-of-creating-an-automated-research-intern/, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/07/openai-research-automation-intern/, Sep 2026)
- Anthropic published its first R&D automation measurement around 17 Sep 2026. Using Epoch AI's automation-level scale, Claude "leads" (AL4) 26% of Anthropic's AI R&D work as of Aug 2026, up from under 1% in Feb 2026, with no fully autonomous (AL5) subset. Other figures: about 30,000 internal agents, 100% online-monitor coverage, a 0.002% block rate, and 6% of AI R&D compute allocated to safety (source: https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.unite.ai/anthropic-says-claude-leads-26-of-its-ai-research-and-development/, Sep 2026)
- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published "We Must Pace the Frontier" on 12 Sep 2026, proposing a deliberately slower pace of capability gains and independent evaluators with access inside labs. Sam Altman, Elon Musk and Demis Hassabis publicly endorsed it within a day, and Anthropic called Opus 5.5 "our first release since we called for pacing the frontier" (source: https://www.aa.com.tr/en/science-technology/musk-altman-hassabis-back-amodei-s-call-to-slow-pace-of-ai-development/4055591, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5, Sep 2026)
- President Trump rejected the pacing call on 14 Sep 2026 ("whoever wins AI wins") and called AI safety warnings a "hoax". White House AI czar David Sacks said labs could slow down voluntarily without government mandates (source: https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/09/trump-redoubles-pro-ai-stance-amid-calls-slow-development/415981/, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/article/trump-responds-to-call-by-ceos-of-anthropic-openai-and-xai-to-slow-ai-down-whoever-wins-ai-wins-182008851.html, Sep 2026)
- Four subscribers sued Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google in the Northern District of California on 18 Sep 2026 (Buist v. Anthropic PBC, No. 3:26-cv-10693). They allege a Sherman Act Section 1 output-restricting agreement to slow capability growth, with the pacing essay and the endorsements as the centerpiece, and seek class certification, an injunction and damages (source: https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/openai-anthropic-google-spacexai-hit-with-antitrust-lawsuit, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/09/311731-anthropic-openai-spacexai-and-google-face-antitrust-suit-over-alleged-pact-to-slow-ai-development/, Sep 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- METR's predeployment summary of Claude Opus 5.5 (22 Sep 2026) reports that a separate elevated-access METR team estimated "~1.5X overall acceleration in capabilities due to AI" during development, with roughly 30% probability of 2X. This is the first third-party estimate of AI-driven self-acceleration attached to a frontier release (source: https://metr.org/blog/2026-09-22-claude-opus-5-5/, Sep 2026)
- Capital is now pricing recursive self-improvement as a standalone product category: Mirendil, founded by former Anthropic researchers, is in talks to raise up to $1B at a $5B valuation led by Kleiner Perkins, three months after a $1B-valuation seed, and plans a frontier model by early 2027 (source: https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/ex-anthropic-staffers-self-improving-161648217.html, Sep 2026)
- Harness choice is becoming a first-order variable in capability measurement: ARC Prize now reports a neutral Standard harness and a lab-specific Provider Adapter harness separately, and they differ by 37 points on the same model (source: https://arcprize.org/blog/astra, Sep 2026)
- The US-to-PRC capability gap now has a government-published number: CAISI estimates the open-weights GLM-5.3 lags the US frontier by about four months on an aggregate of its cyber benchmarks, while calling it the most cyber-capable open-weight model released to date (source: https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/09/caisis-assessment-zais-glm-53-cyber-capabilities, Sep 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- METR judges Claude Opus 5.5 "unlikely to be able to fully automate AI R&D", citing weak researcher judgment and long-horizon reasoning, and describes it as only a modest improvement over Fable 5.1. That cuts against the automated-research framing in the lab announcements (source: https://metr.org/blog/2026-09-22-claude-opus-5-5/, Sep 2026)
- Headline capability numbers are basis-dependent: after Artificial Analysis moved to Intelligence Index v4.3 on 7 Sep 2026, Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra score 53 (against Fable 5.1's 66 six days earlier), so a month of "record" scores is partly an artifact of index version (source: https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v4-3, Sep 2026)
- Expert reaction to GPT-6 Astra stressed unevenness rather than generality. Toby Walsh said intelligence in AI "is still today very jagged" (source: https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/4/openai-unveils-gpt-6-astra-amid-rising-scrutiny-and-safety, Sep 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- The European Commission confirmed at its 1 Sep 2026 midday briefing that the AI Office had sent its first AI Act enforcement requests for information to more than 30 AI companies, on safety and security, copyright and transparency. It did not name the recipients (source: https://allegiance.law/news/eu-ai-act-enforcement-has-started-commission-sends-first-information-requests-to-ai-companies/, Sep 2026) (source: https://thenextweb.com/news/virkkunen-us-guardrails-inevitable, Sep 2026)
- NIST CAISI published an assessment of Z.ai's GLM-5.3 cyber capabilities on 17 Sep 2026. It is the fourth CAISI model assessment of 2026, and all four evaluate PRC models; none evaluates a US developer's model, and the publication does not mention EO 14409 (source: https://www.nist.gov/caisi, Sep 2026)

Technical Changes:
- Artificial Analysis released Intelligence Index v4.3 on 7 Sep 2026. It upgrades Terminal-Bench 2.1 to 4.0, replaces tau3-Banking with AutomationBench-AA (657 held-out tasks, with Zapier) and raises private-test-set weighting from 40% to 45%, as a partial step toward Index v5 (source: https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v4-3, Sep 2026)
- On the live Index v4.3.2, Claude Opus 5.5 (max effort, default fallback) is the top model at 58, followed by its own xhigh (56) and high (54) configurations (source: https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index, Sep 2026)
- SpaceXAI released Grok 4.7 on 21 Sep 2026 on a new, larger base model with longer reinforcement learning weighted toward multi-hour tasks, at unchanged $2/$6 per million token pricing, trailing GPT-6 Astra on EEBench chip-design tasks (source: https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/21/spacex-launches-grok-4-7-with-long-horizon-processing-safety-upgrades/, Sep 2026)
- Z.ai released GLM-5.3 on 14 Aug 2026 on the same 743B base as GLM-5.2 with improved post-training (Terminal-Bench 3.0 from 4.6 to 28.3, ExploitBench from 24.4% to 54.4%), with open weights promised about two weeks later after safety hardening (source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/glm-5-3-is-here-with-advanced-cyber-capabilities-and-reportedly-already-found-a-serious-vulnerability-in-cursor, Aug 2026)

Contradictions:
- Frontier labs endorsed "pacing" on 12-13 Sep 2026, and within ten days the same labs shipped Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol/Luna and Grok 4.7. Anthropic presents Opus 5.5 as consistent with pacing, while the antitrust complaint treats the endorsements as evidence of an output cartel. The same statements are read as both restraint and collusion.
- Date of the first AI Act information requests: some secondary coverage attributes the announcement to 29 Aug 2026, while law-firm and press coverage cites the Commission's 1 Sep 2026 midday briefing. Unverified claim circulating: an aggregator (winzheng.com) names OpenAI, Anthropic and Google as recipients. The Commission did not name recipients, and CALIBRATION.md (CAL-005) records this attribution as contamination. It is not used here.
- OpenAI's 3.1 agent-workdays per human workday figure is dated "mid-August" in one secondary summary and "as of June 2026" by Help Net Security. The value is reported here without a reference month.
- Anthropic's baseline for the 26% figure is "under 1%" in Feb 2026 per Unite.AI, while several headlines say "1% in March". Only the 26% (Aug 2026) figure is used.
- Source contamination caught: a techjuice.pk item dated 17 Sep 2026 says "OpenAI Files Confidential IPO", but the confidential filing was reported on 8 Jun 2026. It is stale content re-dated and is not used.
- ARC Prize lists two verified ARC-AGI-3 scores for the same model 37 points apart (62.7% Standard, 99.9% Provider Adapter), so "ARC-AGI-3 is near saturation" and "ARC-AGI-3 is two-thirds solved" are both accurate readings of one page.
