# Evidence sweep, truth, 2026-09-25

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The largest movement in 43 days came from outside every open claim and outside the Anglophone corpus. It came through statistical detection, not watermarks. On 21 Sep 2026 an anonymous X account ("Balance ton Claude") posted Pangram scans of Thélyson Orélien's Grasset novel "C'était ça ou mourir". Plagiarism findings in his 2010s texts followed. On 25 Sep 2026 the Académie Goncourt withdrew the novel from its first selection, citing both the plagiarism and "convergent analyses" that the book is "very largely the product of an artificial intelligence" (source: https://www.guadeloupe.franceantilles.fr/actualite/culture/rentree-litteraire-le-goncourt-ecarte-thelyson-orelien-apres-les-soupcons-de-plagiat-et-dia-1094111.php, Sep 2026)
- The vendor watermark route widened, and it did not reverse. Anthropic's first post-2 Aug 2026 models (Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1, 1 Sep 2026) shipped with text watermarks. The detection API opened in private preview to eligible organizations. Text marking reached Claude Opus 5 on cloud partner platforms from 14 Sep 2026 (source: https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark, Sep 2026) (source: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content, Sep 2026)
- California moved legislatively rather than through enforcement. SB 1050 (synthetic performer disclosure in ads) was signed on 16 Sep 2026. SB 1000 (drops the SB 942 user threshold and the visible-disclosure duty) and AB 2713 (a platform provenance-inspection interface) were still awaiting the Governor, who has until 30 Sep 2026 (source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billHistoryClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB1000, Sep 2026)
- Still no Article 50 enforcement step by any EU national authority, no SB 942 civil action, and no banking mandate for out-of-band verification. This matches the 2026-09-25 grading pass, where all 10 live truth claims stayed open (source: https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260911-full-transparency-organizations-sign-eu-code, Sep 2026)

Confirmed Developments:
- Governor Newsom signed SB 1050 (Ashby) on 16 Sep 2026, chaptered as Chapter 246, Statutes of 2026. It requires a clear and conspicuous disclosure on any advertisement that uses an AI-generated synthetic performer, and it bars continued use of a violating ad. SAG-AFTRA sponsored the bill (source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billHistoryClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB1050, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/16/governor-newsom-signs-new-law-to-protect-workers-require-disclosures-on-ai-generated-advertising/, Sep 2026)
- On 1 Sep 2026 Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1 (generally available) and Claude Mythos 5.1 (trusted access program), the first models launched after the 2 Aug 2026 date, with text watermarking from launch. On the same date it updated its watermark post to open a detection API in private preview for regulators, law enforcement, media, fact-checkers, independent researchers and other eligible organizations (source: https://www.pcworld.com/article/3224834/claudes-updated-fable-and-mythos-models-watermark-their-replies.html, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark, Sep 2026)
- On 25 Sep 2026 the Académie Goncourt removed Thélyson Orélien's "C'était ça ou mourir" (Grasset) from its first selection for the 2026 prize. It cited plagiarism in the author's earlier texts and the "convergent result of several analyses by credible researchers and journalists" that the novel is very largely AI-generated. It said the aim was to send "a clear message" that it does not endorse AI-generated texts (source: https://www.guadeloupe.franceantilles.fr/actualite/culture/rentree-litteraire-le-goncourt-ecarte-thelyson-orelien-apres-les-soupcons-de-plagiat-et-dia-1094111.php, Sep 2026) (source: https://next.ink/brief-article/lacademie-goncourt-se-positionne-vite-et-maladroitement-contre-lia-generative/, Sep 2026)
- Actors outside the Goncourt moved first, within about 24 hours. On 24 Sep 2026 the novel's Quebec publisher, Éditions du Boréal, suspended its promotional activities, saying it could not yet confirm or refute the allegations. The same day, the Salon du livre de l'Estrie withdrew Orélien's guest-of-honour title, citing the plagiarism findings rather than the AI allegations (source: https://www.lesoleil.com/arts/livres/2026/09/24/le-boreal-suspend-les-activites-de-promotion-du-livre-de-thelyson-orelien-Y42HPANVN5BNVEV7KHGRUATETY/, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/thelyson-orelien-la-polemique-bouleverse-le-monde-du-livre, Sep 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Private juries are applying detection verdicts before any public authority does. On 25 Sep 2026 the jury of the new prix Strasbourg met in emergency session and gave the Strasbourg Espoir CIC prize to another author, Amandine Agic, instead of Orélien. As of that day the novel was still on the first lists of the Médicis and Femina (source: https://www.dna.fr/culture-loisirs/2026/09/25/accuse-d-avoir-utilise-l-ia-et-de-plagiat-thelyson-orelien-suspendu-du-prix-strasbourg-espoir-cic, Sep 2026)
- The accusation spread before any institution had examined it. The anonymous account posted screenshots of Pangram results on 20 passages, the day the novel won the Prix du Roman Fnac, and the post drew close to 3 million views within hours (source: https://www.lopinion.fr/economie/quest-ce-que-pangram-le-detecteur-dia-qui-aurait-demasque-le-roman-de-thelyson-orelien, Sep 2026)
- Public broadcasters are running their own replication tests. Radio-Canada's Décrypteurs submitted the full text of the novel to Pangram and got 94 percent AI, while six other Goncourt-listed novels available digitally in Quebec and eight books by Haitian-origin authors scored 100 percent human. An SFU researcher who first benchmarked Pangram in 2024 put its current French-language false-positive rate at 0.0026 percent The same researcher said that changing a few words flips a passage to 100 percent human, because the tool trades higher false negatives for low false positives (source: https://ici.radio-canada.ca/rci/fr/nouvelle/2286476/thelyson-orelien-pangram-test-roman-ia, Sep 2026)
- Book-world institutions are drafting AI policies of their own. The Salon du livre de l'Estrie says it has been working on an AI-use policy since June 2026, in discussion with the Université de Sherbrooke and the Regroupement pour l'Art Humain (source: https://www.lesoleil.com/arts/livres/2026/09/24/le-boreal-suspend-les-activites-de-promotion-du-livre-de-thelyson-orelien-Y42HPANVN5BNVEV7KHGRUATETY/, Sep 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- Detector reliability is still contested. Corriere della Sera recalls a 2023 Stanford study of seven detectors (not including Pangram) that found a 61.3 percent average false-positive rate on essays by non-native English writers. It also reports that WSJ editor James Taranto called a Pangram-based study "a defamation machine", and that Pangram's CEO declined to say whether news outlets are clients (source: https://www.corriere.it/tecnologia/26_settembre_25/pangram-rilevatori-scrittura-ai-9a665af0-a067-4937-ba88-cffbf7fafxlk.shtml, Sep 2026)
- The Goncourt reversed its own evidentiary standard within 48 hours. Earlier that week, Académie president Philippe Claudel said the author should not be condemned immediately and that "irrefutable proof" would be needed. The withdrawal then rested on plagiarism in unrelated older texts plus detector-based analyses, a move Next, an independent French tech outlet, called quick and clumsy (source: https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/thelyson-orelien-la-polemique-bouleverse-le-monde-du-livre, Sep 2026) (source: https://next.ink/brief-article/lacademie-goncourt-se-positionne-vite-et-maladroitement-contre-lia-generative/, Sep 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- California SB 1000 would amend the AI Transparency Act (SB 942 as amended by AB 853). It would remove the 1,000,000 monthly user threshold from the definition of covered provider, drop the manifest (visible) disclosure duty and keep the latent (machine-readable) disclosure duty, with civil penalties of 5,000 dollars per violation per day. It passed as an urgency statute (Assembly 72-0 on 25 Aug, Senate concurrence 39-0 on 27 Aug 2026) and was presented to the Governor on 2 Sep 2026, with no action recorded at the time of this sweep (source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billHistoryClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB1000, Sep 2026)
- California AB 2713 would require large online platforms to provide an interface that shows whether content was generated or substantially altered by generative AI, or captured by a recording device, and would let users download its provenance data. It includes a safe harbour for provenance data that does not comply with widely adopted specifications. It was presented to the Governor on 8 Sep 2026 and was pending at the time of this sweep (source: https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/californias-2026-legislative-session-wraps-a-wave-of-privacy-and-ai-bills-reaches-the-governor-with-key-child-safety-and-ai-measures-signed-into-law, Sep 2026)
- Morrison Foerster's 11 Sep 2026 review puts the EU Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content at about 190 signatories by late July 2026, with Google, Anthropic, OpenAI and Synthesia among them. It says the 2 Dec 2026 compliance date for systems already on the market still stands (source: https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260911-full-transparency-organizations-sign-eu-code, Sep 2026)

Technical Changes:
- Lasso Security's "Provenance Tax" study (17 Sep 2026) tested SynthID-Text watermarking on seven open models. It measured an average 6.5 percent paired disagreement in tool-calling verdicts across 21 model-temperature combinations. For Gemma-3-27b, disagreement on refusals rose from 6.0 percent on bare harmful requests to 23.5 percent under prompt injection, and effects varied by watermark key (source: https://www.lasso.security/blog/the-provenance-tax-understanding-the-impact-of-llm-watermarking-on-ai-agent-behavior, Sep 2026) (source: https://www.unite.ai/lasso-study-finds-text-watermarking-shifts-llm-refusals-and-tool-calls/, Sep 2026)
- Anthropic's help center dates the gradual availability of text watermarking for Claude Opus 5 on cloud partner platforms (AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Foundry) from 14 Sep 2026. It keeps 2 Dec 2026 as the date by which all covered models should be marked, and lists no opt-out (source: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content, Sep 2026)
- Anthropic says its mark is a modified version of Google DeepMind's SynthID-Text approach. Light editing does not fully remove it, a complete rewrite does, and code carries little watermark signal because the model has limited freedom when producing functionally correct output (source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/15/anthropic-shares-more-details-about-how-claudes-new-watermarks-will-work/, Aug 2026)
- IEEE Spectrum reports that Google's Gemini text watermark is deployed and that OpenAI still plans one but has not implemented it. It quotes a researcher saying a roughly 20-word text would need 50 to 60 percent green-list words to be detected well, which puts short-form content outside practical watermark detection (source: https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-watermark-text-anthropic-openai, Sep 2026)
- On 14 Aug 2026 Google let Gemini and Flow users switch off the visible watermark on Nano Banana, Omni and Lyria outputs, keeping only SynthID and C2PA metadata (source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/google-will-now-allow-users-to-remove-visible-watermark-from-its-ai-generations/, Aug 2026)

Contradictions:
- The most consequential public authorship verdict of the period came from the detection route (Pangram, a statistical classifier with no mark to read), not the statutory watermark route. The accusing account was named "Balance ton Claude". Yet no third party outside Anthropic's eligible-organization private preview can check whether the text carries a Claude mark, and the novel's first manuscript is dated by its author to 2019.
- California is removing a visible AI-disclosure duty and adding one in the same month. SB 1000 would drop manifest disclosure for generative AI providers, while SB 1050, signed on 16 Sep 2026, mandates visible labels for synthetic performers in ads.
- Anthropic keeps marking global with no opt-out, while Google made its visible mark optional. Each describes its invisible layer as the transparency guarantee.
- The prize juries acted on a mix of plagiarism (verifiable by text comparison) and AI-authorship (probabilistic). The Salon du livre de l'Estrie said plagiarism alone drove its decision, while the Goncourt cited both. So the case does not isolate how much weight institutions give to detector output.
- Unverified claim circulating, not used: an MSN syndication dated 2 Sep 2026 is headlined "Anthropic's content checker tool is here, with one big catch". Its body could not be retrieved and its original publisher is unidentified.
- Unverified claim circulating, not used: a Daily Kos community post (5 Sep 2026) alleges that Pangram relied on a pirated book to flag a novel. It is user-generated and was not corroborated.
