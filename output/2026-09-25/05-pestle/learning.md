### Political
Core Shift Thesis:
Student AI use has become an electoral and executive position rather than a technical or curricular one, so its rules now change on mayoral, board-election and school-year cycles and are written as time-limited moratoria that force a renewal decision each year.
Forces:
1. One-year executive moratoria with scheduled sunsets: New York City's 2026-27 suspension of student-facing generative AI for grades 2-K to 8 and Los Angeles Unified's all-grade restriction on school devices for 2026-27 both require a fresh decision for 2027-28, with LAUSD already routing that decision to a committee (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).
2. Ballot-borne AI positions: 24 of 45 candidates for Chicago's first fully elected 21-member board (election 3 Nov 2026) pledged a three-year moratorium, so the first elected board takes office with a publicly stated AI position before any policy review (sweep, Emerging Signals, Sep 2026; ledger claim learning-2026-09-01).
3. State boards as the fastest formal actor: Florida's State Board moved its AI amendment from a proposed-rule notice (26 Aug 2026) to adoption (16 Sep 2026) in under a month, while the named university-system channel did not move (grading file, learning-2026-08-05 and learning-2026-07-03, Sep 2026).
4. National reversibility: South Korea's AI digital textbooks fell from 34.2 percent of schools to 5.2 percent after being downgraded to optional material, a demonstration that national mandates can be unwound within one political cycle (sweep, Counter-Signals, Sep 2026).

### Economic
Core Shift Thesis:
The market for AI in learning is splitting between institution-wide vendor partnerships in higher education and direct-to-family products that sell into the gap left by K-12 restrictions, while the evidence of learning return stays modest.
Forces:
1. All-student enterprise licences in higher education: the University of Sydney (14 Sep 2026) and UNSW (9 Sep 2026) committed to ChatGPT Edu for all students and staff, with student access from 2027 (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).
2. Direct-to-family exam tutoring: Medly AI raised 8 million dollars (Aug 2026) on more than 400,000 UK users for GCSE, A-Level and IB preparation, a channel that no school rule governs (sweep, Capital Movements, Aug 2026).
3. Modest measured return: the two-year Khanmigo cluster trial found 0.06 to 0.08 SD per year, similar to non-AI practice, so districts cannot yet justify licence costs on outcome grounds (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Aug 2026).
4. Contract-layer switching costs: NYC's implementation switches off AI components inside 38 existing edtech contracts, so every renewal cycle becomes a negotiation over which features a district is paying for and which it has disabled (sweep, Technical Changes, Sep 2026).

### Social
Core Shift Thesis:
The social unit of AI-in-learning governance is shifting from the school to the student's week and the family's kitchen table, as students move between rooms and devices governed by incompatible rules and parents are handed consent decisions they are poorly placed to make.
Forces:
1. Parental consent as the governing mechanism: Florida's amended Rule 6A-1.0957 requires parental notice and opt-out with non-AI alternatives, plus extra review for PreK-5, with district policies due 1 Jul 2027 (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).
2. Opposite sector norms inside one household: K-12 restriction in NYC and LAUSD and all-student university access at Sydney and UNSW were announced within the same fortnight (sweep, Contradictions, Sep 2026).
3. Human-centred tutoring as the evidenced model: Stanford's SCALE brief found strong evidence only where humans remain central, and one cited study found 40 to 47 percent of students never used the AI platform at all (sweep, Emerging Signals, Aug 2026).
4. Contested procurement: the NTEU and the Sydney SRC said neither was consulted before the OpenAI agreement was signed, turning access into an industrial and student-representation issue (sweep, Emerging Signals, Sep 2026).

### Technological
Core Shift Thesis:
Control over student AI access is being exercised through configuration (defaults, organizational units, per-contract feature switches) rather than through bans on whole platforms, so the rule a student meets depends on which device and which admin console they are under.
Forces:
1. Vendor default-on: Gemini in Google Classroom became available by default to students of all ages (web 10 Aug, mobile 17 Aug 2026), and restricting minors requires admins to build a separate organizational unit (sweep, Technical Changes, Aug 2026).
2. Feature-level disabling: NYC disables AI components in 38 approved products (including Amira and Gemini on student Chromebooks) while allowing five tools into high school pilots (sweep, Technical Changes, Sep 2026).
3. Device-scoped rules: LAUSD's restriction applies to school-issued devices, leaving personal devices and home computers outside the rule (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).
4. Low actual use even under full access: the median Khanmigo student messaged the tutor on a third of practice days despite 96 percent trying it (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Aug 2026).

### Legal
Core Shift Thesis:
The legal architecture is statutory and rule-based at state level, but its operative clauses delegate the actual decision downward, to parents in K-12 and to individual instructors in colleges, so the enforceable rule is a signature or a syllabus line.
Forces:
1. Default prohibition with instructor override: Florida Rule 6A-14.0719 requires all 28 Florida College System institutions to bar student AI on graded work unless the instructor permits it, covering more than 700,000 students (sweep, Regulatory Shifts, Sep 2026).
2. Categorical bans alongside consent: the same Florida K-12 amendment bans relationship-simulating AI tools outright, and NYC bans companion chatbots in all grades, so companion-style tools are excluded even where tutoring tools are allowed (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).
3. Statutory policy windows with uncertain dates: Maryland's AI Ready Schools Act requires all 24 systems to adopt aligned policies within 120 days, with sources disagreeing on whether the deadline fell in September or on 22 Oct 2026, and its collaborative not reporting until 1 Dec 2027 (sweep, Regulatory Shifts and Contradictions, Sep 2026; grading file, learning-2026-08-02).
4. Governance by policy versus by agreement: the University of Sydney argues AI should sit in updatable policy rather than a three-year enterprise agreement, the precise point contested in the 2 Sep 2026 strike (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026; ledger claim learning-2026-09-03).

### Environmental
Core Shift Thesis:
The physical settings of learning, school devices, home computers and supervised exam rooms, are becoming the boundaries along which AI rules are enforced, and institutions are starting to attach environmental commitments to AI deployments without measuring them at the student level.
Forces:
1. Device fleets as enforcement boundaries: restrictions scoped to school-issued Chromebooks and devices in NYC and LAUSD make the home computer and personal phone the unregulated learning environment (sweep, Confirmed Developments and Technical Changes, Sep 2026).
2. Return to supervised physical assessment: single institutions (Princeton ending unproctored exams from 1 Jul 2026; Bath, Cardiff, Birkbeck and Swansea shifting to invigilated in-person formats) are moving graded work back into controlled rooms (ledger, learning-2026-07-03 grade of 2026-08-04, Jun 2026 source; not from this run's sweep).
3. Sustainability funds attached to AI partnerships: the University of Sydney paired its OpenAI agreement with an AUD 1 million AI Sustainability Research and Innovation Fund (sweep, Capital Movements, Sep 2026).
