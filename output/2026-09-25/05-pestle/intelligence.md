# PESTLE, intelligence, 2026-09-25

### Political
Core Shift Thesis:
Capability velocity has become a public political object that labs can pledge to slow, the US executive can refuse to slow, and governments measure mainly in relation to rivals, so no public authority owns a domestic reading of pace.
Forces:
1. Executive preference for speed over pacing: the President rejected the labs' pacing call on 14 Sep 2026 and the White House AI czar said any slowdown should be voluntary, which removes federal backing from any shared pace reference for at least the current term (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).
2. Comparative rather than domestic government measurement: NIST CAISI's four 2026 model assessments all cover PRC models, and its 17 Sep 2026 GLM-5.3 assessment publishes a four-month US-to-PRC lag figure, so the state's public capability number answers "how far ahead" rather than "how fast" (sweep, Regulatory Shifts and Emerging Signals, Sep 2026; ledger candidate intelligence-2026-09-02).
3. Stalled US designation machinery: no EO 14409 covered-frontier-model framework or criteria had appeared in the Federal Register or on the CAISI page by 25 Sep 2026, so there is no public threshold a reading could trip (grading file, intelligence-2026-08-02, Sep 2026).
4. Pacing as a lab-side commitment without a metric: in the absence of a statute, pacing survives as a lab-side commitment measured by lab-chosen markers, such as Anthropic describing Opus 5.5 as its "first release since we called for pacing the frontier" (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).

### Economic
Core Shift Thesis:
Capability readings are becoming priced inputs, for model pricing, private valuations and buying decisions, while the cost of producing a trustworthy reading rises because each one consumes held-out tasks and tens of thousands of dollars of evaluation compute.
Forces:
1. Price compression at constant reading: Opus 5.5 is presented as Fable 5.1-level at 40% lower cost than Opus 5, and GPT-6 Sol and Luna as Astra-level reliability at half the prior price, so buyers begin to purchase "a reading per dollar" rather than a model (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).
2. Capital pricing self-acceleration directly: Mirendil is in talks to raise up to $1B at a $5B valuation for recursive self-improvement, and Cognition raised over $2B at $48B on agent revenue, so investors attach valuations to R&D-automation narratives before any neutral R&D-automation reading exists (sweep, Capital Movements, Sep 2026).
3. The cost of a verified reading: ARC Prize's Standard-harness run on GPT-6 Astra cost about $26K and the Grok 4.6 ARC-AGI-3 run about $5,612, so neutral verification becomes a recurring line item that evaluators, not labs, must fund for each release (sweep, Confirmed Developments; grading file, intelligence-2026-08-05, Sep 2026).
4. A market for unseen problems (inference from sweep): Artificial Analysis raised private-test weighting to 45% and built AutomationBench-AA from 657 held-out tasks on 7 Sep 2026, which implies paid commissioning of novel, unpublished tasks as the scarce input to credible measurement (sweep, Technical Changes, Sep 2026).

### Social
Core Shift Thesis:
The public meets capability as a schedule of readings rather than as demonstrations, and households increasingly inherit someone else's choice of which reading to believe.
Forces:
1. Release cadence as ambient news: six frontier-class releases from four labs in 43 days turn capability into something checked like weather, with two headline readings per model (sweep, Delta Since Last Sweep, Sep 2026).
2. Contradictory accurate headlines: "near saturation" (99.9%, Provider Adapter) and "two-thirds solved" (62.7%, Standard) are both correct readings of one ARC Prize page, so the public frame depends on which figure a publisher leads with (sweep, Contradictions, Sep 2026).
3. Jaggedness as expert consensus that does not travel: expert reaction to GPT-6 Astra stressed that intelligence in AI is "very jagged", a caution that cannot be expressed as a single number and so is dropped in everyday decisions (sweep, Counter-Signals, Sep 2026).
4. Consumer litigation as a channel for velocity preferences: four subscribers, not a regulator, brought the first legal challenge to the pacing statements, showing that ordinary users are willing to claim a stake in how fast capability grows (sweep, Buist v. Anthropic PBC, Sep 2026).

### Technological
Core Shift Thesis:
The protocol under which a capability is measured is now as consequential as the model measured, and published indices are rebased faster than the institutions that cite them can update.
Forces:
1. Harness forking: ARC Prize reports a neutral Standard harness and a lab-specific Provider Adapter harness separately, 37 points apart on the same model, making harness choice a first-order variable (sweep, Emerging Signals, Sep 2026).
2. Index rebasing: Artificial Analysis moved to Index v4.2 on 4 Sep and v4.3 on 7 Sep 2026, changing components and private-test weighting, which moved Fable 5.1 from 66 to 53 and Opus 5 from 63 to 51 without any model changing (sweep, Technical Changes and Counter-Signals; grading file, intelligence-2026-08-06, Sep 2026).
3. Lab-internal R&D automation instrumentation: OpenAI's 3.1 agent-workdays per human workday and Anthropic's 26% AL4 share on Epoch AI's scale, with about 30,000 internal agents under 100% online monitoring, are the most detailed velocity measures in existence and are self-published (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).
4. Open-weights parity on selected axes: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash under an MIT licence matches Opus 5 on DeepSWE (74.2 vs 74.0) while trailing on Terminal-Bench (30.0 vs 43.3), so any single reading can be made to show parity or a gap depending on the benchmark chosen (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).

### Legal
Core Shift Thesis:
Law is being asked to decide which capability reading is a fact before any statute defines one, first through antitrust and information requests rather than through capability-threshold regulation.
Forces:
1. Antitrust over pace: Buist v. Anthropic PBC, No. 3:26-cv-10693 (N.D. Cal., filed 18 Sep 2026), alleges a Sherman Act Section 1 output-restricting agreement built on the pacing essay and endorsements, which puts any shared, multi-lab velocity metric at legal risk and requires the court to adopt some reading of how fast capability "would have" grown (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).
2. EU enforcement without named targets: the AI Office's first AI Act information requests went to more than 30 unnamed companies (Commission briefing, 1 Sep 2026), and no formal step against a named GPAI provider was reported through 25 Sep 2026, so enforcement is active but not attached to any capability reading in public (sweep, Regulatory Shifts; grading file, intelligence-2026-08-04, Sep 2026).
3. Export control keyed to named models rather than readings: the only known US model-access restriction remains the 12 Jun 2026 BIS letter on Fable 5 and Mythos 5, and none of the post-August releases carried one, so export law responds to named products, not to published scores (grading file, intelligence-2026-08-03, Sep 2026).
4. Self-classification against lab frameworks: OpenAI gated GPT-6 Astra to its Daybreak program first because it "meets the Critical threshold in cybersecurity under our Preparedness Framework", so the reading that legally and commercially gates access is a lab's own classification (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).

### Environmental
Core Shift Thesis:
The physical cost of both producing and measuring capability is rising in step, and the resource footprint of verification becomes part of who can afford to publish a neutral reading.
Forces:
1. Evaluation as a compute burden: single verified runs on one benchmark cost from about $5.6K to $26K per model configuration, so full neutral coverage of six frontier releases in 43 days implies a sustained evaluation compute load that only funded evaluators can carry (sweep, Confirmed Developments; grading file, intelligence-2026-08-05, Sep 2026).
2. Research inference at scale: OpenAI reports median researcher inference use above $600 per day and Anthropic about 30,000 internal agents, which implies large, continuous energy draw attributable to self-acceleration rather than to customer use (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026; energy attribution is inference).
3. Efficiency claims as a reading in their own right: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's 8B to 16B active parameters inside a 552B mixture-of-experts model, and Opus 5.5's 40% cost reduction at constant capability, make capability-per-unit-of-compute an alternative number that a procurement or sustainability office could adopt (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).
4. Safety compute share as a disclosed allocation: Anthropic's report that 6% of AI R&D compute goes to safety establishes a disclosed resource ratio that other buyers or reviewers could ask every lab to report on the same basis (sweep, Confirmed Developments, Sep 2026).
