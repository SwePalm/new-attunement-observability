## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)
Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
The political layer has declined to own a domestic reading of capability pace: the executive rejected pacing on 14 Sep 2026 and its only published capability figure measures a rival's lag, not the home frontier's speed (Political forces 1 and 2). The social layer, meanwhile, consumes capability as a schedule of readings and wants one number to plan a life around (Social forces 1 and 2). People are handed the readings that publishers choose to lead with, while the one public actor that could declare a reference number refuses to, because declaring it would imply a pace someone should keep. The result is a public that is saturated with figures and has no authority to ask which one is meant for it.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
Pacing needs a shared velocity reference, and the market prices exactly such references (Economic forces 1 and 2: reading-per-dollar pricing, valuations on self-acceleration). But the moment competing labs agree on a common reading and a common pace, the agreement becomes the centrepiece of a Sherman Act output-restraint complaint (Legal force 1, Buist v. Anthropic PBC, filed 18 Sep 2026). Restraint needs coordination on a number; antitrust treats coordination on output as the harm. Grounded directly in the sweep Contradiction that the same September statements are read as both restraint and collusion. The generative consequence: any credible shared reading has to come from a party that is not a competitor, such as a neutral evaluator, an insurer or a large buyer, which moves the power to set pace away from both labs and government.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
Philosophically, the frontier readings describe progress toward machines that can meet genuinely novel problems, and the most credible readings are those taken on problems nobody has seen. In lived reality, novelty is a finite, human-made supply: every held-out task that keeps a benchmark honest is a problem somebody invented and then agreed never to show anyone (Economic force 4, Technological force 2; Artificial Analysis raising private-test weighting to 45%, 7 Sep 2026). The measurement of machine ingenuity therefore runs on human ingenuity that must be withheld from other humans in order to count.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)
Normal Absurdity 1:
People quote a model the way they quote blood pressure: two numbers, the neutral reading over the lab's reading, "sixty-three over a hundred". Nobody finds it strange that the same machine has two true scores 37 points apart, and arguments at dinner are about which number is "the real one" in the way people once argued about which poll to believe. (Lived expression of the ARC Prize Standard and Provider Adapter figures, 3 Sep 2026.)

Normal Absurdity 2:
A good puzzle is drawn on paper and kept away from anything connected, because a puzzle photographed is a puzzle spent. Puzzle-makers sell unseen problems to evaluators under terms that forbid showing them to anyone, including their own families, and this is considered sensible, like not posting a house key online.

Normal Absurdity 3:
Every release announcement arrives with a sentence explaining why it is slow. Labs ship on the same afternoon and each notes that its release is consistent with pacing, and readers treat the sentence the way they treat an allergen notice on a packet: present, required by custom, unread. (Lived expression of the 22 Sep 2026 Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Sol releases following the 12 Sep pacing essay.)

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing
The Irreplaceable Thing:
Being stumped together: a table of people facing a fresh problem none of them can look up, arguing it out until one of them sees it.

Explanation:
In this world almost every problem has a reading attached. A puzzle that a machine has seen has a published success rate; a puzzle that a machine has not seen has a buyer. What cannot be manufactured or bought is the shared, noisy stretch in which several people are baffled by the same unseen thing at once, try wrong answers out loud, and then watch one of them find the rule, with no number waiting on the far side to say how well anyone did. It is scarce because the freshness that makes the moment possible is the input evaluators pay for and then lock away, and a problem used for measurement cannot be given back unseen. It is physical and communal, paper, pencils and a hall table, and a protagonist can pursue it only by declining to sell the problem.
