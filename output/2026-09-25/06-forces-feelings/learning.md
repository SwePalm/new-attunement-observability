## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)
Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
Political instruments set AI rules by school year and by sector: one-year moratoria in New York City and Los Angeles, ballot pledges in Chicago, a state board rule in Florida with policies due 1 Jul 2027 (Political forces 1 to 3). Social life does not arrive in those units. A student's week runs through a restricted classroom, a college course under a different rule, a phone that is always on and a kitchen table that has no rule at all, and a household can hold a child in a restricted district and an older sibling at a university that has just committed to all-student access (Social force 2, grounded in the sweep's Contradictions: NYC and LAUSD restricting while Sydney and UNSW expanded in the same fortnight, Sep 2026). The political system produces clear rules per jurisdiction; the social system produces a student who is under four jurisdictions before lunch and has to work out, alone, which one is watching this assignment.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
The legal architecture delegates the decision downward and makes it reversible: Florida's college rule bars AI on graded work unless the instructor permits it, the K-12 rule gives parents an opt-out with a non-AI alternative, and moratoria expire each year (Legal forces 1 and 3). The economic architecture runs the other way. Vendors set defaults once and at scale (Gemini in Classroom default-on for all ages, Technological force 1), universities sign multi-year all-student licences (Economic force 1), and districts that switch features off do it contract by contract across 38 products (Economic force 4). The law says "ask the instructor, ask the parent, decide again next year"; the market says "on, for everyone, for three years, unless an administrator builds a separate unit". Every time the two meet, the default of the multi-year contract outlasts the single-year permission that was supposed to govern it.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
The stated purpose of every rule in the window is to protect learning: from harmful companions, from dishonest work, from untested tools. But learning, in the sense of a capability the student comes to own, depends on a stretch of being stuck that has to be tolerated long enough to reorganise into understanding, and the evidence available says that stretch is best supported by a person (Stanford SCALE brief, Aug 2026: strong evidence only where humans remain central). In lived reality, the rules convert being stuck into something else. Where AI is banned, it becomes a compliance condition endured alone under suspicion; where AI is permitted, it becomes an optional delay a student chooses against classmates who do not; where the rule is unclear, it becomes a risk calculation. None of the instruments protects the stuck stretch itself, and none puts anyone beside the student during it.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)
Normal Absurdity 1:
Parents receive a consent form each term asking them to opt their child in or out of tools they have never seen used in a classroom, and the "non-AI alternative" that opting out guarantees is routinely a printed worksheet handed to one student while the rest of the class works on screens. Families compare which box they ticked the way they once compared bedtimes. (Lived expression of Florida's parental notice and opt-out rule, sweep, Sep 2026.)

Normal Absurdity 2:
The same essay prompt is governed by different rules depending on which laptop it is typed on. On the school device the assistant has been switched off by the district's contract; on the family laptop at home the same vendor's assistant sits in the sidebar, on by default. Students learn to say "school computer" or "home computer" when asked how they did their work, as if naming a location answered a question about honesty. (Lived expression of NYC's contract-layer switch-off and LAUSD's device-scoped restriction against Google's default-on expansion, sweep, Aug to Sep 2026.)

Normal Absurdity 3:
A syllabus carries a line that changes mid-term: "AI permitted for brainstorming" is struck through in January and replaced with "AI not permitted on graded work; drafts at instructor discretion". Nobody considers this unusual. Students photograph each week's version of the syllabus in case a grade is later disputed, and teachers keep a dated log of what they said aloud about AI on which day.

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing
The Irreplaceable Thing:
Company in the stuck part: someone beside you while you do not yet understand something, stuck too or willing to wait, who neither ends the not-understanding for you nor treats it as suspicious.

Explanation:
Every rule in this world addresses the tool: whether it is on, who consented, who may override. None addresses the stretch of confusion in which learning happens, and that stretch has become lonely in a new way. At school, being stuck is either enforced under suspicion or skippable at a tap; at home, one tap ends it. The evidence that exists says human presence is what makes tutoring work, and presence is exactly what the configuration layer cannot supply. It is scarce because the rules sort students into separate conditions (opted in, opted out, school device, home device) and because adults are busy. It can be pursued, though: by finding the other students on the same side of a rule, by getting a ride to a table with no rule on it, and by staying there without fixing anything.
