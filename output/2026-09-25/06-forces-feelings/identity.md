# Forces and feelings, identity, 2026-09-25

## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)

Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
Governments are building agent identity for the agents they run and human
identity for the citizens they serve, and neither regime reaches the situation
people actually live in, which is one person acting for another, or sending
something to act for them. The Political forces show inward hardening (ISM-2133
to ISM-2135 give every agency-run agent a unique identity and a register entry)
and state wallets arriving late but device-bound (d-you from 2 Jan 2027,
credentials held on the device alone). The Social forces show households whose
real unit of identity is shared: a mother handling a son's registration, a son
handling a parent's bank. This is grounded directly in the sweep's own
contradiction: states are hardening agent identity inside their estates while
the observed failure was an outside agent at a government portal, which an
internal register does not cover. The result is generative: a public front door
that can verify a citizen to a high assurance level and a government-run agent
to a register entry, and has no category at all for the citizen's agent, or for
the citizen's mother.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
Capital is making agents cheap to verify where the buyer is a bank, while law is
making humans expensive to verify wherever a statute or proposal names a
checkpoint. On the Economic side, Baselayer's $35 million round funds
credentials an agent presents to prove delegated authority during a
transaction, and the Blueprint Alliance turns agent identity into ecosystem
membership. On the Legal side, SI 2026/1022 admits only DVS-certified providers
for age proof, the EU KIDS Act proposal requires verification at account
opening through a public-authority route, and no statute in the record defines
an agent's authority to act for a person. So a vendor-issued agent credential,
checked by a scheme the bank joined, can clear a payment in a second, while the
person who authorised it cleared four stacked checks that morning to reach the
same account. The tension is generative because the faster channel is the less
accountable one, and both sides are behaving exactly as their incentives say.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
Philosophically, identity is who a person is: continuous, embodied, known to
others over time. In lived institutional reality it is an answer a relying
party accepts at a moment, and the answer is increasingly supplied by a device
and an issuer rather than by presence or acquaintance. The contradiction
sharpens around presence. Being physically present, with a face and a voice and
a history, is now the slowest and most doubted way to establish oneself, because
faces and voices can be synthesised and history cannot be checked. Being absent,
and sending an agent with a scoped credential, is fast. The self that is there
is the one being questioned; the proxy that is not a self is the one waved
through.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)

Normal Absurdity 1:
Adults in their forties and fifties hold their phones to a self-checkout
reader to prove they are over eighteen, every time, while the cashier who has
served them for a decade watches from the next till, not permitted to override
the machine. The credential shares only "over 18", which is its privacy
virtue, so the machine that decides knows less about the customer than the
person standing beside it. (Lived expression of SI 2026/1022 and d-you's age
verification launch function.)

Normal Absurdity 2:
Every phone call to the bank begins with a recorded voice explaining that your
voice is being checked, and that your voice alone is not enough. Then the app
asks you to turn your head slowly to the left. Nobody finds this strange any
more; people learn to hold still and to answer the typing-pattern check without
hurrying. (Lived expression of banks stacking checks on top of voice rather than
retiring it, identity-2026-07-01.)

Normal Absurdity 3:
To help an adult child or an elderly parent with a government form, you walk
into their bedroom and take their phone off the charger, because the credential
lives in the device and nowhere else. Families keep each other's phones'
unlock codes as a matter of course, and say they are "doing" someone's
registration the way they once said they were posting someone's letter.
(Lived expression of device-only storage and key-bound tokens.)

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing

The Irreplaceable Thing:
Being recognised without being checked: a person who knows your face, and your
history, and takes you as yourself on sight.

Explanation:
In this world recognition is not abolished; it is outcompeted. Every checkpoint
that replaces a known person with a credential is individually justified,
because faces and voices can now be faked and acquaintance cannot be audited.
What disappears is not trust in general but the particular experience of being
accepted as oneself because someone remembers you. It survives in kiosks,
neighbourhood shops, a few staffed counters and families, and people who have it
feel its value precisely because the rest of the day consists of proving
things. It is scarce because it cannot be delegated, stored on a device or
issued; it has to be accumulated slowly, in person, by being the same person in
the same place for years. A protagonist can pursue it concretely: by walking
further to the shop where nobody asks, by choosing the counter over the app, by
being known on purpose.
