## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)

Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
The political layer is specifying disclosure for the viewer's eyes, and the social layer is removing it before it reaches them. Korea's casebook refuses a hidden watermark alone for deepfakes and demands a mark visible across full playback, the EU's machine-readable marking date of 2 Dec 2026 held at the AI Board's September plenary, and a Korean subcommittee is weighing prison or fines for removing marks. Socially, the first mass response to a text watermark was a 60% week-over-week rise in searches for removers and a remover repository past 15,000 stars, and Korea's operative regime has no provision against stripping and exempts individual creators. This is the Political thesis (legislatures converging on visibility) colliding with the Social thesis (disclosure populated by the conscientious rather than the deceptive). Conflict generator: the more precisely the law defines what a viewer must see, the more reliably the mark appears only on material whose makers were willing to leave it there, so the law's viewer learns to trust the unmarked.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
The law regulates labels; the market enforces through reach. Statute in Korea, the EU and California specifies what disclosure must look like and who must apply it. The one consequence that actually operated in this interval was economic and silent: Instagram withholds undisclosed AI-generated profiles from Reels, Explore and suggestions to non-followers, while existing followers keep seeing them unbadged, and detection of unlabeled profiles was promised rather than shipped. No statute in the record addresses a distribution decision, and the reach rule is not a disclosure to anyone. This is the Economic thesis (reach as the enforcement currency) set against the Legal thesis (form of disclosure specified, distribution unaddressed). Conflict generator: a creator can satisfy the platform by labeling or by relying on an existing audience, and a viewer's exposure to undisclosed synthetic content becomes a function of their own follow history, which no disclosure law reaches.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
The premise of disclosure is that telling people what they are looking at lets them see more accurately. The lived reality is that the instruments of telling alter what is seen. A badge that arrives mostly on accounts that chose to disclose teaches viewers that the absence of a badge means human. A reach limit means that two people in the same room are shown different worlds and neither is told. And the mark itself may not be inert: Lasso's 17 Sep 2026 study reports that SynthID-Text watermarking shifted refusal behaviour and tool-call arguments on open-weight models, while the vendor states it has no practical impact on output. Conflict generator: every layer built to help a person perceive the synthetic also changes the conditions of perception, and none of those changes is itself perceptible.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)

Normal Absurdity 1:
The badge lands on the one who told. When an account a person has followed for a year finally discloses and acquires its "AI-generated profile" badge, followers unfollow it, angry at the account that was honest, while the accounts that never disclose keep their audiences and never provide a moment of betrayal. People say "at least that one admitted it" in the tone once used for a friend who confessed to something small.

Normal Absurdity 2:
The same account wears a badge in one app and nothing in its sibling app, and a clip from Seoul carries its mark in the corner for the whole of playback while a clip from closer to home carries a word under the caption that nobody reads. Nobody finds this strange. People learn which app to check, the way they learned which weather app lies about rain.

Normal Absurdity 3:
The mark is for someone else. A bank's video assistant is watermarked on every frame, and the customer is told so in the terms, and cannot see it, and understands vaguely that the mark exists so that someone, at some later date, could establish something about the conversation she is currently having. She thanks the avatar anyway.

Normal Absurdity 4:
Removing a mark from your own writing is hygiene, like clearing the location from a photo before posting it. A teenager runs his essay through a remover before submitting it, not to cheat but because a marked essay invites a conversation he does not want to have.

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing

The Irreplaceable Thing:
The same screen: knowing that the person next to you, looking at the same account, is being shown the same thing with the same words around it.

Explanation:
In this world nothing is false by design, yet two people rarely see the same thing. What reaches a viewer depends on which app they use, which country a clip came from, whether they followed an account before it disclosed, and what their own settings and history have taught the feed. Labels exist, but they arrive unevenly and mostly on the honest. Withholding exists, but it is silent. The result is that a shared act of looking, holding up a phone and trusting that the person beside you would have seen the same thing, becomes rare and deliberate. People recover it by comparing screens side by side or watching together on one device. It can be pursued, and it is felt as a relief when found, because it is the only condition under which a disagreement about what is real can start from agreement about what was seen.
