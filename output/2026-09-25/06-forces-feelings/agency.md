# Forces and feelings, agency, 2026-09-25

## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)

Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
The political safeguard and the social practice point in opposite directions.
Supervisors and product designers answer public distrust by keeping a human
approval in the loop (the FCA-hosted GoCardless payment with payer final
approval, Mastercard Agent Pay's approval-before-checkout option), and the
Political forces treat that approval as the protection. Socially, the Social
forces show the approval multiplying with recurring and replacement tasks
until it becomes reflex. The same tap is, politically, the consumer's
control and, in the dispute file, the consumer's signature. The more
carefully the system keeps the human in the loop, the more completely the
human is bound by what passed through it.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
The Economic forces fund verification of who the agent is and what it was
permitted (the Visa, Mastercard and Ant KYA announcement of 10 Sep 2026,
Baselayer's $35M on 23 Sep 2026, Verifiable Intent as a paper trail), while
the Legal forces leave the allocation of loss for an agent that acts inside
its limits but outside its instruction to dispute codes built for fraud and
non-delivery. The market sells ever better proof of authorisation into a
legal gap where the question is meaning, and better proof of authorisation
makes it harder, not easier, for the customer to win. Directly grounded in
the sweep: Mastercard, asked who is liable when an agent exceeds
instructions, pointed to its paper trail rather than a rule, and Usio
observed that verifying the agent does not show it did what the customer
asked.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
Philosophically, the West is settling on the person as the actor: the Ninth
Circuit holds that the user, not the provider, accesses the site, and every
network record names the cardholder as the one who authorised. In lived
reality the person was not present at the purchase, did not see the listing,
and did not write the mandate; the agent drafted it from their words. The
principal is the author of record and the least informed party in the chain.
Beside it sits the sweep's contradiction that Amazon blocks outside agents
for lack of consent while its own agent enrolled retailers by opt-out:
consent to agents is whatever the stronger party in each pair says it is.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)

Normal Absurdity 1:
Signing your own paraphrase. Before any agent spends money, it shows you a
three-line summary of what you want, which it wrote, and you approve it with
a thumb. Nobody reads the summary against what they said, in the same way
nobody reads the terms on a parking ticket machine. Everyone knows the
summary is the version that counts.

Normal Absurdity 2:
Knowing which assistant a shop lets in. People keep a mental list, the way
they once knew which shops took which cards: one retailer admits only
registered agents, another blocks a large platform's assistant with a pop-up
calling it unauthorised, a third admits everything. Switching assistant to
reach a shop is ordinary. This is the lived expression of Amazon's Conditions
of Use block of Muse (about 20-21 Sep 2026).

Normal Absurdity 3:
Being asked forty times a week whether you are sure. Because almost nobody
lets an agent buy unapproved (7% in the Sep 2026 survey), the agent that
saves you shopping sends a steady stream of confirmation requests, which you
clear in the queue at the chemist or at a red light, and which each carry the
legal weight of a signature.

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing

The Irreplaceable Thing:
Having one's own words read back and taken as the measure, by someone with
the power to act on them.

Explanation:
Every party in an agent purchase keeps an excellent record of what the
customer authorised, and none of those records is the customer's own
sentence. The mandate is the agent's summary; the network's intent record
is a signature on the summary; the merchant's log shows an identified agent
arriving within limits. What a person said, in the words they used, exists
only in the agent provider's history, and there is no category in the
dispute system for it. To have someone retrieve that sentence, set it beside
the mandate, and decide by it, is rare and cannot be bought, because nobody
sells it and no rule requires it. A protagonist can pursue it: by asking, by
insisting, by finding the one person in the chain whose job allows them to
look.
