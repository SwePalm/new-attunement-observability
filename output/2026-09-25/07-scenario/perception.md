## 1. Title & Core Question
- Title: Two Phones on the Table
- Core Question: When the one consequence that reliably follows undisclosed synthetic content is that new people are not shown it, what does a label tell the people who already were, and how would two people at the same table know they are looking at the same world?

## 2. Context Summary (Translation Layer) – Why This Future Exists
In the autumn of 2026 three ways of telling people what they are looking at went live at once, and each addressed someone different. A badge addressed the viewer, and appeared only when the creator added it. An invisible watermark addressed a detector, and the detector was offered to regulators, journalists and researchers rather than to the person scrolling. A reach limit addressed nobody: undisclosed AI-generated profiles were quietly kept from people who did not already follow them and left in place for those who did. Lawmakers pushed toward visible marks, one country requiring them for the whole of a deepfake's playback, and toward punishing their removal, but on calendars measured in sessions and grace periods rather than weeks. A year on, in October 2027, marks are common, labels are familiar, and the practical question of who is shown what has been settled by feeds, one follow at a time.

## 3. Future World Snapshot (The Lived Experience) – A Day in This Future
Monday. The pastry account Odete has followed since the spring of 2026, a smiling woman in a tiled kitchen who answers every comment, has a new line under its name: AI-generated profile. The recipes are the same. Odete unfollows before lunch, feels foolish, and then feels angry at the woman in the kitchen for telling her.

Tuesday. On Facebook, the same woman, the same kitchen, no line at all. Odete's cousin shares a tart from it with three hearts.

Wednesday. Odete photographs her cakes for the parish bazaar in Porto and lets the phone rub out the rubbish bin behind the table. The post goes up with a small tag: AI-edited. Two neighbours ask, kindly, whether the cakes are real. She deletes it and posts the one with the bin.

Thursday. Her nephew in Busan sends a clip of a singer who was never on that stage. A mark sits in its corner the whole way through, steady as a clock. Her grandson Tiago, sixteen, says the Korean ones are honest about it. The ones from here say so once, under the caption, and the caption is folded.

Friday. The bank's video assistant has a pleasant face and knows her account. The terms say every frame is marked. She looks for the mark and there is nothing to see. It is for someone else, Tiago explains later, in case. She thanks the face anyway.

Saturday. Tiago runs his history essay through a remover before handing it in. Not cheating, he says. He wrote it. He only used the assistant to fix the commas, and a marked essay means a meeting.

Sunday. Her daughter Sara comes for lunch, and Odete shows her the account she has read all week about the flooding by the river: a young reporter, careful, local, always first. Sara has never come across it. It is not in her suggestions or anywhere her phone puts things in front of her. No warning, no label. Odete has followed it since last winter, so it still comes to her, unmarked. To Sara it has never existed.

They put both phones on the table between the plates and scroll side by side, looking for one post they are both being shown. When they find one, a photo of the bridge, they go quiet and look at it for longer than the photo deserves.

## 4. Behavioral Shifts (Human Lens) – How People Adapt
People learn to read a badge as a fact about the creator rather than the content. "At least that one admitted it" becomes the common reaction, and the disclosure moment, not the synthetic content, is what gets punished with an unfollow. Accounts that never disclose never offer that moment, so their audiences stay.

The absence of a label comes to mean human, by default, for most people most of the time. The more careful learn the opposite habit and treat an unlabeled account as undecided, which is tiring, and so most do it only for things that matter to them: news about their street, money, health.

Removing marks from one's own writing becomes ordinary hygiene, like clearing location data from a photo. Students, applicants and anyone who used an assistant for grammar strip first and explain never.

Households start comparing screens. It begins as curiosity ("do you see this too?") and becomes a small ritual before forwarding anything important, especially across generations, since the older relative's follow list, built over years, now determines a different world from the younger one's. Families learn which app carries which badge, and which country's clips can be trusted to say so for the whole of playback.

Some people give up on discovery feeds for anything that matters and return to named sources they can visit directly. Inference, not established by this run's evidence: that return is uneven and mostly among people with time to do it.

## 5. Structural Forces (System Lens) – What Holds This World Together
The enforcement that bites is distribution. When Instagram introduced its AI-generated profile badge in August 2026, the consequence it attached to non-disclosure was a withdrawal of reach, not a warning: undisclosed profiles kept out of Reels, Explore and suggestions for non-followers, untouched for followers, with detection of unlabeled profiles promised to follow. It works on creators quickly and tells viewers nothing.

The marks that multiply are invisible, and their readers are gated. Text watermarking went live in new models in September 2026 with a detection interface for approved third parties, and Google's live avatars shipped with SynthID on every frame. These protect a later reader, not a present viewer, and Apple's announced watermarking missed its launch release.

The law specifies form and leaves distribution alone. Korea's regulator insists that a deepfake's mark stay visible for the whole of playback and rejects a hidden watermark on its own, but exempts individual creators, defers fines past a grace period and has no penalty for stripping; its parliament held tampering sanctions and platform preservation duties for later review. The EU's machine-readable marking date held, and enforcement trailed it. None of these instruments reaches a decision about who is shown what.

Removal is cheap and wanted. A week after a major text watermark, searches for removers rose sharply and one removal tool drew more than fifteen thousand stars on the developer site where it was posted, so marks survive mainly on material whose makers had no reason to remove them.

## 6. Reflection & Implications – Questions This World Asks Us
If labels arrive mostly on the honest, are we teaching people to distrust candour and to trust silence?

A warning can be argued with. A withholding cannot, because the person it affects never sees it. Who should be able to ask what they were not shown, and whom would they ask?

When two people disagree about whether something is real, what should they be able to establish first: what it is, or whether they were both shown it?

## 7. Pullback Layer: From Possibility to Probability
### 7.1 Signals Emerging (Plausible Zone) – Early Signals We Already See
- On 31 Aug 2026 Instagram replaced its "AI creator" label with an "AI-generated profile" badge and said undisclosed AI-generated profiles would be withheld from Reels, Explore and suggestions to non-followers until labeled; on launch day none of six prominent AI influencer accounts checked by CNET carried the label.
- On 1 Sep 2026 two new Claude models began watermarking text and file outputs, and a detection interface opened in private preview for regulators, law enforcement, media, fact-checkers and researchers, not for the general public.
- Korea's science ministry now requires deepfakes to carry a visible mark for the whole of playback and does not accept a hidden watermark alone, while a parliamentary subcommittee on 15 Sep 2026 deferred bills that would punish removal of marks and oblige platforms to preserve them.
- Within about a week of the Claude watermark announcement in Aug 2026, searches for "AI watermark remover" rose 60% week over week and a remover project passed 15,000 stars.
- Apple shipped iOS 27 on 14 Sep 2026 without the SynthID watermarking it had announced for it in June, moving it to a later update.
- A security study published 17 Sep 2026 reported that the SynthID-Text scheme, tested on open-weight models, changed how often they refused harmful requests and altered tool-call arguments.

### 7.2 Probable Direction (Near-Term Future) – Where We're Likely Headed
The scenario sits about a year out, in October 2027, because that is where the checkable events in this evidence land: the EU marking date in December 2026, the end of Korea's grace period in January 2027, and the open questions about Meta's rule, Korea's tampering bills and Apple's point release, all resolving by early to mid 2027. Expect uneven layering rather than a settlement. Badges spread where reach is at stake and remain dependent on self-disclosure, because detection of unlabeled content has so far been promised more readily than shipped. Invisible marks keep spreading at the model layer, with readers gated. Statute keeps specifying visibility and keeps arriving late; announced dates in this field have tended to hold as events and slip as dates, and enforcement has trailed commencement. This scenario assumes, as a condition rather than a finding, that Meta's reach rule has not reached Facebook by late 2027. If it has, the household divergence narrows but does not close, because the rule still leaves followers untouched.

### 7.3 Preferred Path (Intentional Future) – The Path We Could Choose Instead
- Platforms: when a reach limit is applied to undisclosed synthetic content, apply the badge to existing followers at the same moment, so the people already exposed are the first to be told rather than the last.
- Platforms: publish, per account on request, whether a distribution restriction tied to AI disclosure is in effect, so a viewer can learn that something was withheld even if not what.
- Legislators working on tampering sanctions: pair them with a duty that visible labels travel with content across a company's own sibling apps.
- Vendors: open a viewer-facing way to check a mark on a single item, even a coarse one, rather than reserving detection for institutions.
- Families and schools: make "do you see this too?" a normal step before sharing anything about health, money or local emergencies.
- Individually: keep a few sources you reach by name rather than by feed, and notice when your sense of what is common comes only from what you are shown.

## 8. Connect to Today
### Skills We May Need
- Reading a label as a statement about who chose to disclose, not a verdict on the content.
- Treating an unlabeled account as undecided rather than as human.
- Asking the person next to you what their screen shows before arguing about what is real.
- Knowing which of your apps, and which countries' rules, carry a mark for how long.
- Noticing when your own follow history, not the world, is deciding what you see.
- Explaining to an older or younger relative why their feed and yours disagree without either being wrong.

### Signals & Refractions
- A disclosure rule that penalises non-disclosure through reach already exists and, by design, leaves existing followers unaffected, so the divergence between follower and non-follower is present today on at least one major app.
- A watermark whose reader is offered to regulators and journalists but not to the public is already live, which is the "mark is for someone else" condition in present form.
- A viewer-controlled setting for how much AI content one is shown has existed on TikTok since late 2025, so perception of synthetic prevalence is already partly a personal preference.

## 9. Final Insight
Most arguments about synthetic media assume the problem is that people cannot tell what they are looking at. The quieter change is that people are no longer looking at the same things, and nothing tells them so. A badge is a sentence, and a sentence can be read, doubted and argued with. A withholding is not a sentence; it has no reader, only an absence that differs from one phone to the next. The regimes being built now are good at deciding what content should say about itself and have barely begun to consider what a viewer is owed about the arrangement of what they see. Odete still reads the river reporter every morning. She has stopped sending its posts to Sara, not because she has decided they are false, but because she has learned that forwarding something is no longer the same as showing it.
