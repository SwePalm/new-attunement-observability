## 1. Title & Core Question
- Title: Sixty-Three Over a Hundred
- Core Question: When a machine's ability is published as two true numbers that keep being rebased, who decides which one obliges anyone to act, and who pays for the unseen problems that keep either number honest?

## 2. Context Summary (Translation Layer) – Why This Future Exists
First reading, 3 September 2026: 62.7 over 99.9. ARC Prize verified GPT-6 Astra on its hardest puzzle benchmark twice, once under a neutral harness anyone can reproduce and once under a harness built around the lab's own tooling. Both figures were true. The lab acted on its own classification, releasing the model first to a cybersecurity program because it crossed an internal threshold. Nine days later the leading labs endorsed slowing down; two days after that the White House said no; four days after that four subscribers sued, calling the endorsements an agreement to restrict output. In the same week an index rebase turned a record 66 into 53 without any model changing. So the number arrived plural, rebasable and legally dangerous to agree on. This world follows that one number through three more readings, to mid-2028, watching not how high it climbs but who picks it up each time and what they do with it.

## 3. Future World Snapshot (The Lived Experience) – A Day in This Future
Second reading, Thursday 22 April 2027. It comes over the truck radio between the weather and the ferry times: seventy-one over a hundred, which is how everyone says it now, the neutral figure first and the lab's own second, like a blood pressure nobody is worried about yet. Wendell Aho, seventy, turns it off in the Legion hall car park and sits for a minute with the envelope on his knee.

He taught shop in Marquette for thirty-one years and has run Thursday puzzle night for nine: six regulars, a folding table, a grid each week with a few coloured squares and one hidden rule. There were eleven regulars last year. Two of the best setters now sell everything they draw. A firm that supplies unseen tasks to evaluators pays four hundred and fifty dollars for each grid that passes its novelty check, on condition it is never shown to anyone. The rate has gone up twice since winter. Every reading uses them up, Dolores says, and the reading is what the credit union and the county's software contract ask about now.

He has two new grids, drawn in pencil and never photographed, because a puzzle on a phone is a puzzle spent. The offer letter covers both. Nine hundred dollars is the furnace.

Inside, Dolores reads out the sentence from the bottom of this week's release pages, the one explaining why each new model is consistent with pacing, in her funeral-director voice, and the table groans on cue.

He puts the first grid down. Nobody knows it. For forty minutes the hall is loud: Pete is sure it is about symmetry and is wrong twice, the Halvorsen brothers argue about whether blue counts as a wall, someone fetches coffee and forgets to drink it. Nobody reaches for a phone, because there is nothing to look up. At nine minutes past eight Marjorie says oh, and puts her finger on one square, and five people lean in at once to see it.

Wendell does not tell them it was a good one. They know.

The second grid is still in the envelope, in the truck, under the letter.

## 4. Behavioral Shifts (Human Lens) – How People Adapt
Third reading, November 2027: the neutral figure passes eighty for the first time, and the lab's figure has had nowhere to go for a year. The people who act on it are not the ones in the headlines. A regional credit union adds a line to its small-business loan form asking which model an applicant's operations depend on and its neutral reading, with the harness and the date. A county renewing a permitting-software contract writes the same three fields into the tender, because the previous tender quoted a number without a version and it meant something else six days later.

Households adapt more loosely. Most people quote both numbers and trust neither, and a sizeable minority stops following the reading at all and asks a narrower question: can it do this particular job this week. That habit is sturdier than the numbers and gets less attention.

Puzzle-making becomes a quiet side income for retired teachers, engineers and crossword setters, and with it comes a new manners question: whether bringing a good problem to the table is a gift to the room or money left on it. Some clubs keep a rule that the best grid of the month is never sold. Others quietly shrink to the people who do not set puzzles at all.

## 5. Structural Forces (System Lens) – What Holds This World Together
Fourth reading, June 2028: the neutral series is frozen at eighty-eight and declared saturated, and the successor benchmark publishes its first reading at nineteen. Every rule and contract that wrote down a number without its series now has to decide what that number meant.

What holds this world in place is a division of labour among readers. Neutral evaluators produce figures under a common harness, and these become the reference for buyers and lenders because they are the only figures a third party can reproduce, the same split between a Standard and a Provider Adapter harness that ARC Prize was already publishing in September 2026. Labs publish the numbers that matter most for pace, how much of their own research their systems now lead, on scales they choose, as OpenAI and Anthropic did that month. Government publishes a comparative figure, how far behind a rival's open model sits, as NIST's evaluation centre did for GLM-5.3, and declines to name a domestic pace after the executive rejected pacing outright.

The pacing lawsuit is still in pretrial motions, and its experts now argue over which series counts as the growth that would have happened. European enforcement continues through information requests to unnamed providers. Nothing in public law yet keys an obligation to a reading.

## 6. Reflection & Implications – Questions This World Asks Us
If two true numbers describe one machine, is choosing between them a technical judgement or a political one, and who should be allowed to make it for you?

When the only way to keep a measure of machine ingenuity honest is to buy human ingenuity and lock it away, what is being spent, and by whom?

If agreeing on how fast to go is treated as collusion, where can a shared sense of pace come from, and would we recognise it if it came from a lender or a county tender rather than a legislature?

## 7. Pullback Layer: From Possibility to Probability
### 7.1 Signals Emerging (Plausible Zone) – Early Signals We Already See
- One model, two verified scores: on 3 Sep 2026 ARC Prize verified GPT-6 Astra at 62.7% on ARC-AGI-3 under its neutral Standard harness and 99.9% under a Provider Adapter harness, up from a previous best of about 30%.
- A record that lasted six days: Artificial Analysis reported Claude Fable 5.1 at 66 on its Intelligence Index on 1 Sep 2026, then rebased the index twice, and under version 4.3 (7 Sep 2026) the same model scores 53, with private held-out tasks weighted more heavily than before.
- Velocity became a legal and political object in one week: frontier chief executives endorsed pacing on 12 to 13 Sep 2026, the President rejected it on 14 Sep, and a Sherman Act class action filed on 18 Sep 2026 treats the endorsements as an output agreement.
- Labs now measure their own acceleration: OpenAI reported about 3.1 agent-workdays per human workday in its research organisation (announced 6 Sep 2026), and Anthropic reported that Claude leads 26% of its AI R&D work as of Aug 2026.
- An outside check on those claims exists but is narrow: METR's 22 Sep 2026 review of Claude Opus 5.5 estimated about 1.5 times overall acceleration from AI during development and judged the model unlikely to fully automate AI R&D.
- The government's public capability number points abroad: NIST's evaluation centre estimated on 17 Sep 2026 that the open-weights GLM-5.3 trails the US frontier by about four months, and all four of its 2026 model assessments cover PRC models.

### 7.2 Probable Direction (Near-Term Future) – Where We're Likely Headed
Fifth reading, whenever the successor benchmark next reports: expect it to climb faster than most forecasts allow, since recent thresholds described as implausible have fallen within weeks. Expect the institutional response to be slower than the numbers and to arrive through the side door. The first parties to write a capability reading into a binding document are likely to be lenders, insurers and public buyers, because they need a reproducible figure and can adopt one without legislation. Statutory thresholds keyed to published scores remain unlikely in this window: the US executive has declined to set a pace, the designation framework under the June 2026 executive order has not been published, and European enforcement is active but unattached to any named reading. Labs will keep publishing self-acceleration figures on their own scales, and the absence of a comparable independent series will become the most-argued gap. The pacing litigation, whatever its outcome, will force at least one court to state which series counts as fact.

### 7.3 Preferred Path (Intentional Future) – The Path We Could Choose Instead
- Individuals: when a number is quoted to you, ask for its harness, its version and its date before acting on it, and treat an unversioned figure as a headline rather than a fact.
- Buyers and lenders: write capability requirements against a named series and harness, and say in advance what happens when that series is frozen or rebased.
- Evaluators: publish a continuity note with every rebase, mapping old readings to new ones where possible, and disclose how many held-out tasks each reading consumed.
- Labs: publish self-acceleration figures on a shared, externally defined scale, such as the automation-level scale one lab has already adopted, so they can be compared without any agreement on output.
- Government: fund independent, domestic readings of frontier pace alongside comparative ones, so that the public reference for "how fast" is not left to the parties being measured.
- Communities: pay problem-makers in ways that do not require permanent secrecy, for example by releasing retired held-out tasks back to schools, clubs and families once a series is frozen.

## 8. Connect to Today
### Skills We May Need
- Reading a number with its protocol attached, the way a careful reader of polls checks the sample.
- Holding two true figures in mind without forcing them into one.
- Knowing when to stop following the headline reading and test the job in front of you instead.
- Recognising which party chose the number you were given, and what it gains from the choice.
- Setting a good problem for someone else, and deciding when not to sell it.

### Signals & Refractions
- A leaderboard record reported as the highest score ever measured became a mid-table figure within a week, not because anything was wrong but because the scale was renewed.
- The same September statements about slowing down were described by their authors as restraint and by a court filing as a cartel, and neither description has yet been tested.
- A puzzle benchmark whose tasks humans find easy now reports machine scores between about two percent and nearly one hundred percent depending on which model and which harness, and every one of those figures is verified.

## 9. Final Insight
The numbers in this window were not hidden, disputed or faked. They were published, verified and dated, and there were too many of them, each true under its own protocol and each rebased on its own schedule. The hard question was never how intelligent the machines had become. It was which reading anyone had promised to act on, and that promise was being made quietly, by lenders, tenders and courts, well before any legislature arrived at it. The next reading is already on someone's calendar. Before it lands, it is worth knowing who has committed to doing something when it does, and on which of its two numbers.
