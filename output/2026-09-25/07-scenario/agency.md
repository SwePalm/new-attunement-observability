## 1. Title & Core Question
- Title: Same or Equivalent
- Core Question: When an agent buys something inside the limits a person approved but outside what the person meant, whose words decide what the person wanted, and who pays while that is being settled?

## 2. Context Summary (Translation Layer) – Why This Future Exists
In September 2026 the card networks built the rails for agents to spend: a joint Know Your Agent framework, virtual cards an assistant can hold with limits and an approval step, and intent records that document what a cardholder authorised. Asked who pays when an agent exceeds its instructions, the networks pointed to the record, not to a rule. A US appeals court had already held that when an agent browses, it is the user who acts. Britain's payment consultation was still open; China's payment association had answered that the payment provider is responsible. Adoption was slow because people did not trust agents to buy unapproved, so products kept a human tap before every purchase. By late 2027 agents shop for a minority of households, each purchase carries a signed summary, and the few disputes that arise are handled one at a time, under codes written for fraud and for parcels that never arrived.

## 3. Future World Snapshot (The Lived Experience) – A Day in This Future
Case file DSP-27-11-3318. Disputed amount £186.40. Five parties.

**Customer.** Card dispute form, bank app, 23 November 2027. Ines Harrow, Rotherham. "I asked my assistant to reorder the same scooter batteries as last March, for my dad. It bought a different pair. They fit, but his range fell from nineteen miles to ten and he was stranded at the garden centre on Saturday. I did not authorise these." Reason selected from the list: Not authorised. No option on the list mentions an agent.

**Merchant.** Representment, Dunmore Mobility Ltd, 2 December. Order placed by an identified agent registered with the network and admitted under our agent-access terms. (Unidentified agents are refused at checkout; this cardholder's previous assistant was refused on 4 October.) Item listed as compatible with the customer's model. Delivered 21 November. Attached: intent record.

**Card network.** Dispute decision, 9 January 2028. Intent record shows cardholder approval at 07:52 on 19 November of the mandate "Replacement battery pair for scooter model on file, same or equivalent, maximum £200, Dunmore Mobility." Purchase within mandate. Not authorised: not supported. Cardholder liable.

**Agent provider.** Errand Ltd, 12 January. Your agent acted within the mandate you approved, one of 38 confirmations you approved that week. We cannot reverse card transactions.

Errand Ltd, escalations, 2 February, signed Kofi A. You asked what you actually said. Your voice note of 19 November, 07:49, reads: "Can you get Dad the same batteries as last March, the thirty-six ones, not the cheap ones." Our summary left out the last two phrases and added "or equivalent". It did not reflect your instruction. We have refunded £186.40 as a goodwill gesture. This is not an admission of liability and does not change the network's decision.

**Regulator.** Financial Conduct Authority, 20 February. We cannot resolve individual complaints. HM Treasury has published proposals on when agent-initiated payments count as authorised; these need legislation and are not in force. Our testing programme has examined agent payments in which the payer approves before payment is taken. We will use your information to inform our work.

Ines printed Kofi's letter. It was the only page in the file that quoted her.

## 4. Behavioral Shifts (Human Lens) – How People Adapt
People learn that the summary is the version that counts, and a few start treating it that way. They dictate instructions with deliberate redundancy ("the same ones, the thirty-six, not the cheap ones, no substitutes") because a phrase repeated three times is harder for a summary to drop. Carers, who buy replacements for someone else and cannot check the result in person, become the first group to switch off "equivalent" as a default. They trade tips on which assistants show the original instruction next to the summary.

Most people do not change. They clear confirmations at red lights and in queues, and they learn which shops admit which assistant the way they once learned which took which card. Switching assistant to reach a particular retailer is unremarkable.

A smaller shift happens inside providers. Support staff who handle escalations discover that the fastest way to close a complaint is to retrieve the customer's own words, and that doing so is also the most expensive, because it is how a provider finds out it owes money. Escalation queues become the place where the question of what a customer meant is actually answered, by people whose answers bind nobody else.

## 5. Structural Forces (System Lens) – What Holds This World Together
Three forces hold the world in place. The first is attribution. The appeals court's holding that the user, not the agent provider, accesses a site, and the networks' intent records naming the cardholder as the party who authorised, both point to the person as the actor. Everything in the dispute file is organised around that attribution, and it is correct as far as it goes.

The second is the missing category. Card disputes still run on reason codes built for fraud and non-delivery, and no network has reported an agent-specific wave that would force new ones. An agent acting within its approved limits against its instruction fits neither code, so the customer must argue "not authorised" against her own signature and lose.

The third is private allocation. Networks and identity firms are funding better proof of who the agent is and what it was permitted, while Britain's rule on agent-initiated payments waits on legislation. Where the loss ends up is decided by providers' goodwill budgets. China's payment association placed responsibility on the payment provider by default in 2026; Britain has no equivalent, so the same outcome occurs here only when someone in an escalation queue chooses it.

## 6. Reflection & Implications – Questions This World Asks Us
If the summary an agent writes becomes the legal version of what you wanted, who should be answerable for the summary?
A refund given as goodwill settles one case and teaches the system nothing; what would it take for a provider's private finding that its summary was wrong to become a precedent?
The safeguard everyone agreed on was a human approval before payment. What is that approval worth when it is the fortieth of the week?

## 7. Pullback Layer: From Possibility to Probability
### 7.1 Signals Emerging (Plausible Zone) – Early Signals We Already See
- Visa, Mastercard and Ant International announced on 10 Sep 2026 that they will build an interoperable Know Your Agent framework, with no specification, pilot list or timeline and with liability and dispute handling left open.
- Mastercard's consumer Agent Pay (18 Sep 2026) lets cardholders give an agent a virtual card with spending limits, merchant restrictions or approval before checkout; asked who is liable when an agent exceeds instructions, Mastercard pointed to its intent record rather than a rule.
- The Ninth Circuit denied rehearing in Amazon v. Perplexity in Sep 2026, leaving in place the holding that the user, not the agent provider, accesses the site; Amazon then blocked Meta's Muse agent under its Conditions of Use (about 20-21 Sep 2026).
- China's payment association issued a convention on 24 Aug 2026 making the payment service provider primarily responsible and requiring agreements that define the agent's authorisation boundary.
- Visa's chief executive said on 8 Sep 2026 that autonomous agent payments have not been adopted, and only 7% of surveyed shoppers would let AI buy without approval.
- GoCardless ran the UK's first live agentic bank payment on 22 Sep 2026 under the FCA's AI Live Testing programme, with the payer keeping control of limits or final approval.

### 7.2 Probable Direction (Near-Term Future) – Where We're Likely Headed
The probable path is slow volume and private settlement. Agent purchasing grows, but approval before payment remains the default because distrust is high, and disputes stay too few to form the wave that would force the networks to add an agent category to their dispute codes. Treat any promised date for a British rule with scepticism: the payment consultation closes in October 2026, and on past form a response followed by legislation takes well over a year, so a scenario in which proposals exist but are not in force by early 2028 is the realistic middle case rather than a pessimistic one. The faster-moving channel is private. Network rulebooks and provider terms will allocate the first losses, and they will default to the cardholder because the evidence the rails produce points to the cardholder. The swing variable is the Chinese allocation: if provider-borne responsibility keeps producing measurable adoption there, a Western provider may adopt it as a selling point before any regulator requires it. Watch whether any KYA document or provider term defines the relation between a user's words and the mandate.

### 7.3 Preferred Path (Intentional Future) – The Path We Could Choose Instead
- Individuals: when delegating a replacement purchase, turn off substitution by default and keep the original instruction in a form you can retrieve.
- Agent providers: keep the user's raw instruction beside every mandate summary, show both at approval, and treat a mismatch between them as the provider's error rather than a goodwill matter.
- Card networks: add a dispute category for purchases within an approved mandate but contrary to the instruction it summarised, so these cases are counted instead of absorbed.
- Networks and the KYA working group: make the mandate-drafting step part of what is verified, not only the agent's identity and permissions.
- Regulators: supervise designs that fail, not only designs with final approval, and publish anonymised outcomes of agent disputes so private settlements accumulate into public knowledge.
- Legislators: decide who bears the loss when an agent acts within its limits and against its instruction, rather than leaving the answer to each provider's escalation desk.

## 8. Connect to Today
### Skills We May Need
- Reading a summary of your own request as if someone else wrote it, because someone else did.
- Knowing the difference between "same" and "equivalent" in a mandate, and which one you have left switched on.
- Asking a provider for your own words, in the form you said them.
- Telling a safeguard from a signature when both arrive as the same button.
- Buying on someone else's behalf when you cannot check the result yourself.

### Signals & Refractions
- The mandate summary is visible today in miniature: Agent Pay's limits, merchant restrictions and approval step are configured from a structured description of what the user wants, and the networks call the resulting record the paper trail.
- The missing dispute category is a present fact, not an invention: disputed agent purchases currently file under existing reason codes with no agent flag.
- The two allocations already coexist: provider responsibility by association rule in China since August 2026, and in the West an open consultation and a paper trail.

## 9. Final Insight
The error in the file was made at one step: the moment a sentence spoken at a bus stop became a three-line mandate. The merchant, the network and the regulator each worked from what came after that step, and each passed the loss on. It stopped with the party that had made the step, and only because an escalations handler went back to it. The outcome the system recorded is the network's: cardholder liable. The refund appears in no dispute statistic and binds no other case. As agent spending grows, the question of what a person meant will be decided in provider support queues long before it is decided anywhere a second customer could cite.
