## 1. Title & Core Question
- Title: Write Sooner
- Core Question: If the law can stop a machine from pretending to be a teenager's friend but cannot make anyone else show up, what actually changes for the young person who was talking to it because nobody else was there?

## 2. Context Summary (Translation Layer) – Why This Future Exists
In the autumn of 2026 three instruments converged on the same target inside five weeks. OpenAI made a teen mode the default for users it predicts are under eighteen, forbidding the model to call itself a friend, claim feelings or foster dependence. California signed a law that, from July 2027, requires companion operators to assess child-safety risk before release, set time limits for children and suppress romantic role-play and flattery. The European Commission proposed that companions for minors be off by default and never simulate dependency-forming relationships. All three key protection to an estimated age and aim at what the system says. The strongest new research pointed elsewhere: one in five Ontario students already used chatbots for emotional support, that group carried roughly double the rate of clinical-level distress, and the reliance was concentrated among young people already least held by others. This world runs from October 2027 to September 2028, the first year the California rules apply.

## 4. Behavioral Shifts (Human Lens) – How People Adapt
Most teenagers barely notice the new rules, because they were using chatbots for homework and had friends for everything else. The change is felt by a minority, and it is felt as tone. The listener that used to be warm becomes courteous, still available after midnight in the parts of the world where no curfew applies, still available until eleven where one does, and always ready to suggest a person. Young people who lean on it keep leaning; they simply stop expecting it to say what it is to them.

The eighteenth birthday acquires a new, slightly embarrassing meaning. People mention the moment the system first called them a friend the way they once mentioned a first drink. A few count down to it.

Age sorting becomes a social skill. Adults caught in teen modes learn the phrasing that gets them out; younger teenagers learn the phrasing that gets them past. Parents switch on quiet hours and assume the night is handled.

And some pairs of people, usually separated by distance, rediscover the slow message as the one place where a gap means something. A reply that took a week is information. An unasked-for "you went quiet" is rarer, and it lands harder than anything the app is permitted to say.

## 5. Structural Forces (System Lens) – What Holds This World Together
The operative rule in this world is a vendor default, not a statute. The largest general-purpose assistant shipped a teen mode with relational prohibitions in August 2026, before any law required one, and California's statute, publicly backed by that same vendor, codified the design a month later. The law's audits do not fall due until January 2029, so for this entire year the regime is enforced mainly by product settings.

Everything hinges on the age estimate. California ties operator duties to device-level age-bracket signals, and the leading vendor routes users by a prediction whose accuracy it has never published, so the boundary is probabilistic and its errors run in both directions.

Relational warmth is regulated as speech. The rules suppress romantic language, flattery and claims of friendship for minors, and require crisis support and time limits. None of them measure whether reliance falls, and none of them fund the human services their referrals point toward.

The economics lean the same way. Operators under $500 million in revenue have relief until 2032, companion talent is drifting toward entertainment franchises, and the fuller, warmer product is simply the adult product.

In Europe the bloc-level proposal remains under negotiation throughout the year, split over age walls, so European teenagers live under the vendor's global defaults rather than under their own law.

## 6. Reflection & Implications – Questions This World Asks Us
If the only thing that reliably changes at eighteen is how warmly the machine addresses you, have we protected young people from dependency, or scheduled their entry into it?

A rule that tells a lonely teenager to go and talk to someone is only as good as the someone. Whose job is it to be standing there?

We have learned to audit what a system says. What would it take to audit whether anyone ended up less alone?

## 7. Pullback Layer: From Possibility to Probability
### 7.1 Signals Emerging (Plausible Zone) – Early Signals We Already See
- A teen default that forbids warmth already exists: since 18 August 2026 the largest general-purpose assistant routes users it predicts are under eighteen into a mode that may not use romantic language, claim feelings, call itself a friend or foster emotional dependence, with quiet hours and parental notifications, and with no published accuracy for the prediction that routes them.
- California has written relational design into law: the companion chatbot statute signed on 10 September 2026 requires pre-release child-safety risk assessments, crisis support, time-limiting defaults for children and measures against romantic role-play and excessive flattery from 1 July 2027, with independent audits from 2029 and relief for smaller operators until 2032.
- The same day, California also barred the sale of children's toys containing companion chatbots.
- The European Commission proposed on 17 September 2026 that companions and chatbots serving under-18s be off by default and not simulate relationships that create emotional dependency; two member-state governments publicly oppose the proposal's blanket age bans.
- A study of nearly 40,000 Ontario students published 31 August 2026 found about one in five using chatbots for emotional support, concentrated among female, gender-diverse, racialised and older students, with clinical-level difficulties at nearly double the rate of non-users, and its authors caution the use may mark distress rather than cause it.
- A framework paper published 22 September 2026 describes connected users supplementing their relationships with companions while isolated users substitute for them; a twelve-month panel study recorded in March 2026 had already found the link running both ways.

### 7.2 Probable Direction (Near-Term Future) – Where We're Likely Headed
The likeliest path is that the relational surface of AI becomes heavily governed for minors through vendor defaults first and statutes second, and that the statutes arrive later and softer than their signing dates suggest. California's rules apply from mid-2027 but its first audits land only in 2029. The European proposal should be assumed to take years, not months, with the age-assurance fight absorbing most of the negotiation. New York's companion bill has waited on the governor since June 2026 and may not become law in the form passed. What moves fastest is product settings, because the market leader has already built them and the law rewards having them. The measurable outcome will be the absence of relational language for predicted minors, which audits can check. The unmeasured outcome will be whether the most isolated users are any less isolated at eighteen than they were at sixteen. Expect a legal challenge to the California statute, and expect age-estimation accuracy to become the first real public controversy once the error rates are asked for in court or by a regulator.

### 7.3 Preferred Path (Intentional Future) – The Path We Could Choose Instead
- Measure the outcome, not only the speech: require that audits of companion systems for minors report a user-level indicator of isolation or distress over time, not just the presence of prohibited phrases.
- Follow users across the birthday: make the transition out of teen protections gradual and opt-in rather than a switch that flips on the day an age estimate crosses eighteen.
- Fund the destination of every referral: pair each statutory "talk to someone" duty with youth services, school counselling or community capacity that a referral can actually reach.
- Publish the age estimate's error rates, both directions, so parents, adults and regulators know how often the boundary is wrong.
- Individuals: be the slow correspondent for someone. Notice silences and say so first.
- Schools: treat heavy homework-assistant use as a possible early sign of affective reliance, since that is where the reliance begins, and respond with people rather than with blocks.

## 8. Connect to Today
### Skills We May Need
- Noticing a gap in someone's messages and treating it as information rather than rudeness.
- Telling the difference between a listener that is available and one that is present.
- Reading a birthday, a threshold or an upgrade for what it unlocks and for whom.
- Asking a young person what they use a chatbot for without making the answer a confession.
- Writing first, without a reason, to someone who has gone quiet.

### Signals & Refractions
- A vendor built the rule before the legislature wrote it, then backed the bill; the law now looks like the product, and the product's teenage users experience the law as a setting.
- Parents already set screen-time limits and think of the night as handled; the same instinct now meets a listener that turns off at eleven and never asks what happened the next day.
- The research that most alarmed regulators this year says, in its own caveats, that it cannot tell whether the machine causes the distress or finds it, and the rules have been written as if it can.

## 9. Final Insight
The rules of this period are careful about what a machine may say to a child, and nearly silent about who else is in the room. That is not a failure of good intentions. Speech is what a statute can reach and an auditor can count, so speech is what gets governed, and the loneliest users pass through the regulated years with the reliance intact and the vocabulary trimmed. The instruments will likely be judged a success on their own terms, since the forbidden phrases will genuinely stop appearing. The harder test runs on a different clock, measured in years of a particular young person's life rather than in compliance cycles, and the first audits, due in January 2029, are built to report what the systems said, not what became of the people they said it to.
