## 1. Title & Core Question
- Title: Page Two, Third Version
- Core Question: When the permission to use AI on a piece of schoolwork is set by a state board, a district, a vendor's default, a teacher's syllabus and a parent's signature, each on its own calendar, who stays with a student through the part of learning that no permission covers?

## 2. Context Summary (Translation Layer) – Why This Future Exists
In the autumn of 2026 the rules for AI in classrooms moved in several directions at once. New York City and Los Angeles suspended student-facing generative AI for one school year. Google made its classroom assistant available by default to students of every age. Florida's State Board adopted two rules on the same day: one giving K-12 parents notice and an opt-out with a non-AI alternative, with district policies due by July 2027, and one barring AI on graded college work unless the instructor permits it. Universities in Sydney signed all-student licences while their staff went on strike over who had been consulted. The largest trial of a production AI tutor found small gains, much like practice without AI, and was still a working paper. None of these rules replaced the others. They stacked, and the school year that follows is the first one in which a single family has to live under all of them at once.

## 3. Future World Snapshot (The Lived Experience) – A Day in This Future
**August 2027.** Marisol Reyes is sixteen and has two rulebooks for one class. ENC 1101 meets in Room 214 of her central Florida high school, taught by a high school teacher, for college credit. Page two of Ms. Okafor's syllabus says AI may be used for brainstorming, which the college rule allows when the instructor permits it. The district's own AI policy, due in July, has not been adopted yet; an interim notice asks parents to opt in or out.

Her father, Ruben, reads the form in his truck cab before the first delivery of his route. He has never seen the tool used in a classroom. He ticks "out" because it is the box he can explain. The next week Marisol is one of two students in Room 214 handed a printed brainstorming worksheet. She learns to say "school computer" when asked where she drafted, because at home the family laptop has the assistant in its sidebar, on, and nobody there has switched it off.

**January 2028.** Page two has changed. "Brainstorming permitted" is struck through; the new line reads "No AI on graded work. Drafts at instructor discretion." Ms. Okafor says the college asked for tighter wording after a grade appeal in another section. Marisol photographs the page, as everyone does now, and files it beside the August version. The district adopted its policy in October. It says the opt-out applies on district devices; the counselor thinks the college rule governs dual enrollment; nobody can tell Ruben which of his signatures counts.

Her research essay stalls on a paragraph she cannot make true. The sidebar at home is one tap away. Instead she texts Dev, the other student in Room 214 whose parent ticked "out", and on Tuesday Ruben finishes his route early, drives them both to the county library, and waits in the truck with the radio on. They sit at the long table by the periodicals, each stuck on a different paragraph, and read them aloud to each other badly, then less badly. Neither can fix the other's. They stay until the library closes at eight. Ruben does not ask how it went.

**May 2028.** The district announces that a committee will recommend rules for 2028-29 in the fall. The essay gets a B-plus. The new form arrives with three boxes instead of two.

## 4. Behavioral Shifts (Human Lens) – How People Adapt
Students become readers of jurisdiction before they are readers of texts. They learn which device, which room and which version of the syllabus applies, and they keep dated photographs of every rule in case a grade is later disputed. Many settle into two modes of working: with AI where it is allowed and for practice, without it where work is graded, and they notice that the two modes do not always transfer.

Parents discover that consent is now their job and that they have little to base it on. Some opt out because it is the choice they can defend, and then watch their child become the one with the worksheet. Others opt in and feel they have signed something they could not read. A few, with money, buy exam-preparation tutoring directly, because it sits outside every school rule.

Teachers keep logs of what they said aloud about AI and on which day. Older siblings at universities with all-student access become the family's informal advisers on a tool the younger children are not allowed to touch at school. And a habit forms that no rule asked for: students sorted onto the same side of a rule find each other and meet somewhere no rule reaches, a library table or a bus bench, to be stuck in company rather than alone, with a parent's car idling outside.

## 5. Structural Forces (System Lens) – What Holds This World Together
The world holds because each layer of authority is individually coherent and nobody owns the order in which they apply. Florida's K-12 rule, adopted 16 Sep 2026, routes the decision to parents through notice and opt-out, with district policies due 1 Jul 2027, and the deadline is treated as a direction rather than a date: districts adopt when their boards can. Florida's college rule, adopted the same day, routes it to instructors through a default prohibition with override across 28 institutions. Neither says which governs a college course taught on a high school campus.

Beneath the rules sits configuration. A vendor default, like Gemini in Classroom switching on for students of all ages in August 2026, persists until an administrator builds a separate unit to contain it, while a district that disables features, as New York did across 38 contracts, has to renegotiate that choice at every renewal. Restrictions scoped to school devices leave the home laptop outside every rule.

Moratoria are written for one year, so each spring produces a committee rather than a settlement. The outcome evidence that could arbitrate between layers remains thin: the largest production-tutor trial measured 0.06 to 0.08 standard deviations a year, similar to practice without AI. With nothing decisive to cite, each layer governs by protection, integrity or contract, and the student absorbs the gaps between them.

## 6. Reflection & Implications – Questions This World Asks Us
- If a student learns more about which rule applies than about the subject the rule was written to protect, which outcome is the school actually producing?
- A parental opt-out is a real protection. What would it take for the signature to carry information as well as weight?
- When every instrument governs the tool, what would a rule look like that governed the conditions of being stuck instead?

## 7. Pullback Layer: From Possibility to Probability
### 7.1 Signals Emerging (Plausible Zone) – Early Signals We Already See
- New York City has suspended student-facing generative AI for grades 2-K to 8 for the 2026-27 school year, and it is doing so by switching off AI components inside 38 existing edtech contracts rather than blocking whole platforms (Sep 2026).
- Los Angeles Unified has restricted student generative AI on school-issued devices across all grades for 2026-27 and handed the question of later years to a committee (Sep 2026).
- Florida's State Board of Education adopted, on 16 Sep 2026, a K-12 rule built on parental notice, opt-out and non-AI alternatives, with district policies due 1 Jul 2027, and a college rule barring student AI on graded work across 28 institutions unless the instructor permits it.
- Gemini in Google Classroom became available by default to students of all ages in August 2026, and districts that had not already configured a separate unit for minors learned of it from news coverage.
- The University of Sydney and UNSW committed to all-student ChatGPT Edu within five days of each other in September 2026, while the staff union and student representatives said they had not been consulted.
- A two-year randomized trial of a production AI tutor across 18 Tennessee middle schools found gains of about 0.06 to 0.08 standard deviations per year, similar to practice without AI, published as a working paper in August 2026.

### 7.2 Probable Direction (Near-Term Future) – Where We're Likely Headed
The most likely near-term direction is that layering becomes formal rather than resolved. Large districts facing the end of one-year moratoria will renew in part: younger grades kept restricted, high school pilots widened, and the harder questions assigned to committees that report the following year. Florida's first school year under its amended rule, 2027-28, will show that parental opt-out is administrable on paper and uneven in classrooms, with some district policies adopted after the July deadline. Colleges will keep a default prohibition for graded work while universities elsewhere extend all-student access for learning, settling on a split in which students learn with AI and are assessed without it. Vendor defaults will change more often than rules, and each change will reach districts as news rather than as notice. Peer-reviewed outcome evidence will arrive slowly, so rules will continue to be justified on protection and integrity rather than on measured learning, and will move with elections, contracts and budgets.

### 7.3 Preferred Path (Intentional Future) – The Path We Could Choose Instead
- Individuals: parents treat the consent form as the start of a conversation with the teacher rather than its end, and ask what the non-AI alternative will actually be before choosing.
- Individuals: students keep one assignment a term in which they explain their own stuck point aloud to another person before using any tool at all.
- Organizations: districts publish a single precedence note for every course that sits under two rules, such as dual enrollment, before the term starts rather than after a grade appeal.
- Organizations: vendors ship new student-facing features switched off for minors by default and announce changes to districts before they reach classrooms.
- Society: moratoria are renewed or ended on published outcome evidence, and education funders pay for the peer-reviewed trials that would let rules cite learning rather than only protection.
- Society: universities negotiate AI terms with staff and student bodies before signing multi-year licences, so access decisions carry consent at the institutional layer too.

## 8. Connect to Today
### Skills We May Need
- Reading which rule is actually in force for this assignment, on this device, this week.
- Tolerating a stretch of confusion long enough for it to become understanding, without a tap to end it.
- Sitting beside someone who is stuck without taking the problem away from them.
- Asking an institution which of its rules wins when two of them disagree.
- Telling the difference between a protection and a decision that has simply been handed to you.

### Signals & Refractions
- The form is already real: Florida's amended rule guarantees parents notice, an opt-out and a non-AI alternative, and every district in the state has to write the policy that turns that into a sheet of paper by July 2027.
- The two computers are already real: restrictions in New York and Los Angeles govern school-issued devices, while the same month's default-on expansion of Gemini in Classroom reached students wherever no administrator had acted.
- The human is already the evidence: a Stanford research brief in August 2026 found strong evidence for AI tutoring only where humans remain central, and in one study it cited, 40 to 47 percent of students never used the platform at all.

## 9. Final Insight
A school year used to be the unit in which rules held still long enough to be learned once and then forgotten about. In this one, the rules ran on four calendars at once: a board that met when it could, a college that tightened after an appeal, a vendor that shipped in August, a committee that will report next fall. Learning runs on a fifth calendar, the slow one, measured in paragraphs that will not come right and then do. The rules were written to protect that calendar and mostly interrupted it, not by forbidding anything important but by making each week's permission a question to be answered before the work could start. The people who did the most for the fifth calendar that year were a classmate and a man in a parked truck, and neither had any say over the other four. In June, Ruben reads the new form in the truck cab and does not tick a box. He writes a question in the margin, asking who to call, and puts it in the glovebox to finish later.
