Structural Question:
Between now and late 2028, as the labels and marks attached to synthetic media come to depend on self-disclosure (a badge the creator adds) and on gated readers (a watermark only an approved detector can see), while the only penalty that actually operates is a platform withholding undisclosed content from people who do not already follow it, does an ordinary viewer's perception of what is synthetic come to be set by what their feed shows and withholds, and by whom they already follow, rather than by any label they can read, so that two people looking at the same account are no longer looking at the same thing?

Why It Matters:
The first platform-imposed consequence for AI content in this theme's record is a distribution rule, not a label: on 31 Aug 2026 Instagram replaced "AI creator" with an "AI-generated profile" badge and said undisclosed AI-generated profiles would be withheld from Reels, Explore and in-feed suggestions to non-followers until labeled, while CNET found none of six prominent AI influencer accounts labeled on launch day (source: https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/instagram-ai-generated-profile-label-fake-influencer-profiles-2/, Aug 2026). This event confirmed perception-2026-08-04 forward (a correction appended after Phase B), and the confirmation arrived through a penalty on non-disclosure rather than friction on labeled content, the opposite of the mechanism the claim was written to detect. In the same interval the marks that do exist became readable only to gated third parties: Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 shipped on 1 Sep 2026 watermarking text and files, with detection offered in private preview to regulators, media, fact-checkers and researchers (source: https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark, Sep 2026), which confirmed perception-2026-08-05 on a gated reading. Apple shipped iOS 27 on 14 Sep 2026 without the SynthID integration it had announced for it (source: https://www.macworld.com/article/2986799/ios-27-features-compatiblity-siri-ai-updates.html, Sep 2026), and the dominant visible user response to marking was stripping, with "AI watermark remover" searches up 60% week over week (source: https://gizmodo.com/people-are-rushing-to-find-ways-to-remove-claudes-ai-text-watermark-2000800433, Aug 2026). Against that, the statutory layer is moving toward visible, viewer-facing disclosure: Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT casebook refuses a hidden watermark alone for deepfakes and requires a visible mark across full playback (source: https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2026092412193369072, Sep 2026), and the EU's 2 Dec 2026 Article 50(2) marking date survived the AI Board's 17 Sep 2026 plenary with no further relief (source: https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article50-watermarking-deadline/, Sep 2026). The question is structural because it decides whether disclosure reaches the viewer as something they see or as something that silently shapes what they are shown, and it resolves on observable events inside the horizon: whether Meta extends the reach limit to Facebook or Threads (perception-2026-09-02), whether Korea's subcommittee approves a tampering sanction or a platform preservation duty (perception-2026-09-01), whether any Article 50 action names a provider or deployer (perception-2026-08-02), and whether SynthID reaches iOS in a point release (perception-2026-08-03).
