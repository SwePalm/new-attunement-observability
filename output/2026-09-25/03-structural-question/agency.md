# Structural question, agency, 2026-09-25

Structural Question:
Over the next 6 to 24 months, when a purchase an AI agent made is disputed in
the UK or US, which document will fix what the customer actually authorised
(the agent provider's mandate, the card network's intent record, the
merchant's agent-access terms, or a statute), and will the loss from an agent
acting inside its written limits but outside the customer's meaning be
allocated by network and provider rulebooks, defaulting to the cardholder,
before HM Treasury or any other Western authority states who is responsible?

Why It Matters:
The theme's centre moved in this run from containment incidents to payment
rails and access rights, and every new rail was built with the liability
question deliberately left open. Visa, Mastercard and Ant International
announced an interoperable Know Your Agent framework on 10 Sep 2026 with no
specification and with liability, revocation and dispute handling unaddressed,
and when Mastercard rolled out consumer Agent Pay on 18 Sep 2026 and was asked
who is liable when an agent exceeds instructions, it pointed to its Verifiable
Intent "paper trail" rather than to a rule (source:
https://www.pymnts.com/cybersecurity/2026/visa-mastercard-team-with-ant-know-your-agent-framework,
Sep 2026) (source:
https://fortune.com/2026/09/18/mastercard-visa-ai-shopping-payment-land-grab/,
Sep 2026). The attribution of action is already moving toward the person: the
Ninth Circuit's denial of rehearing in Amazon v. Perplexity leaves in place the
holding that the user, not the agent provider, "accesses" the site
(agency-2026-08-05 graded confirmed this run; source:
https://www.courthousenews.com/no-9th-circuit-rehearing-in-amazon-perplexity-case/,
Sep 2026). Meanwhile China's payment association answered the same question
the other way on 24 Aug 2026, "whoever provides the payment service is
responsible", with mandatory authorisation agreements defining the agent's
boundary (source: https://www.news.cn/20260824/4efa360edc8a486dbf0b19d0fb37ef41/c.html,
Aug 2026), while HM Treasury's consultation remains open to 6 Oct 2026 with
no response (agency-2026-08-01, open). The commerce claims have graded open
three times because volume is still absent (Visa's CEO, 8 Sep 2026), which
means the allocation rule is being written before the disputes arrive, by the
parties who will later adjudicate them.
