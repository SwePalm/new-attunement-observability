# The Outlook

## July to September 2026, debug draft

*A reading of where human and AI systems are heading, drawn from the past three months of research. It is not a prediction and not advice. The accountability record, how earlier claims have held up, lives in the companion Delta Report.*

---

In many small places at once, the benefit of the doubt is changing hands. When you said who you were, what you meant or what you wrote, the working assumption used to be that you were telling the truth. Over the summer of 2026 that order began to turn. The account a system produces about a person, an age estimate, a summary of an instruction, a score for a paragraph, a remembered profile, is increasingly the thing an institution acts on first. The person is left to rebut it.

Nobody decided this. It is arriving as a series of sensible fixes, each moving the presumption a little further from the person and toward the record made about them.

---

Start with what you meant. On 18 September Mastercard let cardholders hand an AI agent a virtual card with spending limits and an approval step. Asked who pays when an agent exceeds its instructions, the company pointed to its record of what the cardholder authorised, not to a rule. Days earlier a US appeals court declined to revisit its holding that when an agent browses a shop, it is the user, not the agent's maker, who acts. Both point the loss at the person, though the mandate was drafted by the agent, and, as one payments firm put it, proving an agent is legitimate does not show it did what the customer asked. Picture a woman who tells her assistant to reorder "the same batteries as last March, not the cheap ones", and whose approved mandate reads "same or equivalent". Her tap of approval becomes the evidence against her. What grows scarce is having your own words read back and taken as the measure.

Then who you are. From 15 September pubs and shops in England and Wales may accept digital ID for age checks. On 17 September the European Commission proposed age verification at account opening for chatbots and AI companions. OpenAI already routes anyone its system predicts to be under eighteen into a teen version of ChatGPT, and has published no figure for how often that prediction is wrong. Meanwhile, on 22 September, a start-up raised 35 million dollars to issue the passes agents show banks to prove they act for someone. The person with a face, a voice and a history is asked to prove more at each counter, while the agent with none of those carries a pass designed to clear. What grows scarce is being taken as yourself without first producing proof.

Then what you wrote. On 21 September an anonymous account posted detector scans of passages from Thélyson Orélien's novel on the day it won a French prize, and the post drew close to three million views within hours. By 24 September his Quebec publisher had suspended promotion; on 25 September the Académie Goncourt withdrew the book from its first selection, citing plagiarism in the author's earlier texts and "convergent analyses" that the novel was largely machine-written, days after its president had said irrefutable proof would be needed. A researcher who has tested the detector says false accusations are rare but that changing a few words turns a passage fully human, so it mostly catches text nobody troubled to disguise. The author dates his first manuscript to 2019. Whatever the truth of this case, the order is now visible: the number arrives before the reading. What grows scarce is the unscanned first reading, a stranger taking your words at face value before asking how they were made.

Then what you said. ChatGPT's new privacy centre, rolling out from late September, tells users that deleting a conversation does not remove what memory saved from it. What an assistant believes about you is a paraphrase it keeps rewriting. The machines do not check their own records either: on 24 September Darktrace showed that four widely used coding-agent tools accepted fabricated conversation history as genuine, and the fix it recommends is one only the providers can deploy. What grows scarce is one sentence you actually said, dated, that nothing has since rewritten.

And what you are shown. On 31 August Instagram began badging "AI-generated profiles" and withholding undisclosed ones from people who do not already follow them; that day, reporters found none of six prominent AI influencer accounts labelled. Within about a week of Anthropic announcing its text watermark, a tool for stripping such marks had passed 15,000 stars on GitHub, while the detector that reads the mark is open only to vetted organisations. Labels land mostly on the candid, and a student who used an assistant only for commas learns to strip the mark. What grows scarce is the same screen: knowing that the person beside you is being shown what you are.

The thread through all five is one demotion: a person's own account has moved from the starting point to one piece of evidence among several, usually the slowest to retrieve.

---

The reasons are ordinary, which is why they hold. A record is cheap to consult and a person is expensive: someone has to find the voice note, read the novel, know the face. Once voices and faces can be faked, stacking checks on customers looks prudent, and no one is paid to retire the old ones. Liability follows the record, because the record is what a dispute can see. People accept the trade because it brings real protection for children, real convenience, and an approval button that feels like control. Visa's chief executive said on 8 September that three in four people surveyed distrust agents with authority to pay; the industry's answer was an approval step, which is also a signature. And every check is sized to its own risk, so nobody sees the whole day in which one person proves themselves again and again.

The strain shows where institutions must decide how much a machine's account weighs. Within days the Goncourt abandoned the standard of proof its own president had just set out; a book fair that had withdrawn the author's guest-of-honour title the day before said plagiarism alone drove its decision; the novel stayed on the lists of the Médicis and the Femina. One set of facts, several weightings of one number. It shows, too, where the record cannot reach people. On 16 September Malawi launched a national digital identity wallet and, the same day, abolished expiry dates on physical ID cards, in a country where nearly everyone holds the card and, by its own minister's account, about a third of people own a smartphone. The older proof was kept valid alongside the new.

---

Much of the machinery looks hard to reverse: Germany's state wallet is due on 2 January 2027, EU marking duties bite on 2 December 2026, and agent credentials are going into the card networks. What is not settled is which way the presumption runs, and here the evidence is genuinely mixed. Some rules place the burden on the system. The Commission's KIDS proposal would make the largest platforms prove their services are safe by design. From 9 December 2026 Europe's revised product liability regime lets courts presume a defect when a producer fails to disclose evidence. China's payment association, on 24 August, made whoever provides the payment service responsible for agent payments. California bills awaiting the governor by 30 September would require a human to corroborate an automated system before a worker is disciplined, and Colorado's right to human review of adverse automated decisions begins on 1 January 2027. Several may stall or soften.

The choices that matter in the next two years are narrow: whether dispute systems learn to count purchases made within the limits but against the instruction; whether a wallet can lend a bounded mandate instead of a whole identity; whether a detector's verdict must come with a stated error rate before it can cost someone a prize, a grade or a job; whether a remembered profile keeps the original beside the paraphrase.

---

For the people building these systems: when your product writes a summary of what someone wanted, who answers for the summary? If your records become the evidence, would you show the person the words they started from?

For the people who make rules: where a system's account and a person's account disagree, who should have to prove it, and should the answer differ at a till, a bank, a school and a prize jury? Is a check that is never retired still a safeguard?

For the rest of us: if you had to prove you meant what you said, what would you show? Which accounts of yourself still exist only in your own words, and who reads them first?
