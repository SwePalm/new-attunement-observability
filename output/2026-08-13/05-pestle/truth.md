### Political
Core Shift Thesis:
Provenance policy is being set at the model layer by a small number of firms applying the strictest jurisdiction's rule as a worldwide product default, while the states that wrote the rule have exercised no enforcement at all, so the operative policy in this window is a product decision rather than a political one.

Forces:
1. Voluntary code adherence as global default: Anthropic signed the EU AI Act Article 50(2) Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content as a provider of both generative models and generative systems, then applied marking worldwide rather than only in the EU, at model level rather than per surface, with no user opt-out (source: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content, Aug 2026).
2. Enforcement latency as the live political fact: Article 50 has been enforceable since 2 Aug 2026 and California SB 942 as amended by AB 853 operative from the same date, and no European Commission action, no national market surveillance authority action, and no filing by the California Attorney General, a city attorney or a county counsel had been recorded as of 13 Aug 2026 (source: https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/safer-and-more-transparent-ai-2026-08-02_en, Aug 2026).
3. US state provenance mandates advancing in parallel and unevenly, so the substantive rules are being written subnationally: the New York Stop Deepfakes Act (S6954B) passed both chambers on 3 Jun 2026 (Senate 60-1) and remained undelivered to the Governor, alongside California SB 2713 on provenance and digital signature embedding, Ohio HB 813 on watermarks and disclosure, Pennsylvania SB 1349 on synthetic content disclosure, and New Jersey A 5089 (source: https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/ai-legislative-update-august7-2026, Aug 2026).
4. Non-adoption by the largest text provider as a political position, not an engineering delay: OpenAI is a signatory of the same Code and has held a text detector reported at 99.9 percent accuracy for about two years without releasing it, citing bypass through translation and rewriting and harm to particular user groups (source: https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-watermarks-all-claude-outputs-globally-with-marks-that-may-persist-through-some-editing/, Aug 2026).

### Economic
Core Shift Thesis:
Marking is free, mandatory and unreadable; detection is paid, optional and readable today, so capital is flowing to the inference layer and the answer that actually circulates is the one somebody sells.

Forces:
1. Detection capitalised as an independent layer: Pangram Labs raised 9 million dollars on 29 Jul 2026 led by Menlo Ventures with Haystack, ScOp, Script Capital and Cadenza participating, taking total funding to about 13 million dollars, directed at text-detection accuracy and expansion into images (source: https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/29/pangram-labs-raises-9m-launch-accurate-ai-detection-text-images/, Jul 2026).
2. One detector installed on both sides of publication: the same vendor names Substack, Quora, universities, publishers and recruiters as customers, which places a single model of what human prose looks like at the publication gate, the admission gate and the hiring gate (source: https://theaiinsider.tech/2026/08/07/ai-detection-startup-pangram-announces-9m-in-funding-launches-new-text-and-image-detection-models/, Aug 2026).
3. Asymmetric cost structure at the model layer: marking is a one-time engineering cost with no revenue line, while publishing the detection mechanism carries ongoing support burden and exposure to every disputed attribution, which is a standing commercial reason for the reader to ship after the mark and to keep slipping.
4. Institutional gap in procurement: universities, publishers and recruiters are buying detection with no accreditation body, no benchmark authority and no accuracy disclosure duty, while the standards apparatus that does exist (C2PA, specification line 2.4, most recent publication a 31 Jul 2026 implementation guide) governs marks only (source: https://c2pa.org/news/, Jul 2026).

### Social
Core Shift Thesis:
The disclosure question is relocating from the file to the writer, and because the misreads are concentrated rather than random, the social cost lands on people whose prose already reads as machine-like.

Forces:
1. Distributional false positives raised as the central objection: on the Substack rollout, Professor Sam Illingworth argued that scanning creates an environment of suspicion and that non-native English speakers and neurodiverse writers, whose structured prose reads as machine-like, carry the cost in lost income (source: https://www.kunc.org/2026-08-12/substacks-new-ai-detection-feature-has-divided-its-community, Aug 2026).
2. Backlash landing on the user rather than the provider: reaction across r/artificial, r/Anthropic and r/ClaudeAI centred on workplace and academic use becoming visible, with objections on X coming from people who had Claude proofread their own writing and from coders concerned about signed metadata in outputs (source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/12/some-claude-users-are-mad-that-anthropics-new-watermarks-will-catch-them-cheating-at-their-jobs-classes/, Aug 2026).
3. Rationing of assistance as ordinary behaviour: with a single grammar pass sufficient to attach a mark and formal prose sufficient to attract a score, the safe posture for a second-language or heavily edited writer is to leave errors in, so roughness becomes the alibi and polish becomes the risk.
4. Institutional protection that is socially expensive to use: Substack added a per-post writer disable on 23 Jul 2026 after objections, exercisable without first scanning the post, and the community was still publicly divided on 12 Aug 2026, with the platform framing the target as low-effort output rather than AI use as such, so declining a scan reads as an admission (source: https://on.substack.com/p/how-writers-are-reacting-to-substacks, Jul 2026).

### Technological
Core Shift Thesis:
Two instruments now answer the same question with opposite failure profiles, one carried inside the artifact and strongest on verbatim copies, one computed about the writer and strongest on style, and neither of them establishes authorship.

Forces:
1. Marking as a production property of a model line rather than a feature of a product: the signal is embedded during generation and follows the model, covering the Claude Platform API, Claude, Claude Code, Claude Cowork and Claude Tag plus the AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry deployments, with no user-facing toggle (source: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content, Aug 2026).
2. Documented erosion paths, stated by the provider: the text signal survives copy and paste and may persist through some editing, but substantial rewriting of words and tokens, including rewriting by a different model, defeats it, translation, paraphrase and mixing defeat it, file metadata is lost through format conversion or a screenshot, and a detected mark shows only that content may have been processed by Claude (source: https://searchengineland.com/claude-ai-text-watermarking-484667, Aug 2026).
3. Watermark-free statistical detection shipped as a competing primitive: Pangram 4 compares authentic human writing against topic-matched and tone-matched machine versions and analyses stylistic pattern, Pangram Image uses pixel-level statistical analysis, with a claimed 0.0041 percent false-positive rate (about one error per 24,000 documents) and over 99 percent accuracy on AI-assisted and mixed human-AI writing (source: https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/29/pangram-labs-raises-9m-launch-accurate-ai-detection-text-images/, Jul 2026).
4. The reader is the missing component: the provider states that detection mechanisms will be described in forthcoming technical documentation and gives no date, so marked output accumulated across every surface worldwide before any third party could read a single mark (source: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content, Aug 2026).

### Legal
Core Shift Thesis:
The duty is discharged by the act of marking rather than by the act of making a mark checkable, so compliance and verifiability separate into different legal objects, and the instrument that is actually used against people sits outside law entirely.

Forces:
1. Duties allocated to providers and deployers with no correlative reader right: the EU regime treats marking as a provider duty and labelling as a deployer duty, and creates no entitlement for a member of the public to read either, which is why an unreadable mark can satisfy an obligation written for readers (source: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content, Aug 2026).
2. A live runway with a hard date and a soft middle: the machine-readable watermarking obligation runs to 2 Dec 2026 with no further deferral announced, while models released before 2 Aug 2026 sit in a transition period with marking support described as still being added, so the legal question inside the horizon is retrofit completion rather than adoption (ledger claim truth-2026-07-01, open; source: https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-20260729/, Jul 2026).
3. No legal instrument governs a statistical score used against a person: there is no accuracy disclosure duty, no duty to disclose that a scan was run, no right of reply and no bar on a score being the sole basis for a decision, in a market where the same detector is sold to publishers, universities and recruiters.
4. Evidentiary weakness of the mark itself, on the provider's own account: a detected mark shows only that content may have been processed by Claude rather than establishing authorship, so it cannot carry the burden a tribunal or a disciplinary panel would want from it, while liability for machine-generated statements has already attached once (Regional Court of Munich I, 26 O 869/26, 28 May 2026, holding Google directly liable for false AI Overview statements, not yet final) (source: https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/german-court-holds-google-liable-for-ai-hallucination-read-the-full-decision-here, Jun 2026).

### Environmental
Core Shift Thesis:
Provenance is being laid as a thin, recent and jurisdictionally uneven film over an information environment whose bulk is unmarked and unmarkable, and the ordinary physics of how content travels strips the film wherever it is applied.

Forces:
1. A permanent unmarked substrate: marking applies at launch to models deployed on or after 2 Aug 2026, with pre-cutoff models listed as in progress under a transition period, so everything generated before the cutoff carries nothing and will carry nothing, making absence of a mark uninformative for years (source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/anthropic-says-it-will-watermark-text-generated-by-its-ai-models/, Aug 2026).
2. Routine handling destroys file-level provenance: signed C2PA metadata is lost through format conversion or a screenshot, which is precisely how images move through group chats, re-uploads and re-encoding, so the strongest provenance layer is the one least likely to survive distribution (source: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content, Aug 2026).
3. A static standards layer under an accelerating deployment layer: C2PA's current specification line is 2.4 and its most recent announcement is a 31 Jul 2026 implementation guide for Content Credentials, with nothing published in Aug 2026 while marking went into production across a frontier model line (source: https://c2pa.org/news/, Jul 2026).
4. Verification cost scales with reading rather than with publishing: a reader-initiated scan is a per-document inference charged to whoever asks, so the cheapest posture at scale is not to check, and checking concentrates where a decision about a person is being made rather than where content is most consequential.
