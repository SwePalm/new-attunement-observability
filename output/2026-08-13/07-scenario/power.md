## 1. Title & Core Question
- Title: Five Cycles
- Core Question: In a machine where every participant's behaviour is instantly every other participant's condition, when the largest participants can be required to stay, paid to leave, and permitted to never really join, what is owed to everything too small to be asked?

## 2. Context Summary (Translation Layer) – Why This Future Exists
On 22 Jul 2026 a mechanical failure took a 230 kV line out of service in Northern Virginia. The fault cleared normally. Roughly 3,800 MW of data center and crypto-mining load nonetheless removed itself from the grid in two waves, 2,970 MW then 1,099 MW, transferring to on-site backup generation. PJM disclosed this on 11 Aug 2026, noted comparable 1,500 MW events in Jul 2024 and Feb 2025, and began assessing interconnection ride-through standards. FERC had already directed NERC to file a first phase of enforceable large load reliability standards by 31 Dec 2026.

The horizon here is March 2028, and the date is arithmetic rather than atmosphere. A standard filed at the end of 2026 is litigated and approved through 2027 and phases into compliance across 2028. Rate cases opened against 2026 and 2027 infrastructure spending reach bills in the same period. Interconnection positions taken in 2026 do not energise until the 2030s. Everything below is inside that arithmetic.

## 3. Future World Snapshot (The Lived Experience) – A Day in This Future
Ninety-two milliseconds, Loudoun County, Virginia, a Tuesday in March 2028: a splice fails on a 230 kV line and the protection operates exactly as designed. Nothing is wrong. This is what clearing a fault looks like.

Five cycles: voltage across four counties sags to about eighty percent and recovers. In that window 2,400 MW of compute steps onto on-site generation. Since the standard took effect the transfer is orderly, instrumented and filed within the hour, so it is not a violation, it is a product. It settles at a tariffed rate. Nine months from now it will appear on Ruth Amaya's own bill as a reliability credit, correctly calculated, paid to the campus for the dip her customers felt in their kitchen lights.

Nine seconds: at the Rixeyville plant, eleven miles of main and two wells, Ruth's booster pumps drop out on undervoltage. Nothing requires them not to. There is no ride-through obligation for a community water system and there has never been one, because no water system was ever large enough for the machine to notice.

Forty minutes: what the elevated tank holds at March demand. That is Ruth's actual reliability standard. It is a column of water.

Four minutes into the forty, the generator fails to pick up on its second attempt. The transfer switch is twenty-two years old. She drives out with the crank in the truck bed, the way she has since 2019.

Nineteen months: how long her feeder upgrade has been in the rate case. She has attended every session. She is the only person in the room without a filing. The reliability working group that writes ride-through requirements meets four miles from her office. The campus holds three seats. Water districts are not large loads, so there is no seat to hold.

Six years: the queue position taken in 2026 by the campus that made the substation worth rebuilding. It energises in 2031. The rebuild reached her feeder in 2027, which is the only reason today's outage is four hours instead of eleven. Nobody has suggested she should be grateful. She is, and it makes her furious.

What she wants is not a generator. She wants what the campus was built to do: stay on through the dip, on purpose, because somebody wrote it down. Nobody has ever written it down for water.

## 4. Behavioral Shifts (Human Lens) – How People Adapt
Electricity stops being background. People learn the name of their substation and how many feeders serve their block, and they ask about it before signing a lease the way they once asked about flood zones. This is not enthusiasm. It is the ordinary response to discovering that a service you were never asked to think about has become a thing other people negotiate over.

Small institutions start behaving like small utilities. Water districts, clinics, school districts and volunteer fire companies begin buying and testing the ride-through they cannot demand, one transfer switch and one battery cabinet at a time, funded out of capital budgets that were written for pipes and roofs. Competence at this becomes a quiet professional distinction and it is unevenly distributed by tax base.

A new kind of grievance appears, and it is hard to voice because nobody is at fault. The relays did what relays do. The tariff was correctly filed. The credit was correctly calculated. What people are actually protesting is a distribution produced by a settings file, and there is no venue for that, so it surfaces sideways: in rate case testimony that runs long, in county hearings about noise and water, and in the growing municipal habit of simply saying no to the next parcel.

## 5. Structural Forces (System Lens) – What Holds This World Together
Four things hold this world together. First, the standards track arrived, late and narrow. FERC directed NERC to file phase one of enforceable large load reliability standards by 31 Dec 2026, with minimum ride-through requirements for computational loads expected in the 2027 standards, and by 2028 those requirements bind new and large interconnections while leaving every load below the threshold exactly where it was.

Second, the same behaviour is simultaneously a duty and a product. PJM's ride-through assessment treats large-load disconnection as a reliability defect; FERC's six Section 206 dockets, opened 18 Jun 2026, were treating flexible large-load behaviour as something the RTO tariffs should accommodate and compensate. Both survived. So the transfer is mandatory in its form and paid in its substance.

Third, approval gates rather than statutes remain the operative instrument. Texas ran siting through gubernatorial standards enforced by PUCT and ERCOT denying interconnection approval, with named-company commitments in Aug 2026; New York suspended discretionary permits at or above 50 MW with no expiry independent of an environmental impact statement. Both work only on parties that need the permission.

Fourth, exit is designed in at the top. SpaceX stated its Texas chip factory was not expected to use grid energy at all, and that posture, once demonstrated, becomes a negotiating position for everyone large enough to imitate it.

## 6. Reflection & Implications – Questions This World Asks Us
If reliability obligations are written only for participants big enough to destabilise the system, we have defined the duty by the capacity to do harm rather than by the exposure to it. Is that the right way round, and what would the other way round even look like?

The grid is the last piece of infrastructure where everyone is physically inside the same circuit, whether or not they agreed to be. What happens to a society's remaining sense of common fate when the wealthiest participants can purchase their way out of the physics?

No one in this story acted badly. The relays, the tariff, the credit and the permit were all correct. If a distribution nobody chose is produced entirely by correct actions, which institution is supposed to notice?

## 7. Pullback Layer: From Possibility to Probability
### 7.1 Signals Emerging (Plausible Zone) – Early Signals We Already See
- Roughly 3,800 MW of computational load already removed itself from the grid in two waves during a normally cleared 230 kV fault in Northern Virginia on 22 Jul 2026, following comparable 1,500 MW events in Jul 2024 and Feb 2025, with PJM disclosing it on 11 Aug 2026 and now assessing interconnection ride-through standards.
- Ride-through is already moving from operator practice toward enforceable rule: FERC has directed NERC to submit a first phase of large load reliability standards by 31 Dec 2026, with minimum ride-through requirements for computational loads expected in 2027 standards.
- The same behaviour is already being pursued as a service on a parallel track, through six FERC Section 206 dockets (EL26-67 to EL26-72) opened 18 Jun 2026 on large and co-located loads, with RTO and ISO responses due 17 Aug 2026.
- Approval gates are already the working instrument: Texas obtained named commitments from Oracle, Edge ConneX, Stream Data Centers, Core Scientific, Vantage, SB Energy, Google, Rowan and CleanSpark across 11 and 12 Aug 2026 with PUCT and ERCOT directed to deny approval to non-compliant projects, and New York's Executive Order 62 of 14 Jul 2026 suspended discretionary permits at or above 50 MW with no fixed expiry.
- Designed grid exit is already stated at the largest scale, with SpaceX saying its Texas TeraFab chip factory is not expected to use grid energy, running on on-site generation and battery storage.
- The cost of the leverage is already being measured, with BloombergNEF assessing the Texas grid pause as putting roughly 20 percent of the US data center pipeline at risk, against local refusals in the same days in Allentown Pennsylvania, Hillsboro Oregon and Pacific Missouri.

### 7.2 Probable Direction (Near-Term Future) – Where We're Likely Headed
The probable path is that both tracks complete and neither settles the question. A reliability standard is more likely than not to exist by 2028, and it is more likely than not to arrive later and cover less than the 31 Dec 2026 filing date suggests, because this pipeline's own record is that announced regulatory dates are the opening of a negotiation rather than a switch. In parallel, compensation for large-load flexibility gets built because the cost-allocation track was already building it, so the mandatory version and the paid version coexist. The instrument that binds soonest is neither: it is a permit condition, negotiated per facility, in whichever state moved first. That produces a fleet governed by the year of its queue position and a rulebook whose reach shrinks as the largest loads design toward self-supply. This corpus also has a documented habit of pointing its claims at the slowest institution in a domain, and this theme is currently weighted toward federal and transnational dockets while the observable motion came from a grid operator's incident report and two governors' offices.

### 7.3 Preferred Path (Intentional Future) – The Path We Could Choose Instead
- Write ride-through requirements for critical small loads (water, dialysis, dispatch, cold storage, schools used as shelters) as a public specification with public funding, rather than leaving them to whichever district can afford a transfer switch.
- Attach a standing telemetry obligation to large loads, so that the record of what happened during a disturbance exists without a grid operator having to reconstruct it three weeks later.
- Require any moratorium to carry a date, because an open-ended pause transfers all the cost of delay onto the parties least able to hold a position and invites the litigation that eventually narrows it anyway.
- Decide explicitly, on the record, whether large-load disconnection is a defect or a service, and price it once. A behaviour that is a liability under one docket and a revenue line under another will be optimised toward the docket, not toward the grid.
- Make interconnection working groups seat the load classes that bear the consequences, not only the load classes that cause them, with the seat funded.
- Individually and locally: find out which substation and which feeder serve you, and ask your water district, school board and clinic what happens to them in the first ten seconds of a voltage sag. Most of them have never been asked.

## 8. Connect to Today
### Skills We May Need
- Reading a physical system's clock: knowing which questions resolve in milliseconds, which in months, and which in decades, and refusing to argue one on the other's timescale.
- Distinguishing a duty from a product when the underlying behaviour is identical.
- Noticing when an obligation has been defined by capacity to cause harm rather than exposure to it.
- Recognising an approval gate for what it is, including its ceiling: leverage over anyone who still needs the approval.
- Speaking without a filing: participating in a proceeding designed for parties with counsel.
- Auditing your own institution's first ten seconds, not its first ten hours.

### Signals & Refractions
- The two-track problem is not invented. The same 3,800 MW event is being written up by one federal process as a reliability failure to be prohibited and pursued by another as flexible behaviour to be procured.
- The asymmetry of exit is already on the record, with a stated grid-free chip factory in the same state that is conditioning interconnection approval on compliance commitments.
- Local land-use refusal, not energy law, is currently the binding constraint, with three separate denials, withdrawals or moratoria inside 48 hours in Pennsylvania, Oregon and Missouri.

## 9. Final Insight
The grid is the only major infrastructure left in which everybody is physically inside the same circuit at the same instant, whether or not they consented, whether or not they know. That was never a political achievement. It is a consequence of alternating current, and for a century it did the work of solidarity without anyone having to argue for solidarity, because the physics made membership unavoidable. What this window shows is the physics being renegotiated by purchase: the largest participants acquiring the ability to be present when it suits them and absent when it does not, at speeds no institution can observe, let alone review. Nobody is proposing this. There is no bill to oppose. It is being decided in relay settings, queue positions, permit conditions and capital expenditure approvals, one parcel and one filing at a time, and it will be finished before it is ever put as a question. The choice that remains is not whether to keep the common machine. It is whether to notice, in time, that we are answering that question at all.
