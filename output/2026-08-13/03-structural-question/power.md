Structural Question:
Over the next 6 to 24 months, when several thousand megawatts of computational load drop off the grid during a disturbance, will the American system classify that behaviour as a reliability failure to be prohibited by enforceable standard, or as a flexible-load service to be procured and paid for, and will the classification be made on the record by the bodies with jurisdiction (FERC, NERC, the RTOs) or settled first by governors' compliance pledges, interconnection conditions and on-site generation decisions that reach the outcome before any docket closes?

Why It Matters:
This run's confirmed record contains a single physical event that both answers are already being built on: on 22 Jul 2026 a mechanical failure removed a 230 kV line in Northern Virginia, the fault cleared normally, and roughly 3,800 MW of data center and crypto-mining load nonetheless self-disconnected to on-site backup generation in two waves (2,970 MW then 1,099 MW), following comparable 1,500 MW events in Jul 2024 and Feb 2025, with PJM disclosing this on 11 Aug 2026 and now assessing interconnection ride-through standards while FERC has directed NERC to submit a first phase of enforceable large load reliability standards by 31 Dec 2026 (source: https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-dominion-review-large-load-transfer-event/, Aug 2026). The event is genuinely bivalent and the sweep names this as its central contradiction: read as a reliability failure it justifies binding ride-through obligations on computational load, and read as demand response it is precisely the flexible behaviour FERC's six Section 206 large load dockets have been trying to procure from the RTOs, so the same 3,800 MW is either a violation to be standardised away or a service to be compensated, and nothing in the physics distinguishes them. Meanwhile the channel that actually moved in this window was not the one the theme's claims are pointed at: this run's grading pass records that the only ledger movement in nine days came from Colorado's state administrative calendar (proposed ADMT rules filed 11 Aug 2026, comments to 26 Oct 2026), while FERC show-cause responses were not due until 17 Aug 2026, H.R. 9340 sat through August recess after clearing House Energy and Commerce 52-0 on 22 Jul 2026, and the Cloud and AI Development Act remained at examination stage, so three of four of this theme's newest claims could not have moved at all, and the observable events of the window were instead a grid operator's incident disclosure and two governors acting by executive instrument (Abbott extracting named-company commitments from Oracle, Edge ConneX, Stream Data Centers, Core Scientific, Vantage, SB Energy, Google, Rowan and CleanSpark across 11 and 12 Aug 2026 with PUCT and ERCOT directed to deny approval to non-compliant projects; New York Executive Order 62 of 14 Jul 2026 suspending discretionary permits at or above 50 MW with no fixed expiry).
