# Evidence sweep, governance, 2026-08-13

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The nine days since 2026-08-04 produced one qualifying instrument event worldwide: Colorado HB 26-1263 took effect on 12 August 2026. The US tracker used by the previous sweep lists no other federal or state development dated 4 to 13 August 2026, and its next tracked milestone is 1 September 2026 (source: https://vorplabs.com/ai-regulatory-updates/united-states, Aug 2026).
- Still no named Article 50 action. Eleven days after the AI Office gained enforcement powers, the Commission's own news feed shows no AI Act proceeding, information request, or fine against any identified provider, so governance-2026-08-02 remains untriggered on primary evidence rather than on absence of search (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news, Aug 2026).
- Two instruments the previous sweep missed entirely are now in the corpus: the California AI Transparency Act became operative on 2 August 2026, the same day as Article 50 and covering the same marking problem, and the EU's only platform enforcement in the period ran through the DSA against TikTok on 24 July 2026. Both sit outside the AI Act instrument set the ledger is written against.
- Supervisory capacity data has not moved. The national implementation tracker still reports the 17 June 2026 designation counts and was not refreshed through the Article 50 commencement, so the previous sweep's counter-signal stands unchanged rather than confirmed anew (source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/national-implementation-plans/, Jun 2026).

Confirmed Developments:
- The California AI Transparency Act (SB 942 as amended by AB 853) became operative on 2 August 2026, requiring generative AI providers with more than one million monthly users accessible in California to embed latent machine-readable disclosures, offer users a manifest visible disclosure option, and provide a free public AI detection tool accepting uploads and offering API access, enforced by the Attorney General with no private right of action at USD 5,000 per violation with each day a discrete violation; large online platform and hosting platform duties follow on 1 January 2027 and capture device manufacturer duties on 1 January 2028 (source: https://www.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB853, Oct 2025) (source: https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/08/new-california-ai-disclosure-rules-become-operative, Aug 2026)
- Colorado HB 26-1263, the Conversational Artificial Intelligence Service Operator Requirements act signed 29 May 2026, took effect on 12 August 2026, the only AI statute anywhere with an effective date inside this sweep window; its substantive operator duties (commercially reasonable age estimation, minor-specific disclosure, prohibition on engagement rewards and on generating sexual content or simulated emotional dependence for minors, self-harm response protocols, a bar on claiming licensed-professional equivalence, and annual reporting to the attorney general) apply from 1 January 2027 (source: https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb26-1263, May 2026) (source: https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/watershed-year-for-chatbot-safety-measures-14-new-state-laws-enacted-so-far-in-2026, Jul 2026)
- The European Commission issued preliminary findings against TikTok on 24 July 2026 under Article 28(1) of the Digital Services Act, finding that minors can set accounts to public and that content from 16-17 year olds is recommended through the For You Feed to any user including non-account holders, with exposure to a fine of up to 6 percent of global annual turnover if a non-compliance decision follows (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-preliminary-finds-tiktok-breach-digital-services-act-failing-ensure-safe-accounts-minors, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/eu-commission-finds-tiktok-failed-to-protect-minors-privacy-under-digital-services-act/, Jul 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- The fourth GPAI Signatory Taskforce readout, published 3 August 2026, records the AI Office discussing "marginal-risk" clauses that would let providers match competitors' unsafe deployments under exceptional circumstances subject to evidentiary and procedural safeguards, a competitive-parity exception being negotiated inside a voluntary code rather than in statute (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/fourth-gpai-signatory-taskforce-meeting, Aug 2026)
- California SB 1000 is pending and would remove the one-million-user threshold from the AI Transparency Act, extending marking and detection duties to providers far below the frontier tier the act currently reaches (source: https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/08/new-california-ai-disclosure-rules-become-operative, Aug 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- The EU's binding AI instrument is idle while adjacent platform instruments carry the enforcement load: between 31 July and 13 August 2026 the Commission's AI-related output was a Code of Practice signatory tally and a taskforce readout, while its actual enforcement in the surrounding period was a DSA preliminary finding against TikTok and an EUR 890 million DMA fine against Google on 23 July 2026 (source: https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/commission-fines-google-eur890-million-breaches-digital-markets-act-2026-07-23_en, Jul 2026)
- Member State supervisory capacity remains as thin as at the commencement date, with 9 of 27 Member States having designated both a market surveillance authority and a notifying authority, 12 partial and 6 with neither, against a legal deadline of 2 August 2025 (source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/national-implementation-plans/, Jun 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- Texas TRAIGA (HB 149) Section 8 sets a hard 1 September 2026 deadline for the Texas Attorney General to post on its website the online mechanism through which consumers may submit AI complaints under Section 552.102, the first dedicated US state intake channel for AI complaints and a duty falling on the regulator rather than on industry (source: https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB00149F.htm, Jun 2025) (source: https://vorplabs.com/ai-regulatory-updates/united-states, Aug 2026)
- Post-commencement practitioner guidance restates the Article 50(2) transitional relief without change: providers of generative AI systems already on the EU market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 to comply with marking and detection duties, while systems placed on the market after that date were bound immediately, and no Commission signal of further deferral has appeared (source: https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-03-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-take-effect-2-august-2026, Aug 2026)

Capital Movements:
- None

Technical Changes:
- California's operative regime specifies the marking stack at statute level rather than by guidance: latent disclosures must carry the provider name, system name and version, and the time and date of content creation, and the free detection tool must accept user content uploads and expose an API, putting a US state marking mandate into force roughly four months ahead of the EU's 2 December 2026 Article 50(2) marking date (source: https://www.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB853, Oct 2025) (source: https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/08/new-california-ai-disclosure-rules-become-operative, Aug 2026)

Contradictions:
- Secondary commentary surfacing in this window asserts that the AI Office issued roughly EUR 47 million in Article 50 penalties against three companies, including an EUR 15 million fine against a European retail chain for in-store emotion recognition. No such action appears on the Commission's news feed, and the page the assertion traces back to does not contain the claim when opened. Treated as fabricated and excluded. Either item, if accepted, would have wrongly confirmed governance-2026-08-02.
- Two of the three US instruments now in this theme's corpus separate the date a law takes effect from the date its duties bind: Colorado HB 26-1263 took effect 12 August 2026 with operator duties from 1 January 2027, and Illinois SB 315 starts the disclosure filing duty on 1 January 2027 with framework and audit duties on 1 January 2028. A sweep keyed to effective dates over-reads both.
- California and the EU regulate the same object, machine-readable provenance on synthetic content, on incompatible clocks and with incompatible triggers: California binds by monthly user count with a fixed per-day penalty, the EU binds by role in the value chain with turnover-proportionate fines, and the two marking deadlines sit four months apart. Providers serving both markets face a single technical artifact governed by two non-aligned mandates.
- Article 50 has been enforceable since 2 August 2026 with fines to EUR 15 million or 3 percent of worldwide turnover, and the AI Office holds directly usable information-request and model-access powers, yet the conduct most visibly analogous to an Article 50 deepfake-labelling failure is being pursued under the DSA instead.
