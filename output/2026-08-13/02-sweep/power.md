# Evidence sweep, power, 2026-08-13

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- A new axis opened that no prior sweep carried: large load reliability behavior. PJM disclosed on 11 Aug 2026 that roughly 3,800 MW of Dominion-zone computational load self-disconnected during a normally cleared 230 kV fault on 22 Jul 2026, and is now evaluating ride-through requirements. Every previous sweep framed the grid question as whether utilities can serve data centers; this reframes it as whether data centers behave as grid citizens.
- Governors moved through executive and permitting leverage rather than statute, in the window and just before it. Texas announced rolling per-company commitments to Governor Abbott's Data Center Standards on 11 and 12 Aug 2026. New York's Executive Order 62 imposed a moratorium on discretionary permits for facilities at or above 50 MW on 14 Jul 2026; that order is real and primary-verified but predates the 2026-08-04 sweep, which missed it, so it is new to this corpus rather than new to the world.
- The federal and EU tracks this theme's open claims are weighted toward produced nothing in nine days. FERC RTO/ISO show cause responses are not due until 17 Aug 2026, H.R. 9340 sat through August recess after its 22 Jul committee vote, and the Cloud and AI Development Act remains at examination stage. Three of four newest claims could not have moved.
- Local refusal continued at the Q1 2026 pace with three separate in-window events (Allentown PA denial 13 Aug, Pacific MO withdrawal 12 Aug, Hillsboro OR 120-day moratorium 12 Aug), consistent with rather than additive to the previous sweep's counter-signal.

Confirmed Developments:
- Texas converted data center siting into a gubernatorial compliance regime, with Governor Abbott announcing named-company commitments to his Data Center Standards on 11 and 12 Aug 2026 (Oracle, Edge ConneX, Stream Data Centers on 11 Aug; Core Scientific, Vantage, SB Energy, Google, Rowan, CleanSpark on 12 Aug), requiring operators to pay their own electric infrastructure costs, reuse water, avoid residential disruption, forgo taxpayer incentives, and disclose incentives, on-site generation plans and water sourcing before approval, with the PUCT and ERCOT directed to deny approval to non-compliant projects (source: https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-announces-google-rowan-and-cleanspark-commit-to-comply-with-his-data-center-standards, Aug 2026) (source: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/bloomberg-nef-texas-grid-pause-puts-20-percent-of-us-data-center-pipeline-at-risk/, Aug 2026)
- PJM opened a reliability review of large computational load after a 22 Jul 2026 mechanical failure removed a 230 kV line in Northern Virginia and roughly 3,800 MW of data center and crypto-mining load disconnected to on-site backup generation in two waves (2,970 MW then 1,099 MW) on a fault that cleared normally, following comparable 1,500 MW events in Jul 2024 and Feb 2025; PJM is assessing interconnection ride-through standards for large loads (source: https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-dominion-review-large-load-transfer-event/, Aug 2026) (source: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/pjm-considering-changes-to-interconnection-reliability-requirements-for-data-centers-following-mass-outage/, Aug 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Grid exit is being designed in at the largest scale rather than treated as backup: SpaceX stated its TeraFab chip factory in Texas is not expected to use grid energy, running on on-site generation and battery storage (source: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/spacex-to-power-terafab-chip-factory-in-texas-via-on-site-generation-and-battery-storage/, Aug 2026)
- Debt-funded compute capacity continued to close at facility level rather than hyperscaler level, with Lambda raising a $926M senior secured loan facility for GPU acquisition and buildout on 13 Aug 2026 (source: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/lambda-raises-926m-senior-secured-loan-facility/, Aug 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- The state leverage documented above carries a measured cost to the buildout: BloombergNEF assessed that the Texas grid pause puts about 20 percent of the US data center pipeline at risk (source: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/bloomberg-nef-texas-grid-pause-puts-20-percent-of-us-data-center-pipeline-at-risk/, Aug 2026)
- Local refusal remains the binding constraint and is not tracking the federal cost-allocation agenda: a 2.6 million sq ft Air Products site outside Allentown, Pennsylvania was dropped after officials denied the application on 13 Aug 2026, and Hillsboro, Oregon enacted a 120-day moratorium on 12 Aug 2026 that two operators filed under hours before it took effect (source: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/plans-for-26-million-sq-ft-data-center-outside-allentown-pennsylvania-dropped-after-officials-deny-application/, Aug 2026) (source: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/data-center-operators-sneak-in-pair-of-projects-hours-before-moratorium-enacted-in-hillsboro-oregon/, Aug 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- New York Executive Order 62, signed 14 Jul 2026, suspended discretionary DEC permits and approvals for construction or expansion of data centers consuming or capable of consuming 50 MW or more where applications were incomplete before that date, directing DPS to prepare a Generic Environmental Impact Statement, Empire State Development to publish a Community Investment Framework within 60 days, DPS to establish a Data Center Interconnection Working Group within 60 days, and DEC to review water withdrawal regulations within 12 months, with the moratorium running until DPS submits the final GEIS and findings statement and no fixed expiry (source: https://www.governor.ny.gov/executive-order/no-62-establishing-temporary-moratorium-data-centers-new-york-while-state-develops, Jul 2026)
- The Colorado Department of Law filed proposed Automated Decision-Making Technology and Conversational AI Service rules on 11 Aug 2026 and opened written comment from 11 Aug 2026 to 26 Oct 2026, with comments received by 5 Oct 2026 considered for proposed revisions ahead of the rulemaking hearing (source: https://coag.gov/ai/, Aug 2026)

Capital Movements:
- Lambda closed a $926M senior secured loan facility on 13 Aug 2026 for GPU acquisition and facility development (source: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/lambda-raises-926m-senior-secured-loan-facility/, Aug 2026)
- STT GDC secured up to $1.37B on 13 Aug 2026 for a 166 MW data center in Johor, Malaysia, with an initial 16 MW phase (source: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/stt-gdc-secures-up-to-137bn-in-funding-for-166mw-data-center-in-johor-malaysia/, Aug 2026)

Technical Changes:
- Voltage and frequency ride-through performance for computational load is moving from operator practice toward enforceable standard: FERC directed NERC to submit a first phase of large load reliability standards by 31 Dec 2026, with minimum ride-through requirements for computational loads expected in 2027 NERC standards (source: https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-dominion-review-large-load-transfer-event/, Aug 2026)

Contradictions:
- Texas is simultaneously tightening interconnection approval through the Data Center Standards and hosting the largest declared grid-exit project, SpaceX TeraFab, whose stated design avoids the grid the standards govern. Conditioning access to interconnection creates the incentive to not need interconnection, which removes the load from the leverage.
- The same 3,800 MW event supports opposite conclusions. Read as reliability failure it justifies binding ride-through obligations on data centers; read as demand response it is exactly the flexible load behavior FERC's Section 206 orders asked the RTOs to accommodate. The disconnection that destabilized the system is the behavior the cost-allocation track has been trying to procure.
- State executive action on the physical layer accelerated in Texas and New York during the same nine days that Colorado's state authority over the algorithmic layer remained suspended by federal court order and DOJ intervention.
