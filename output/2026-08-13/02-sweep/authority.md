# Evidence sweep, authority, 2026-08-13

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The previous sweep ran on 2026-08-04, nine days ago, and the window is genuinely thin. The Transparency Coalition's 7 Aug 2026 legislative update characterises the period as the quietest week of 2026, with most state legislatures on summer break.
- One substantive new US event: the Colorado Department of Law filed proposed Automated Decision-Making Technology and Conversational AI Service rules on 11 Aug 2026, opening a comment period to 26 Oct 2026. This postdates the previous sweep and is the first implementing rulemaking under SB 26-189.
- New to this corpus though not new in the world: Colorado HB 26-1263, a standalone conversational-AI operator statute signed 29 May 2026 and enforced by the Attorney General under consumer-protection law, was absent from the 2026-08-04 sweep and is now half the subject matter of the Colorado rulemaking.
- No EU AI Act enforcement step against any named general-purpose AI model provider has been announced in the eleven days since the AI Office's powers commenced on 2 Aug 2026. The Commission's own announcement of the milestone names no provider and opens no proceeding.

Confirmed Developments:
- The Colorado Department of Law filed proposed Automated Decision-Making Technology and Conversational Artificial Intelligence Service rules with the Secretary of State on 11 Aug 2026, implementing both SB 26-189 and HB 26-1263, with written comments accepted from 11 Aug 2026 to 26 Oct 2026 and a public rulemaking hearing on 26 Oct 2026; the underlying obligations take effect 1 Jan 2027 (source: https://coag.gov/ai/, Aug 2026) (source: https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/technology/1829704/colorado-publishes-draft-admt-regulations, Aug 2026)
- Colorado HB 26-1263 requires operators of conversational AI services to disclose that the user is interacting with AI rather than a human, estimate user age by commercially reasonable methods, bar points or rewards designed to increase minor engagement, apply technical safeguards against sexual content involving minors, maintain suicide and self-harm response protocols, disclose that outputs are not licensed professional services, and file annual reports with the Attorney General; it was signed 29 May 2026, its general provisions took effect 12 Aug 2026, and operator requirements begin 1 Jan 2027 (source: https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb26-1263, May 2026) (source: https://coag.gov/ai/, Aug 2026)
- California SB 813, which would create a state Artificial Intelligence Standards and Safety Commission to set AI safety standards and auditor qualification criteria, was set for hearing and placed on the Assembly Appropriations suspense file on 5 Aug 2026, with suspense-file votes in both chambers scheduled to begin 13 Aug 2026 (source: https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202520260sb813, Aug 2026) (source: https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/ai-legislative-update-august7-2026, Aug 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Colorado has consolidated algorithmic-discrimination successor rules and chatbot safety rules into a single Attorney General docket, so one state law-enforcement office rather than an AI-specific regulator becomes the operational venue for both instruments (source: https://coag.gov/ai/, Aug 2026)
- California's two appropriations committees deferred nearly all AI bills to suspense rather than deciding them at the 3 and 5 Aug 2026 hearings, concentrating the fate of dozens of measures into votes beginning 13 Aug 2026 (source: https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/ai-legislative-update-august7-2026, Aug 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- The Commission's announcement of the 2 Aug 2026 enforcement milestone names no provider and opens no proceeding, describing instead complaint and whistleblower tools and noting that over 180 organisations have signed the voluntary Code of Practice, so the visible posture after commencement is voluntary adherence rather than enforcement (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august, Jul 2026)
- AI-generated regulatory commentary is fabricating dated, citable-looking enforcement actions: a 1 Aug 2026 "AI Regulation News August 2026" roundup carries no citations, asserts that Colorado's SB-205 algorithmic discrimination rules are "fully active" when that statute was repealed and replaced by SB 26-189 with obligations starting 1 Jan 2027, and describes a California floor vote on 10 Aug 2026 that postdates its own publication date (source: https://cubbbix.com/blog/ai-regulation-august-2026-global-update/, Aug 2026) (source: https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/06/colorado-repeals-and-replaces-its-ai-act, Jun 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- Article 50 transparency obligations took effect on 2 Aug 2026, requiring interactive AI systems to tell users they are dealing with AI rather than a human, deepfakes to be labelled, and AI-generated or altered content to carry machine-readable marks enabling automated detection (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august, Jul 2026)
- Colorado routes conversational-AI oversight through consumer-protection machinery rather than a technology regulator: HB 26-1263 places annual operator reporting and enforcement with the Attorney General, and its implementing rules sit in the same Attorney General docket as the ADMT rules (source: https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb26-1263, May 2026)

Technical Changes:
- The IETF individual submission draft-klrc-aiagent-auth reached revision -03 on 6 Jul 2026, adding co-authors from OpenAI and Okta and updating terminology and security sections; it profiles WIMSE and OAuth 2.0 for agent authentication and delegation, expires 7 Jan 2027, is assigned to no working group, and states on its face that it is not endorsed by the IETF and has no formal standing in the standards process (source: https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-klrc-aiagent-auth/, Jul 2026)
- The OpenID Foundation announced the COAZ Framework and the COAZ-MCP Binding on 31 Jul 2026 as draft specifications for externalised authorization of agent tool calls, explicitly stating that they have not reached Implementer's Draft or Final Version status and must still pass through the Foundation's maturity process (source: https://openid.net/getting-cozy-with-coaz-securing-apis-and-ai-agents-with-standardized-authorization/, Jul 2026)
