# Evidence sweep, learning, 2026-08-13

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- Nine days since the 2026-08-04 sweep. Only two events dated inside the window were located: the National University of Singapore opening OpenAI ChatGPT Edu and Codex to its entire community (11-12 Aug 2026) and the Cohere plus University of Waterloo workplace AI certificate (6 Aug 2026). Everything else in this file is either a corpus gap from before the window or an explicit non-movement.
- OpenAI announced three education plugins on 4 Aug 2026, the same calendar day the previous sweep was written, and the previous sweep did not capture them. This is the first vendor move since Claude for Teachers (14 Jul 2026) that targets students directly rather than only verified educators.
- The previous sweep counted Oklahoma only as one of ten enacted 2026 state bills. Its substantive content was not recorded: SB 1734 constrains what AI may be used for, not merely that districts must write a policy, which is a different regulatory shape from the Ohio and Maryland duties the previous sweep led with.
- Nothing moved on the two things the theme is waiting for. The Florida rule has no filing after 21 Jul 2026 despite the 5 Aug 2026 workshop, and Eedi's second AI tutor RCT (results expected summer 2026) is still unpublished.

Confirmed Developments:
- The National University of Singapore extended OpenAI access from its School of Computing to every student, faculty member and staff member, covering ChatGPT Edu and the Codex coding agent with enterprise-level security controls, under a multi-year partnership that also covers teaching pilots, hackathons and joint research (source: https://edunewsletter.openai.com/p/nus-openai-collaboration, Aug 2026) (source: https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/the-national-university-of-singapore-brings-chatgpt-edu-and-codex-to-all-students-faculty-and-staff, Aug 2026)
- OpenAI shipped three education plugins on 4 Aug 2026: a K-12 Educator plugin for differentiated resource creation aligned to local standards, a College Educator plugin for syllabus revision and multimedia assessment building, and a College Student plugin for guided tutoring, quizzes, flashcards and study guides built from student-selected course materials (source: https://www.forbes.com/sites/rayravaglia/2026/08/04/openai-education-plugins-move-ai-from-answers-to-workflows/, Aug 2026) (source: https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/04/openai_wants_teachers_and_profs_to_foist_their_work_off_on_chatgpt/5283064, Aug 2026)
- Oklahoma SB 1734, the Oklahoma Responsible Technology in Schools Act, was approved by the Governor on 12 May 2026 after passing the Senate 42-0 on 23 Mar 2026 and the House 89-0 on 6 May 2026, and it bars AI tools from being primarily used for grading, discipline or other high-stakes educational decisions, permits parental opt-out without academic penalty, and requires annual disclosure of AI tools in use and student data collected (source: http://www.oklegislature.gov/BillInfo.aspx?Bill=SB1734&Session=2600, May 2026) (source: https://www.k12dive.com/news/4-more-states-require-districts-to-adopt-ai-policies/824749/, Jul 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- NUS is pairing the access expansion with a compulsory first-year module, THE1008 Applied Generative AI: From Prompting to Evaluation, required of all first-year undergraduates from academic year 2026/27, with ChatGPT Edu access starting 31 Aug 2026 (source: https://mothership.sg/2026/08/nus-get-chatgpt/, Aug 2026)
- Charleston County School District launched a districtwide AI policy for the 2026-27 school year that is guidance rather than mandate, leaving adoption to individual teachers, extending AI literacy instruction from early grades through high school, and adding a one-year MagicSchool pilot later in the autumn (source: https://www.live5news.com/2026/08/11/students-return-school-ai-takes-seat-charleston-county-classrooms/, Aug 2026)
- Cohere and the University of Waterloo announced a non-credit AI Transformation and Change Management certificate on 6 Aug 2026, run by Waterloo's Future of Work Institute, combining classroom learning with structured co-op placements on real organizational AI adoption problems (source: https://cohere.com/blog/cohere-university-of-waterloo-announcement, Aug 2026)

Technical Changes:
- The OpenAI education plugins run inside ChatGPT Work rather than consumer ChatGPT, operate on approved course documents through a Learning Commons connection, and hold context across multi-source, multi-deliverable tasks instead of answering single questions (source: https://www.forbes.com/sites/rayravaglia/2026/08/04/openai-education-plugins-move-ai-from-answers-to-workflows/, Aug 2026)
- The NUS deployment combines a vendor product with an institution-built layer, pairing ChatGPT Edu and Codex with the university's own AI-Know platform, and gates rollout through selected course pilots before general undergraduate use (source: https://edunewsletter.openai.com/p/nus-openai-collaboration, Aug 2026)

Contradictions:
- The two education tiers moved in opposite directions in the same window. A university opened institution-wide frontier-model access to every student while the newest K-12 statute on record restricts AI from being the primary basis for grading, discipline or other high-stakes decisions.
- Student-facing tooling shipped ahead of any reported measurement of its effect. OpenAI's College Student plugin generates tutoring, quizzes and study guides, while the largest AI tutoring trial in the field, Eedi's four-arm RCT with 1,525 students, has still published nothing.
- NUS is simultaneously the strongest case for governed expansion and for compulsion: access is voluntary and pilot-gated, but the accompanying generative AI module is mandatory for an entire first-year cohort.
