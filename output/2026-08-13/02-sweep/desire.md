Confirmed Developments:
- US grocery retailers put their assortments inside assistant surfaces in the first week of Aug 2026, with paid placement built in at launch rather than retrofitted: Kroger launched an AI shopping assistant developed with Cooklist across most banners on 4 Aug 2026, carrying product listing ads labeled as sponsored alongside organic results with no additional advertiser setup required and measurement tied to the loyalty data covering 95% of Kroger transactions, and Albertsons launched a Safeway app inside ChatGPT on 6 Aug 2026 where users build carts with an @Safeway mention and are redirected to Safeway's own e-commerce platform to check out, following Albertsons' participation in an OpenAI conversational-advertising pilot in Feb 2026 (source: https://www.modernretail.co/technology/krogers-ai-shopping-assistant-has-ads-at-launch/, Aug 2026) (source: https://www.grocerydive.com/news/albertsons-ai-safeway-plugin-chatgpt-technology/827147/, Aug 2026)
- Google moved Maps from recommendation to transaction assembly on 6 Aug 2026: Ask Maps now finds restaurants and adds items to carts on a spoken instruction, with Square and Toast as launch partners and Uber Eats named as coming, plus hotel discovery on qualitative criteria and event listings with external ticket links; checkout stays outside Maps and payment runs through the restaurant's own system, and the food, hotel and event features are rolling out in the US first (source: https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/order-food-in-ask-maps/, Aug 2026) (source: https://agenticplug.ai/current-state-of-agentic-commerce, Aug 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Two Dutch regulators have concluded that the AI Act's chatbot transparency rule is too abstract to govern conduct and are writing supplementary national guidance: the ACM and the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens closed a joint call for input on 17 May 2026 for a chatbot leidraad covering transparency, answer reliability, accessible human contact and data security, with a draft planned for summer 2026 and a final guideline for autumn 2026 (source: https://www.acm.nl/nl/publicaties/oproep-van-de-acm-en-ap-denk-mee-over-chatbots-bij-klantenservice, Apr 2026)
- The French opinion documents structural closure and answer-set asymmetry that the Anglophone corpus had not recorded: Amazon blocks third-party AI agents from accessing its marketplace, and on identical transport-reservation queries OpenAI cited 42 domains against Gemini's 112, so which merchants exist depends on which assistant is asked (source: https://www.silicon.fr/data-ia-1372/agents-ia-autorite-concurrence-228336, Jul 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- No EU member state is live for ChatGPT advertising as of 6 Aug 2026. OpenAI's Ads Manager availability page lists seven countries (Australia, Canada, Japan, Korea, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and the United States) and Germany is absent from the availability table entirely, five weeks after the announced expansion (source: https://www.notebookcheck.net/ChatGPT-ads-where-they-run-now-and-how-to-switch-them-off.1361301.0.html, Aug 2026)
- Agent-mediated demand remains small where a regulator has measured it directly rather than projected it: AI agents generate less than 5% of French e-commerce traffic as of the Jul 2026 opinion, against a projection that they could capture a quarter of merchant traffic by 2030 (source: https://www.silicon.fr/data-ia-1372/agents-ia-autorite-concurrence-228336, Jul 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- AI Act Article 50 became applicable on 2 Aug 2026, making non-human disclosure a direct EU-wide duty on providers and deployers of systems that interact with people, enforceable by national market surveillance authorities from that date, with notification required from the start of the first interaction in a clear and distinguishable manner unless the AI nature is obvious, and penalties up to EUR 15 million or 3% of global turnover; the Digital Omnibus deferred only the machine-readable marking sub-obligation for AI-generated content to 2 Dec 2026, leaving the interaction-disclosure duty undeferred (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act, Jul 2026) (source: https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-20260729/, Jul 2026)
- France's Autorite de la concurrence published opinion 26-A-05 on 17 Jul 2026 from an own-initiative inquiry opened in Jan 2026 with input from roughly 40 stakeholders. It finds OpenAI, Google and Anthropic held more than 84% of the global AI-agent sector in May 2026, warns of concentration, opacity in purchase processes and loss of informed consumer choice, flags self-preferencing, tying and contractual lock-in by vertically integrated firms, and issues six recommendations, the sixth requiring that agentic-commerce standards be developed and maintained transparently, openly and collaboratively so no dominant actor controls them (source: https://www.autoritedelaconcurrence.fr/fr/avis/relatif-au-fonctionnement-concurrentiel-du-secteur-des-agents-dintelligence-artificielle, Jul 2026)

Contradictions:
- Article 50 now compels an AI system to disclose that it is not human, while the instrument that would govern what that system is commercially incentivised to say, the Digital Fairness Act, has no proposal text and is expected only in Q4 2026.
- The two national authorities closest to enforcing the chatbot disclosure duty say the duty is not concrete enough to answer the operative questions, on the same regime whose penalties reach 3% of global turnover.
- Retailers shipped sponsored placement inside their own assistants in the same fortnight that a competition authority named opacity in agent-mediated purchase processes as its central consumer risk.
- The French opinion measures agent-mediated share below 5% of e-commerce traffic while the same document treats the sector as concentrated enough to warrant six structural recommendations, so the market is being regulated on its projected rather than its observed size.
