# Evidence sweep, responsibility, 2026-08-13

Delta Since Last Sweep:
- The previous sweep ran nine days ago and found responsibility being allocated by statute, administrative guidance and product liability doctrine. On 4 Aug 2026 a US appellate court allocated it directly for the first time, and allocated it to the user: the Ninth Circuit vacated Amazon's preliminary injunction against Perplexity, holding that when a user directs an AI agent, the user and not the agent provider is the party that "accesses" the site under the CFAA and CDAFA. The panel stated on the record that there is little to no existing caselaw on ascribing responsibility for AI agents.
- The previous sweep listed New York among fourteen US states with enacted 2026 companion-chatbot laws. The primary record does not support that: S9051B passed both chambers in Jun 2026 and is with Governor Hochul, who has until 31 Dec 2026 to act, so it is not yet law.
- The individual-remedy lane the previous sweep tracked narrowed rather than widened. The private right of action that appeared in the S9051A amendment was removed from the enacted-through version, leaving Attorney General enforcement only. Oregon SB 1546 remains the outlier the previous sweep identified, not the leading edge of a trend.
- The 2 Aug 2026 date on which the Commission's final Article 73 serious-incident guidance was expected to apply passed inside this window with only the Sep 2025 consultation draft standing.
- No movement in the insurance channel, no new capital event, and no ruling in JCCP 5431 during the nine-day window.

Confirmed Developments:
- On 4 Aug 2026 the Ninth Circuit, in Amazon.com Services, LLC v. Perplexity AI, Inc., No. 26-1444, vacated the 9 Mar 2026 preliminary injunction and remanded, holding Amazon unlikely to succeed on its CFAA and CDAFA claims because the party that accessed Amazon's computers was the user employing Perplexity's Comet Assistant as a tool, not Perplexity; the opinion states "However advanced the Assistant currently is, it is a tool, not a person for statutory purposes," records that "Agentic AI is an emerging technology. There is thus little to no existing caselaw directly dealing with how to ascribe responsibility for AI agents," invokes the rule of lenity on the ground that Amazon's theory would expose end users to criminal liability for using agents, and expressly disclaims creating "a new legal regime governing agentic AI" (source: https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-06-ninth-circuit-rules-on-ai-agent-access-to-third-party-websites-under-cfaa, Aug 2026) (source: https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2026/08/ninth-circuit-lifts-restrictions-on-agentic-ai-accessing-amazon.htm, Aug 2026)
- New York S9051B, restricting AI companion chatbot features for minors, passed the Senate 60-0 on 4 Jun 2026 and the Assembly 137-0 on 5 Jun 2026 and has been delivered to the Governor, who has until 31 Dec 2026 to sign, veto or allow it to become law; enforcement runs to the Attorney General alone, who may seek civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation, and the private right of action carried in the S9051A amendment (injunctive relief, restitution, actual and punitive damages, fees, with rebuttable presumptions of causation where a chatbot encouraged self-harm) does not appear in the B amendment; the restrictions take effect 1 Jan 2027 (source: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S9051/amendment/B, Jun 2026) (source: https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/new-york-lawmakers-pass-kids-ai-chatbot-safety-bill-and-two-transparency-acts, Jun 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- The user-as-responsible-party allocation is being described as creating a monitoring gap rather than closing a liability question: commentary published 5 Aug 2026 argues the user is now legally answerable for an agent's actions while lacking the technical capacity to observe them, cites a consumer trust figure of 14% for autonomous AI purchasing, and characterises the spending limits, merchant allowlists and transaction caps built by Mastercard, Visa and Cloudflare as a private substitute for absent law (source: https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/9th-circuit-browser-analogy-leaves-142824841.html, Aug 2026)
- State legislatures are opening a second responsibility route that runs through process rights rather than damages: Pennsylvania HB 1857 would require businesses to disclose AI use and establish a consumer right to human review in high-impact decisions, and California AB 2 would make platforms liable in damages for failure to exercise ordinary care or skill causing child injury, with AB 2 sitting in Senate Appropriations as of 2 Jul 2026 (source: https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/ai-legislative-update-august7-2026, Aug 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- The Ninth Circuit's allocation is narrower than its framing suggests: the holding is fact-specific and rests on the technical detail that agent traffic was routed through the user's own computer rather than from Perplexity's servers, the panel preserved other theories by noting that "the same conduct could be the basis for other types of claims, such as claims sounding in tort or contract," and Amazon's trademark and state-law claims remain live on remand (source: https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-06-ninth-circuit-rules-on-ai-agent-access-to-third-party-websites-under-cfaa, Aug 2026)
- The strictest per-violation penalty yet written into a US companion-chatbot statute belongs to a bill that gives the harmed individual nothing: New York's $25,000 per violation is recoverable only by the Attorney General after the private right of action was dropped between amendments (source: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S9051/amendment/B, Jun 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- The final version of the Commission's Article 73 serious-incident reporting guidance and template, which the Commission had signalled would apply from 2 Aug 2026, had not been published as of 13 Aug 2026: the Commission's own AI Act regulatory framework page lists no final Article 73 guidance, its most recent entries being the 20 Jul 2026 transparency guidelines, the 9 Jul 2026 opinion on the AI-generated content Code of Practice and a 31 Jul 2026 enforcement notice (source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai, Jul 2026) (source: https://www.lw.com/en/insights/european-commission-publishes-draft-guidance-reporting-serious-ai-incidents, Oct 2025)

Capital Movements:
- None

Technical Changes:
- Agent deployment topology became a liability variable rather than an engineering choice: the Ninth Circuit's CFAA holding is conditioned on the AI provider's servers not communicating directly with the target site, so a client-side agent routed through the user's browser and a server-side agent calling the same site produce different statutory exposure for identical behaviour, and the panel noted that agents with greater autonomy or direct server-to-server communication could still give rise to CFAA and CDAFA liability (source: https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-06-ninth-circuit-rules-on-ai-agent-access-to-third-party-websites-under-cfaa, Aug 2026)

Contradictions:
- The first appellate allocation of responsibility for an AI agent puts it on the user, while every legislative instrument moving in the same window puts it on the provider, and neither route delivers a damages remedy to the person harmed.
- Responsibility now turns on network architecture rather than on capability: a more autonomous agent proxied through the user's machine falls outside the statute while a less autonomous one calling from provider infrastructure may fall inside it.
- New York wrote the largest per-violation penalty in the US companion-chatbot lane and simultaneously removed the mechanism by which the injured person could invoke it.
- The Commission is enforcing Article 50 transparency obligations with penalties from 2 Aug 2026 while the Article 73 machinery for reporting the incidents those obligations exist to surface remains a consultation draft.
