Surprises:
- The claim most likely to resolve early is the one with the least visible
  motion. Article 50 became enforceable on 2 August 2026 with real fine
  exposure, and the AI Office has directly usable powers, yet eleven days in
  there is no named action. The observable pattern is that the EU front-loaded
  the legal switch and back-loaded the enforcement posture, routing early
  pressure through the voluntary Code of Practice on Transparency of AI
  Generated Content instead. This makes governance-2026-08-02 a genuine test of
  whether a signed code substitutes for enforcement rather than precedes it.
- The regulatory action against Grok and X sits entirely in the Digital
  Services Act, not the AI Act, even though the underlying conduct (sexually
  explicit deepfakes) is squarely what Article 50 deepfake labelling addresses.
  The pipeline's governance claims are written almost exclusively against AI
  Act instruments, which risks systematically missing enforcement that arrives
  through DSA, GDPR, or consumer-protection channels. This is an instrument
  blind spot rather than a wrong claim.
