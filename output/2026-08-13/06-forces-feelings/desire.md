# Forces and feelings, desire, 2026-08-13

## A. Systemic Contradictions (The Engine of the Story)

Contradiction 1 (Political vs. Social):
The only party that has actually measured this market has no households in it. France's Autorite de la concurrence ran the comparative queries, counted 42 cited domains against 112 on identical requests, established that agents carry under 5% of French e-commerce traffic, and found that Amazon blocks third-party agents outright. That work reaches French merchants and French consumers. The assistants themselves are operated by three firms holding more than 84% of the global sector, whose answer-set policies are set once and applied everywhere. So the socially binding facts about a household's choice set are produced by a political authority the household has never heard of, in a language it does not read, with recommendations rather than remedies, while its own national institutions have no equivalent number and no habit of going to look. Political knowledge and social exposure are in different countries.

Contradiction 2 (Economic vs. Legal):
The law reaches the label and the economics live in the ranking. Since 2 Aug 2026 an assistant must tell a person it is not human, and a sponsored item must be identifiable as sponsored. Kroger shipped exactly that on 4 Aug 2026, ads marked as sponsored alongside organic results, with no additional advertiser setup, priced and measured against loyalty data covering 95% of the retailer's transactions. Every visible requirement is satisfied. What is not regulated, not disclosed and not recorded is which merchants were candidates before the list was assembled, which is the decision that actually allocates the sale. A fully compliant answer and a fully closed answer are the same artifact, and the compliance is the part the customer can see.

Contradiction 3 (Philosophical vs. Lived Reality):
The stated purpose of an assistant is to serve what you want. The only thing it can operate on is what you have already been recorded wanting, and the record is owned by the party that also profits from what it proposes. In lived terms this does not feel like manipulation, because the recommendations are good: accurate, convenient, appropriate to the household. The gap opens only in the counterfactual, at the edge where someone might have wanted something they had never bought before. That edge has no user interface, produces no notification, and generates no dissatisfaction, so the harm is real and unfelt at the same time, which means nothing in the ordinary machinery of complaint, review or regulation can find it.

## B. The Normalization of the Absurd (The Tone)

Normal Absurdity 1:
Households ask two assistants the same question as a matter of routine, the way an earlier generation checked two weather apps, and treat the disagreement itself as the only usable information. Nobody expects to learn why the two lists differ. The practice has its own etiquette: you quote both when telling a friend where you bought something, and quoting only one reads as slightly credulous.

Normal Absurdity 2:
People keep a second, deliberately empty account for looking at things, and lend it to relatives. Browsing on your own login is understood to be a purchase you have not made yet. Parents tell teenagers to use the clean account for gift shopping, in the same tone they once used about leaving the car unlocked, and nobody finds it strange that looking is the part with consequences.

Normal Absurdity 3:
Small merchants carry a visibility subscription on the same line of the books as rent and refuse insurance, and say so out loud. A chalkboard in a shop lists the month's costs and one of them is being findable. Customers read it, nod, and do not ask who is being paid, because the answer is understood to be nobody they could write to.

## C. The Single, Irreplaceable Thing

The Irreplaceable Thing:
A recommendation from someone with nothing to gain.

Explanation:
In this world every ordering is somebody's revenue: the sponsored slot, the retailer's own catalogue, the visibility subscription, the marketplace that refused access so that its own surface would win. None of it is dishonest and all of it is interested, and after a few years people can feel the difference between an interested suggestion and a disinterested one even when they cannot prove it. What becomes scarce is not information, which is abundant, and not accuracy, which is high. It is the experience of being told about something by a person who does not benefit from your buying it. This is socially producible, which is what makes it worth chasing: a neighbour, a relative, a shop owner talking about a competitor, a stranger at a counter. It cannot be bought at scale, it does not survive being turned into a product, and a household that wants it has to go and be among people to get it.
