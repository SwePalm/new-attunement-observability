# Theme exploration, desire, 2026-08-13

Structural question: In the 6 to 24 months before agent-mediated purchasing is large enough for any household to notice it in its own spending, will an independent party acquire both the standing and the instrument to measure what an assistant's answer set actually contains, or will that answer set be settled commercially first, by merchants shipping paid placement into assistant surfaces and by large marketplaces refusing agents access at all?

Turn 1 – Conceptual Frame

The inherited model of commercial desire is a model of persuasion. Someone wants something vaguely, a seller intervenes with an argument or an image, and the want acquires a shape and a brand. Every consumer-protection instrument of the last century is built on that picture: it polices the intervention. Misleading claims, hidden inducements, unfair commercial practices, all of them assume an act of speech aimed at a person who could in principle have chosen otherwise, and all of them locate the harm in what was said.

Assistant mediation does not fit that picture, and the single most useful number produced this month shows why. On identical transport-reservation queries one assistant cited 42 domains and another cited 112. Nothing in that gap is persuasion. No one was told anything false about the seventy merchants who appeared in one answer and not the other. They were simply not enumerated, and non-enumeration leaves no trace on the person it acts upon. The operative act in an agent-mediated market is omission, and omission is invisible from the demand side by construction: a shopper cannot notice the absence of a shop they have never heard of, and has no way to discover that the absence was a property of the tool rather than of the world.

This displaces the seat of desire. What the system can act on is not what a person wants but what a person can be shown to have wanted, since a preference must be recorded before it can be priced. Kroger's assistant is measured against loyalty data covering 95% of its transactions, which means the instrument that evaluates the recommendation is the same instrument that recorded the appetite. Desire under these conditions is not falsified. It is closed: served accurately against the past, and narrowed at the edge where a person might have become someone slightly different. The classical question was whether a want was authentically yours. The live question is whether the set it was chosen from was ever yours to see.

Turn 2 – Societal Reframing

Read socially, the shift is from a persuasion economy to an enumeration economy, and the distributional consequences fall in a different place than the old ones did. In a persuasion economy the party with money buys attention and the harm lands on the person persuaded. In an enumeration economy the party with money buys presence in an answer, and the first harm lands on whoever is absent from it. That party is not the shopper. It is the merchant small enough that inclusion is a decision someone else makes about them.

Two events from this month describe that structure from opposite ends. Amazon blocks third-party AI agents from its marketplace, which is a large seller deciding that the answer set will not contain it on anyone else's terms. Kroger and Albertsons moved in the other direction inside the same fortnight, putting their assortments into assistant surfaces with sponsored placement built in at launch rather than retrofitted, requiring no additional advertiser setup. Between the refusal and the embrace, the shape of the market is being written by parties large enough to choose a posture, while the ones without a posture to choose are simply ranked.

The most disorienting fact in the corpus is scale. Agents generate less than 5% of French e-commerce traffic, measured, against projections that they might reach a quarter of merchant traffic by 2030. The arrangement is therefore being fixed at a moment when almost nobody is using it, which is not an anomaly but the ordinary way infrastructure settles. Rail gauges, container dimensions and card interchange were all decided before the traffic arrived. The consequence is that the constituency with standing to object does not yet exist. Households cannot mobilise around a mediation they have not adopted, and by the time the mediation matters to a household budget, the terms will be a decade old and defended as settled practice. Meanwhile a competition authority in one member state has produced the only hard number anyone has, in a market whose participants are not French, and its six recommendations reach a jurisdiction that is not where the assistants are being built.

Turn 3 – Psychological Implications

For the person on the demand side, the felt experience is not oppression. It is convenience with a faint and unlocatable wrongness. The assistant is genuinely useful; the basket it assembles is genuinely the one wanted; the substitutions are genuinely reasonable. What is missing has no phenomenology. This is why the emerging behaviours are superstitious rather than strategic: people begin asking two assistants the way one checks two weather apps, not because they can adjudicate the disagreement but because the disagreement itself is the only evidence available that a disagreement is possible.

A second adaptation is the management of one's own record. Once it is common knowledge that recommendation quality and advertising exposure are both computed against a purchase history, the history becomes a thing to curate. Looking at something becomes an act with consequences, and people develop small hygiene rituals to keep exploratory attention off the record. This is a genuinely new form of self-consciousness in ordinary consumption. Browsing used to be the least consequential thing a person could do in a shop. It is now the most legible.

The third effect is subtler and concerns what the disclosures actually answer. Since 2 Aug 2026 an EU system that interacts with a person must tell them it is not human. That duty answers a question people had largely stopped asking, while leaving untouched the one they cannot answer for themselves: whose interest is expressed in this ordering. The Dutch competition and data-protection authorities have said as much in substance, concluding that the rule is too abstract to govern conduct and opening work on a national supplementary guideline covering reliability of answers and access to human contact, in the same period the duty became enforceable. Psychologically, a disclosure that names the speaker but not the interest teaches a durable and corrosive lesson: that the honest answer to the question you care about is structurally unavailable, so you had better stop asking it. What replaces inquiry is trust by default, punctuated by unfocused suspicion, which is the worst of both dispositions and the most likely one to spread.

Turn 4 – Institutional Dynamics

The institutional finding of this run is not about a rule. It is about capacity, and specifically about the capacity to look. France's Autorite de la concurrence opened an inquiry on its own initiative in Jan 2026, took input from roughly 40 stakeholders, ran comparative queries across assistants, counted the domains each one cited, and published opinion 26-A-05 on 17 Jul 2026 with six recommendations, the last requiring that agentic-commerce standards be developed and maintained transparently, openly and collaboratively so no dominant actor controls them. Nothing in that sequence required a complaint, a victim, a statute aimed at agents, or a finding of infringement. It required an institution with a mandate to study a sector before anything has gone visibly wrong, and the technical willingness to run the queries itself.

That is a rare configuration, and its rarity is the structural point. Complaint-driven regimes cannot reach omission, because omission produces no complainant. Enforcement-driven regimes cannot reach it either, since there is no act to enforce against. Only a body that can measure a market unprompted can see an answer set at all, and the number of such bodies operating on this sector is currently one, in one language, with recommendations rather than remedies.

Against that stands a measurement apparatus of extraordinary density on the other side. A retail media network measures its own assistant's advertising performance against its own loyalty data, at 95% transaction coverage, continuously, with the results proprietary. The asymmetry is not close: the seller observes the buyer completely and the buyer observes the seller through periodic foreign scholarship. Meanwhile the instrument that would in principle govern commercial ordering rather than mere disclosure, the Digital Fairness Act, has no proposal text and is indicated for Q4 2026, and the FTC's companion study from Sep 2025 has produced no public output, with its chairman pointing to next year. The institutions with speed have no mandate to look outward, and the institutions with a mandate move on a calendar measured in quarters and years.

Turn 5 – Long-Term Trajectory

Over the next three to five years the likeliest trajectory is not capture and not correction, but hardening by default along lines already drawn. The refusal posture and the embrace posture both consolidate: large marketplaces continue to keep third-party agents out and to run their own, retailers continue to put assortments inside assistant surfaces where placement is a purchasable position, and the intermediate space where an independent agent shops the whole market on a household's behalf stays thin because neither of the large parties has an interest in it existing. The sub-5% figure rises, but the rise is composed mostly of traffic inside surfaces that the seller controls, which means penetration and closure grow together rather than trading off.

Two things could bend that. The first is measurement becoming routine rather than exceptional. If a second and third national authority run the same comparative query exercise, the domain-count gap becomes a time series instead of an anecdote, and a time series is the minimum object that any remedy could be attached to. The evidence gives no reason yet to expect this; there is exactly one such document and its author had to choose to write it. The second is the arrival of an instrument aimed at ordering rather than at disclosure. Here the honest expectation should be slow. A proposal indicated for Q4 2026 is a text in 2027 at the earliest, a negotiation through 2028, and obligations after that, and this pipeline's record on treating announced dates as firm argues for adding time rather than subtracting it.

That leaves the near term to private ordering, which is where the actual hardening will occur: visibility services sold to merchants as the price of appearing, sponsored positions shipped without advertiser setup, and access agreements between assistants and large sellers negotiated bilaterally and never published. Five years out, the plausible world is one where agent-mediated purchase is normal, its answer sets are commercially determined, its terms are private, and the only public account of how any of it works is a small number of studies in languages most of the market does not read.
