Delta Since Last Month:
- First sweep under method v2 (baseline: ungrounded 2026-02 v1 run).
- Responsibility found its legal form in H1 2026: product liability. Courts are treating AI systems as products with defects rather than services with disclaimers, and the supply chain, not just the deployer, is on the hook.

Confirmed Developments:
- A federal court ruled Character.AI's app is a product under product-liability law (strict liability, negligence, and wrongful-death counts survived; First Amendment defense dismissed); Character.AI and Google settled the Garcia case and four related suits in Jan 2026 with committed minor-safety features (source: https://www.awkolaw.com/when-the-chatbot-becomes-the-defendant/, 2026) (source: https://news.northeastern.edu/2026/05/22/chatgpt-lawsuit-ai-ethics/, May 2026)
- The court found Google plausibly liable as a "component part manufacturer" because the underlying LLM allegedly made the app defective, responsibility now reaches through the AI supply chain (source: https://www.awkolaw.com/when-the-chatbot-becomes-the-defendant/, 2026) (source: https://www.klgates.com/AI-Product-Liability-The-Next-Wave-of-Litigation-3-27-2026, Mar 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- AI litigation is consolidating around product-liability theory: design defect, inadequate warning, foreseeable misuse, the doctrinal tools of automobiles applied to conversational systems (source: https://www.klgates.com/AI-Product-Liability-The-Next-Wave-of-Litigation-3-27-2026, Mar 2026)
- Section 230 immunity is eroding for AI outputs: insurers and courts increasingly treat generated content as the provider's own speech, not hosted third-party content (source: https://www.moodys.com/web/en/us/insights/insurance/230-immunity-for-AI-chatbot-lawsuits.html, 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- Statutory responsibility is receding even as judicial responsibility advances: the EU deferred high-risk obligations and the US executive suppresses state rules, courts are carrying the responsibility load alone, case-by-case and slowly (source: https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/, Jun 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- New York's S7263 would make companies liable when chatbots give legal advice illegal for unlicensed persons, state legislatures are drafting liability-specific (not framework) AI statutes (source: https://www.vasquezlawnc.com/blog/ai-chatbot-liability, 2026)

Capital Movements:
- Liability is being securitized: affirmative AI insurance (Armilla, Testudo) and default AI exclusions (ISO CG 40 47/48) are creating a market price for responsibility before courts finish allocating it (source: https://www.theactuary.com/2026/06/25/price-proof-insurance-policies-ai-enabled-world, Jun 2026)

Technical Changes:
- Decision-trace and audit-trail tooling (agent gateways, event logs) is becoming responsibility infrastructure, the technical record determines who can be blamed (source: https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-agent-governance-framework-gap-20260403/, Apr 2026)

Contradictions:
- The component-part theory makes model providers responsible for downstream harms they cannot observe, while deployers claim they cannot be responsible for model behavior they cannot inspect, each layer points at the other.
- Insurers demand logged, auditable systems to grant coverage while the most capable deployments are the hardest to audit.

Ledger Candidates:

- A court applies component-part liability to a frontier model provider in a NEW case (beyond Character.AI/Google) by mid-2027. Resolve by 2027-09. (motivated by: https://www.awkolaw.com/when-the-chatbot-becomes-the-defendant/, 2026)
