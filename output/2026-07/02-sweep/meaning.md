Delta Since Last Month:
- First sweep under method v2 (baseline: ungrounded 2026-02 v1 run).
- Meaning got its first solid 2026 measurements, and they are stratified: AI is making work MORE satisfying for those who master it and hollow for those processed by it, with the divide falling along income, gender, and adoption lines.

Confirmed Developments:
- US job satisfaction hit 69%, the highest since 2010, but gains concentrate among higher-income workers, men, and the AI-confident; women, lower-income, and AI-lagging workers report lower satisfaction (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/survey-job-satisfaction-hits-record-high-despite-growing-divides-across-gender-income-and-ai-use-302788714.html, 2026) (source: https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/research/navigating-ai-in-the-workplace/full-report, 2026)
- 67% of regular AI users say the technology made work more enjoyable; AI power users (30% of employees) report more learning time and stronger team relationships (source: https://www.peoplemanagement.co.uk/article/1960284/two-thirds-workers-using-ai-say-made-work-enjoyable-survey-finds, 2026) (source: https://www.gensler.com/press-releases/global-workplace-survey-2026, 2026)

Emerging Signals:
- Professional identity effects split by sector: IT workers treat AI as a technical peer (sometimes raising satisfaction), while service and healthcare workers experience AI involvement as a threat to core professional identity (source: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28680v1, May 2026)
- Edelman: 54% of low-income respondents believe generative AI will leave them behind, meaning pressure is anticipatory, arriving before displacement does (source: https://www.edelman.com/trust/2026/trust-barometer, Jan 2026)

Counter-Signals:
- The "meaning crisis" framing overreaches: for majorities of active users the lived experience of AI at work is currently positive (enjoyment, learning, connection), erosion is concentrated, not general (source: https://www.gensler.com/press-releases/global-workplace-survey-2026, 2026)

Regulatory Shifts:
- None, meaning has no regulator; its institutional expression is bargaining (union AI clauses) and retention statistics.

Capital Movements:
- None verified this sweep.

Technical Changes:
- None specific; the mechanism is organizational (who gets AI leverage) rather than technical.

Contradictions:
- The same tools produce record satisfaction for one cohort and anticipatory dread for another inside the same companies, aggregate satisfaction statistics conceal the redistribution of meaning.
- Entry-level work is being automated away precisely where career meaning is seeded (first competence, first mastery), while employers report the resulting senior-skill shortage as if unrelated.

Ledger Candidates:
- The satisfaction divide widens: the next annual survey wave shows the gap between AI-confident and AI-lagging workers growing, not converging. Resolve by 2027-07. - At least one major employer introduces explicit "meaningful work" provisions (task-mix guarantees, human-contact quotas, craft-time protections) in response to AI restructuring by mid-2027. Resolve by 2027-09. (motivated by: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28680v1, May 2026)
- Healthcare or service-sector unions cite professional-identity harm (not only job loss) in a formal AI-related dispute or bargaining position by mid-2027. Resolve by 2027-09. (motivated by: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28680v1, May 2026)
