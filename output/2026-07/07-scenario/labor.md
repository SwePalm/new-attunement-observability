## 1. Title & Core Question
- Title: The Last First Job
- Core Question: When firms liquidate the roles where people learn judgment while demanding judgment at entry, who is left to become senior, and who answers for the decision if the substitution story turns out to have been partly a story?

## 2. Context Summary (Translation Layer) – Why This Future Exists
The restructuring wave of the mid-2020s ran ahead of its own evidence. Six-figure annual job cuts carried AI attribution that markets rewarded on announcement, while measured returns stayed stubbornly uncorrelated with the reductions. Entry-level hiring contracted hardest, support desks, junior code, data operations, because those tasks were the most routinizable and the people in them the least protected. Employment overall held: healthcare, government, and hospitality absorbed workers, and a stratum of agent-supervisors emerged, fewer and more loaded than the teams they replaced. What broke was quieter: the apprenticeship layer through which working judgment had always been formed. By decade's end, firms demand senior discernment at entry, educators are asked to synthesize what workplaces once grew, and the only workers whose restructurings require advance justification are the minority whose contracts say so.

## 3. Future World Snapshot (The Lived Experience) – A Day in This Future
Dele is twenty-four and holds the job title his university's career office calls a unicorn: junior analyst, actual entry level, at a regional insurer whose union contract, one of the famous "first-rung clauses", obliges the firm to maintain apprenticeship seats at one per ten agent-supervised workflows. His badge says TRAINEE in letters he has stopped being embarrassed by. Friends from his cohort carry "agent-portfolio manager" titles at firms where they have never been supervised by anyone; they credential supervision without having received it, and they text him questions at night that their own dashboards can't answer.

His morning is spent shadowing Ruth, who spans what the org chart calls a pod and what she privately calls "my former department." Ruth approves agent dispositions on injury claims: forty a morning, each one a judgment the system frames but a human must own. Her screen carries the standard fatigue meter, mandated after the span-of-control arbitrations, which she ignores professionally. She thinks aloud as she works because the contract says formation time is work time, and thinking aloud is how judgment is transmitted.

At lunch the floor watches the quarterly call. The CEO announces expanded AI investment, "role rebalancing" of two hundred positions, and a search for senior claims talent, consecutive sentences, no questions. On the floor, the attribution guess runs three-to-one: balance sheet, not substitution. HR's channel posts the notice-and-bargain timeline within the hour; the union's AI committee has fourteen days to receive the workflow evidence.

Afternoon brings Dele's supervised error. He mis-weights a contested liability estimate; the agent flags the divergence; Ruth lets the error run to its consequence in the sandbox, then walks him backward through his own reasoning. It costs the firm ninety minutes of two salaries. It is, both of them know, the entire point of the clause, the expensive, unautomatable purchase of a person who will one day be worth trusting.

## 4. Behavioral Shifts (Human Lens) – How People Adapt
Workers learn to read restructurings the way analysts read earnings: attribution skepticism is now a workplace literacy, and the private "AI-attribution guess" accompanies every announcement. The young split into strategies, credential-stackers racing the frontier with certificates, and rung-hunters who accept lower pay at the shrinking set of employers known to actually form people. "Was trained by a human" quietly enters CVs as a differentiator.

Retained seniors adapt to supervisory solitude by re-creating collegiality sideways: cross-firm guilds of agent-supervisors who share disposition dilemmas the way house officers once shared cases, because no one inside their own building holds comparable judgment. And union AI committees become the most sought-after assignment in organized workplaces, the one seat from which the restructuring story must be shown, not just told.

## 5. Structural Forces (System Lens) – What Holds This World Together
Three forces stabilize this world. First, bargaining as labor law: with statutes deferred and state action suppressed, notice-and-bargain clauses, severance formulas, and first-rung quotas spread contract by contract, enforceable, fast, and covering only the organized. Second, the reliability floor: agents still fail enough in production that human exception-handling is structurally required; every "automated" process quietly re-employs judgment at its edges, which is where the remaining careers live. Third, the deferred-cost ledger: the senior-skill shortage created by liquidating junior pipelines arrives on a lag, and firms that kept formation capacity, often because a contract obliged them, command premiums in the talent market that finally price what the announcements never booked. The system's tension is that the second and third forces reward exactly what the first force protects, but only after years the quarterly cycle does not see.

## 6. Reflection & Implications – Questions This World Asks Us
If judgment is learned only through supervised error, what is the minimum viable apprenticeship a society must preserve, and who is obliged to fund it?
When a restructuring's stated reason cannot be verified for years, what would honest labor-market communication even look like?
Is "entry level" a job category or a social institution, and did we notice which one we were dismantling?

## 7. Pullback Layer: From Possibility to Probability
### 7.1 Signals Emerging (Plausible Zone) – Early Signals We Already See
- 123,000+ H1-2026 US tech job cuts with AI the most-cited reason; Salesforce support 9,000→5,000 with agents; Klarna and Paycom analogous.
- US entry-level postings down ~35% since early 2023, junior software/data down as much as 67%; ~14% lower job-finding rate for young workers in exposed occupations.
- ~80% of executives report reductions with no measured ROI correlation ("budget room, not return"); analysts describe AI as partial scapegoat for over-hiring.
- Entry roles 7x more likely to demand senior skills (judgment, leadership); only 23% of graduates AI-trained while 47% report AI-changed hiring.
- 58 NewsGuild contracts with AI clauses; Culinary Union bargain-before-deployment and tech-layoff severance; 38% of union members report automated-management provisions.
- Frontier models failing ~1 in 3 production attempts, the reliability floor that preserves exception-handling work.

### 7.2 Probable Direction (Near-Term Future) – Where We're Likely Headed
The likeliest near-term path is a blended verdict arriving in stages: genuine substitution consolidates in bounded support and junior-cognitive domains, narrative excess surfaces as scattered public reversals (rehiring, reinstated human support) around 2027–2028, and the senior-formation shortfall becomes an acknowledged cost category by decade's end. Bargaining coverage grows but remains a minority phenomenon; statutory response lags the whole window (per CAL-002, announced regulatory timelines deserve discount). Applying CAL-001's lesson, this scenario's own claims are deliberately falsifiable and already logged: full-year 2026 AI-cited layoffs above 150,000 (labor-2026-07-01), entry-level postings still ≥25% below baseline through mid-2027 (labor-2026-07-02), five more major AI bargaining agreements (labor-2026-07-03), and at least one public reversal (labor-2026-07-04). The grading of those four claims is the scenario's own audit.

### 7.3 Preferred Path (Intentional Future) – The Path We Could Choose Instead
- Make displacement claims auditable: require firms attributing reductions to AI to report the substitution outcome (what the agents now do, at what error rate) within 24 months.
- Fund the first rung directly: apprenticeship tax treatment or wage subsidies for genuinely supervised entry roles, priced against the deferred senior-shortage cost.
- Extend notice-and-bargain norms beyond union coverage via procurement: public buyers require workforce-transition plans from AI-restructuring vendors.
- Build formation into agent deployment: certify "teaching workflows" where human oversight seats double as training seats.
- Treat young-worker exposure as a tracked public statistic, not a private externality, publish cohort job-finding rates alongside employment data.

## 8. Connect to Today
### Skills We May Need
- Supervising what you did not produce: owning agent output at span without losing the thread of judgment.
- Teaching aloud, making tacit reasoning transmissible during supervised work, since formation time is now a negotiated resource.
- Reading a restructuring: distinguishing substitution, allocation, and narrative in corporate communication.
- Negotiating AI clauses, notice, evidence, severance, first-rung quotas, before deployment.
- Designing careers without ladders: sequencing one's own formation when no employer owns it.

### Signals & Refractions
- Cross-firm supervisor guilds already exist in embryo as practitioner communities for AI-oversight roles.
- "Trained by humans" as a premium claim has a present-day echo in firms advertising human customer support as a feature.
- The fatigue meter descends from today's span-of-control and automated-management bargaining provisions.

## 9. Final Insight
The deepest cost of the restructuring wave was never the jobs eliminated; it was the verification never demanded and the formation never replaced. A labor market can absorb displacement, 2026's aggregate numbers prove it. What it cannot absorb silently is a decade without first rungs: the judgment shortage arrives later, unattributed, in the exact executives' vocabulary, as a talent problem, a quality problem, a "nobody wants to work" problem, never as the receipt for a decision that was cheered when announced. The last first job is not a nostalgia; it is infrastructure. Societies that treat it that way will own the seniors of 2035.
