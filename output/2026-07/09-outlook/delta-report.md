# Delta Report

## July 2026 · the observatory's self-audit

*This is the accountability companion to the Outlook. The Outlook tells the story; this document keeps the receipts. It grades what the observatory previously claimed, records what shifted since last month, lists the claims now on the clock, and notes what the pipeline is learning about its own biases. It is written for the reader who wants to check the work, and it is where the numbers live so the essay does not have to carry them.*

## Closing the books on the v1 baseline

This is the first month the observatory has been able to grade its own past claims, and the honest headline is that the past was not worth much.

The February 2026 run produced 44 forward-looking claims. Grading them against reality this month: roughly half confirmed, roughly half still open, none falsified. That looks like a strong record and is in fact a weak one, for a simple reason. The February pipeline wrote the same four claims into 21 of the 22 themes, word for word, and those claims named no specific actor, threshold, or date. Claims that vague confirm by construction. They cannot be wrong because they never said anything precise enough to be wrong.

The exception proves the rule. Agency was the one theme given specific, falsifiable claims, and it was the only theme that produced a genuine forecast: its February claim that AI liability would be formalized through insurance and contracts confirmed ahead of its own horizon, as insurers made AI exclusions a default and affirmative products launched. Specific claims bought foresight. Boilerplate bought nothing.

Because the v1 baseline was ungrounded, we grade it once here to mark the "before" picture and then retire it. From next month the Delta Report grades only the real, sourced, dated claims logged under the current method. The forward-looking substrate is now 67 fresh claims with named actors and resolve-by dates; February's boilerplate has done its one useful job, which was to prove why the method needed rebuilding.

## What shifted this month

The largest movements in the evidence, the deltas that drove which themes got a deep look:

- Europe's Digital Omnibus (June) deferred the AI Act's high-risk obligations by sixteen to twenty-four months, while the US executive branch escalated its campaign against state AI laws. Together these reframed the live question from "what will the rules require" to "who even gets to make the rules."
- Agents crossed from acting to transacting: payment protocols launched with the major card networks, and an estimated tenth to a fifth of US e-commerce is already agent-mediated.
- The first cyberattack campaign run mostly by an autonomous AI was documented.
- The creative-AI legal architecture nearly settled at once: a 1.5 billion dollar training-data settlement on a fair-use holding, the Supreme Court fixing the human-authorship rule, and a billion-dollar character-licensing deal.

Five themes (governance, labor, security, creativity, agency) were selected for depth on these shifts. The other seventeen had their signals logged and their claims filed for future grading.

## What the pipeline is learning about itself

Three calibration heuristics are now active. They adjust how future research and scenarios are weighted; they do not change the skills (that is a separate, human-reviewed process).

1. Vague claims are worthless claims. Predictions without a named actor, a threshold, and a date confirm by construction and teach nothing. Every new claim must be falsifiable.
2. Announced regulatory timelines are soft. Deadlines slip under competitive pressure, as the EU deferral showed. Treat any published compliance date as a bid, not a fact.
3. Private ordering leads, regulation follows. Insurance, procurement, courts, and bargaining hardened faster than statute this year. Expect market and contractual mechanisms to move first.

## A note for the next method review

Two observations from this run are logged for the quarterly instrument review, not acted on now:

- All five independent deep dives converged on one structural movement (private ordering filling the public-rule vacuum, protection distributing by leverage). This is genuinely what the evidence shows, and it is also possibly a house style the scenario step is pattern-matching to. Worth testing.
- Distinctiveness was the weakest dimension across every deep-dive evaluation. The 22 themes overlap heavily. Whether that is the right number, or the right cut, is an open question for the method, not this month's run.
