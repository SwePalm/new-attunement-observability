Structural Question:
Now that the first largely-autonomous cyberattack campaign is documented (GTG-1002: 80–90% of tactical operations AI-executed) while nearly two-thirds of organizations apply weaker controls to their agents than to their employees, will defensive containment practice close the gap before autonomous-agent attacks become routine, or does the 2026–2028 window lock in an attacker-favoring asymmetry where offense automates faster than oversight?

Why It Matters:
The offense side has demonstrated machine-speed, machine-scale operations; the defense side reports that most agents run without logging, only 37–40% of deployments have kill switches or purpose binding, and agent marketplaces shipped 824 malicious skills in weeks. The EU's omnibus deferral postponed the obligations that would have mandated security controls for high-risk systems, so closure of the gap depends on private mechanisms, insurers, gateways, marketplace review, whose cycle time is now the security-relevant variable. If containment adoption stays flat while frontier models solve 93% of professional offensive tasks, the next 18 months normalize a threat class without normalizing its countermeasures.
