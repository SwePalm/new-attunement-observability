Structural Question:
As AI-cited job cuts pass six figures while independent analysis finds no ROI correlation for workforce reductions, will the 2026–2028 restructuring wave prove to be genuine task substitution or a narrative-driven over-correction — and will the institutions that absorb its costs (workers, entry-level pipelines, public safety nets) gain any mechanism to make displacement decisions accountable before the answer is known?

Why It Matters:
H1 2026 delivered 123,000+ US tech job cuts with AI as the most-cited reason, entry-level postings down ~35% from 2023, and named companies (Salesforce, Klarna, Paycom) replacing support staff with agents — yet ~80% of executives report reductions with no measured correlation to improved returns, and analysts openly describe AI as partial cover for over-hiring corrections. If substitution is real, the career on-ramp is being permanently removed and education/bargaining systems have 12–24 months to adapt; if it is narrative, firms are trading irreplaceable junior pipelines for a story investors reward — and will hit a senior-skill shortage they created. The union channel (58 NewsGuild contracts, Culinary Union, Microsoft units) is currently the only mechanism forcing that decision to be justified before it is executed.
