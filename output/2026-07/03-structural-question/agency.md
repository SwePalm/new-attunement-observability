Structural Question:
Now that AI agents can transact — with live payment protocols, card-network purchase protection, and an estimated 10–20% of US e-commerce already agent-mediated — will the next five years see human agency over consequential decisions genuinely preserved through bounded delegation, or does the combination of transacting agents, persistent preference-modeling, and lagging containment (37–40%) quietly shift the locus of everyday economic decision-making from people to their delegates?

Why It Matters:
Agency's February seeds were the corpus's only specific claims and graded 3/4 confirmed — including liability formalization arriving ahead of its horizon — so this theme is where the pipeline has the most credibility and the most reason to look closely. In H1 2026 agents crossed from acting to transacting: the Machine Payments Protocol launched with Visa/Mastercard/Amex, agent purchase protection debuted, and agents began mediating a fifth of some commerce flows. Delegation is expanding through the exact tiered pattern the evidence supports, but containment controls lag badly and preference inference from persistent memory means the agent increasingly shapes the choice set it "executes." The 0–5 year question is whether tiered delegation keeps humans meaningfully in command of consequential choices or gradually relocates the formation of preference and the moment of decision into systems people nominally direct.
