### Political
Core Shift Thesis:
Public AI rulemaking is in active retreat in both major Western blocs — by deferral in the EU, by federal suppression of state law in the US — making "who may govern AI" itself the primary political contest.
Forces:
1. EU Digital Omnibus (approved Jun 2026) defers Annex III obligations to Dec 2027 and Annex I to Aug 2028, recasting enacted statute as negotiable under competitiveness pressure.
2. US executive order (Dec 2025) operationalizes negative governance: DOJ AI Litigation Task Force (Jan 2026) sues states, and $42B BEAD funding is conditioned on repeal of state AI laws.
3. Bipartisan congressional resistance to broad preemption leaves a stalemate: no federal framework advances while state frameworks are besieged — governance vacuum as policy outcome.
4. China's operative Deep Synthesis watermarking mandate creates an asymmetry where the most binding AI content rules run in the least liberal jurisdiction, weakening the West's "values-based governance" narrative.

### Economic
Core Shift Thesis:
Governance obligations are being repriced from compliance costs (fixed, statutory) into transaction costs (variable, contractual), concentrating rule-setting power in large buyers and insurers.
Forces:
1. ISO's default AI exclusions in commercial general liability (CG 40 47/48) force every AI-deploying firm to either buy specialty coverage (Armilla, Testudo) or self-insure the gap — insurance terms become de facto deployment rules.
2. Procurement gates scale governance through supply chains: audit rights, incident reporting, and documentation demands are now standard vendor-contract terms, enforced by deal flow rather than regulators.
3. Early-compliance investments stranded by the omnibus deferral teach the market that regulatory diligence is a competitive disadvantage, discounting all future announced deadlines.
4. Compliance-readiness budgets are being reallocated toward litigation reserves and insurance premiums — from preventing harm to pricing it.

### Social
Core Shift Thesis:
Protection under private ordering tracks bargaining power, creating a two-tier society of governed relationships (buyers, unionized workers) and ungoverned exposure (consumers, applicants, gig workers).
Forces:
1. Union AI clauses (58 NewsGuild contracts; Microsoft notice provisions; Culinary Union bargain-before-deployment) give ~10% of US workers enforceable deployment rules the other 90% lack.
2. The 123,000+ H1-2026 AI-cited job cuts proceeded outside any governance framework, concentrating the costs of the interregnum on the least-protected.
3. Trust polarization hardens: 34% comfortable with government AI use, 54% of low-income respondents expecting to be left behind (Edelman Jan 2026) — legitimacy erodes fastest where protection is thinnest.
4. Recourse uncertainty suppresses contestation: people who cannot tell which rules protect them behave as if none do, reducing appeals and masking failure rates.

### Technological
Core Shift Thesis:
Control-plane technology (gateways, logs, kill switches) is becoming the enforcement medium for private governance — rules are implemented as configurations, not statutes.
Forces:
1. Agent gateways that policy-check every tool invocation make contractual obligations technically enforceable at runtime, turning insurance and procurement terms into executable code.
2. Audit-trail and decision-trace tooling determines what can be proven and therefore who can be blamed — evidentiary capability becomes governance capability.
3. Only 37–40% of organizations report containment controls (purpose binding, kill switches) versus ~81% deploying agents — the control-plane deployment gap is the operative governance gap.
4. Persistent agent identity frameworks (registered-agent purchase protection, Apr 2026) bind delegated systems to accountable principals, prefiguring a private registration regime.

### Legal
Core Shift Thesis:
Courts are the only public institution whose AI authority grew in H1 2026 — product-liability doctrine now reaches through the AI supply chain, substituting case-by-case deterrence for absent statute.
Forces:
1. The Character.AI product classification (strict liability; First Amendment defense dismissed) and Jan 2026 settlement make consumer AI systems defect-analyzable products.
2. Component-part liability reaching Google as model provider extends responsibility upstream, forcing every layer of the stack to price downstream harms it cannot observe.
3. Section 230 erosion for generated content converts platforms' AI outputs into their own speech, removing the immunity architecture the internet economy assumed.
4. DOJ preemption litigation against state laws will produce federalism precedents that outlast the AI question, raising the constitutional stakes of every filing.

### Environmental
Core Shift Thesis:
The governance vacuum extends to AI's physical substrate: ~$700B in 2026 datacenter capex proceeds under energy-planning regimes never designed for it, making grid capacity an ungoverned constraint.
Forces:
1. Electricity demand from AI infrastructure is outrunning planning and permitting systems, forcing competitor cooperation structures that strain antitrust boundaries.
2. Energy scarcity is becoming a de facto siting regulator — the binding constraint on AI expansion is increasingly the grid interconnection queue, not any AI-specific rule.
3. Long-term hyperscale tenancy concentrates infrastructure risk in a handful of landlords, without any resilience obligations attached to that concentration.
